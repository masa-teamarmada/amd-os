## Chapter-by-chapter criticism

### Ch00 Prologue

The opening scene is strong. The book finally starts with a problem that can move: one useful sentence becomes too strong and begins to travel.

Weakness: after the first scene, the chapter still stops to define the book. The definition is not wrong, but it asks the reader to accept the thesis before they have earned enough fear. The paragraph stacking disclosure, customer truth, CEO function, small earning, J-curve, and survival probability should later be compressed or split into artifacts discovered across Act I-II.

Order: keep the opening intact. Shorten the definition section after Act III is rewritten.

### Ch01 Research Results Are Not Companies

The chapter has a clean narrative beat: `面白いですね` becomes `顧客候補`, then the budget-owner question weakens the sentence in a trustworthy way.

Weakness: the survival / earning / J-curve directive arrives too early as direct explanation. It belongs here as foreshadowing, but the real emotional payload should occur in Ch06-Ch09 through a paid-signal event.

Order: keep the budget-owner scene. Later move part of the small-revenue argument either into Ch06's attempted payment scene or a toolkit note.

### Ch02 Different Clocks

The first half is working. The Friday deck deadline creates a believable compression of research, IP, corporate, support-program, and investor clocks.

Weakness: the middle section becomes an elegant stakeholder list. The reader no longer has to infer; the narrator tells them what each clock means.

Order: let the memo do more work. Shorten the stakeholder explanation and make more of the conflict appear as comments on the deck.

### Ch03 Support Can Isolate Researchers

The four-room opening is one of Act I's best pieces of structure. The researcher is asked to be more entrepreneurial, more cautious, more specific, and more CEO-like in one week.

Weakness: after that, the chapter explains support fragmentation in a way the scene has already shown. The public-body `Act I` label must go. The CEO-binary setup overlaps with Ch04.

Order: in the later surgical pass, cut the architecture label, compress support-menu explanation, and leave Ch04 to handle the full emotional displacement.

### Ch04 GAP / VC / CEO Function

This chapter is the clearest evidence that Act II rewrite worked. The researcher hears praise and displacement back-to-back, and the external CEO question becomes a trust problem before it becomes a role map.

Weakness: the chapter still briefly risks solving the pain too quickly by listing functions. The function list is useful, but Act III/Ch08 is the better place for the full responsibility map.

Order: keep the researcher's line about fearing where they will be removed. Protect it. It is the emotional center of the CEO-function theme.

### Ch05 Before Disclosure

This is the strongest chapter in Act I-II.

The send reservation, slide eight, red/yellow/blue marks, and researcher's silence make disclosure feel like a live accident, not a compliance lecture. It also ties back to Ch01 cleanly: the same conversion from interest to proof now threatens IP and trust.

Weakness: the red/yellow/blue protocol is close to a toolkit entry, but because it is physically used in the scene, it can remain in the main body for now. Later appendix can hold the formal protocol.

Order: use Ch05 as the standard for Act III. Every tool should enter because a character needs it under pressure.

### Ch06 Incorporation Timing

The opening is strong. `登記だけ先に` after a stopped send is exactly the right temptation. The researcher's question about responsibility gives the chapter a concrete hinge.

Weakness: the paid-signal / J-curve section is important but not yet fully embodied. Two opinions appear, then the narrator explains the balance. The scene needs a concrete outside event: a quote request, PoC budget comment, tiny paid analysis, or low-ceiling service offer that forces the distinction.

Second weakness: the ending names judgment labels just before Ch07 currently explains those labels. That creates a high risk of falling back into textbook mode.

Order: Act III should begin with the 90-day WAIT ledger already alive, not with the five labels as vocabulary.

## Act III rewrite orders

1. Start Ch07 after the WAIT decision, not with `GO / WAIT / HOLD / NO_GO / RESOURCE_SHIFT` as a glossary. Open on day 12 or day 37 of the 90-day WAIT ledger, when someone has failed to reach the budget owner or has received a revealing answer.

2. Make WAIT visible as work. Show owner, return condition, review date, and "what we will not do this month" being used in a tense meeting. The reader should see WAIT cost social capital, not merely read that WAIT is active.

3. Convert the judgment labels into behavior. `GO` is a decision to carry a specific risk. `WAIT` is a dated work plan. `HOLD` is a short information bridge. `NO_GO` is a protected alternative route. `RESOURCE_SHIFT` is a cut in attractive activity. Do not introduce them as a table before the case needs them.

4. Rewrite Ch08 around one concrete responsibility failure. Use the existing `company management practice` blank cell from Ch07/Ch08, but make someone assume the researcher's name fills it, then show why that is false. Bring back Ch04's fear of being removed from the center.

5. Move Ch08's responsibility allocation table to the toolkit unless the main body shows a partially filled version being argued over in-scene. The main narrative should follow who gets uncomfortable when a blank remains blank.

6. Rewrite Ch09 as evidence update, not investor-prep advice. The investor should ask a sharp question that reveals one low condition. The team should return with a changed deck sentence, a changed budget-owner line, or a changed funding-use statement.

7. Make the small earning directive pay off in Ch09. Use a budget-owner email or PoC-fee discussion to separate "evidence that raises survival probability" from "low-ceiling work that narrows the company." This is where Ch06's thesis becomes story.

8. Rewrite Ch10 as relationship repair after an actual bruise. The researcher, university-side reviewer, young lead, or external CEO candidate should have a reason to lose trust. The learning log should appear because the relationship would otherwise break.

9. Keep tables out of the Act III main body by default. The current Ch07-Ch10 tables are useful material, but most should become Field Toolkit entries. In the narrative, show one filled record at a time, under pressure.

10. End Act III with the reader wanting a map. The point is not to teach every decision tool. The point is to make the reader feel: technology, customer, IP, founder function, institution, capital, and survival evidence are all partially true and conflicting. Only then should Act IV introduce readiness axes / BZM as relief.
