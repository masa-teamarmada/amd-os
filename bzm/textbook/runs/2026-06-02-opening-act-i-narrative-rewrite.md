## Executive verdict

Act I was rewritten from "scene plus explanation plus table" into a continuous opening movement. The new opening follows one recurring incident: a deck sentence becomes slightly too strong, then travels into customer evidence, clock collisions, and unsupported responsibility.

This pass does not make the whole book sale-ready. It establishes the shape the rest of the book should now follow: consequence before framework, artifact inside scene, and theory only after the reader has felt why a map is needed.

## What changed in Act I

- The Prologue no longer opens with a general explanation of Before Zero. It opens with applause after a pitch and one overstrong deck sentence.
- Chapter 1 follows the sentence as it turns corporate interest into overclean customer evidence.
- Chapter 2 continues the same artifact into a Friday deck deadline, where research, IP, company, support-program, and investor clocks collide.
- Chapter 3 moves from the clock collision into researcher isolation, showing how more support can still leave the center responsibility unowned.

## Narrative spine used

1. A young commercialization lead strengthens one sentence in a deck.
2. The sentence works in the room.
3. The same sentence begins to travel through enterprise meetings, support materials, university review, and investor-style questions.
4. The project looks more company-like than its evidence allows.
5. The researcher does not reject commercialization, but starts to feel the project moving away from the truth they can safely stand behind.
6. Act I ends by requiring the next chapter's CEO-function decomposition.

## Chapter-by-chapter rewrite summary

### 00 Prologue

Old function: define Zero and Before Zero, then state the reader contract.

New function: start with applause, one overstrong sentence, and the realization that a useful phrase can carry a promise too early. Before Zero is defined only after the reader sees why the phase matters.

### 01 Research results are not companies

Old function: explain the difference between research strength and company readiness, with tables classifying customer signal.

New function: follow an enterprise meeting where "interesting" gets turned into customer proof, then show the missing budget owner and validation condition. Survival probability and earning capability enter as questions, not a finished framework.

### 02 Different clocks

Old function: explain stakeholder clock differences and list them in a table.

New function: use one Friday deck deadline to show five clocks colliding through contradictory comments on the same document.

### 03 Support can isolate researchers

Old function: explain that support menus do not automatically integrate responsibility.

New function: move the researcher through four rooms in one week, show support as locally rational but globally fragmenting, and pull directly into the next CEO-function chapter.
