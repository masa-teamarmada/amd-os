## What improved

Ch01 now understands that the danger is not the phrase `顧客候補` by itself. The
danger is that the phrase travels. Once it appears in a support progress memo
and interview practice, the correction becomes socially expensive. That is the
right kind of cost.

Ch06 finally makes "do not chase small money blindly" feel like a decision
rather than a thesis. The short-term paid analysis is not a strawman. It helps
the support interview, reassures the researcher, and could be shown to external
people. Refusing it now creates a higher burden for the next ninety days.

Ch09 now has real narrative pressure. The investor has seen the old version,
asks when the wording changed, and forces the young commercialization lead to
explain why non-payment is not hidden. This is exactly the kind of public-
facing consequence the previous review asked for.

Ch10 helps because it refuses instant healing. The researcher skips one
practice session, the next corporate email is delayed, and the learning log is
framed as a tool for not pretending the relationship is fine.

Ch19 gives J-curve/IPO ambition a rational advocate. That keeps the book from
turning support people or investor-experienced actors into villains. The
RESOURCE_SHIFT section also now names attractive things that are cut, which is
much closer to a real decision.

## Where it still reads like responsible explanation

Ch08 starts with a good authority conflict, then expands into a broad role map.
The blank cell and the question "who may bring back weak facts?" are strong
enough. The chapter does not need to restate every actor's proper domain in
full. After the authority conflict, the prose becomes responsible, correct, and
slower than the scene deserves.

Ch13 still spends too much time proving that external CEO is not replacement.
The new beat, where the candidate first strengthens the payment-refusal story
and then repairs it, is strong. But the chapter repeats the same first-ninety-
days sheet and founder-function distribution already present elsewhere.

Ch18 tries to solve the overlap by saying the repeat-back is not new, only a
translation into theory language. That helps editorially, but it also admits
the problem. A reader still sees another meeting, another repeat-back, another
ninety-day role paper, and another "FRL is not personality scoring" passage.

Ch19's RESOURCE_SHIFT is better, but the artifact is still mostly narrated. The
reader should be able to see the actual subtraction memo: what got crossed out,
who objected, what date changed, and what downstream chapter inherits the cost.

## Actor rationality check

Supporters are not villainized. Ch06 and Ch08 now let supporters argue that
visible sales, stronger wording, and forward momentum matter. That is good.
They are not stupid; they are responding to reporting, confidence, and program
momentum.

Investor-experienced actors are treated fairly in Ch19. The J-curve defense is
not a strawman. The text understands that over-caution can shrink a genuinely
large opportunity.

Universities and industry-collaboration staff remain mostly rational, but Ch08
should be careful not to make them the source of permission for every weak fact.
Their real job is not just saying yes to honesty; it is holding institutional
risk, IP order, and researcher trust together.

External management candidates are safer than before. Ch13 shows the candidate
repairing the over-strong wording and also being frustrated because strong
language is a learned professional weapon. That is humane. Ch18 should not
repeat the same test so often that the candidate starts to feel like a device.

Researchers are better protected. The strongest improvement is that their
silence and distance are not treated as irrational resistance. The manuscript
now shows that language written around the researcher's work can move faster
than the researcher can consent to.
