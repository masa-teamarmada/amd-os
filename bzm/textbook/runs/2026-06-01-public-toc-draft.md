## 1. Editorial Thesis

The public book should not begin with BZM theory or with AMD. It should begin
with the moment readers already recognize: a strong research result is present,
everyone says "startup," but nobody has yet named what must be decided before a
company can safely exist.

Reader promise:

- Industry collaboration staff and URA can see what to ask before pushing a
  research result toward incorporation.
- Researchers can understand why commercialization pressure feels confusing,
  and what should be separated before accepting the startup frame.
- Young venture builders can learn where founder function, capital, IP,
  institution, and timing break before "startup execution" begins.

Public stance:

- Field-first, then theory.
- Anonymous and generalized scenes, not event reports.
- Author/company presence as evidence and craft background, not the subject.
- BZM parameters introduced first as field factors, then formalized later.

## 5. Author / Company Presence Policy

The author/company should appear as:

- One short author note: the book is based on field experience supporting
  research commercialization before company formation.
- Occasional methodological disclosure: "the model was developed from field
  practice, literature, and retrospective case analysis."
- Acknowledgements and appendix notes where needed.

The author/company should not appear as:

- Repeated chapter subject.
- Proof by internal authority.
- A product pitch for AMD OS.
- A claim that readers should adopt AMD's internal workflow.
- A named actor inside anonymized field scenes unless a deliberate public case
  permission exists.

Recommended phrasing pattern:

- Avoid: "AMD sees..."
- Use: "In the field, the support team must ask..."
- Avoid: "まさの判断では..."
- Use: "Repeated field observation suggests..."
- Avoid: "AMD OS / L2 / candidate / applier..."
- Use: "field notes," "approved case material," or omit the production process.

## 6. Differentiation From Adjacent Books

Compared with startup playbooks:

- This book starts before the team can honestly say "we are a startup."
- It treats incorporation timing, researcher role, IP order, institution
  readiness, and founder-function decomposition as first-class issues.

Compared with technology-transfer texts:

- This book focuses on live judgment moments, not only institutional process.
- It gives field questions and decision gates that practitioners can use before
  company formation.

Compared with deep-tech policy/program guides:

- This book explains why good programs can still create local optimization and
  researcher loneliness.
- It connects program design to human roles, readiness axes, and timing.

Compared with theory/model books:

- This book earns the math by first making the reader feel the field problem.
- Every parameter must answer a human question before it becomes a formula.
