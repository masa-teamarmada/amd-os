## Executive verdict

Chapters 04-06 were rewritten away from framework-first prose, visible tables, and tidy checklist endings. The new Act II follows the same composite case from Act I: the deck sentence that worked in a pitch now creates role displacement, disclosure risk, and premature incorporation pressure.

This is not a final publication pass. It is a structural rewrite that gives the next editor/rewrite cycle a continuous story to criticize instead of a workbook chapter set.

## Narrative spine used

1. The researcher is praised as founder-like after the pitch.
2. The next room asks who will actually run the company, and external CEO lands as displacement.
3. The team reframes CEO as unowned functions without removing the researcher from the center.
4. The same deck travels into a scheduled email and almost crosses a disclosure line.
5. The team stops the send, marks the slide, and creates a disclosure map.
6. Someone suggests incorporating anyway, because a company would make the conversations easier.
7. The incorporation discussion becomes a survival / earning capability decision rather than a generic timing framework.

## Chapter-by-chapter rewrite summary

### 04 GAP / VC / CEO function

Old function: explain the GAP/VC/CEO contradiction and show a CEO-function table.

New function: stage the contradiction as an emotional and operational displacement. The researcher is praised for standing forward, then hears that an external CEO may be needed. CEO-function decomposition appears only after the room has felt why the binary is false.

### 05 Before disclosure

Old function: explain disclosure layers, IP, papers, collaboration trust, and red/yellow/blue classification in a table-heavy way.

New function: begin with a scheduled email seventeen minutes before sending. The too-strong deck sentence and the overclean customer-evidence sentence almost travel outside the protection boundary. Red/yellow/blue remains, but only as marks placed on a slide in the room.

### 06 Incorporation timing

Old function: explain company timing, earning capability, WAIT/HOLD/GO, and decision tables.

New function: make 登記だけ先に a pressure point immediately after a disclosure stop. The chapter dramatizes the founder economics directive: small paid signals can either raise survival probability or narrow the future. WAIT appears as a dated, owned decision with a return condition, not as a table.
