# Referee Report — "The Before Zero Model: measuring deep-tech ventures and their institutional nurseries before day zero"

**Journal:** Research Policy
**Reviewer lens:** real options and investment under uncertainty (optimal stopping, regime-switching investment models, Dixit–Pindyck tradition)
**Materials reviewed:** main text (esp. Section 5) and Supplementary Material (esp. SM-C), including the SM-C.5 numerical specification and the authors' own [GAP] inventory.

---

## 1. Summary

The paper proposes a two-ledger measurement system for pre-founding ("Before Zero") deep-tech ventures: a multiplicative venture score PRS = P·R·S, an additive institutional score ERS (shown unique under conjoint-measurement axioms, Theorem 1), an impossibility theorem (Theorem 3) showing no monotone single score can merge the two ledgers under axioms C1–C4, and falsifiable statistical signatures of the violation (Corollary 3.1). Section 5 then argues the system is *operable*: the go/wait founding decision is derived as an optimal stopping problem on a regime-modulated diffusion for the constructed macro state σ_SU, with a runway state F absorbed at zero (financing constraint), a deterministic binary technology gate g_TRL, value-matching/smooth-pasting thresholds θ*_σ(k) per regime (Theorem 4), four comparative statics as policy levers (Proposition 2), and a multiplicative factorization of the gate (Lemma C.2). A retrospective calibration on eight composite projects illustrates the system.

The measurement core (Theorems 1–3) is, to this reviewer's eye, the paper's genuine contribution and largely outside my lens; I defer to the measurement/social-choice referee on its details. Within my lens, Section 5's *conception* — marrying a stopping rule to an explicitly constructed, auditable state variable for an entity that does not yet exist — is a real and worthwhile idea, and the authors' candor infrastructure (Tier discipline, explicit [GAP] markers, the refusal to report unverified numerics) is commendable and rare. But the execution of the stopping theory has substantive holes: the two headline results (Theorem 4, Proposition 2) rest on deferred steps that are not routine, one comparative-statics sign is in unacknowledged tension with a well-known result in the financing-constrained timing literature and with the paper's own Section 7, the main text makes several claims the formal model does not support, and the deferrals point to a "companion monograph" that is cited nowhere in the reference list and is apparently inaccessible. The numerical exercise that could underwrite the theory is specified to pseudocode level but not executed.

## 2. Verdict

**Major revision.** The paper is publishable in concept: the measurement-to-stopping integration is novel in this domain, and every defect I list below is repairable. But Theorem 4 and Proposition 2 cannot stand as "theorems" on the current proof record; the F-comparative-static and its policy reading must be reconciled with the literature and with Section 7; the main-text claims must be brought down to what the model actually delivers; and the SM-C.5 numerics must be run. I would want to see the revision.

## 3. Major concerns

### M1. Theorem 4's proof does not go through as recorded: the Step-4 reduction drops the runway state, and the constant-counting is wrong even ignoring that.

The exact locus is SM-C.2, Step 4: "At each runway level the σ-sections of the continuation equation form three coupled second-order equations. [GAP: the skeleton treats these sections as an ODE system, leaving the runway-drift term's role in that reduction unrecorded.]"

This [GAP] is not a technicality; it is the theorem. Under the stated dynamics dF = (R_net − B)dt with R_net < B (the empirically relevant deep-tech case), F is a *deterministic clock*: F_t = F_0 + (R_net − B)t, and the pre-entry problem is a **finite-horizon, time-inhomogeneous** free-boundary problem in σ with deadline T_0 = F_0/(B − R_net) — an American option with expiry, not a perpetual one. The generator in SM-C.0 correctly contains the transport term (R_net − B)φ_F; the "σ-sections at each runway level" reduction is valid only in the knife-edge case R_net = B, where F freezes. Otherwise the ∂_F V term couples the sections, the "integration constants" become functions of F, and the counting argument (three constants + three boundaries vs six pasting conditions) collapses. The implicit system Ψ(θ; P,F,B,Π) = 0 as constructed does not exist for the model as specified. Notably, the *numerical* scheme in SM-C.5 keeps the upwind F-drift term and solves the genuinely two-dimensional problem — i.e., the paper's own code disagrees with its own proof.

Second, even in the stationary case the counting in Step 4 is incorrect for a regime-switching system with *ordered* thresholds θ*(S_0) > θ*(S_1) > θ*(S_2). On the interval (θ*(S_2), θ*(S_1)), regime S_2 is stopped and its value V_{S_2} = G_{S_2} − I enters the continuing regimes' equations as a source; on (θ*(S_1), θ*(S_0)) the active set changes again. Each continuing component therefore has a *different general solution on each interval*, contributing additional constants that must be matched by C⁰/C¹ interface conditions at the other regimes' boundaries. The correct bookkeeping is done in exactly this structure by Guo, Miao and Morellec (2005, JET) for the two-regime case; the paper's 3+3-vs-6 count omits the interface layer entirely.

Third, uniqueness is circular as recorded: Step 4 claims "Step 1's up-set geometry upgrades local to global uniqueness," but Step 1's up-set geometry is itself a [GAP] (see M2). What remains proven is: *if* the entry region is an up-set per regime, and *if* Ψ is well-defined and its Jacobian non-singular (another [GAP]), *then* the threshold is locally unique and continuous. That is a conditional statement, not Theorem 4 as stated in the main text ("their solution defines a unique implicit threshold").

**Required:** Either (a) prove Theorem 4 for the honest time-inhomogeneous problem, delivering θ*(k; F) as a free-boundary *curve* (with the deadline limit F↓0 handled — see M4), with the interface counting done correctly; or (b) explicitly restrict to R_net = B (or F treated as a fixed parameter with an infinite-horizon option), say so in the theorem statement, and discuss what is lost. Option (a) is more work but is the model the paper actually wrote down.

### M2. Single crossing (SM-C.2, Step 1) is load-bearing and not guaranteed by the stated assumptions; the continuation region can in principle be disconnected, and the numerical threshold extractor would mask it.

Step 1 asserts the entry region is an up-set in σ "from monotonicity of V and G in σ" with verification deferred. Monotonicity of *both* V and G in σ (Lemma C.1(c)) does not order their *difference*; the obstruction is exactly that D := V − (G − I) ≥ 0 solves a coupled system with regime sources, and nothing in (A1)–(A5) makes D monotone or single-crossing in σ. The flow π(σ,k) = P·R(σ,k)·S(σ,k) − B is only assumed Lipschitz and non-decreasing in σ; it may have arbitrarily long flat segments in one regime (economically natural: in the dormant regime S_0, R·S may be insensitive to σ), in which case entry in S_0 is driven almost entirely through the switching term, and V and G can run nearly parallel over intervals, allowing multiple tangencies/crossings. Regime-switching stopping problems are precisely the class where naive threshold geometry is known to require proof, not assertion (Guo and Zhang, 2004; Jobert and Rogers, 2006; Guo, Miao and Morellec, 2005 — none cited).

Two consequences. (i) Theorem 4's "unique threshold per regime" should be restated as conditional on an explicit single-crossing/up-set assumption (call it SC), or proven under strengthened primitives (e.g., π strictly increasing in σ with a uniform slope bound in the relevant band, plus conditions on the coupling). (ii) The numerical scheme's threshold extractor in SM-C.5, `theta_hat(k;F) <- min{σ_j : V − (G − I) ≤ eps_b}`, returns the *lowest* crossing and would silently conceal a disconnected stopping set. Add a diagnostic that verifies the entry set {V − (G−I) ≤ ε_b} is an up-set on the grid for every (k, F); report it alongside the mesh-refinement and degenerate-limit checks.

### M3. The financing side is internally inconsistent across main text and SM, and the treatment of the sunk cost I undoes the financing-constraint story.

Exact loci: the displayed problem in §5 / SM-C.0; state equation (ii) in SM-C.0; value-matching in SM-C.2 Step 2; (A6) and its [GAP].

(a) The main-text constraint "B(t) − R_net(t) ≤ F(t)" compares a *flow* (burn net of revenue, per unit time) to a *stock* (slack) — dimensionally incoherent as displayed. The SM quietly replaces it with the dynamic implementation dF = (R_net − B)dt, F = 0 absorbing, which is the sensible object. The main text should display the constraint it solves (F_t ≥ 0 under the funds law), not a flow-vs-stock inequality.

(b) The sunk cost I is paid from outside the runway: value-matching is V(θ*,F,k) = G(θ*,F,k) − I with the *same* F on both sides, so entry at F < I is feasible and costless in runway terms. For a paper whose distinctive state variable is a financing constraint, frictionless external financing of the founding cost is a first-order modeling choice, not a detail: with the natural alternative F ↦ F − I at entry (or feasibility F ≥ I), the F- and B-comparative statics change and the F↓0 behavior becomes "forced non-entry," not "forced entry or exit." Choose, justify, and state it.

(c) The objective never books the cost of waiting: pre-entry burn depletes F (shortening the option's life) but is not subtracted from the payoff, and unspent F has no terminal or entry value (G does not receive F; V(σ,0,k) = 0). This is coherent only under an "earmarked, non-fungible funds" reading (use-it-or-lose-it gap-fund money) — which is defensible and even apt for this institutional setting, but it is nowhere stated, and it interacts with (b): if F is earmarked pre-entry money, it certainly cannot pay post-entry burn, yet G's exit barrier runs on the same F (A6, itself flagged [GAP: post-entry drift never separately specified]).

(d) Double-counted mortality: S is defined in §3 as "the survival probability that the venture-to-be endures long enough," while SM-C adds an independent financing-death channel (absorption at F = 0). If S already includes financing survival, death is counted twice; if it does not, §3's definition must say so. One sentence fixes this, but at present the flow payoff P·R·S and the barrier are not mutually consistent objects.

### M4. Proposition 2(iii): the sign ∂θ*/∂F < 0 is in unacknowledged tension with the financing-constrained-timing literature, with the SM's own F→0 heuristic, and with Section 7's policy reading — and the instrument it is sold with (gap funds) works through the channel the model's single F cannot represent.

Exact loci: SM-C.3(iii) and its [GAP]; main text §5 ("runway substitutes for tailwind") and §7 ("gap funds and fellowships raise F" vs "extending a viable project's WAIT (bridging fellowships…) buys option value").

There are two opposing channels, and the paper's own SM records both. More slack raises the entered value G (post-entry exit risk recedes) — pushes θ* down; more slack lengthens the option's life (deadline T_0 recedes) — restores option value and pushes θ* *up*. The second channel is the celebrated Boyle–Guthrie (2003, Journal of Finance) result: liquidity-constrained firms invest *earlier* than unconstrained ones because prospective funding shortfalls erode the value of waiting — i.e., θ* *increasing* in slack. The SM's own F→0 heuristic ("threshold collapse, forced entry or exit", [GAP]) is this deadline effect, and it points the opposite way from the claimed interior monotonicity; the [GAP] defers the reconciliation to the monograph's exit-hazard chapter while region R1 simply bounds F away from zero. That is not a reconciliation; it is an evasion of the sign fight on the boundary where it is sharpest.

Worse, the policy mapping inverts the instrument. Real gap funds and bridging fellowships are *pre-entry-restricted* money: they extend the runway of the option, not of the entered firm. In the model those instruments act through the option-life channel — the Boyle–Guthrie channel, whose sign is opposite to the claim — whereas the claimed sign ∂θ*/∂F < 0 is explicitly driven by the *post-entry* exit-risk channel ("exit risk near the barrier depresses the entered value"). Because the model has a single F financing both phases, it cannot distinguish the two, and §7 duly sells both stories at once: "gap funds and fellowships raise F" (lowering the bar, hastening GO) and, three sentences later, "extending a viable project's WAIT (bridging fellowships, laboratory residence) buys option value" (funding patience). Both cannot be the headline reading of the same derivative.

**Required:** split F into pre-entry and post-entry slack (or add an entry-time F-jump per M3(b)), derive the two partials separately, engage Boyle–Guthrie (2003) and the deadline literature explicitly, and rewrite the §7 instrument mapping so that each named instrument is attached to the channel it actually operates. If the authors can show that for empirically sensible parameters the post-entry channel dominates for the composite F, fine — but that is a quantitative claim, which is one more reason to run SM-C.5 (M7).

### M5. Proposition 2(iv): the persistence sign is claimed uniformly in k with the perturbation scheme unstated, and the main text's "policy credibility" reading requires cross-regime derivatives Prop 2 does not contain.

Exact loci: SM-C.3(iv) and its [GAP: the skeleton does not record which compensation scheme its sign is proved under]; region (R2) "Π stationary over the decision horizon"; main text §5 "credible policy commitment is itself a founding subsidy."

(a) ∂θ*(k)/∂π_kk < 0 is asserted for all k. For k = S_2 (aligned) it is plausible: nothing better to wait for, and persistence raises regime-k flow weight in G. For k = S_0 and S_1 there are opposing channels: higher own-persistence lowers the option value of waiting to switch *up* (θ* down) but also lowers expected flows in G when the current regime is bad (Marshallian root moves up). The net sign depends on the compensation scheme for the off-diagonal mass — proportional rescaling versus draining the up-transition only give different answers — and that scheme is exactly the [GAP]. A uniform-in-k sign needs either a proof under a stated scheme or a per-regime restriction.

(b) The policy story is about the credibility of the *program* regime: "credible, persistent programs raise π_kk." But a founder in the warming regime S_1 cares about π_{S_2S_2} — the persistence of the aligned regime she may switch into — and Prop 2(iv) says nothing about ∂θ*(S_1)/∂π_{S_2S_2}, which has its own ambiguity (a more durable good regime raises both the value of entering now and the value of waiting for it). As written, the main text's "cheapest lever" paragraph attributes to Prop 2(iv) a cross-regime comparative static it does not prove.

(c) (R2)'s second clause, "Π stationary over the decision horizon," is vacuous in the paper's model, where Π is a constant by construction; it imports the monograph's two-stage Π^pre → Π^post apparatus that Remark C.3 explicitly excludes from this paper. Delete or replace with a condition expressible in the paper's own setting.

### M6. The main text claims the stopping model reads the two ledgers and that the institutional channel is "formally" present; the formal model in SM-C contains neither.

Exact loci: main text §5, "The information set is exactly the two ledgers plus the macro state — nothing is assumed observable that Section 3 did not construct"; and the closing §5 paragraph, "Formally, institutional investment shifts θ*'s arguments and the state's law of motion."

In SM-C the state is (σ, F, k). R and S are *static reduced-form functions* R(σ,k), S(σ,k) "treated here as primitives"; the venture ledger's own dynamics (R-drift, S-hazard), which §3 sets up and §5 narrates, appear nowhere; and no object in SM-C — not the flow, not the funds law, not Π, not the response functions — carries ERS or any A_k. There is no lemma of the form ∂θ*/∂ERS < 0; there is no ERS-indexed drift or hazard; the word "formally" in the §5 closing paragraph has no referent. Likewise "nothing decision-relevant was lost by refusing the merger" is a separation/sufficiency claim (the optimal policy depends on A only through its (R,S,F)-effects) that would need a theorem — it is C3 elevated from a measurement axiom to a decision-theoretic result without proof.

This matters beyond hygiene because the paper's positioning (M8) rests on the marriage of measurement to stopping. As formalized, the stopping rule conditions on σ and F only; the venture ledger enters as a constant P and a deterministic gate; the institutional ledger enters only in prose. **Required:** either (a) add the formal channel — let R and S be state variables with ERS-shifted drift/hazard as §3 promises, and prove the comparative static of θ* (or of the value function) in ERS — even a modest version (ERS as a parameter shifting R(σ,k) multiplicatively) would license the paragraph; or (b) rewrite the two flagged passages as interpretation/conjecture and weaken the abstract's "the go/wait decision emerges as the optimal-stopping rule operating on the two ledgers."

### M7. Lemma C.2 is sound but nearly definitional as stated, and the "theorem rather than an assumption" framing in §5 overclaims; the factorization assumes away precisely the tech-side interactions that make deep-tech timing interesting.

Exact loci: SM-C.4 (TRL-orthogonality; Lemma C.2 and its [GAP]); main text §5 "the factorization is a theorem rather than an assumption."

Since g_TRL is *declared deterministic*, the stated conditional-independence assumption σ_SU ⊥ g_TRL | k is vacuously true — a deterministic function is independent of everything. The assumption doing all the work is determinism-plus-exogeneity of the maturation ramp itself: maturation speed does not respond to regimes (no funding-cycle acceleration in hot fields), is not chosen (no endogenous development intensity), and never fails (g_TRL non-decreasing — technical risk, the modal deep-tech killer, cannot strike). Under those premises the binary-gate lemma is a two-line restart argument (correctly sketched), and the "two non-compensable failure modes" tagline is a *consequence of the exogeneity assumption*, not a discovery about deep tech. Meanwhile the classic tech-side option — waiting to *learn* whether the technology works, the compound/sequential R&D option of McGrath (1997), which the paper cites, and of Roberts–Weitzman-style staged development — is entirely absent: in this model waiting reveals only macro information. The graded-ramp case is declared, not proved ([GAP]), yet Table 2 already uses graded-gate language ("gate set at elevated threshold," "GO conditional on milestone") that the binary formal object cannot produce; note also that GO(t,i) ∈ {0,1} while §6 reports a GO/WAIT/NO_GO trichotomy whose NO_GO branch (presumably V ≡ 0 on the reachable set, e.g. P → 0) is never defined.

**Required:** (i) restate the assumption as exogeneity/determinism of maturation and *defend it* in the SM (the main text says "an assumption we state, defend, and flag" — the defense is not there); (ii) soften "theorem rather than an assumption"; (iii) add a limitation paragraph: with regime-dependent or stochastic maturation the policy does not factorize and θ* becomes gate-state-dependent; (iv) define NO_GO formally and align Table 2's vocabulary with the binary gate or prove the graded case.

### M8. Positioning: "the threshold is endogenous, unlike off-the-shelf real options exercises" is an overclaim, and the regime-switching and financing-constrained timing literatures are missing.

Exact loci: main text §5 ("Second, the threshold is *endogenous*…") and §1 ("an *endogenous* threshold θ*_σ … derived from project economics …, not asserted"); §2's real-options paragraph.

Endogeneity of the trigger to project economics is the *defining property* of the tradition the paper cites: McDonald–Siegel's threshold is an explicit function of (I, r, δ, σ²); Dixit–Pindyck chs. 5–7 is a book of such comparative statics. Regime-wise entry thresholds with a Markov-modulated environment, including their comparative statics in the generator, exist in essentially the paper's mathematical form: Guo, Miao and Morellec (2005, JET, irreversible investment with regime shifts); Hassett and Metcalf (1999, Economic Journal, policy uncertainty); Driffill, Kenc and Sola; Jobert and Rogers (2006); Guo and Zhang (2004); Boyarchenko and Levendorskii. Financing-constrained timing with a cash-stock state is Boyle and Guthrie (2003, JF) and successors (Décamps–Mariotti–Rochet–Villeneuve; Hugonnier–Malamud–Morellec). None are cited. Against that literature, Proposition 2's four signs are not new economics — the legitimate and sufficient novelty claim is the one the paper also makes: the monitored state is *constructed by an auditable measurement layer* rather than assumed observable, and the threshold rule is welded to that layer. Keep that claim; delete "endogenous, unlike off-the-shelf"; and position Theorem 4 relative to Guo–Miao–Morellec explicitly (whose interface bookkeeping would incidentally repair M1's counting). Contrasting the *checklist folklore* of practice with derivation is fine; contrasting the paper with the RO literature on endogeneity grounds is not.

Related bibliographic defects: SM-C's citation note says "full entries in the paper's reference list: Bertola 1998; Caballero 1999; …" — neither Bertola (1998) nor Caballero (1999) appears in the reference list. The "companion monograph," to which at least eight load-bearing [GAP]s defer (Appendix A.5.5, Ch 5.5, exit-hazard chapter, Ch 11), is cited nowhere and appears to be non-public: a [GAP] pointing to an accessible appendix is honest bookkeeping; a [GAP] pointing to an unavailable manuscript is a promissory note the referee cannot audit. Every deferral must resolve to either the SM itself or a citable document.

### M9. The numerical exercise (SM-C.5) must be executed before acceptance.

Yes — I would insist. The specification is complete to pseudocode, so execution cost is low; the calibration-constant withholding (PF-010) is no obstacle, since the figure is declared to run on placeholders anyway — run it with fully disclosed *synthetic* parameter values and say so. Given the density of deferred steps (12 [GAP]s in SM-C alone), the numerics are the cheapest available evidence that (i) a non-empty region exists on which the threshold geometry of Theorem 4 holds, (ii) Proposition 2's four signs hold on it, and (iii) the degenerate single-regime limit reproduces McDonald–Siegel (the specified diagnostic (ii), which is good practice). I would add three requirements to the diagnostics already listed: (a) the up-set check of M2 (the current θ̂ extractor masks disconnected stopping sets); (b) at least one parameterization *outside* region R exhibiting the B-sign reversal, to demonstrate the (R2) qualifier has content rather than serving as an unfalsifiable shield; (c) the θ*(k;F) *curve* over F (not a single F_ref), which will directly reveal whether the F-slope and the F→0 deadline behavior of M4 coexist or contradict. Absent an executed Figure 3, Section 5 asks the reader to accept two theorems on twelve IOUs; with it, most of Proposition 2 becomes defensible as "proved on R, verified numerically on a disclosed neighborhood."

### M10. Observability and state-space coherence: the rule is sold as operable, but the regime is assumed fully observed and σ_SU is assumed a noiseless unbounded diffusion, while the measurement layer delivers a bounded, smoothed, low-frequency index.

Exact loci: SM-C.0 ("the regime is fully observed (belief-dependent extension out of scope)"); SM-C.0 state (i) σ ∈ ℝ with boundary conditions at σ → ±∞; §3's definition σ_SU = ∛∏(μ+1) − 1 with momenta "mapped to a bounded momentum scale" (SM-D.3).

Three mismatches. (i) σ_SU as constructed is bounded (and bounded below by −1 by construction); the stopping model runs an unbounded diffusion and proves interiority (Step 5) via σ → ±∞ limits. On a bounded state space, thresholds can sit at the boundary — θ*(S_0) above the attainable maximum is economically meaningful ("never found in the dormant regime") but contradicts "strictly interior." Either transform the index or restate interiority. (ii) The GO rule conditions on k_t, a latent regime whose identification the paper explicitly defers (Tier-B); the main text never tells the reader that operating the rule requires a filtered regime estimate, and with filtering the rule changes character (belief thresholds). The SM's out-of-scope declaration is honest; the main text's "operable" framing (§5, §7's registry that will file GO/WAIT/NO_GO readings *ex ante*) needs one candid paragraph on how σ and k are to be read in real time from smoothed annual/quarterly series with revisions, and what threshold-rule timing error that implies. (iii) The declared simplifications relative to the monograph (constant B, no belief state, full regime observation) are otherwise handled honestly in SM-C.0 and Remark C.3 — my complaint is confined to the main text's silence on (ii) and to the passages flagged in M6.

## 4. Minor issues

1. **(A1) "non-decreasing in σ (implicit in the monograph)"** — an admission that the monotonicity of π in σ is nowhere assumed even in the source. It is load-bearing (Lemma C.1(c), Step 1, Step 5). State it as an explicit assumption on the measurement layer: R(σ,k)·S(σ,k) non-decreasing in σ, with a one-line economic defense.
2. **Theorem 4's argument list** θ*_σ(P,F,B,Π) omits r, I, R_net, and the functions R(·,·), S(·,·) on which the threshold also depends. Harmless but sloppy; it also flatters the "policy levers" reading by hiding I (whose comparative static, via Remark C.2(c), is a fifth lever worth reporting).
3. **Remark C.1's normalization [GAP]** (translation between the flow HJB and the entry-payoff normalization via Feynman–Kac on G): one paragraph; write it.
4. **Uniformization**: "row-stochastic (uniformized) transition matrix" with switching term Σ_j π_kj[φ_j − φ_k] implies a uniformization rate normalized to 1. Say so, or use generator (Q-matrix) notation; as written, π_kk's units (probability per what?) are ambiguous, which aggravates M5(a).
5. **Smooth pasting (Step 3)**: fine in outline given s_k > 0, but cite the applicable regularity results (e.g., Peskir–Shiryaev; for regime-switching systems, Guo–Zhang 2004) rather than only Dixit–Pindyck's perturbation heuristic.
6. **§5 "Waiting yields no flow"**: waiting burns B − R_net from F; the sentence is correct only in the objective-function sense (see M3(c)). Reword to avoid the misreading that waiting is costless.
7. **(A5) "P/B non-extreme"** is not a condition; define the excluded set (presumably: Marshallian root exists and is finite, i.e., B/P inside the range of R·S).
8. **Main text §5** says g_TRL "ramps deterministically" while the object is binary {0,1}; "ramp" language belongs to the unproved graded case (M7).
9. **G's boundary conditions**: SM-C.0 gives G's PDE and G(σ,0,k) = 0 but no σ-asymptotics; add them (they are used implicitly when V's upper envelope is invoked).
10. **Notation collision**: SM-A.4 sets "P := PRS" while P is potential scale everywhere else. Rename.
11. **Reference-list hygiene**: Bertola (1998) and Caballero (1999) cited in SM-C but absent from the list (M8); Atkinson (1970) and Cobb–Douglas (1928) are in the list but appear uncited in the main text. The monograph must be cited if it is to absorb deferrals.
12. **"The technical development adds nothing economic" (§5)** — the F = 0 absorbing boundary *is* economics (forced exit); the sentence undersells the paper's own most interesting constraint. Cosmetic, but symptomatic of Section 5's tendency to wave at the SM.
13. **Founder–institution pair as a single joint-value-maximizing agent** (SM-C.0): assumes away founder–TLO bargaining (equity, licensing), which the paper's own institutional literature review says is first-order. One acknowledging sentence suffices at this scope.
14. **Table 2 / Project N**: "GO conditional on milestone" is not in the operator's range (M7(iv)); either define conditional readings or relabel.

## 5. [GAP] triage for SM-C

Labels in order of appearance. Twelve [GAP]s; the changelog's own count agrees.

**Must be closed in the SM (or the results restated) — publication-blocking:**

- **C-G5 (Thm 4, Step 1: single crossing / up-set geometry).** Load-bearing for global uniqueness and for the meaning of "threshold." Close by proof under strengthened primitives, or promote to a named assumption (SC) in the statement of Theorem 4 and add the numerical up-set diagnostic (M2, M9).
- **C-G7 (Thm 4, Step 4: ODE reduction dropping the runway-drift term).** This is not a deferrable detail; as recorded, the proof addresses a different model (M1). Close by solving the time-inhomogeneous problem or by restricting the model and saying so in the theorem.
- **C-G8 (Thm 4, Step 4: Jacobian non-singularity).** The IFT is the paper's uniqueness engine; "checked in monograph appendix" is not auditable (M8). Provide the verification or a checkable sufficient condition.
- **C-G9 (Prop 2(iii): F→0 heuristic vs interior monotonicity).** The unreconciled deadline effect is the sharp end of M4 and touches the paper's headline policy lever. Must be resolved, not fenced off by R1.
- **C-G10 (Prop 2(iv): compensation scheme for the π_kk perturbation).** The sign is not well-defined without it (M5). State the scheme and prove the sign under it, per regime.

**Should be closed — short, and the paper is stronger for it:**

- **C-G1 (A6: post-entry funds law).** One displayed equation; it also forces the authors to confront M3(b)–(c).
- **C-G4 (Lemma C.1(d): regime ordering of V)** and **C-G11 (Prop 2(v): threshold ordering).** Standard coupling arguments, a paragraph each; the ordering is quoted in the abstract-level narrative, so it should not rest on an inaccessible appendix.
- **C-G2 (Remark C.1: normalization translation).** One paragraph (minor issue 3).

**Acceptable as deferrals *provided* they resolve to citable sources:**

- **C-G3 (Lemma C.1(b): comparison principle for the weakly coupled K=3 system).** Acceptable with citations to the weakly-coupled-systems viscosity literature (e.g., Ishii–Koike; Barles) in place of the monograph pointer.
- **C-G6 (Step 3: C¹-pasting regularity).** Acceptable with citations (minor issue 5); this is genuinely standard given s_k > 0.
