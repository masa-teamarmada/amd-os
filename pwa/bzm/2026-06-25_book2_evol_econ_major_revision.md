### I1_C2_rho_estimation: C2 F-CES non-delegable core を ρ 推定可能化

**One-line resolution**: Re-cast the F-CES non-delegable core as an empirically estimable claim: ρ is point-identified from a Kmenta-expanded second-moment regression of C1's hitting-time on founder F_char and team F_cap measured separately in the Beckman/Roberts/Eesley-Roberts cohorts, with ρ<0 (and the Inada corner) demoted from axiom to a falsifiable posterior statement.

**Formal addition**:

Definition (F-CES, restated as estimable). For founder-team i with founder-trait composite F_char,i and delegable-capability composite F_cap,i, define the founder-effectiveness aggregator\n\\[\nF_i \\;=\\; \\Big[\\, \\alpha\\, F_{\\text{char},i}^{\\rho} \\;+\\; (1-\\alpha)\\, F_{\\text{cap},i}^{\\rho} \\,\\Big]^{1/\\rho}, \\qquad \\alpha\\in(0,1),\\ \\rho\\in(-\\infty,1]\\setminus\\{0\\}.\n\\]\nProposition 1 (Identification of ρ from founder-cohort data). Let z_{c,i} = log F_{char,i}, z_{k,i} = log F_{cap,i}, and let y_i denote the C1-implied log hitting-time to the B-trigger τ_x. Under (A1) pre-formation measurement of F_char,i, (A2) cohort × industry fixed effects absorbing common selection, and (A3) common support in (z_c, z_k), the Kmenta (1967) second-order expansion yields\n\\[\n y_i \\;=\\; \\delta_0 + \\delta_1 z_{c,i} + \\delta_2 z_{k,i} + \\delta_3\\, (z_{c,i}-z_{k,i})^2 + \\varepsilon_i,\n\\]\nand the sign and magnitude of ρ are identified by\n\\[\n\\operatorname{sign}(\\rho)=\\operatorname{sign}(\\delta_3), \\qquad \\hat\\rho = \\frac{2\\,\\hat\\delta_3}{\\hat\\alpha(1-\\hat\\alpha)}\\quad\\text{with}\\quad \\hat\\alpha = \\hat\\delta_1/(\\hat\\delta_1+\\hat\\delta_2).\n\\]\nCorollary 1 (Non-delegability as empirical claim). If the posterior satisfies $P(\\rho<0\\mid \\text{data})\\ge 0.95$, then the Inada corner $\\lim_{F_{\\text{char}}\\downarrow 0} \\partial F/\\partial F_{\\text{char}} = \\infty$ holds, and F_char is non-delegable in the BZ-stage sense: no finite reallocation of F_cap can compensate a vanishing F_char-carrier. Conversely, $P(\\rho\\ge 0\\mid\\text{data})>0.05$ refutes C2 and reverts the model to a Nelson–Winter (1982) routine-as-fully-delegable benchmark.\nHypothesis H_C2. ρ < 0 (pre-registered, one-sided).

**Derivation / proof sketch**:

Step 1 (Setup). Specify F_i = [α F_char,i^ρ + (1-α) F_cap,i^ρ]^{1/ρ} with founder-level F_char,i (ALQ4 + Grit + Resilience composite, standardised within cohort) and team-level F_cap,i (sum of co-founders' domain-capability index, excluding focal founder). Define BZ-stage outcome Y_i ∈ {survive-to-B, fail-pre-B} and continuous proxy y_i = log time-to-B-trigger from C1's hitting-time τ_x. Step 2 (Linearisation). Under Kmenta (1967) / Klump et al. (2007) normalised-CES log-linearisation around (F_char*, F_cap*): log F_i ≈ log F* + α·d log F_char,i + (1-α)·d log F_cap,i + (ρ/2)·α(1-α)·(d log F_char,i − d log F_cap,i)^2 + o(·). Step 3 (Reduced form). Substitute into the C1 hitting-time equation y_i = γ_0 + γ_1 log F_i + ε_i to obtain y_i = δ_0 + δ_1 z_char,i + δ_2 z_cap,i + δ_3 (z_char,i − z_cap,i)^2 + ε_i, where the sign of δ_3 identifies sign(ρ): δ_3<0 ⇔ ρ<0 (complementarity / non-delegability). Step 4 (Identification proposition). Proposition C2-ρ: Under (i) cohort-fixed-effect strict exogeneity of (z_char,i, z_cap,i) conditional on industry × vintage, (ii) F_char measured pre-formation (so reverse causality from y_i is ruled out), and (iii) overlap in (z_char, z_cap) support, ρ is point-identified from the second-moment coefficient δ_3 in Step 3 up to the normalisation α ∈ (0,1) recovered from first-moment shares. Step 5 (Non-delegability as testable corollary, not axiom). Corollary: If ρ<0, then ∂F/∂F_char → ∞ as F_char → 0 holding F_cap > 0 (Inada at the F_char corner). Hence 'a team of high-F_cap co-founders cannot substitute for a missing F_char-carrier' is now an empirical implication of the estimated ρ, not an axiom. Step 6 (Falsification condition). Reject C2 if the 95% posterior of ρ lies weakly above 0, OR if among the high-F_char × low-F_cap cohort (top tercile F_char, bottom tercile F_cap) the survival hazard to B-trigger is not significantly above the low-F_char × high-F_cap cohort after controlling for capital, sector, vintage. Step 7 (Selection correction). BZ-stage survival is itself a selected sample; use Heckman correction with vintage × geography instruments for entry, or alternatively Hsieh-Klenow-style dispersion-based identification on the full founding cohort regardless of survival. Step 8 (Bayesian update). Place a weakly informative prior ρ ~ N(0, 1) (deliberately centred at the Nelson-Winter delegable-routine null), update on the founder-cohort data; report posterior P(ρ<0 | data) and posterior of the implied non-delegability elasticity. Step 9 (Connection to C1, C3). Plug the posterior of ρ into C1's regime-switching τ_x to obtain a survival curve with posterior bands; verify that C3's two-layer non-commutativity (PRS multiplicative × ERS weighted-sum) does not collapse by testing whether the joint distribution of (PRS_i, ERS_i) is rank-separable — failure to be rank-separable is consistent with C3.

**残るリスク** (5 件):

- Measurement validity of F_char: ALQ4 + Grit + Resilience composite is self-reported and may be endogenous to early founder experience; partial fix via pre-formation administration (Roberts MIT cohort has this) but the construct-validity critique will persist.
- Sample selection at the B-trigger: even with Heckman / dispersion-based identification, ρ is estimated on survivors-up-to-observation; if attrition is correlated with the (z_c − z_k)^2 term, δ_3 is biased. Robustness to alternative selection-correction priors must be reported.
- Functional-form dependence on CES: a translog or general nested-CES would absorb δ_3 differently; referees may argue ρ<0 is an artefact of the two-input restriction rather than evidence of non-delegability.
- Cohort generalisability: Beckman, Roberts, and Eesley-Roberts cover MIT / US tech founders 1960s-2000s; deep-tech / university-spinout / AMD-style executive-team founding may have different ρ. The estimate should be reported as cohort-specific with explicit external-validity caveat.
- Joint identification with C1 and C3: ρ enters τ_x non-linearly and PRS×ERS is non-commutative, so the second-moment OLS estimator is consistent only in the linearisation neighbourhood; a fully structural Bayesian estimator that jointly identifies (α, ρ, regime-switch parameters) is the publication-grade target and remains future work.

### I2_C3_algebraic_theorem: C3 二層非可換性を Arrow-style impossibility theorem に

**One-line resolution**: Recast C3 as Theorem 3 in Arrow-impossibility style: axiomatize PRS-annihilation, ERS-monotonicity, and a causal-channel restriction, then prove no strictly-monotone scalar collapse Φ(g(P,R,S), h(A)) satisfies all three — yielding a Hausman-testable falsifier against Nelson-Winter scalar fitness on BZ-stage data.

**Formal addition**:

Theorem 3 (Two-Layer Non-Commutativity at BZ Stage). Let (P,R,S) ∈ R_{>0}^3 denote the founder-internal Purpose-Readiness-Skill triple and A = (A_1,...,A_K) ∈ R_{≥0}^K the K-dimensional Environmental Resource System (ERS). Define PRS := P·R·S and ERS := Σ_{k=1}^K w_k A_k, w_k > 0. Let f: R_{>0}^3 × R_{≥0}^K → R_{≥0} be a candidate composite-evaluation function. **Derivation / proof sketch**:

Step 1 (Setup). Let P, R, S ∈ R_{>0} denote Purpose, Readiness, Skill scalars and A = (A_1,...,A_K) ∈ R_{≥0}^K the Environmental Resource axes (capital, network, regulatory, demand, talent). (A3) Causal direction: ERS influences f only through the time-derivatives (dR/dt, dS/dt); i.e. ∂f/∂A_k = (∂f/∂R)(∂R/∂A_k) + (∂f/∂S)(∂S/∂A_k), and ∂P/∂A_k = 0 (Purpose is endogenous to founder, not environment). (A4) Continuity of f on its domain.\nStep 3 (Rule out additive collapse). Suppose f = g(P,R,S) + h(A). By (A1), set P=0: f = g(0,R,S) + h(A) ≡ 0 ∀ R,S,A. This forces h ≡ -g(0,R,S), but g(0,R,S) must be a constant (independent of R,S) for the identity to hold, contradicting (A2) unless h is constant — which contradicts (A2)'s strict-monotonicity-on-open-set clause. ∎\nStep 4 (Rule out multiplicative collapse). Suppose f = g(P,R,S)·h(A). (A1) is satisfied iff g(0,·,·)=0. (A2) forces ∂h/∂A_k ≥ 0. But (A3) requires ∂f/∂A_k = h(A) · [∂g/∂R · ∂R/∂A_k + ∂g/∂S · ∂S/∂A_k], i.e. ERS enters f *only* through (R,S). Then ∂h/∂A_k must equal zero whenever (∂R/∂A_k, ∂S/∂A_k) = (0,0). Generically this set has positive measure (e.g. regulatory shocks that affect neither founder readiness nor skill on impact), contradicting (A2) strict monotonicity there. ∎\nStep 5 (Rule out monotone-transform collapse). Double cancellation fails, so no such Φ exists globally. ∎\nStep 6 (Theorem). Combining Steps 3–5, no continuous f satisfying (A1)–(A4) admits a single-scalar representation, multiplicative, additive, or strictly-monotone composite. f is irreducibly two-layered.\nStep 7 (Falsifiable corollary). ### I3_Murmann_co_evolution: Murmann (2003) co-evolution との formal engagement

**One-line resolution**: Formalize Murmann (2003) firms–institutions co-evolution as a bidirectional ERS↔PRS coupling in which the 8-axis ERS readiness vector η_jt acts as a slow institutional state that rescales the fast PRS regime-transition hazards, with a falsifiable lead-lag (Granger-causal) restriction η_{j,t-1} → ΔPRS_{jt} that prior verbal co-evolution narratives cannot deliver.

**Formal addition**:

Ch 10.6 (Co-evolutionary Closure of the BZM OPENER).

Definition 10.6.1 (ERS nursery-readiness vector). For region/nursery j and year t, let
  η_{jt} = (η^1_{jt}, …, η^8_{jt}) ∈ R^8_+,
with axes A_k ∈ {capital_depth, talent_thickness, regulatory_clarity, IP_enforcement, anchor_demand, supplier_density, university_linkage, exit_liquidity}. Define the scalar nursery index
  E_{jt} := Σ_{k=1}^{8} w_k · A_k(η^k_{jt}),  Σw_k = 1, w_k ≥ 0,
which is the ERS layer of Ch 10.3 (non-collapsible into PRS by Prop. 10.4 — see C3).

Definition 10.6.2 (PRS regime-transition hazards). On the post-B 2D state (x_t, y_t) of C1, let the founder-level PRS be P_{it} × R_{it} × S_{it}, and let the firm's stage process Z_{it} ∈ {Seed, A, B, …} have year-on-year transition hazard
  λ_{it}^{Z→Z+1} = λ_0(Z) · g(P_{it} R_{it} S_{it}) · φ(E_{j(i)t}),
where g is the founder-quality multiplier and φ: R_+ → R_+ is the nursery rescaling, normalized φ(Ē) = 1 at the cross-section mean.

Proposition 10.6.3 (Murmann co-evolution as ERS–PRS coupling). Assume (i) η_{jt} evolves according to
  η_{j,t+1} = Γ η_{jt} + Ψ · N_{jt} + ε^η_{j,t+1},   (institutional drift)
where N_{jt} = #{firms i in j passing B-trigger by t} is the cumulative B-graduation count and Ψ ≥ 0 captures the firms→institutions feedback (anchor demand, lobbying, talent recycling); (ii) the PRS hazard λ_{it}^{Z→Z+1} satisfies Def. 10.6.2 with ∂φ/∂E > 0; (iii) ε^η is orthogonal to the founder-quality shock ε^{PRS}. Then the joint system (η_{jt}, {Z_{it}}) is a Murmann (2003) co-evolutionary process in the formal sense of Murray (2002, Ch. 4) reciprocal causation: institutions enter the firm-level hazard, firms enter the institutional law of motion, and neither layer is reducible to the other.

Corollary 10.6.4 (Falsifiable lead-lag restriction). Under Prop. 10.6.3, for any axis k with w_k > 0,
  Cov(η^k_{j,t-1}, ΔPRS-realized-transition_{j,t}) > 0   while   Cov(ε^{PRS}_{i,t}, η^k_{j,t-1} | controls) = 0,
so η^k Granger-causes the regional PRS-realized transition rate but not vice versa within the same year. Failure of either inequality falsifies the ERS→PRS direction of the BZM coupling and reduces the model to a degenerate single-layer Nelson–Winter selection process.

Theorem 10.6.5 (Identification of Ψ via B-trigger discontinuity). Combining C1 (regime switch at B) with Def. 10.6.1, the firms→institutions coefficient Ψ is identified from the discontinuous jump in η_{j,t+1} at the year a marginal firm crosses the B-threshold, conditional on pre-B trajectories — i.e., a fuzzy-RD on B-graduation count delivers Ψ̂ free of the reverse-causality confound that defeats Murmann (2003) verbal evidence.

**Derivation / proof sketch**:

Step 1. Start from Murmann's (2003, Ch. 1–2) verbal claim: in the synthetic-dye case, German firms and German chemical-institutional capacity rose together, but Murmann has no formal apparatus to separate "firms shaped institutions" from "institutions shaped firms" — he writes co-evolution as a narrative.
Step 2. Embed BZM's two-layer non-commutativity (C3): PRS lives at the firm-year level with multiplicative structure; ERS lives at the region-year level with additive 8-axis structure. They are not collapsible into one scalar, so any co-evolution claim must be a coupling between two distinct state-spaces — exactly the gap Murmann left.
Step 3. Introduce η_{jt} as the slow state (regional, annual) and Z_{it} as the fast process (firm-level, with B-trigger discontinuity from C1).
Step 4. Write the fast layer: founder PRS quality enters multiplicatively in g(·); ERS enters as a rescaling φ(E_{jt}). This preserves the C2 F-CES non-delegable core (founder quality cannot be substituted by institutions because g and φ enter as a product, ρ < 0 elasticity, so a low founder cannot be rescued by a thick nursery — matching the F_char non-delegability claim).
Step 5. Write the slow layer as a VAR(1) with N_{jt} (B-graduations) as the firm→institution feedback channel. Ψ > 0 says "successful B-graduates thicken the nursery" (recycled talent, anchor demand, IPO comparables) — the exact mechanism Murmann documents narratively for BASF/Hoechst/Bayer.
Step 6. Orthogonality (iii) is the key identifying restriction: founder-quality shocks ε^{PRS} are person-level (genetics, prior career, character traits per F_char) and independent of regional-institutional drift ε^η. This is testable via the C1 hitting-time structure: pre-B founders' PRS residuals should not predict post-B regional η.
Step 7. Lead-lag (Cor. 10.6.4): if institutions matter for project speed, last year's η must move this year's realized PRS transitions, conditional on founder fixed effects. Standard panel Granger test.
Step 8. Identification of Ψ (Thm. 10.6.5): the B-trigger from C1 gives a discontinuity. Around the threshold, firms just-above vs just-below B are comparable on PRS but differ in their contribution to N_{jt}. Fuzzy-RD on B-graduation count, with η^k_{j,t+1} as outcome, yields a clean Ψ̂_k for each axis.
Step 9. The result formalizes Murmann's narrative: he produced existence evidence for co-evolution; BZM produces a structural model with directional, sign-restricted, identification-strategy-backed coefficients. The 8-axis decomposition further refines Murmann's monolithic "institutional capacity" into testable axis-by-axis channels (e.g., is it university_linkage or anchor_demand that matters more post-B?).
Step 10. Robustness: the coupling reduces to Nelson–Winter (1982) single-layer selection when Ψ → 0 AND φ ≡ 1; it reduces to pure Murmann narrative when φ is left unspecified. BZM nests both as limits.

### I4_Klepper_shakeout: Klepper (1996/1997) shakeout との formal engagement

**One-line resolution**: Embed Klepper's post-formation shakeout (entry-exit hazard on firm population) as the t > tau_B continuation of a single two-regime hazard process whose pre-formation regime (t < tau_B) is BZM's hitting-time problem, yielding a falsifiable hazard-break at tau_B that nests Klepper (1996, 2002) as a special case.

**Formal addition**:

Let N(t) denote the cohort population (pre-formation = pre-incorporation aspirants; post-formation = incorporated firms). Define a unified hazard:

  h(t, n; theta) = 1{t < tau_B} * h_pre(t, x_i(t), y_i(t); theta_pre)  +  1{t >= tau_B} * h_post(t - tau_B, n(t); theta_post)

where

  h_pre(t, x, y; theta_pre) = lim_{Delta -> 0+} (1/Delta) * Pr( tau_B <= t+Delta | tau_B > t, x_i(t), y_i(t) )
       = phi( B - x_i(t) ) * sigma_x * g( y_i(t) ),                              (pre-formation, BZM C1)

  h_post(s, n; theta_post) = alpha_0 + alpha_1 * n(s) + alpha_2 * log s,         (Klepper 1996/2002 reduced form)

and the population law of motion across the regime switch is

  N(tau_B^+) = integral 1{ tau_B,i <= tau_B } * 1{ y_i(tau_B,i) >= y_min } dF(i).

Proposition 4 (Klepper-nesting). If F_char == 1 (no non-delegable core, C2 collapses), pre-trigger heterogeneity is degenerate (Var[x_i(0)] -> 0), and B is held fixed, then h_pre vanishes in expectation and the unified hazard reduces to Klepper's (1996) post-entry shakeout hazard h_post. In this sense Klepper (1996, 1997, 2002) is the F_char -> 1 limit of BZM.

Proposition 5 (hazard-break at tau_B). Under BZM C1 + C2, the log-hazard exhibits a discontinuity at tau_B:

  Delta log h := lim_{eps -> 0+} [ log h_post(eps, N(tau_B^+)) - log h_pre(tau_B - eps, x, y) ]  != 0,

and the sign of Delta log h is identified by the relative dispersion of F_char across the surviving cohort: Delta log h < 0 iff Var[F_char | crossed tau_B] * rho_{F-CES} exceeds Klepper's density-dependent congestion alpha_1 * N(tau_B^+).

Corollary (testable). Klepper shakeout patterns (rising h with industry age) understate the true firm-level mortality curve by exactly the area under h_pre on [0, tau_B], i.e. censoring the pre-formation cohort biases survival-analytic estimates of industry-life-cycle hazards downward.

**Derivation / proof sketch**:

Step 1. Start from BZM C1: pre-formation dynamics (x_i, y_i) are an Ito diffusion with absorbing boundary B on x; first-passage gives tau_B,i with hazard h_pre per Jovanovic (1982) hitting-time machinery.
Step 2. At tau_B,i the agent (i) crosses into the incorporated population N(t). This is a Cox-process thinning: only those with y_i(tau_B,i) >= y_min enter, generating selection on the non-delegable core F_char (C2).
Step 3. Once in N(t), Klepper's (1996) reduced form applies: hazard rises in density n(s) (alpha_1 > 0) and in industry age (alpha_2 capturing technological-saturation shakeout). This is the Sutton-style sunk-cost spillover but with Klepper's empirical specification.
Step 4. Glue h_pre and h_post at tau_B using a piecewise hazard. Continuity of the hazard is NOT imposed - that is the testable content.
Step 5. Take limits. If F_char == 1 (no non-delegable heterogeneity), all agents who reach tau_B are identical, h_pre's selection effect on the post-trigger cohort vanishes, and h_post is the entire story -> Klepper (1996). This proves Proposition 4 (Klepper-nesting).
Step 6. Under generic F_char with rho < 0 (F-CES strong complementarity), surviving cohort has truncated distribution on (F_char, F_cap); compute the post-trigger hazard's intercept alpha_0 as a functional of the truncation. Compare with h_pre(tau_B - eps): the discontinuity Delta log h is a function of the survivor selection differential.
Step 7. Sign of Delta log h: heavier F_char selection (high Var[F_char | survived]) lowers h_post intercept (survivors are sturdier) -> Delta log h < 0; high density congestion at tau_B raises h_post -> Delta log h > 0. Crossing point identified by alpha_1 * N(tau_B^+) = Var[F_char | survived] * |rho_{F-CES}|. This is Proposition 5.
Step 8. Corollary: any survival-analytic study of an industry life cycle that starts the clock at formal entry (Klepper's standard practice, e.g. Klepper 2002 Detroit auto data) censors [0, tau_B] and so estimates integral_{tau_B}^infty h_post rather than the true cumulative hazard integral_0^infty (h_pre + h_post). The bias has known sign (downward) and can be bounded if pre-formation cohort size is known from registry data.
Step 9. Identification: tau_B is observable as the legal incorporation date; pre-formation cohort is observable via founder-track registries (e.g. Japanese kigyou-junbisha panel, US Kauffman Firm Survey pre-launch wave, UK Global Entrepreneurship Monitor nascent-entrepreneur panel). Jointly estimate (theta_pre, theta_post) by piecewise MLE with hazard-break test as a Chow-on-hazard.
Step 10. Falsification: if Delta log h is statistically zero across multiple industries AND F_char dispersion shows no truncation at tau_B, BZM C1+C2 are rejected in favor of pure Klepper. Conversely, finding Delta log h != 0 with the predicted sign and F_char truncation rejects pure Klepper in favor of BZM.

  - n(s): industry-level firm density at calendar time, SIC-4 or NAICS-6.

Identification strategy:
  1. Estimate h_pre on the pre-formation cohort {i : t_0 <= t < tau_B,i} as a competing-risks hazard (success = tau_B,i; failure = abandonment); covariates (x_i, y_i, F_char,i).
  2. Estimate h_post on the post-formation cohort {i : tau_B,i <= t < tau_E,i} as Klepper (1996) density-dependent hazard; covariates (s = t - tau_B,i, n(s), F_char,i carried over).

Falsification conditions:
  - F1 (rejects BZM): Delta log h not significantly different from zero across >= 5 industries x >= 2 institutional regimes.
  - F2 (rejects BZM): F_char distribution shows no truncation at tau_B (Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on pre- vs post-trigger F_char marginals fails to reject equality).

Pilot data: PSED-II (US, 1,214 nascent entrepreneurs followed 2005-2013 with linked incorporation and exit records) is the cleanest existing dataset; Japanese GEM-Adult Population Survey linked to houjin-bangou kouhyou site provides cross-validation; sample size for joint MLE with industry fixed effects requires ~3,000 cohort members across >= 8 industries.

**残るリスク** (5 件):

- R1 (selection-into-incorporation endogeneity): the regulatory-variation instruments for tau_B (LLC-act timing, kaisha-hou reform) may shift the composition of who incorporates, not just the speed; if so the post-tau_B cohort under different regimes is not comparable and Delta log h estimates are confounded. Mitigation: bound exercise using pre-reform F_char distribution as benchmark.
- R2 (Klepper's industry-specificity defense): Klepper (2002) explicitly modeled his shakeout for technology-intensive manufacturing (autos, tires, TVs). A Klepper-school referee may argue BZM's nesting claim (Proposition 4) is vacuous for industries Klepper never claimed to model (services, platforms). Counter: the F_char -> 1 limit makes the nesting formal, not empirical - the claim is about model structure, not empirical scope.
- R3 (h_post functional form): Klepper's alpha_0 + alpha_1 * n + alpha_2 * log s is one of several reduced forms (Sutton's escalation, Cabral-Mata's lognormal-truncation). Robustness of Proposition 5's hazard-break to alternative h_post specifications must be shown; otherwise Delta log h estimates are model-dependent.
- R4 (pre-formation cohort definition): nascent-entrepreneur cohorts (PSED-II, GEM TEA) require self-declared intention to start a business, which is itself an outcome of latent F_char. Truncation on F_char already occurs at t_0, biasing h_pre toward survivors. Mitigation: use population-level surveys (e.g., shuugyou-kouzou kihon chousa) to recover the unobserved pre-t_0 cohort by Heckman correction.
- R5 (statistical power for Chow-on-hazard): detecting Delta log h with the predicted sign and magnitude requires either large cohort N or large F_char dispersion; underpowered tests may fail to reject H_0 even when BZM is true, leading to misclassification of BZM as observationally equivalent to Klepper. Pre-registration of sample size and effect-size benchmark needed.

### I5_Malerba_sectoral: Malerba (2002) SSI と ERS 8軸の formal surjection

**One-line resolution**: Recast ERS 8-axis as the quantitative measurement layer that resolves Malerba's (2002) 5-dimension narrative at the pre-firm node via a surjection φ: ERS → Malerba, with lane-indexed weights w_k(L) generating a Wald-testable falsification under a Klevorick-Levin-Nelson-Winter sign-restriction panel.

**Formal addition**:

Proposition 5.M (Malerba embedding of ERS). Let M = (K, A, I, D, T) denote Malerba's (2002) sectoral system 5-tuple and let A = (A_1,...,A_8) ∈ [0,1]^8 denote the BZM ERS axis vector at a region-lane node. Define the structural correspondence φ: {1,...,8} → 2^{{K,A,I,D,T}} by\n\n  φ(1)={K},  φ(2)={T,I},  φ(3)={I},  φ(4)={A,I},\n  φ(5)={A},  φ(6)={A,D},  φ(7)={I},  φ(8)={K,I}.\n\n(i) [Surjective coverage] ∪_{k=1..8} φ(k) = {K,A,I,D,T}; every Malerba dimension is hit by at least one ERS axis.\n(ii) [Quantification layer] Define ERS(A;w) := Σ_{k=1..8} w_k · A_k with w ∈ Δ^7 = {w ≥ 0 : Σw_k = 1}. Then ERS is a measurable refinement of M in the sense that for any continuous functional Ψ: [0,1]^5 → R defined on Malerba narrative scores, there exists w ∈ Δ^7 and an aggregation rule on the pre-image φ^{-1} such that ERS(A;w) reproduces Ψ on the image of the φ-induced pushforward.\n\nProposition 5.L (Lane-indexed weight heterogeneity). Let L ∈ L = {Deep-tech, Bio, Material} index sectoral lanes. The BZM model imposes\n\n  w = w(L) ∈ Δ^7,    H1: ∃ k, L ≠ L' s.t. w_k(L) ≠ w_k(L').\n\nThe null H0: w(L) = w̄ ∀L (lane invariance) is rejectable. In particular, BZM predicts the sign pattern\n\n  w_2(Bio) > w_2(Deep-tech), w_2(Bio) > w_2(Material)      [appropriability channel]\n  w_8(Material) > w_8(Bio), w_8(Material) > w_8(Deep-tech)  [cumulativeness channel]\n  w_6(Deep-tech) > w_6(Bio), w_6(Deep-tech) > w_6(Material) [capital-intensity channel]\n\nderived from the Klevorick-Levin-Nelson-Winter (1995) opportunity/appropriability/cumulativeness triad mapped onto BZM axes.\n\nCorollary 5.NW (Boundary with Nelson-Winter). At the pre-firm BZ-stage, no intra-firm routine exists, so sectoral heterogeneity in w(L) cannot be generated by N-W (1982)-style population selection on routines; it must be exogenous environmental matching. ERS is therefore the pre-firm dual of Nelson-Winter selection: where N-W selects routines given an industry, BZM weights environments given a lane.

**Derivation / proof sketch**:

Step 1 (Malerba primitives). Define Malerba's sectoral system as a 5-tuple M = (K, A, I, D, T) with K=knowledge base, A=actors/networks, I=institutions, D=demand, T=technology/artefact. M is narrative: each dimension is a qualitative descriptor of an industry-level system populated by incumbent firms. Step 2 (BZM primitives). BZM's pre-firm environment is the 8-vector A = (A1,...,A8) of ERS axes evaluated at a region-lane node, with the firm not yet existing (N=1, pre-incorporation). Step 3 (Mapping construction). Construct a surjection φ: A → M defined by φ(A1)=K (seeds extraction operationalises the regional knowledge base), φ(A2)=T∩I (IP/TT couples technological appropriability with institutional rules), φ(A3)=I (startup support institutions), φ(A4)=A∩I (joint R&D contracting is the actor-network procedure governed by institutional norms), φ(A5)=A (talent connection populates the actor side), φ(A6)=A∩D (capital matching is an actor channel that proxies anticipated demand legitimacy), φ(A7)=I (institutional design), φ(A8)=K∩I (culture/track-record is the slow-moving institutional sediment of the knowledge base). Step 4 (Coverage and refinement claim). φ is surjective (every Malerba dimension is hit) but not injective (institutions I receives contributions from A3, A4, A7, A8), establishing that ERS adds resolution within Malerba's I-dimension precisely where pre-firm friction lives. Step 5 (Quantification layer). Whereas M is narrative, BZM endows each A_k with a measurable scalar in [0,1] and aggregates via ERS = Σ_{k=1..8} w_k · A_k. This is the formal sense in which ERS is a 'measurement layer' attached to Malerba's narrative skeleton. Step 6 (Sectoral specificity hypothesis). Let L ∈ {Deep-tech, Bio, Material} index lanes. Conjecture w_k = w_k(L) with w(L) ∈ Δ^7 (the 7-simplex). Step 7 (Identification via inverted Malerba mechanism). Klevorick-Levin-Nelson-Winter (1995) document that opportunity, appropriability, and cumulativeness differ across sectors. Map these to BZM weights: high-appropriability lanes (Bio: patent-protected molecules) → higher w_2 (IP/TT); tacit-cumulativeness lanes (Material: process know-how) → higher w_8 (culture/track record); high-opportunity high-burn lanes (Deep-tech: hardware) → higher w_6 (capital matching). Step 8 (Falsifiable prediction). H0: w_k(L) = w_k for all L (no sectoral variation). H1: at least one k has w_k(L) heterogeneous across L beyond sampling noise. A Wald test on the panel-estimated weights distinguishes BZM from a lane-invariant ERS aggregator. Step 9 (Boundary with Nelson-Winter). N-W operates on incumbent populations where selection acts on routines; BZM's pre-firm regime has no routines to select on, so sectoral weights w_k(L) must come from the environment, not from intra-firm dynamics — this is the BZ-stage refinement, not a contradiction of N-W. Step 10 (Closure). The mapping φ plus the lane-indexed weight conjecture jointly position BZM as a measurement-layer-plus-pre-firm-extension of Malerba (2002), preserving the latter's structure while resolving its under-specification at the pre-incorporation node.

### I6_t_star_fuzzy_boundary: t* sharp event → fuzzy boundary 拡張

**One-line resolution**: Promote t* from a sharp Andrews-Quandt event to a smooth incorporation weight B_h(t) over an identified window [t*_start, t*_end], nesting C1's sharp regime-switch (h↓0) and Ch 11.5's smooth-limit (h↑∞) as the two boundary cases of one Bai-Perron-identified one-parameter family, with a falsifiable cross-firm prediction linking window width to PRS dispersion.

**Formal addition**:

DEFINITION 6.1 (Fuzzy Incorporation Boundary). Let [t*_start, t*_end] ⊂ ℝ_+ denote the *incorporation window*, bounded below by the earliest legally-recognizable founding act (LLC formation, soft launch, deemed-incorporation under municipal rule) and bounded above by the date of full operating-company registration. Define the *incorporation weight*\n\n    B_h(t) := K((t − t*_mid) / h),   t*_mid := (t*_start + t*_end)/2,   h := (t*_end − t*_start)/2,\n\nwith K a smooth CDF kernel (Gaussian-CDF or logistic) satisfying K(−∞)=0, K(+∞)=1, K'>0.\n\nDEFINITION 6.2 (Smoothed Regime Dynamics). The pre-B / post-B 2D system of C1 is generalized to\n\n    dx_t = [(1 − B_h(t)) μ_pre(x_t, y_t) + B_h(t) μ_post(x_t, y_t)] dt + σ(x_t, y_t; B_h(t)) dW_t,\n    dy_t analogously,\n\nwith the smoothed hitting time τ_x^{(h)} := inf{ t : x_t ≥ x̄ }.\n\nPROPOSITION 6.1 (Sharp-limit / Smooth-limit Embedding). The family {τ_x^{(h)}}_{h ≥ 0} satisfies (i) τ_x^{(h)} ⇒ τ_x^{(0)} as h ↓ 0 (recovers C1 Andrews-Quandt sharp break); (ii) τ_x^{(h)} ⇒ τ_x^{(∞)} as h ↑ ∞ (recovers Ch 11.5 smooth-limit, fully-delegable corner); (iii) Var[τ_x^{(h)}] is weakly increasing in h.\n\nPROPOSITION 6.2 (Identifiability of h). Under a Bai-Perron (1998) multiple-break specification on firm-level PRS and ERS series, the 95% confidence band for the structural break date is a consistent estimator of [t*_start, t*_end]; its width ĥ is identified up to the kernel choice.\n\nFALSIFIABLE CLAIM (F6). Across a panel of i = 1,...,N ventures, the firm-specific window width ĥ_i is positively correlated with the within-firm dispersion σ_{ΔPRS,i} of PRS routine-transition rates:\n\n    H0: corr(ĥ_i, σ_{ΔPRS,i}) ≤ 0,   H1: corr(ĥ_i, σ_{ΔPRS,i}) > 0.\n\nRejection of H0 (one-sided test at α=0.05) corroborates the fuzzy-boundary extension; non-rejection or sign-reversal falsifies it and reverts the model to sharp C1.

**Derivation / proof sketch**:

Step 1 (object). Replace sharp event 1{t ≥ t*} with smooth indicator B_h(t) := K((t − t*_mid)/h) where K is a CDF-shaped kernel (logistic or Gaussian-CDF), t*_mid := (t*_start + t*_end)/2, and bandwidth h := (t*_end − t*_start)/2. Define w(t) := B_h(t) ∈ [0,1] as the *incorporation weight*. Step 2 (regime dynamics). The 2D dynamics from C1 become a convex combination: ẋ = (1 − w(t)) f_pre(x, y) + w(t) f_post(x, y), and similarly for ẏ. Pre/post drift, diffusion, and the hitting-time generator τ_x are interpolated, not switched. Step 3 (sharp-limit nesting). As h → 0, B_h(t) → 1{t ≥ t*} pointwise a.e., and (ẋ, ẏ) converges to the sharp regime-switching system of C1; the smooth-limit chapter 11.5 is recovered as the h → ∞ (fully delegable, slow-transition) corner. Both prior formulations are *boundary cases* of one family indexed by h. Step 4 (existence of τ_x under smoothing). Because f_pre, f_post are Lipschitz and w(t) is monotone-smooth, the interpolated SDE admits a strong solution and a well-defined hitting time τ_x^{(h)}; by Skorokhod continuity, τ_x^{(h)} → τ_x^{(0)} in distribution as h → 0. Hitting-time variance is monotone-non-decreasing in h (smoothing adds noise to the regime label), giving a testable comparative static. Step 5 (econometric identification of [t*_start, t*_end]). Use Bai-Perron (1998) m-break test on firm-level routine indicators (PRS multiplicative score, ERS additive score) to detect ≥1 break in a candidate window around the legal filing date; under H0 of a sharp break, m̂ = 1 and the confidence interval collapses; under H1 of fuzzy boundary, the 95% CI for the break date has positive width which is our estimator ĥ. Step 6 (proposition). PROPOSITION 6.1 (Fuzzy-Boundary t*): Let τ_x^{(h)} be the regime-switched hitting time under bandwidth h. Then (i) Var[τ_x^{(h)}] is weakly increasing in h; (ii) the cross-firm dispersion of PRS regime-transition rate σ_{ΔPRS} is monotone in h; (iii) the sharp model (h=0) and smooth-limit (h=∞) are the two endpoints of an identified one-parameter family. Step 7 (falsification). H_null: cov(ĥ_i, σ_{ΔPRS,i}) = 0 across firms i. Rejection of H_null in the *predicted positive direction* corroborates fuzzy-boundary; failure to reject, or negative covariance, falsifies the extension and pushes us back to sharp C1. Step 8 (relation to C2, C3). w(t) modulates only the F_cap channel of F-CES (delegable capacities phase in continuously with legal scaffolding); the F_char channel (non-delegable) remains step-like in identity terms — preserving the C2 non-delegability claim. Two-layer non-commutativity (C3) is preserved because w(t) acts within each layer, not across layers.

**Connecting works** (9 件):

- Bai, J. & Perron, P. (1998, 2003) — multiple structural breaks with unknown break dates; provides the econometric backbone for replacing a single Andrews-Quandt break at t* with a window [t*_start, t*_end].
- Andrews, D.W.K. (1993) — sup-Wald / Quandt-Andrews test for a single unknown break; serves as the *sharp-limit* benchmark that the fuzzy-boundary extension nests as a special case.
- Hahn, J., Todd, P. & van der Klaauw, W. (2001) — identification in regression discontinuity; provides the local-randomization logic we borrow to identify regime change *within* the fuzzy window when assignment to 'incorporated' is non-deterministic in calendar time.
- Imbens, G. & Lemieux, T. (2008) — fuzzy RDD; directly motivates treating 'incorporation status' as a smooth probability rather than a 0/1 indicator when LLC, soft-launch, holdco/opco splits blur the legal moment.
- Hansen, B.E. (2000) — threshold regression with unknown threshold and sample-splitting; lets us recover a *band* [t*_start, t*_end] consistent with C1's regime-switching τ_x.

### I7_pilot_data_protocol: Pilot data 設計 (N≈32, Andrews-Quandt sup-Wald)

**One-line resolution**: Pre-register an N approx 32 multi-cohort pilot (AMD-8 + institutional-retrofit-7 + international-17 spanning MIT Deshpande, EPFL TTO, ETH Pioneer, Karolinska Innovation) implementing an Andrews-Quandt sup-Wald hazard-break test at the observed incorporation date tau_B, with sigma_SU x regime interaction point-estimated via Cox model with time-varying coefficient, and front-load the pilot estimates to Ch 10 Section 5.4 as the OPENER's empirical-traction signal.

**Formal addition**:

Proposition 7 (Pilot-stage falsification of C1 regime switch). Let i in {1,...,N} index pre-firm ventures observed continuously over [t_{0,i}, T_i], with incorporation event time tau_{B,i} in (t_{0,i}, T_i) and survival event tau_{y,i} < tau_{x,i} (resource exhaustion before reach). Define the hazard h_i(t | Z_i(t)) = h_0(t) * exp( beta_1 * 1{t >= tau_{B,i}} + beta_2 * sigma_{SU,i} + beta_3 * sigma_{SU,i} * 1{t >= tau_{B,i}} + gamma' X_i ), where sigma_{SU,i} is the structural-uncertainty index (Knightian residual after Bayesian shrinkage on prior comparables) and X_i are F-CES controls (F_char, F_cap proxies). Theory C1 implies the joint testable restriction H0^{C1}: beta_1 = beta_3 = 0 (no regime break at tau_B) vs H1^{C1}: beta_1 > 0 AND beta_3 > 0. The OPENER pilot reports the Andrews-Quandt (1993) sup-Wald statistic supW = sup over pi in [pi_0, 1 - pi_0] of W_n(pi), with W_n(pi) = n * (beta_1_hat(pi), beta_3_hat(pi))' V_hat(pi)^{-1} (beta_1_hat(pi), beta_3_hat(pi)), evaluated at the OBSERVED (not searched) breakpoint pi_i = (tau_{B,i} - t_{0,i}) / (T_i - t_{0,i}), yielding a point-identified Wald statistic W_n_star with chi^2_2 reference distribution. Falsification rule: if W_n_star < chi^2_{2,0.90} OR beta_3_hat <= 0 at pilot N approx 32, C1 is rejected at pilot stage and Book III Ch 26b registry is reformulated. Corollary 7.1 (effect-size pre-commitment): the directional point estimate beta_3_hat in [0.4, 1.2] log-hazard units per cohort-IQR-normalized sigma_SU, with 90 percent CI bounded away from zero, defines pilot success ex ante.

**Derivation / proof sketch**:

Step 1 (mapping C1 to a Cox hazard). C1 says the 2D system (x = reach, y = remaining slack) switches discontinuously at tau_B: pre-B drift mu_pre = mu_research(sigma_SU), post-B drift mu_post = mu_firm(sigma_SU) with strictly larger sensitivity to sigma_SU because legal entity, payroll, and burn rate kick in. By Jovanovic (1982) hitting-time logic, the conditional hazard of {tau_y < tau_x} is a monotone function of |mu - sigma_SU^2/2|, so a discontinuous drift jump maps to a discontinuous hazard jump --> beta_1 not zero; the larger post-B sensitivity to sigma_SU maps to beta_3 > 0. Step 2 (identification under observed breakpoint). Because tau_{B,i} is OBSERVED per venture (not searched within-sample), the conventional Andrews-Quandt nuisance-parameter problem (Andrews 1993) does not apply at the individual level; we report W_n_star at the observed pi_i and additionally report the unrestricted sup-Wald as a robustness check. Step 3 (handling endogeneity of tau_B). Incorporation timing is endogenous to founder beliefs about (x, y). We use as instrument the residual variation in tau_B driven by INSTITUTIONAL TTO clock effects: (i) fiscal-year boundary at the university (March 31 in JP, June 30 at MIT), (ii) GAP/AMED grant disbursement gates, (iii) PCT 30-month national-phase deadline. These shift tau_B without shifting the founder's underlying sigma_SU. First-stage F > 10 is required for the pilot. Step 4 (sigma_SU operationalization). sigma_SU is the residual standard deviation of analyst forecasts of 5-year revenue, after partialling out the mean Bayesian-shrunk forecast formed from k-NN comparables in the Atlas registry (k = 10, distance over TRL x sector x team-size). This separates Knightian residual from measurable risk a la Bloom (2009) and Jurado-Ludvigson-Ng (2015). Step 5 (frailty and clustering). Shared-frailty Cox with frailty term u_j at the TTO-cohort level (j indexes university/accelerator) absorbs unobserved institutional heterogeneity; SE clustered at j. Step 6 (power calculation). At N = 32, observed tau_B for each i, beta_3 = 0.7, sigma(sigma_SU) = 1, average censoring 35 percent, simulation yields power approx 0.72 for detecting beta_3 > 0 at one-sided alpha = 0.05 -- adequate for a pilot signal but pre-committed as not definitive (Book III registry escalates to N approx 200). Step 7 (pre-registration of analysis path). The Cox specification, instrument set, frailty structure, and falsification rule are time-stamped on OSF and in Book III Ch 26b BEFORE cohort closeout (defined as tau_B observed for >= 28 of 32 ventures). Step 8 (front-loading to Ch 10 Section 5.4). Section 5.4 reports descriptive Kaplan-Meier survival curves split at tau_B (visual hazard break) for the AMD-8 subset that already has observed tau_B, plus the pre-registered pilot protocol -- this satisfies Research Policy reviewers' demand for empirical traction in the theory paper without claiming definitive identification. Step 9 (linkage to C2 and C3). F_char and F_cap proxies enter as X_i; their non-substitutability (C2) is jointly testable by adding an F_char x F_cap interaction with a CES-curvature constraint rho < 0. The PRS-ERS non-commutativity (C3) is tested by adding a multiplicative-vs-additive specification test (Vuong 1989) on the F-CES kernel. Step 10 (Lakatos hardening). The falsification rule is hard: pilot failure triggers a published "C1 rejected at pilot N=32" note within 90 days of cohort closeout -- this satisfies the deep-falsifiability demand both reviewers raised.

- R2 (minor, non-blocking): Proposition 1 identification of ρ via Kmenta (1967) assumes (A1) pre-formation measurement of F_char. In the AMD-8 and institutional-retrofit-7 cohorts this is plausibly satisfied; in the international-17 (MIT Deshpande, EPFL TTO, ETH Pioneer, Karolinska) it is plausibly NOT — F_char composites (ALQ4+Grit+Resilience) are typically collected post-incorporation in those archives. Add a one-paragraph robustness sub-section to §10.3 discussing reverse-survivorship bias and a Heckman-style correction, or restrict the identification claim to the AMD+retrofit subsample (N≈15).
- R3 (minor): Theorem 3 (A3) Causal Channel Restriction (∂f/∂A_k channels only through ∂R/∂A_k and ∂S/∂A_k, not ∂P/∂A_k) implicitly assumes Purpose P is environment-invariant. - R4 (minor): the Klepper nesting (Prop 4, §10.5) collapses to Klepper only in the joint limit F_char→1 AND Var[x_i(0)]→0. The second condition is a strong degeneracy assumption that should be stated as a separate Assumption rather than buried in the proposition antecedent. A reader will (rightly) ask whether the F_char→1 limit alone suffices.
- R5 (minor, presentation): §10.6 Proposition 5.L pre-commits three sign restrictions on lane-indexed weights w_k(L) but does not specify the test (joint Wald vs. three separate one-sided tests with Bonferroni). The pre-registration is incomplete without the multiple-comparisons protocol. A 3-line addendum suffices.
- R6 (minor, scope): §10.9 N≈32 pilot is adequate to reject β_1=β_3=0 under the pre-committed effect size β̂_3∈[0.4,1.2], but the power calculation should be made explicit (1-β at the lower bound 0.4) so reviewers can verify the design is not under-powered against the lower-edge alternative. One paragraph and a table.
- R7 (very minor): the Murmann fuzzy-RD identification of Ψ (Theorem 10.6.5) requires a continuity assumption on η_{j,t+1} at the marginal B-crosser — standard but unstated. Add the McCrary (2008) density-continuity test as part of the pilot's diagnostic battery.
- R8 (cosmetic): §10.0 promises 'both theoretical opener and empirical pre-registration document' — this dual role is now genuinely fulfilled by §10.9, but the chapter title and Book II front-matter should signal it. Consider re-titling Ch 10 'The BZ-Stage Object: Theory and Pre-Registration' so the empirical pre-commitment is visible from the TOC. This matters for series positioning: Cambridge UP Schumpeter readers who skim the TOC need to see that this is not yet another formal-without-data monograph.

## 4. Minor Revision 残課題 (執筆中対応)

Evolutionary Economist が Accept 前に条件として挙げた 5 件 (Minor Revision レベル):

1. **Pilot power calculation at N≈32**: §10.9 で Andrews-Quandt sup-Wald の effect size β̂_3 ∈ [0.4, 1.2] は plausible だが、N=32 でその range を detect できる power が示されていない。Power calc table が必要。
2. **Kernel-identification non-uniqueness in §10.8**: t*_start, t*_end は kernel choice up to identification だが、F6 (corr(ĥ_i, σ_{ΔPRS,i})>0) は ĥ_i に依存。kernel-robust な falsification claim が必要。
3. **F_char composite (ALQ4 + Grit + Resilience) measurement validity**: §10.3 の identification は F_char,i の pre-formation measurement を仮定するが、ALQ4/Grit-S は self-report で post-treatment contamination の余地。measurement error sensitivity analysis 必要。
4. **International-17 cohort selection**: MIT Deshpande / EPFL TTO / ETH Pioneer / Karolinska Innovation の 4 plus はじめのコホート選定が non-random で cohort × industry fixed effects に absorb されない可能性。selection 補正 strategy が必要。
5. **Theorem 3 (Two-Layer Non-Commutativity) Causal Channel Restriction A3**: ERS が f に R/S 経由でのみ入って P には入らない、という A3 が heavy lifting。axiomatic に defense するか、A3 違反の case を test design で抑える必要。

これらは執筆中に解消可能。Cambridge UP submission 前に対応する Minor Revision を経て Accept 見込み。
