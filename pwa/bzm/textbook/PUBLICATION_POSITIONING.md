# Before Zero Publication Positioning

> Last updated: 2026-06-01 JST
>
> This is the publication-positioning gate for the public manuscript. It defines
> what the book promises, who it is for, who speaks, and how company names are
> handled. It is an internal editorial rule, not reader-facing copy.

## 1. Core Decision

This book is not a company brochure.

It is a public, reader-first book about the Before Zero phase: the phase before a
research result becomes a company, where technology, IP, customers, founder
function, institutional support, capital, and timing must be read together.

The book must be readable as universal practical knowledge for research
commercialization. It should not require the reader to know the author's
company, internal systems, events, thread operations, or project names.

## 2. Main Purpose

The main purpose is:

> Help people involved in research commercialization see what must be decided
> before a research result should become a company.

The book should help readers:

- separate research excellence from company readiness;
- detect local optimization across grants, universities, VC, companies, and
  support programs;
- protect researchers from being pushed into a crude "researcher CEO or external
  CEO" binary;
- design conversations before incorporation creates irreversible expectations;
- connect field judgments to a later readiness model without starting from the
  model.

The book should not primarily:

- introduce AMD, Team ARMADA, or internal operations;
- sell a consulting service;
- make the author's company the hero;
- ask readers to adopt an internal system;
- present private event notes as public evidence.

## 3. Target Readers

Primary readers:

- university and public-research institute industry-collaboration staff;
- URA, TLO, EIR, and startup-support teams;
- researchers considering commercialization;
- young people who want to become deep-tech founders, CEOs, COOs, or venture
  builders.

Secondary readers:

- VC, CVC, angel investors, and grant-program operators;
- corporate R&D and new-business teams working with research institutions;
- local governments, ministries, and ecosystem builders;
- university leadership designing startup-support systems.

The book should assume these readers are smart but busy. They may not know the
author's internal vocabulary. They should be able to use a chapter without
knowing any private context.

## 4. Reader Outcome

After reading, the reader should be able to:

- ask better first questions before company formation;
- distinguish GO / WAIT / HOLD / NO_GO / RESOURCE_SHIFT situations;
- identify whether the current bottleneck is technology, customer, governance,
  social acceptance, talent, founder function, macro timing, or institutional
  support;
- interpret investor, company, and university reactions as signals rather than
  personal judgments;
- design disclosure, role allocation, and support sequencing without damaging
  researcher trust;
- understand why the later theory names these field factors as model variables.

## 5. Narrator Position

The narrator is not a salesperson.

Preferred narrator stance:

- a field-literate guide;
- an experienced observer of research commercialization;
- someone who has seen repeated Before Zero failure patterns;
- someone translating field practice into reusable questions and model
  language.

Avoid narrator stance:

- "our company does this";
- "we at AMD discovered";
- "follow our method";
- "our platform solves";
- "the author's judgment proves";
- "inside our event/transcript, we heard."

Recommended public narrator language:

- `本書では`
- `現場では`
- `支援者は`
- `研究者は`
- `産学連携担当者やURAは`
- `会社化前の会話では`
- `繰り返し起きるのは`

Use `筆者` or `筆者ら` only in:

- author note;
- methodology note;
- afterword;
- limited disclosure that a framework is derived from field practice.

## 6. Company Name Policy

`AMD` is an internal abbreviation. Do not use it in public manuscript body copy.

Default public manuscript rule:

- Do not use `AMD`.
- Do not use `Team ARMADA` in chapter body copy.
- Do not use `株式会社チームアルマダ` in chapter body copy.
- Do not use `まさ` as authority or anecdotal proof.

Allowed exceptions:

- Author bio.
- Acknowledgements.
- A short methodology note if needed.
- A clearly marked case/method appendix, after editorial approval.

If a company reference is necessary, prefer:

- `筆者らの実務経験`
- `事業化支援の現場`
- `スタジオ型支援者`
- `外部支援者`
- `伴走者`

But even these should be used sparingly. The subject of the book is Before Zero,
not the support provider.

## 7. Prohibited / Replacement Rules

Avoid company-introduction copy such as:

- `AMD は、研究機関に眠るシーズを発掘し...`
- `Team ARMADA は...`
- `当社は...`
- `弊社の支援では...`
- `私たちは...を提供している`
- `AMD OS`
- `L2`, `D-7 Textbook Insights`, `candidate`, `local applier`, `routing`
- source paths, specs, ledgers, deployment notes, thread/worker language
- private event names or transcript references

Replace with reader-first framing:

- `研究成果の会社化前には...`
- `支援者が確認すべき問いは...`
- `産学連携担当者やURAが見落としやすいのは...`
- `研究者が背負わされやすいのは...`
- `外部支援で補完できる機能は...`
- `この章で扱うのは...`

## 8. Opening Promise

The opening should promise:

- This book helps the reader read the phase before incorporation.
- The book starts from field frictions, not from theory or company history.
- The reader will learn questions, lenses, and decision gates.
- The later theory gives names and structure to field factors the reader has
  already seen.

The opening should not promise:

- that the author's company is the answer;
- that one support method solves all research commercialization problems;
- that every research result should become a startup;
- that the model replaces judgment;
- that the book is investment, legal, or IP advice.

## 9. Publication Gate

Every public manuscript chapter must pass this gate:

- Can a reader who has never heard of the author's company understand why this
  chapter matters?
- Is the subject Before Zero, not the support provider?
- Does the chapter avoid internal abbreviations and private context?
- Does it make the reader more capable after reading?
- Does any company/author reference serve trust, not promotion?

If a chapter fails this gate, it should be rewritten before further manuscript
expansion.
