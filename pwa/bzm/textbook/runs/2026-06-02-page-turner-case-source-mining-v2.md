## Executive summary

The current book has a viable throughline, but it still needs more irreversible-feeling moments. The strongest page-turner material is not another concept. It is a chain of small visible artifacts that travel across rooms and become dangerous when they are too clean:

- a deck sentence that is not false, but is too strong;
- a budget-owner cell that stays blank while everyone says "customer";
- a send-scheduled email that must be stopped before it becomes disclosure;
- a WAIT ledger that almost becomes a lost quarter;
- a paid evidence receipt or refusal that reveals whether the project can earn without lying to itself;
- an unknown / not_started matrix that proves the institution has menus but not yet a corridor.

## What the current book still lacks

1. `public_ready`: Cost before concept. Several chapters name the right distinction before a reader feels the damage of not making it.
2. `public_ready`: A relationship scar that persists. The current repair scenes often become too neat; one investor, researcher, company, or university relationship should remain bruised after Ch10.
3. `public_rewrite`: More actual-looking field documents. The book needs safe, composite examples of email subject lines, deck downgrade lines, meeting-close blanks, refusal notes, and owner ledgers.
4. `public_ready`: Paid evidence with two faces. The book has the doctrine, but needs one payment that clarifies and one payment that distracts.
5. `public_ready`: Non-villain conflict. University, program, company, investor, external CEO candidate, and researcher all need visible local rationality.
6. `internal_only`: Source discipline in the background. OS logic such as actual / forecast / unconfirmed, append-only observations, and unknown / not_started should feed scene design, not appear as system explanation.

## Public/internal classification

Public-ready clusters:
- `public_ready`: deck sentence, budget-owner blank, WAIT ledger, disclosure colors, repeat-back, unknown/not_started, ninety-day pilot, relationship repair, support-menu-to-pipeline scenes.
- Use as composite scenes, not factual claims about a specific project or institution.

Public-rewrite clusters:
- `public_rewrite`: external CEO displacement, incorporation obligations, paid evidence vs service detour, grant/funding pressure, J-curve / IPO counterargument.
- Use with balanced local rationality; avoid legal, finance, investment, or employment advice.

Internal-only clusters:
- `internal_only`: DB tables, routes, tokens, exact amounts, source hashes, private URLs, raw transcript wording, exact source row IDs, score thresholds, formula/rubric changes, person-level judgments, legal conclusions.
- These can inspire public artifacts only after translation into anonymous scene structure.

## Safety notes

- Do not quote raw transcript or raw meeting records. Convert to composite scene, tool artifact, or abstract question.
- Do not name private companies, institutions, people, source URLs, DB rows, branch names, tokens, exact payment amounts, or exact internal scores.
- Do not present legal/IP/incorporation/investment/finance advice. Phrase as "questions to ask before moving" and "conversation triggers."
- Keep every actor locally rational. The harm comes from clocks, incentives, missing ownership, and premature language, not stupidity or malice.
- Keep small paid evidence balanced. It is not "earn small money"; it is evidence only when it reveals pain, budget route, repeatability, and opportunity cost.
- Keep ERS / institution readiness separate from individual case readiness. The nursery changes starting conditions; it is not a hidden boost to a specific project score.
- Keep `unknown` and `not_started` distinct in public language: one is confirmation work, the other is building work.
- Treat theory/model/rubric changes as `internal_only` until reviewed elsewhere. This note supplies narrative material, not theory decisions.
