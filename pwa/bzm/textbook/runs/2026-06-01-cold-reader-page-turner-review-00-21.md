## Executive Verdict

**Verdict: 00-14 is not a full reject; 15-21 is a P0 reject before the next
full-book rewrite.**

The `00-14 continuous page-turner rewrite` worked. It is clearly stronger than
the earlier divided drafts: it has a lived opening, concrete rooms, named
decision artifacts, and a recognizable return from Ch03 loneliness to Ch14
institution pipeline. It is now a usable front-half manuscript.

But it is not yet a commercial page-turner. The problem is no longer "too
internal" or "too conceptual." The problem is that many chapters still end as
competent consulting notes. The reader receives a good tool, then is politely
sent to the next chapter. That is useful, but not addictive.

Chapters 15-21 are weaker. They are field-first in intention, but often behave
like compressed theory explainers with a scene at the top. They name TRL, BRL,
GRL, SRL, HRL, sigma_SU, FRL, integrated score, retrofit, validation, and ERS
too quickly, then provide short tables. The reader can understand them, but the
reason to turn the page is not strong enough.

### Recommendation

- **P0: Rewrite 15-21 before treating 00-21 as one book.**
- **P1: Surgical 00-14 strengthening, not wholesale reject.** Focus on Ch05,
  Ch10, Ch11, Ch12, and template-like endings.
- **P1: Run one integrated rewrite across 00-21 after 15-21 is rebuilt**, so the
  recurring cases do not stop at Ch14 and restart as theory examples.
- **P2: Route/title debt for Ch04/Ch05 remains, but do not mix route cleanup
  into manuscript rewrite unless navigation is in scope.**

## Persona Review

### 研究者

**Sticks:** Ch00, Ch03, Ch04, Ch05, Ch10, Ch18.

Researchers will feel seen by the role whiplash, support-room loneliness,
opening disclosure anxiety, and the refusal to reduce founder readiness to
"business sense." Ch03 is the strongest emotional entrance.

**Drop-off risk:** Ch11, Ch12, Ch15, Ch17, Ch19. These can feel like the book has
moved from "my situation" to "supporters' model language."

**Wanted specificity:**

- A researcher almost withdraws after an external CEO conversation, then trust
  is repaired.
- A paper / patent / student / joint-research conflict that makes disclosure
  anxiety concrete.
- A WAIT case where the researcher is protected rather than slowed down.

### 産連 / URA / TLO

**Sticks:** Ch02, Ch03, Ch05, Ch06, Ch07, Ch09, Ch14, Ch21.

This persona gets the most practical utility. The clock map, disclosure layers,
decision log, responsibility table, and nursery pipeline are directly usable.

**Drop-off risk:** Ch04 if it reads too much like VC/CEO debate; Ch17 if policy
waves are too abstract; Ch18 if founder readiness feels outside their remit.

**Wanted specificity:**

- A pre-disclosure review sheet with sample filled entries.
- A "who must be in the room before the next investor meeting" checklist.
- Unknown vs not_started as an institution hearing artifact, not only a theory
  note.

### 若手事業化人材

**Sticks:** Ch01, Ch08, Ch09, Ch10, Ch18, Ch20.

Ch08's first-30-day path is valuable. This reader needs a craft route: how to
be useful before title.

**Drop-off risk:** Ch11-12 and Ch17 if they feel like ecosystem analysis rather
than field work. Ch19 if score language feels like something seniors use to
evaluate them rather than a tool they can operate.

**Wanted specificity:**

- Day 1 / day 10 / day 30 behavior in one recurring case.
- Example questions for company interviews, not just categories.
- A field note to reusable question method from source-mining v3 C06/C10,
  translated without internal OS language.

### 投資家 / CVC / 事業会社

**Sticks:** Ch01, Ch06, Ch09, Ch10, Ch11, Ch16, Ch19.

They will appreciate the distinction between interest and validation, capital
as uncertainty reduction, and investor feedback as a signal rather than a
personal judgment.

**Drop-off risk:** Ch03 and Ch14 if they feel institution-centric without
showing why investor/company behavior contributes to the burden. Ch18 if
external executive critique feels one-sided.

**Wanted specificity:**

- Investor-type differentiated feedback: VC, CVC, strategic company, grant
  committee, business unit.
- A budget-owner / evaluation-owner / procurement-path scene for BRL.
- A "good investor question, bad timing" example that preserves actor
  rationality.

### 研究機関 / 政策側

**Wanted specificity:**

## Theory Bridge Evaluation

15-21 mostly avoid raw formula dumping, but the current bridge is still too
linear:

1. Open with a short scene.
2. Name a theory term.
3. Explain the term.
4. Show a table.
5. End with action and next theory term.

That pattern is readable, but it is not a page-turner. It makes theory feel
like a curriculum, not the answer to pressure the reader already feels.

### Specific Theory Problems

- **Ch15:** "Why model the field" repeats the book's mission. It should instead
  begin with a case where every prior tool gives a different answer unless a
  model separates axes.
- **Ch16:** XRL axes are clear, but the five-axis table arrives too early. Start
  with one wrong decision caused by conflating TRL and BRL, then name axes.
- **Ch17:** sigma_SU is abstract. Add a local PoC corridor where research,
  policy, hospital/user site, manufacturer/company, and social acceptance are
  close but unconnected.
- **Ch18:** Best of the group. Strengthen the external executive trust-fit
  scene and show two bad matches, not just one contrast table.
- **Ch19:** Integrated score must produce a surprising next action. Example:
  high overall promise, but RESOURCE_SHIFT to GRL before investor meetings.
- **Ch20:** Retrofit validation should update the model's evidence rules, not
  merely log a failure.
- **Ch21:** ERS must not repeat Ch14. Make it an institutional design chapter:
  evidence status, owner, hearing route, next review, and non-mixing with
  venture readiness.
