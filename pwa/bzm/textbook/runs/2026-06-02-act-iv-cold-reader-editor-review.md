## Where it still collapses into explanation

The main relapse is list-shaped prose. The manuscript no longer shows markdown bullets or tables, but many paragraphs still behave like lists. This matters because the book's current promise is narrative pressure before theory. Hidden lists cool the pressure.

Ch12 is the clearest example. The first page works because everyone sees a different meaning in "prepared". Then lines 49-57 name five preparations directly. That is probably true, but it turns the scene into an axis preview. Lines 159-165 then explicitly announce TRL, BRL, GRL, SRL, and HRL. The reader has not yet demanded the labels strongly enough.

Ch13 has emotional stakes, but the middle repeatedly enumerates functions. Lines 71-91 and 127-147 are useful editorially, but in the main body they feel like a role-allocation note wearing prose. The researcher's silence is more powerful than the function inventory; the next pass should trust the silence more.

Ch14 has the right conclusion, but the final theory bridge becomes too tidy. Lines 149-177 summarize the whole book and introduce ERS. That is a valid Act V handoff, but it currently sounds like the narrator is clearing the whiteboard before the theory lecture begins.

The visible explanatory headings also remain. They are not fatal, but several chapter subheads are thesis labels rather than scene turns. If the next residue pass keeps headings, they should feel like dramatic turns, not lesson titles.
