## 1. Brutal Executive Verdict

The current public manuscript is no longer a company brochure, and that is a
real editorial improvement. It avoids the worst internal vocabulary, keeps the
reader in the foreground, and explains the important Before Zero concepts in
plain language.

But it is not yet a book someone keeps reading because they need to know what
happens next.

The main failure is not correctness. The main failure is dramatic pressure.
Nearly every chapter behaves like a careful explanatory essay:

- it opens with a definition or generalized claim;
- it names the relevant actors;
- it explains why the situation is complicated;
- it gives a list of useful questions;
- it closes with a clean conceptual sentence.

That shape is useful for a handbook, but weak for a sellable book. The reader
keeps receiving the answer before they have emotionally entered the problem.
The manuscript repeatedly says "this is a structural issue, not someone's
fault" before showing one painful scene where that distinction matters. The
result is that the text is smart, calm, and morally generous, but it rarely
creates narrative hunger.

The book's current page-turner problems:

1. **No recurring case thread.** The GAP fund / VC / CEO contradiction is the
   strongest dramatic material, but it is not used as a case zero that grows
   across chapters. It appears as explanation, not as a story engine.
2. **Pain is abstracted too quickly.** Researcher loneliness, support
   fragmentation, disclosure fear, investor feedback, and role confusion are
   described accurately, but the reader is not made to sit inside the room
   before the diagnosis arrives.
3. **Chapter endings release tension instead of carrying it.** The "明日使える問い"
   sections are valuable, but they often close the topic. A commercial book
   ending should give one usable action and leave one unresolved problem that
   pulls the reader forward.
4. **Chapters repeat the same moral architecture.** Many chapters return to
   "do not blame one actor; separate the functions; read conditions." Correct,
   but the repeated cadence makes the manuscript feel like variations on one
   memo.
5. **Material volume is still too thin for a book.** Current public manuscript
   is about 50,633 characters across 15 chapters. Most chapters are 2,000 to
   4,300 characters. That is a strong skeleton, not a sellable manuscript. The
   next draft needs 2x to 3x more lived material: scenes, cases, tools,
   worksheets, counterexamples, and reader-specific pathways.

Editorial bottom line:

> The manuscript teaches the right lesson too early. It must first make the
> reader feel the contradiction, then let the structure become necessary.

## 2. Who Is Not Being Hit Hard Enough

### Researcher

Current miss: the text tells researchers that their discomfort may be
structural, but it does not yet dramatize the psychological wound sharply
enough.

What is missing:

- the moment a researcher realizes the polished pitch has made them look more
  ready than they feel;
- the embarrassment of not knowing whether "CEO" is praise, burden, or
  replacement threat;
- the fear that external support may move the research faster than the
  researcher has consented to;
- the practical relief of having roles split without losing authorship of the
  technical meaning.

Rewrite demand: give the researcher a repeated internal question:

> "Am I being asked to carry this company, or am I being prepared to be removed
> from it?"

Do not answer this immediately. Let it haunt the opening half.

### URA / Industry-Collaboration / TLO Staff

Current miss: the text gives them good questions, but not enough recognition of
their operational pain.

What is missing:

- being asked by a PI, university leadership, company, funder, and outside
  mentor to satisfy incompatible timelines;
- knowing the IP sequence matters while everyone else wants a pitch next week;
- the awkwardness of saying "wait" when the institution wants startup output;
- the practical burden of being the only integrator of disconnected rooms.

Rewrite demand: include scenes where an institutional staff member must decide
what to slow down, what to let out, and what to refuse.

### Young Venture Builder / CEO Candidate / EIR

Current miss: the manuscript explains that external management can complement
researchers, but it does not give aspiring operators a clear entry path.

What is missing:

- what a young operator can do before there is a company;
- how to earn trust without taking the researcher's center;
- how to distinguish "I can help" from "I am grabbing the CEO title";
- what first 30 days of useful pre-company work looks like.

Rewrite demand: add a pathway that says: "If you want to become a deep-tech
builder, here is how to carry a function before demanding a title."

### VC / CVC / Grant Operator

Current miss: the text politely explains investor logic, but it does not force
funders to see how their reasonable questions can land as identity confusion.

What is missing:

- the moment a reasonable VC question becomes a personal verdict in the room;
- how grant/pitch formats train founder performance and later finance rooms
  penalize missing CEO capability;
- a guide for asking execution questions without collapsing researcher
  authenticity into CEO readiness.

Rewrite demand: funders need an uncomfortable mirror, not only a translation
table.

### Research-Institution Leadership

Current miss: Chapter 14 is promising but arrives late and abstractly.

What is missing:

- the cost of many support menus without one responsibility pipeline;
- the leadership failure mode: celebrating startup output while leaving the
  integration burden to individual staff;
- examples of institutional nursery design, not only principles.

Rewrite demand: show that institution readiness is not a nice appendix. It is
one of the reasons researchers become lonely in Chapter 3.

## 3. Chapter 00-06: Ruthless Page-Turner Critique

### 00 Prologue: 会社になる前に勝負が決まる

Verdict: clear promise, weak hook.

Opening-page test: fails as a page-turner. The first line is conceptually good,
but the opening immediately becomes a summary of typical conditions. It does
not begin with a person in a room, a contradiction, or a question the reader
needs resolved.

Tension: present but diluted. The prologue names many unresolved factors, so
the reader understands the territory. But because all factors arrive together,
none becomes urgent.

Pain specificity: moderate. "真ん中にいる人が孤独になる" is strong, but it
arrives as conclusion. The reader has not yet watched the loneliness happen.

Repetition risk: high. The prologue already previews research readiness,
clocks, support isolation, GAP/VC/CEO, and incorporation timing. Later chapters
then repeat the same diagnosis.

Rewrite instruction:

- Open with the composite case zero, not a conceptual overview.
- Use 500 to 800 characters of scene before defining Zero.
- Required beats: support-program room, polished pitch, applause, later
  investor/finance room, external executive concern, researcher's role
  confusion.
- End with the unanswered question: "How can the same support system ask one
  person to step forward and step aside?"
- Compress factor lists. Let chapters earn them.

Pass condition: by the end of page one, the reader must feel, "I have seen this
room, and I need someone to explain it."

### 01 Research Results Are Not Companies

Verdict: strong concept, not enough false acceleration.

Opening-page test: partially passes as a useful essay; fails as story. The
chapter starts with a thesis. The material example appears, but it is generic
and bloodless.

Tension: the right tension is there: research proof mistaken for company
readiness. But the chapter does not build momentum from a concrete sequence of
events.

Pain specificity: low to moderate. The danger of blaming researchers for
structural unreadiness is important, but no scene makes that blame sting.

Repetition risk: overlaps with prologue, Chapter 6, Chapter 7, and Chapter 12
through readiness lists.

Rewrite instruction:

- Start with "false acceleration": paper -> patent discussion -> company says
  interesting -> support program accepts -> pitch deck appears -> everyone
  feels closer to company formation.
- Then show the missing pieces one by one as interruptions to the acceleration.
- Keep the two-column split: research proof vs company condition.
- Defer CEO-function detail to the new Chapter 4.
- End with the bridge: "Even when everyone agrees this is promising, they may
  be agreeing on different clocks."

Pass condition: the reader should become suspicious of every "this could be a
startup" moment that has not separated research proof from company proof.

### 02 Different Clocks

Verdict: useful metaphor, too much actor-by-actor lecture.

Opening-page test: informative but slow. The first page names every actor and
their clock before staging a collision.

Tension: conceptually good. The "温度差ではなく時計" phrase is keep-worthy. But
the chapter needs one week, one deadline, one document, or one meeting where the
clocks collide.

Pain specificity: low. The reader sees categories, not conflict.

Repetition risk: medium. Actor clocks return in Chapters 7, 10, 11, and 14.

Rewrite instruction:

- Use a single milestone: "support program needs a pitch deck next Friday."
- Put each clock around that same milestone: researcher wants paper caution;
  TLO wants invention review; company wants enough detail; VC wants execution;
  program wants market story.
- Convert actor sections into short scene turns.
- Add one sentence per actor showing rationality and one showing damage if
  their clock dominates.
- End by saying clock mismatch becomes loneliness when no one integrates the
  clocks.

Pass condition: the reader should be able to draw the clock map for a real case
after reading, not just remember that clocks differ.

### 03 Support Can Isolate Researchers

Verdict: emotionally closest to the book's core, but it resolves too early.

Opening-page test: strongest among 00-03, because it names the role
contradiction early. Still, it explains the contradiction before fully making
the reader experience it.

Tension: strong. Support increases while integration decreases. This should be
the emotional peak of Part 1.

Pain specificity: medium. The "different rooms" material is present but should
be staged more vividly.

Repetition risk: high with Chapter 5 and Chapter 8. Current Chapter 3 already
explains CEO-function decomposition too fully, stealing Chapter 5's force.

Rewrite instruction:

- Make the chapter a corridor of rooms: pitch room, IP room, company interview,
  investor prep, university hallway.
- In each room, the researcher receives one locally rational demand.
- Do not list the CEO functions here. Only reveal that the role burden is
  unassigned.
- Final paragraph must hand directly to the central hinge: "The sharpest
  version appears when one room trains the researcher to act like founder and
  another room asks who the real CEO will be."

Pass condition: the reader should feel the researcher's loneliness before the
chapter explains support local optimization.

### 04 Before Disclosure

Verdict: practical and important, but currently placed too early.

Opening-page test: moderate as an advice chapter; weak in the current narrative
order. It interrupts the build from support isolation to GAP/VC/CEO.

Tension: real but procedural. "Open enough to learn, protected enough not to
break" is good. But without the preceding outward-push scene, it reads like a
compliance chapter.

Pain specificity: good in parts: unknown circulation of slides, researcher
consent, paper/IP order. These should stay.

Repetition risk: overlaps with Chapter 9 on investor meetings and with Chapter
12 on governance readiness.

Rewrite instruction:

- Move after the GAP/VC/CEO hinge.
- Open after the pitch has been sharpened and more people want the slides.
- Frame disclosure as consequence of external acceleration: "the story now
  travels faster than the protection."
- Add explicit consent/trust checkpoint: what has the researcher agreed may
  travel?
- Keep investor-meeting content light; Chapter 9 handles risk-capital learning.
- End with: "Even if the outside conversation is protected, the company vehicle
  still creates irreversible timing."

Pass condition: disclosure should feel like a dramatic risk caused by the
previous chapter, not a detachable checklist.

### 05 GAP Fund / VC / CEO Function

Verdict: best core argument, underdramatized and underused.

Opening-page test: conceptually strong. Still not enough scene. It explains the
contradiction in the first paragraphs, but it does not yet let the reader feel
the room change from applause to doubt.

Tension: strongest in the manuscript. This is the book's hinge.

Pain specificity: medium. The researcher's confusion is named, but the
sequence lacks body: who said what, what changed in the deck, what the VC asked,
what the researcher heard, what the support team did next.

Repetition risk: very high unless Chapter 3, Chapter 8, and Chapter 13 are
strictly separated.

Rewrite instruction:

- Promote this to Chapter 4 in the actual reading order.
- Expand opening scene to 1,200 to 1,800 characters.
- Required scene beats:
  - early program asks for founder-like pitch;
  - deck gets sharper and more public;
  - room rewards entrepreneurial performance;
  - later finance/VC conversation asks who will really run it;
  - external executive suggestion appears;
  - researcher hears contradiction;
  - narrator refuses both crude answers.
- Replace the current plain bullet function list with a CEO-function table:
  function / currently carried by / must stay with researcher / can be
  complemented / risk if unassigned.
- Keep formal FRL names out except a short later-theory bridge.

Pass condition: this chapter must become the scene readers quote when
describing why the book is different.

### 06 Incorporation Timing

Verdict: useful framework, not yet enough irreversible stakes.

Opening-page test: good concept, familiar execution. It tells us company
formation creates obligations, but does not start with a case under timing
pressure.

Tension: good: too early hardens; too late misses the window. Needs concrete
time pressure.

Pain specificity: moderate. Fixed costs and expectations are listed. Missing:
6-9 month fundraising/DD horizon, pre-incorporation tasks, and the anxiety of
choosing when conditions are incomplete.

Repetition risk: overlaps with Chapter 7. Chapter 6 should be about timing
pressure and irreversible expectations. Chapter 7 should be the facilitation
chapter for the first conversation.

Rewrite instruction:

- Open with a project after CEO-function and disclosure mapping: still not
  ready, but VC DD / fundraising / license / hiring could take months.
- Show the double bind: waiting improves readiness but may lose people,
  capital, policy, or customer timing.
- Attach GO / WAIT / HOLD / NO_GO / RESOURCE_SHIFT to the case, not as abstract
  definitions.
- Add pre-incorporation work list: VC conversations, IP/license negotiation,
  share/cap-table design, registration preparation, management commitment,
  researcher's role agreement.
- End by handing to Chapter 7: timing words need a first conversation format.

Pass condition: the reader should stop treating incorporation as a milestone
and start treating it as a commitment that changes the cost of every unresolved
condition.

## 7. Chapter-Ending Bridge Rules

Every chapter ending must do three jobs:

1. **Action:** give one thing the reader can try tomorrow.
2. **Unresolved tension:** name what is still not solved.
3. **Next-chapter pull:** explain why the next chapter is necessary.

Do not end only with a list of questions. A checklist closes cognitive work;
a bridge opens narrative work.

Required bridges for the opening arc:

- Prologue -> 01: first separate research strength from company readiness.
- 01 -> 02: agreement on promise hides different clocks.
- 02 -> 03: clock mismatch becomes loneliness when no one integrates.
- 03 -> 04/new hinge: the role question sharpens into CEO-function
  contradiction.
- Hinge -> Disclosure: function mapping is not enough if the story travels
  faster than protection.
- Disclosure -> Timing: protected conversations still require a decision about
  the company vehicle.
- Timing -> 07: timing words need a structured first conversation.
- 10 -> 11: repeated field misses become variables only after learning is
  captured.
- 14 -> theory: institution readiness and venture readiness must be separated
  before the formal model can help.

Ending test:

> If the final paragraph could be replaced by "まとめると..." it is not a
> page-turner ending.

## 8. Editing Principles For A More Addictive Book

1. **Scene before doctrine.** Do not define the lens until a scene has made the
   lens necessary.
2. **One recurring case beats ten abstract examples.** Case zero should mutate
   across chapters: role confusion, disclosure risk, timing pressure, capital
   readiness, founder readiness, institution pipeline.
3. **Actor rationality before diagnosis.** Show why each actor is reasonable
   before showing why the system is incoherent.
4. **Keep one wound open per chapter.** Do not solve the chapter's emotional
   tension in the first third.
5. **Separate relief from resolution.** A chapter can give a tool while still
   leaving the next structural problem unresolved.
6. **Compress repeated morality.** "Nobody is bad; structure is missing" is a
   thesis. Say it powerfully, then vary the evidence instead of repeating the
   sentence shape.
7. **Tools must be filled, not merely named.** A table with one filled example
   is stronger than a list of conceptual questions.
8. **Theory is earned, not announced.** Formal variable names should feel like
   labels for pain the reader already understands.
9. **Reader identity matters.** Each part should make at least one reader group
   feel directly addressed.
10. **Volume must come from specificity, not padding.** Expand with scenes,
    decisions, documents, sample conversations, and case consequences.

## 10. Editorial Pass / Fail Criteria

### Passing Draft

A rewritten draft passes when:

- the first page contains a memorable scene, not a definition;
- every 00-06 chapter follows Scene -> Tension -> Diagnosis -> Tool/Question ->
  Bridge;
- the GAP/VC/CEO contradiction becomes a recurring case zero;
- Chapter 3, the hinge, Chapter 8, and Chapter 13 no longer repeat the same
  CEO-function explanation;
- every chapter ending creates a reason to read the next chapter;
- at least three tools are filled with example content, not only listed;
- reader groups can see their own pain and next action;
- internal/private vocabulary remains out of public body copy;
- the manuscript becomes at least twice as specific through scenes and tools,
  not through abstract expansion.

### Failing Draft

A rewritten draft fails when:

- chapters still open with definitions or broad generalizations;
- "明日使える問い" remains the only ending mechanism;
- the reader receives the diagnosis before the scene;
- GAP/VC/CEO stays one chapter instead of becoming the first-half hinge;
- disclosure remains a detachable compliance chapter;
- Chapter 6 and Chapter 7 both explain the same timing vocabulary without
  different jobs;
- 07-14 continue expanding theory without repairing the opening arc;
- added volume is mostly more explanation, not more case, tension, tool, or
  reader pathway;
- public copy contains internal source/process vocabulary.
