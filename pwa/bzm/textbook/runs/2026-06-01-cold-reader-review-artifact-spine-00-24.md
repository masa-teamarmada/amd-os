## Executive Verdict

**Commercial cold-reader score: 74 / 100.**

The 00-24 draft now reads like one book more than a sequence of essays. The
traveling artifacts are visible enough that a cold reader can remember them the
next day: the three-column memo, the dangerous `顧客候補あり` line, the red /
yellow / blue slide, the investor deck, the readiness table, and the evidence
rule revision. This is the most important improvement since the earlier
00-21 audit.

It is still not sale-ready. The current draft is strong as an editorially
coherent field book, but not yet strong as a page-turner. The book now has a
spine; it still needs more pressure on that spine. A reader can track the
artifacts, but the artifacts do not always force irreversible consequences,
relationship damage, or surprising reversals. Too many chapters still resolve
into a useful table before the emotional tension has peaked.

The best current shape is:

1. **Ch00-10:** strong field-entry narrative; keep and deepen with more
   dialogue and consequence.
2. **Ch11-14:** necessary bridge; Ch11-12 still cool down and need more scene
   pressure.
3. **Ch15-21:** now mostly earned; theory rises naturally from the field, but
   needs more resistance and roughness.
4. **Ch22-24:** useful, but should become a clearly labeled toolkit / appendix
   section, not numbered narrative body chapters in the same mode as Ch00-21.

## 1. Does 00-24 Connect As One Book?

Yes, with a caveat.

The connection now works at the conceptual and artifact level. The prologue
plants the memo. Ch01 turns a company reaction into `顧客候補あり`. Ch05 makes the
slide travel too fast. Ch09 shows the investor-deck version of the same problem.
Ch16 weakens the customer claim into a more honest evidence statement. Ch20
returns to the artifacts and rewrites evidence rules. This gives the reader a
real path through the book.

The caveat is rhythm. The dominant chapter pattern is still:

`scene -> diagnosis -> table -> action -> bridge`

That pattern is clear and useful, but after twenty-four chapters it becomes
predictable. The book needs more variation:

- one chapter that stays inside a bad meeting longer;
- one chapter that starts from a revised artifact rather than a new scene;
- one chapter that lets a reasonable objection win for a few pages;
- one chapter that ends with a changed relationship, not only a next checklist;
- one chapter that shows WAIT later becoming GO or NO_GO preserving value.

The current book connects. It does not yet accelerate.

## 2. Artifact Spine Diagnosis

### What Works

- **Three-column memo:** strong opening object. It quickly tells readers this
  book is about order, disclosure, and responsibility before incorporation.
- **`顧客候補あり`:** the best recurring phrase. It is short, tempting, and
  obviously dangerous once the book teaches the reader how to read it.
- **Red / yellow / blue slide:** Ch05 is much better after this addition. The
  chapter now has an object, not only a principle.
- **Investor deck:** Ch09 correctly shows how the same weak sentence becomes
  more dangerous under capital pressure.
- **Readiness table:** Ch16 and Ch19 use tables as decision tools, not just
  definitions.
- **Evidence-rule revision:** Ch20 finally turns failure into model learning
  instead of moral reflection.

### What Still Needs Work

The artifacts are visible, but they are still a little too well-behaved.

The slide travels, but the damage is mostly potential. The draft should show
one actual cost: a researcher almost cancels a meeting, a TLO pauses sharing, a
company repeats an unsafe claim, an investor asks about a stronger claim than
the team intended, or a hospital stakeholder pushes back.

The best next rewrite target is not "add more artifact mentions." It is:

> Make one artifact create a consequence that the next chapter must repair.

Recommended spine sequence:

| Artifact | Current role | Needed next |
|---|---|---|
| Three-column memo | Opening promise | Return once in Ch07 as the memo that prevents a false GO |
| `顧客候補あり` | Customer-signal warning | Add a budget-owner rejection scene with emotional cost |
| Red/yellow/blue slide | Disclosure tool | Show who almost sent it, who stopped it, and what would have broken |
| Investor deck | Pressure multiplier | Add one investor question that exposes the unsafe wording |
| Readiness table | Theory bridge | Make an actor resist the table before accepting it |
| Evidence-rule revision | Learning close | Add before/after wording so the reader feels the rule actually changed |

## 3. Ch11-12 Cooling Check

Ch11-12 are warmer than before, but they still cool the book.

Ch11 has a better wrong-GO temptation: policy language, regional demo slots,
company exploration, and investor heat make the earlier WAIT conditions look
too cautious. That is the right dramatic engine. The problem is that the
chapter resolves into a phase-difference explanation too quickly. The reader
needs to sit longer in the temptation. Someone should argue, credibly, that the
team will miss the moment if it does not move now.

Ch12 is useful and clean, but it is still one of the most table-forward chapters.
The opening scene works: everyone uses `準備` differently. But after that, the
chapter becomes an excellent lesson more than a page-turning chapter. It needs a
counterfactual: if the lowest axis is BRL, do X; if GRL, do Y; if SRL, do Z; if
HRL, do W. The reader should feel that the same "high-potential" seed produces
different next actions depending on the lowest axis.

Verdict:

- Ch11: keep in main body, add a stronger opponent and delayed resolution.
- Ch12: keep in main body, convert one table into a branching mini-drama.
- Both chapters should avoid explicit "後半の理論への橋" phrasing in final public
  prose unless it is softened. It currently reminds the reader that a curriculum
  is starting.

## 4. Do Ch15-21 Feel Earned?

Mostly yes.

Ch15-21 no longer feel like a hard restart into theory. The medical-adjacent
composite case gives the theory part a real field source. The sequence works:

- Ch15: expectation is too thick to close with `有望`.
- Ch16: readiness axes split the overloaded `準備`.
- Ch17: regional PoC corridor shows macro alignment and phase difference.
- Ch18: founder readiness becomes complementability, not personality scoring.
- Ch19: integrated score changes the branch to RESOURCE_SHIFT.
- Ch20: retrofit revises evidence rules.
- Ch21: individual venture readiness and institution nursery readiness separate.

The strongest chapter in this band is **Ch19**. It produces a real decision
change: high expectation does not mean GO; the next action becomes
RESOURCE_SHIFT. That is exactly how theory earns its place.

The weakest risk in this band is neatness. The case behaves like a teaching
case. The table is created, the team sees the issue, the next action becomes
clear. A paid book needs more friction:

- the external executive candidate should resist the slower process;
- the support-program deadline should pressure the team to overstate;
- a hospital stakeholder should reject an assumption;
- the young operator should make one mistake and correct it;
- a table should be revised because someone objected, not because the narrator
  explains it.

Ch15-21 are structurally earned. They need one "bad meeting" and one "revised
table after resistance" to feel fully alive.

## 5. Ch22-24: Body, Appendix, Or Toolkit?

**Move Ch22-24 into a toolkit / appendix section after the main narrative.**

They are valuable. They should not be deleted. But they do not currently read
like the same kind of body chapter as Ch00-21.

### Ch22

Ch22 is the closest to a real method/afterword chapter. It has a scene, a
clear ethical problem, and a useful method: turning field notes into reusable
questions without exposing private sources.

Recommendation: keep after Ch21, but label it as **Method Note** or **Toolkit
Part 1**. It can be the hinge from theory back to practice.

### Ch23

Ch23 explicitly says it is not a reading chapter: "この章は、読み物としての章ではなく、現場に持ち帰る道具箱である。"

That sentence is editorially honest. It also proves the placement issue.

Recommendation: move to **Toolkit**. Keep the tools, add filled examples, and
stop pretending it is Chapter 23 of the narrative.

### Ch24

Ch24 overlaps heavily with Ch14 and Ch21. It is useful as an institution-facing
checklist, but as narrative prose it repeats the nursery argument.

Recommendation: move to **Institution Appendix / Toolkit**. Keep unknown vs
not_started, the checklist, and the first-90-days plan. Cut repeated explanatory
paragraphs already covered by Ch14 and Ch21.

Recommended final structure:

- Main body: Prologue + Ch01-21.
- Toolkit:
  - A. Field note safety loop.
  - B. Decision record / disclosure map / customer signal ladder / role map.
  - C. Institution nursery checklist and first-90-days plan.
- Optional short afterword: return to the original memo and ask what the reader
  will do in the next meeting.
