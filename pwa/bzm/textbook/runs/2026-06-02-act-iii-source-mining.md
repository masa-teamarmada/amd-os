## Executive Summary

Act III should prove one idea through scenes: `WAIT is work`.

The current Ch07-Ch10 already contain the right topics, but the next rewrite can
make them more bodily and less workbook-like by following the same composite
case from Act I-II through a 90-day WAIT ledger. The rewrite should show how a
team that almost overclaimed a deck sentence now has to keep four kinds of
truth apart: what is known, what is hypothesized, what is merely hoped, and what
must not be promised yet.

The strongest Act III source material is not one raw transcript or one theory
chapter. It is the repeated OS habit of turning messy field events into:

- a decision branch with owner, return condition, and review date;
- a responsibility map that separates names from functions;
- an evidence table that keeps facts, hypotheses, unconfirmed items, and funding
  use apart;
- an append-only learning log that preserves trust while changing the next rule.

For the next rewrite, Ch07 should become the meeting where the WAIT ledger is
argued over. Ch08 should become the thirty days where a young commercialization
lead earns the right to say "unconfirmed." Ch09 should become the night before
an investor meeting, when the team weakens its own best sentence in order to
become more credible. Ch10 should become the repair meeting after a hard
reaction, where the team chooses learning over blame.

## Public-Rewrite Raw Materials

### Decision labels as meeting behavior

Old source language often treats GO / WAIT / HOLD / NO_GO / RESOURCE_SHIFT as
labels. Act III should treat them as behaviors:

- GO means the team knows which uncertainty the company will now carry.
- WAIT means the team knows which uncertainty will be reduced before return.
- HOLD means the team does not yet know which condition matters, and must fetch
  a narrow missing fact.
- NO_GO means the research value is routed away from the wrong company form.
- RESOURCE_SHIFT means attractive activity is cut so the lowest condition gets
  attention.

### WAIT vs abandonment

WAIT becomes real only if all three are visible:

- named owner;
- return condition;
- review date.

Add a fourth Act III-specific line: what the team will not do this month. This
keeps RESOURCE_SHIFT from becoming "more activity" and keeps WAIT from becoming
silent drift.

### Responsibility allocation friction

The sources repeatedly show a gap between role names and actual work. Public
rewrite should avoid blaming any actor:

- researcher: carries technical meaning, protected line, social reason, and some
  founder authenticity;
- young operator / EIR: carries memory, fact discipline, first interviews, and
  unconfirmed labels;
- URA / industry-collaboration staff: carries IP order, institutional trust, COI
  and researcher continuity;
- external CEO candidate: may carry capital, hiring, customer, and company
  operations, but must earn translation trust;
- corporate scout: carries curiosity and technical entry, not necessarily pain,
  budget, or adoption authority;
- investor: carries risk-capital questions that expose missing functions, not a
  verdict on research worth.

### Investor-prep uncertainty split

The next rewrite should preserve this four-way split:

- facts: observed data, held meetings, filed or pending procedural steps;
- hypotheses: use case, pain, business model, budget route;
- expectations: institutional pressure, policy heat, support-program narrative,
  investor appetite;
- unverified / promise-forbidden: budget owner, evaluator, disclosure boundary,
  founder function, cash path.

### Funding use as uncertainty reduction

Do not write fund use as expense categories only. Rewrite as "what the money will
teach":

- whether the result works outside the lab;
- whether the customer pain is repeatable;
- whether a paying path exists without damaging the larger path;
- whether governance / IP / disclosure order is safe enough;
- whether missing operator capacity can be filled.

### Failure as evidence-rule revision

Protocol and Textbook Insight specs are internally useful because they avoid
turning one event into final truth. Public rewrite can borrow the pattern:

- branch point;
- criteria;
- action;
- observation;
- revised rule;
- later observation.

The public chapter should not say "the OS stores this." It should show a team
learning to keep one bad meeting from becoming either blame or amnesia.
