# Referee Report — "The Before Zero Model: measuring deep-tech ventures and their institutional nurseries before day zero"

**Reviewer lens:** measurement theory / social choice (representation theorems; KLST-style conjoint measurement; axiomatics of aggregation).
**Materials reviewed:** main text (`PAPER_P1_DRAFT.md`) and Supplementary Material (`PAPER_P1_SM.md`, SM-A through SM-E), both in full.

---

## 1. Summary

The paper proposes a two-ledger measurement system for pre-founding deep-tech ventures: a multiplicative venture score PRS = P·R·S, an additive institutional score ERS over eight rubric axes, and a shared macro state σ_SU. Four formal results carry the paper: Theorem 1 (ERS is the unique admissible institutional aggregate, by additive conjoint measurement), Proposition 1 (a bylaws precondition gate), Theorem 2 (multiplicative cross-layer combination violates separability), Theorem 3 (no single score Φ(g(P,R,S), h(A)) with strictly increasing components satisfies four axioms C1–C4 — the headline "Arrow-style" impossibility), Corollary 3.1 (three falsifiable regression signatures of single-score practice), and Theorem 4 / Proposition 2 (an optimal-stopping GO operator with comparative statics).

The paper is unusually candid about its own incompleteness (the [GAP] apparatus, the "two readings of C3" discussion in SM-B.3, the Tier discipline in Section 6), the writing is excellent, and the ambition — importing conjoint measurement and impossibility-theorem discipline into innovation evaluation — is exactly the kind of cross-fertilization this literature needs. I want a version of this paper to exist. Unfortunately, the version under review has serious mathematical problems, several of which sit at the exact center of the contribution. The most important: Theorem 3 as stated is trivially implied by axiom C1 alone (three of its four axioms are logically inert in the proof), while the theorem the rhetoric describes — exclusion of *every* monotone composite — is not proven, and under the SM's own permissive reading of C3 is false without an additional (currently unstated) domain axiom. Theorem 2's claimed violation of E1 does not survive the ordinal reading of E1 that the paper's own measurement discipline mandates, and the same cross-partial criterion that convicts the prohibited composite also convicts the paper's own Proposition 1 gate. Corollary 3.1's Simpson-reversal signature appears to be *underivable* — arguably impossible — within the paper's maintained monotonicity assumptions. Theorem 1's proof contains a circular essentiality step, and the theorem as quantified (a single weight vector across all observed sets) is unproven without an additional cross-K axiom the authors themselves flag as missing.

None of these problems is obviously fatal to the research program; most have identifiable repairs, some of which I sketch below. But the repairs to Theorem 3 and Corollary 3.1 are not cosmetic — they change what the paper can claim.

## 2. Verdict

**Major revision** (at the boundary of reject-and-resubmit). The two-ledger architecture, Theorem 1 (repaired), Proposition 1 (repaired), and the Section 5 stopping framework are salvageable with bounded effort. Theorem 3 and Corollary 3.1 — the paper's advertised core — require restatement and genuinely new proof work, not editing. I would need to see the revised Theorem 3 before supporting publication.

## 3. Major concerns

### M1. Theorem 3, as stated, is a one-axiom triviality; C2, C3 and C4 are logically inert; the advertised proof architecture is not the actual proof.

The theorem (main text §4; SM-B): *"There exists no function $f$ satisfying C1–C4 that admits a representation $f = \Phi\big(g(P, R, S), h(A)\big)$ with $g$, $h$, and $\Phi$ strictly increasing in their arguments."*

Observe that C1 alone contradicts the representation class, with no other axiom used. By C1, $f(0,R,S;A)=0$ for all $R,S,A$, so $\Phi(u, h(A)) = 0$ for every $u$ in the image $g(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^2_{+})$. Since $g$ is strictly increasing in $R$, this image contains at least two points $u_1 < u_2$, and $\Phi(u_1, v_0) = \Phi(u_2, v_0) = 0$ contradicts strict monotonicity of $\Phi$ in its *first* argument. Three lines; no C2, no C3, no C4, no continuity, no conjoint measurement. (The SM's own route via the second argument uses C1 + C2 and nothing else; the SM even concedes it: *"No smoothness was used, and only strict monotonicity of $\Phi$ in its second argument was invoked."* Note further that C2 is itself implied by the representation class — any $f = \Phi(g,h)$ with $\Phi$, $h$ strictly increasing is strictly increasing in every $A_k$ everywhere — so C2 adds nothing even in the SM's route.)

Consequences:

(a) The main text's proof-architecture paragraph (§4) — *"C1 forces the venture argument to annihilate the whole expression on the boundary — an infinitely strong interaction between the layers — while C3 confines the institution's contribution to the venture-state response it induces in the interior. No strictly increasing Φ can be simultaneously infinitely sensitive to the venture layer at the boundary and channel-restricted in the interior while remaining continuous (C4) and monotone (C2)"* — does not describe the proof in SM-B.4. C3 is never invoked there (the proof itself admits: "C2 supplies the strict ranking directly"), and C4's only role is a flawed open-orthant variant (see M2(c)).

(b) Remark SM-B.2's independence claims are false for the stated theorem: *"dropping C3 readmits pure multiplication"* — pure multiplication ($\Phi(u,v)=uv$, flat in $v$ at $u=0$) was never in the stated class, so dropping C3 readmits nothing; *"dropping C2 makes the exclusion trivial"* — dropping C2 changes nothing, since C2 is implied by the class; *"dropping C4 readmits step-function composites"* — step functions are not strictly increasing and are already outside the class. Only the C1 claim is correct. For an axiomatic paper, incorrect independence claims about its own central theorem are disqualifying as-is.

(c) The "Arrow-style" framing (abstract, §1, §4) is therefore misleading. Arrow's theorem is a genuine multi-axiom collision in which each axiom is necessary. The stated Theorem 3 is the observation that a function required to vanish on a set where its first factor varies cannot be strictly increasing in that factor — a statement about annihilation vs. strictness that has nothing specifically to do with two-layer aggregation, institutions, or conjoint measurement.

**What to do:** either (i) present the theorem honestly as the (easy) strict-separability result, in one paragraph, and move the real weight onto the weak-monotone case (see M2); or (ii) prove the weak-monotone case. Option (ii) is the paper the authors describe; it requires new work (M2, M3).

### M2. The strictness hypothesis quietly excludes exactly the composites practitioners use; the headline scope claim ("not any monotone combination however sophisticated") is unproven; under the SM's own permissive reading of C3 it is false.

Every practice composite that *satisfies* C1 does so by failing per-argument strictness of $\Phi$ on the annihilation boundary: pure multiplication $g\cdot h$; geometric blends $g^a h^b$; CES with $\rho<0$ (which $\to 0$ as $g\to 0$); $\min(g,h)$ with $g=0$ on the boundary. All of these are flat in $h$ where $g=0$ — hence outside the stated theorem's class. Meanwhile any composite *inside* the class fails C1 for the trivial reason of M1. So the theorem as stated excludes nothing that a C1-respecting practitioner would use, and Table 1's third row — *"General monotone $\Phi(g, h)$ | geometric blends, CES mixes, lexicographic hybrids | **fails sign-consistency** (Theorem 3)"* — is unsupported: geometric blends and CES-complement mixes are not in Theorem 3's class, and for weakly monotone $\Phi$ sign-consistency (Lemma SM-B.2(i)) is *not* a necessary condition for separability — ties on some rows and strict comparisons on others are perfectly compatible with $f=\Phi(g,h)$ when $\Phi$ is weakly increasing (multiplication realizes exactly this pattern). The introduction's claim — *"not the current weighting, not the proposed one, not any monotone combination however sophisticated"* — is not delivered by any theorem in the paper.

Worse, under the SM's own ordinal reading of C3 (*"if $A,A'$ induce the same $(R,S)$-response at $x$, then $f(x;A)=f(x;A')$"*), the weak-monotone impossibility is **false** without further axioms. Counterexample: let $\kappa(A)$ be any strictly increasing functional of the $(R,S)$-response profile induced by $A$, and set $f = \min(PRS,\ \kappa(A))$. Then C1 holds (boundary annihilates $PRS$), C2 holds (strict in $A$ wherever $\kappa$ binds), C4 holds, and C3-ordinal holds ($f$ depends on $A$ only through the response). Yet $f = \Phi(g,h)$ with $g=PRS$, $h=\kappa$, $\Phi=\min$ — a monotone-separable single score. Similarly $f = PRS\cdot e^{\kappa(A)}$ satisfies C1–C4 (ordinal reading) and is separable — indeed *log-additive* — on the entire interior. The intended theorem is true only relative to an additional richness/universal-domain axiom on the space of admissible response structures (see M3), which is currently implicit in one [GAP] remark.

(c) The open-orthant escape hatch in SM-B.4 is also broken: *"by C4, rows with $x^a$ close enough to the boundary are flat within any $\varepsilon>0$ ... while the representation forces a fixed positive gap."* Neither half survives scrutiny. C4 as stated gives joint continuity on Int and continuity *in $A$* on the boundary; it does not give continuity of $f$ in $x$ up to the boundary, so near-boundary rows need not be $\varepsilon$-flat. And a $\Phi$ strictly increasing only on the interior of its range does not force a "fixed positive gap" as $g(x)\to g(x^a)$: for $\Phi=uv$ the gap $\to 0$. On the open orthant the multiplicative composite satisfies all four axioms as literally stated there (C1 is vacuous on the open orthant unless restated as a boundary-limit condition), and *is* separable. Fix: state C1 in limit form ($f\to 0$ as $\min(P,R,S)\to 0$, uniformly in $A$ on compacts) and require continuity of $f$ on the closed orthant.

(d) A related substantive point the paper must confront: because C1's content lives entirely on the boundary, **on the interior domain where committees actually operate, C1–C4-consistent separable scores exist** (the $PRS\cdot e^{\kappa(A)}$ example above is interior-separable and can *equal* the true value). The sentence *"The object the screening committee asked for does not exist"* is false for a committee that pre-screens to live ventures unless the paper proves a quantitative near-boundary version (institutional sensitivity of any admissible score must vanish as $\min(P,R,S)\to 0$ at a stated rate — which would, incidentally, connect directly to signature (ii) of Corollary 3.1 and make the empirical program sharper). Currently the impossibility is a closed-domain artifact dressed as a practice indictment.

### M3. C3 is not a well-formed axiom; the SM's Lemma SM-B.3 misapplies its own display; the "two readings" treatment is honest but does not resolve the problem.

The smooth C3 display,
$$\frac{df}{dA_k}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}\frac{\partial R}{\partial A_k}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial S}\frac{\partial S}{\partial A_k},$$
is ill-typed as an axiom on a static function $f(P,R,S;A)$: $R$ and $S$ appear simultaneously as free coordinates of $f$ and as functions of $A$, and the "response derivatives" $\rho_k, \sigma_k$ belong to a dynamic model that is never specified inside the axiom system. SM-B.1 glosses it as: *"the total derivative of value along an institutional perturbation with the state co-moving; equivalently, the direct partial of $f$ in $A_k$, net of the transmitted terms, vanishes."* Take that gloss at face value (total = direct + transmitted; total = transmitted ⟺ direct = 0) and apply it to the product form in Lemma SM-B.3: the total derivative of $g(x)h(A)$ along the co-moving perturbation is $g\,\partial_k h + h(g_R\rho_k + g_S\sigma_k)$; setting it equal to the transmitted terms yields $g\,\partial_k h = 0$, hence $\partial_k h \equiv 0$ on the interior, contradicting C2 — a one-line proof with no [GAP]. But the SM instead computes *"The product form gives $df/dA_k = g(x)\,\partial_k h(A)$"* — i.e., it equates the *fixed-state partial* (the direct channel alone) to the transmitted side, which is a different and stranger axiom, and it is under that mis-reading that the flagship $g=PRS$ becomes satisfiable by tuned dynamics and the universal-domain [GAP] is needed. The lemma's difficulties are an artifact of a mis-stated axiom.

However, the clean "direct partial = 0" reading has its own pathology, which SM-B.3 correctly identifies for the frozen-state version: if $f$ literally cannot depend on $A$ at fixed $x$, C3 contradicts C2 and the axiom class is empty (*"under it the axiom class is empty and the 'impossibility' is vacuous"* — the authors' own words, and the right standard). The only coherent escape is the one the ordinal version gestures at: $f(x;A) = F(x, r(x,A))$ for a specified response map $r$. But then the entire content of C3 depends on the richness of the family $\{r(x,\cdot)\}$, which is nowhere axiomatized: if $r(x,\cdot)$ is injective for every $x$, C3-ordinal is vacuous (its premise never fires with $A\neq A'$); if null-response perturbations abound, C3 is very strong. The [GAP] at Lemma SM-B.3 — *"the display must hold for every admissible transmission configuration, including configurations with $\rho_k=\sigma_k=0$ at some $(x,A)$ where $\partial_k h>0$"* — is precisely such a richness axiom, used but unstated. To answer the question directly: **the ordinal version of C3 does not smuggle in the conclusion — but it has no determinate content until the response-structure domain is specified, and the theorem the authors want is true or false depending on that specification** (M2's $\min(PRS,\kappa(A))$ counterexample lives at the permissive end).

**Constructive suggestion.** The natural repair unifies the paper with its own Section 5: define $f$ as the expected discounted value of the venture under $A$-dependent dynamics; let C1 = annihilation states are absorbing; C2 = drifts/hazards improve in $A$; C3 = $A$ enters the law of motion of $(R,S)$ only; and quantify the impossibility over a *family of environments* (a universal-domain axiom on dynamics — the genuine Arrow analogue this paper is missing): no single triple $(g,h,\Phi)$ represents the value ordering across all admissible environments. That theorem would be well-typed, non-trivial, and honestly Arrovian. It is not the theorem currently in the paper.

### M4. Non-emptiness of the C1–C4 class is never established, by the paper's own vacuity standard.

An impossibility over an empty axiom class is vacuous — the SM says exactly this when dismissing the frozen-state reading of C3 — yet the paper never exhibits any $f$ satisfying C1–C4 under the operative reading. Corollary 3.1 and the entire empirical program presuppose *"the true data-generating process respects C1–C4"* (SM-B.6), so non-emptiness is not a formality. Under the ordinal reading with response-measurable dependence, candidates exist ($f = PRS\cdot e^{\kappa(A)}$ above; or the Section-5 value function with absorbing zeros) — but the first is separable-on-the-interior (embarrassing for the rhetoric; see M2(d)) and the second is never checked against C1–C4. Add an existence proposition with a verified example. (Mutual consistency of E1–E4 is unproblematic — the weighted sum witnesses it — but should also be stated, and independence examples for both axiom blocks supplied, as is standard in this genre.)

### M5. C1's annihilation clause for the R-coordinate contradicts the paper's own option logic.

C1 requires $f(P,0,S;A)=0$ for every $A$. But $R=0$ ("nothing realized yet") is the *normal* state of an early seed — the very object Before Zero evaluation exists to value — and Section 5's continuation value is strictly positive for exactly such seeds (Project T is read WAIT, not worthless). The defense offered (*"nobody funds a dead project because its university has excellent bylaws"*) is compelling for $P=0$ (no market) and $S=0$ (dead), but gap funds exist precisely to fund $R\approx 0$ projects on institution and potential. As stated, C1 + C4 also imply that all near-$R=0$ seeds have near-zero value insensitive to institutions, which contradicts the paper's own nurturing narrative. The fix is free: restate C1 as annihilation in $P$ and $S$ only ($\min(P,S)=0 \Rightarrow f=0$). Every proof in SM-B survives (the constructions use the $P=0$ face), and the axiom becomes genuinely hard to dispute.

### M6. Theorem 2's "violates E1" does not survive the ordinal reading of E1; the cardinal reading convicts the paper's own Proposition 1 gate; and the theorem is outside E1's stated scope.

Three distinct problems.

(a) **Scope.** E1 (main text §3): *"the contribution of one axis to institutional readiness can be assessed without knowing the level of every other axis"* — an axiom about institutional axes among themselves. Theorem 2's headline computation, $\partial^2 S/\partial A_k\,\partial \mathrm{PRS} \neq 0$, is a cross-layer statement about which E1 as stated is silent. The within-layer computation $\partial^2 S/\partial A_k\partial A_{k'} = \alpha(\alpha-1)E^{\alpha-2}P^{1-\alpha}c_kc_{k'} < 0$ is the E1-relevant one (the algebra is correct; I verified it), so the theorem should be argued on that clause alone — but see (b).

(b) **Ordinal vs. cardinal.** E1's axiomatic content is ordinal (independence of the induced ordering — the SM says so: "Ordinal form: independence"). At any fixed venture state, $S = E^{\alpha}P^{1-\alpha}$ is a strictly increasing transform of $E$, so the institutional ordering it induces is *exactly* the ERS ordering — which satisfies independence. Ordinal E1 is not violated; what fails is additivity of one particular numerical representation, i.e., a cardinal property relative to a scale. The paper's own §3 invokes Stevens (1946) to discipline exactly this kind of scale-relative claim. The log-transform rebuttal in SM-A.4 (*"$\log S$ is not a positive affine transformation of any admissible score"*) is a category error: Theorem 1's uniqueness clause governs representations of the *institutional ordering*, not admissible transforms of a cross-layer composite, which is a new object whose admissibility should be judged against the axioms' ordinal content.

(c) **Internal inconsistency with Proposition 1.** The cross-partial criterion, if accepted, convicts the paper's own gate: $\mathrm{ERS} = 100\,g(A_7)F(A_{-7})$ with the licensed *"steep sigmoid"* $g$ is continuous, strictly increasing in $A_7$, and has $\partial^2 \mathrm{ERS}/\partial A_7\partial A_k = 100\,g'(A_7)\,\partial F/\partial A_k \neq 0$ — a multiplicative combination with nonvanishing cross-partials, of precisely the form Theorem 2 prohibits across layers. The main text's assurance that *"the gate refines Theorem 1 rather than contradicting E2"* addresses E2, not E1; the paper needs a principled criterion distinguishing "gate = admissible refinement" from "multiplicative merger = prohibited" (the honest candidates are ordinal-vs-cardinal, or within-object-vs-across-object — neither is articulated), especially since pure multiplication $\mathrm{PRS}\cdot\mathrm{ERS}$ *is* a liveness gate under exactly the reading Prop 1 uses for $A_7$.

**Recommendation:** restate Theorem 2 as what it is — a double-counting result, i.e., C3-flavored, folded into §4 — or as a statement about preserving the calibrated interval-scale reporting of ERS. As an "E1 violation" it is either false (ordinal) or proves too much (cardinal).

### M7. Theorem 1: circular essentiality; the single cross-K weight vector is unproven (the theorem as quantified does not follow from the listed axioms); E2-s should be surfaced into the statement.

(a) **Circularity.** E4 (SM-A.1): $F_K$ is *"nondecreasing in each $A_k$, strictly increasing in essential axes."* Step 1 of the proof then claims *"essentiality (implied by strict E4)"*. This is circular: E4's strictness clause is conditioned on essentiality, so it cannot imply it. If some axis is inessential, Fact SM-A.1's hypotheses fail (for $|K|=2$ both components must be essential) and the conclusion $w_k>0$ fails. Fix: state E4 as strict monotonicity in every observed axis (which is also what the main text's practitioners would endorse), from which essentiality follows.

(b) **Cross-K coherence.** The theorem asserts a single weight vector $w_1,\dots,w_8$ serving every observed set $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$. The axioms constrain each $\succsim_K$ separately; nothing links trade-off rates across different $K$. The SM flags this — *"[GAP: this cross-$K$ coherence condition is used implicitly ... it is not stated there as a separate axiom]"* — but this is not a deferrable gap: **as stated, the theorem is false** (one can satisfy all listed axioms with weight vectors that differ across $K$ in non-proportional ways). Add the axiom (e.g., trade-off invariance: for $k,k'\in K\cap K'$, the compensating rates agree) and a two-line proof. The needed condition is a cousin of subgroup-consistency axioms in the poverty-measurement literature (Foster–Shorrocks), which would be a natural citation.

(c) **What uniqueness actually rests on.** The linear-in-rubric-numerals form is delivered entirely by E2-s ("a fixed rubric step trades at a level-independent rate"), i.e., by the *assumption* that the rubric's five anchors are equally spaced in readiness terms. Step 2 is admirably explicit about this, but the main text oversells: *"the weighted sum is not one convenient formula among many. Under axioms that any technology-transfer practitioner would endorse ... it is the only admissible form."* Without E2-s the axioms deliver $\sum_k w_k\varphi_k(A_k)$ with nonlinear $\varphi_k$ — a materially different practical instrument. The theorem statement (main text and SM) should name E2-s among its hypotheses rather than leaving it inside "E2".

(d) **Equal weights are not a normalization.** Step 5: *"any normalization such as $\sum_k w_k=1$, including the paper's default of equal weights, is a transparent value judgement."* Scaling $\sum w_k = 1$ is a normalization (order-irrelevant); *equal* weights fix the ratios $w_k/w_{k'}$, which the axioms treat as empirical content recovered from the ordering's trade-offs. Conflating the two undercuts the paper's own uniqueness rhetoric: if the implementation defaults to $w_k \equiv 1$, the ordering has been chosen, not measured. Say so.

(e) The continuum-vs-five-levels [GAP] is discussed under §5 of this report (triage): acceptable if converted into an explicit assumption with the right citations, since restricted solvability genuinely fails on a discrete grid and finite additive representability requires the full cancellation hierarchy (Scott 1964; KLST ch. 9; Fishburn 1970, ch. 4). Note the knock-on cost, which should be admitted: Thomsen and solvability are then untestable on actual rubric data, so Theorem 1's empirical content is conditional on the continuum idealization.

### M8. Proposition 1's gate axioms are mutually inconsistent as stated.

(G1)(a) forces $g(0)=0$: at $A_7=0$ every profile is readiness-equivalent to $\mathbf 0$, i.e., the level-0 section is total indifference. But (G2) states: *"For each fixed $A_7=a$, the induced ordering over $A_{-7}$ is the Theorem-1 ordering on $K_{\mathrm{obs}}\setminus\{7\}$"* — which is not total indifference. So (G1) and (G2) contradict each other at $a=0$ (and at any annihilated level), and the proof's own handling of the $S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})=0$ case — *"by (G2) and E4 all profiles at gate level $a$ score 0"* — derives a section ordering that violates the (G2) it invokes. The fix is easy (restrict (G2) to levels $a$ with $S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})>0$, or weaken "is" to "is a monotone coarsening of"), but as printed the axiom block has no model, so Proposition 1 currently characterizes a member of an empty class. The two [GAP]s inside Prop 1 (the $g(1)=0.25$ convention; (G3) as primitive) are acceptable once this is fixed — (G3) is a legitimate stated axiom, and the within-gate values are honestly labeled convention.

### M9. C3 is inconsistent with the paper's own operating model: Section 5 and SM-D route institutional effects through channels C3 forbids.

C3 permits institutional influence only through $R$ and $S$ (the display has exactly two transmitted terms). But §5 says: *"Higher ERS shifts the drift of $R$ ..., improves the hazard structure behind $S$ ..., and increases the venture's exposure to favorable $\sigma_{SU}$ innovations"* — a third channel; and SM-D.1's own rubric table routes $A_5$ (funding access) as *"feeds $F$ (slack)"*, with $A_4$ and $A_8$ marked *"exposure to $\sigma_{SU}$ innovations."* In the Section-5 model, $F$ raises value directly (Lemma C.1(c)) through the absorbing-barrier geometry, not through $R$ or $S$. So the paper's own structural model violates C3 as stated, and the registered Hansen-J test of SM-B.7 would — by the paper's own design — correctly reject its own axiom. Either C3 must be broadened to "institutional axes act only through the venture's state variables $(R,S,F)$ and its $\sigma$-exposure" (checking that Theorem 3's intended proof survives the broadening), or the $F$- and $\sigma$-channels must be re-derived as operating through $S$ (e.g., slack enters only via the survival hazard) — in which case say so and adjust SM-D. As it stands the axiom and the model contradict each other in print.

### M10. Corollary 3.1 does not follow from Theorem 3; signature (i) appears underivable — arguably impossible — under the maintained assumptions; and the signatures do not test the practice the paper's opening vignette dramatizes.

(a) **Signature (i).** SM-B.6's own anatomy: pooled coefficient = *"a convex combination of within-quartile slopes plus a between-quartile composition term"*, with within-slopes $\ge 0$ (C2 + channel; $\approx 0$ at the bottom stratum) and the composition term generated because *"thick institutions migrate ventures upward across quartiles"* — i.e., positive association of $A$ with the stratum and of the stratum with outcomes: a *nonnegative* composition term. Nonnegative combination + nonnegative composition cannot produce a sign reversal; the clause *"For composition terms of sufficient magnitude the pooled sign opposes some within-quartile sign"* cannot fire — magnitude does not change sign when every ingredient is $\ge 0$. A genuine Simpson reversal needs a negative ingredient: selection into the observed sample (e.g., only funded/founded ventures observed, selected on a score — a Berkson/collider mechanism), or suppressor correlation among axes — none of which is modeled. The [GAP] deferring "the explicit construction" to the monograph is therefore not a deferral of routine work; I do not believe the construction exists within the stated model. Either drop (i), or — better — model selection-on-the-score explicitly. That would also repair a logical oddity: as written, the field's ranking practice (*"Suppose a field nonetheless ranks by some single score"*) plays no role whatsoever in generating the data patterns — the signatures are properties of the DGP and the analyst's regression alone. Selection-on-score is exactly the mechanism that would make single-score *practice* leave scars in *data*, and it delivers reversals honestly.

(b) **Signature (ii) vs. Section 6.** SM-B.6(ii) predicts *"$\gamma_k^{1}\approx 0\neq\gamma_k^{q}$ at operative strata"* — positive within-quartile institutional slopes above the bottom stratum. Section 6 celebrates the opposite conditioning result: *"ERS shows no residual association with outcomes once those trajectories are conditioned on — the qualitative fingerprint C3 predicts."* Both cannot be read off the same regression: whether the within-stratum slope is positive or zero depends on whether stratification is at decision time (institution still to act on future $(R,S)$: slope $>0$) or on realized trajectories (C3 exclusion: slope $\approx 0$). The corollary never specifies timing, and PRS-quartiles are downstream of $A$ per C3, so quartile stratification conditions on a mediator — pooled and conditional estimands then differ as ordinary mediation arithmetic, which is not "Simpson's paradox" and not per se a scar of wrong practice. Specify measurement timing and conditioning sets, and derive (ii) as a proposition inside an explicit statistical model (link, error structure, exogeneity); it is the one signature that is genuinely derivable, and the corollary should be rebuilt around it.

(c) **Signature (iii)** is (ii) wearing different clothes (a Hausman contrast between constrained and unconstrained coefficient vectors is an interaction test), and as displayed has the usual defects: $\chi^2_{\dim\theta}$ has the wrong degrees of freedom (should be the number of equality restrictions / the rank of the variance difference), and Hausman requires one estimator efficient under the null, which pooled OLS under heteroskedasticity is not. A Wald test on the ERS×PRS interaction, already implicit in (ii), dominates.

(d) **Target mismatch.** SM-B.6 restricts the practice score to *strictly increasing* components — but the opening vignette's instrument ("evaluated quality, multiplied by a coefficient reflecting its host university's ... capabilities") is *multiplicative*, hence outside that class, satisfies C1, and generates the same tie-at-death/strict-in-interior pattern as the axioms. Signatures (i)–(iii) cannot distinguish it from the truth; only the Hansen-J channel test (SM-B.7) addresses it. As designed, the falsification program does not test the practice the introduction (and Table 1, row 1) indicts. This should be stated plainly — SM-B.7's decision matrix is honest, but the main text's Corollary 3.1 paragraph implies otherwise.

Main text and SM also disagree on strength: main text asserts *"observable data will exhibit at least one of (i)-(iii)"* (deterministic), the SM hedges each with unstated magnitude conditions. Downgrade the main-text statement to match whatever is actually proven — on the current record, that is: "constant-coefficient institutional specifications are misspecified under C1–C4 with operative channel; we register (i)–(iii) as diagnostics," i.e., a registered-hypothesis block, not a corollary.

### M11. Missed literatures that pre-figure — and partially deflate — Theorem 3's novelty claim.

The claim *"Theorem 3 is, to our knowledge, the first impossibility for aggregation across observation levels"* needs qualification on at least three fronts. (i) **Noncompensatory/veto axiomatics in MCDA:** that an attribute with an annihilation/veto level admits no strictly monotone separable (a fortiori no additive) representation is established territory — Fishburn (1976) on noncompensatory preferences; Bouyssou & Vansnick (1986); Bouyssou & Marchant's axiomatizations of ELECTRE-style sorting with veto (e.g., EJOR 2007); Bouyssou–Marchant–Pirlot, *Evaluation and Decision Models*. The strict version of Theorem 3 is an instance of this folklore transported across levels. (ii) **Separability theory:** Theorem 3 is equivalent to "C1 (+ strictness) violates single-factor independence," i.e., the negation of the Gorman–Debreu–Leontief–Sono separability condition for the institutional block; engaging Gorman (1968) (cited only in passing in SM-A), Leontief's aggregation theorem, and Blackorby–Primont–Russell (1978) would both situate the result correctly and supply exactly the vocabulary ("the institutional sector is not separable from the venture sector under C1") the paper is re-deriving. (iii) **Axiomatic index theory in adjacent applied fields:** Ebert & Welsch (2004, JEEM) do the Theorem-1-style exercise for environmental indices; Foster–Shorrocks subgroup consistency is the template for the missing cross-K axiom (M7(b)). None of this kills the paper — the *application* and the C3-channel framing are genuinely novel — but the lineage paragraph in §4 currently claims a first that is not a first at the level of mathematical structure.

### M12. SM-C (for completeness, though outside my core lens): the proofs are architecture plus 12 gaps, several load-bearing.

Most of SM-C's gaps are closable by citation to published regime-switching optimal-stopping theory and should be (comparison for weakly coupled viscosity systems: Ishii & Koike 1991; smooth pasting and free-boundary regularity for regime-switching diffusions: Guo & Zhang 2004, Pham 2009, Peskir & Shiryaev 2006). Three are not mere citations: (a) Step 4's Jacobian non-singularity — either prove or weaken Theorem 4 to "for generic primitives," and mirror the weakening in the main text; (b) Proposition 2(iv)'s sign is scheme-dependent — *"[GAP: the skeleton does not record which compensation scheme its sign is proved under]"* — and for the worst regime $S_0$, raising own-persistence lowers both the entered value and the option value of waiting-for-improvement, so the net sign is genuinely ambiguous; the claim should either be proven under the stated proportional-rescaling scheme or restricted to $S_2$ (the $S_2$ case is the believable one); (c) Lemma C.1(d)'s regime ordering requires regime-ordered flows ($b_{S_0}\le b_{S_1}\le b_{S_2}$ or ordered $\pi$-levels), which is not among (A1)–(A6) — add it. Also note the main-text problem display omits discounting of the founding cost ($-I$ should be $-e^{-r\tau}I$; SM-C.0 has it right), and the main-text constraint $B(t)-R_{net}(t)\le F(t)$ compares a flow to a stock — the SM's dynamic version ($dF=(R_{net}-B)dt$, $F=0$ absorbing) is the coherent one and should replace it.
