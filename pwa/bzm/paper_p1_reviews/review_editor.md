# Desk Review and Editorial Assessment — Research Policy

**Manuscript:** "The Before Zero Model: measuring deep-tech ventures and their institutional nurseries before day zero"
**Materials reviewed:** Main text (PAPER_P1_DRAFT.md, ~8,700 words incl. abstract and references) and Supplementary Material (PAPER_P1_SM.md, SM-A through SM-E, ~8,100 words).
**Role:** Senior Editor desk review, pre-referee.
**Date:** 2026-07-03

---

## 1. Summary of the submission (in my own words)

The paper claims that the pre-founding stage of university deep-tech ventures — the "five to fifteen" years between a research result and incorporation, which the authors rename "Before Zero" — lacks a measurement theory, and proposes one. The architecture has three parts. (i) A *venture ledger*, PRS = P·R·S (potential scale × realization readiness × survival probability), multiplicative on O-ring grounds: any zero is fatal. (ii) An *institutional ledger*, ERS, a weighted sum over eight rubric-scored capability axes of the host university/lab, with a "bylaws" axis acting as a multiplicative precondition gate (Proposition 1); the weighted-sum form is claimed to be *uniquely* admissible under four axioms via additive conjoint measurement (Theorem 1). (iii) A macro state σ_SU, the shifted geometric mean of academic/industrial/governmental "momenta," Triple-Helix in framing, which drives the venture ledger's dynamics through three regimes.

The centerpiece is Theorem 3, marketed as an Arrow-style impossibility: under four axioms (C1 venture annihilation, C2 institutional monotonicity, C3 institutions-act-only-through-R-and-S, C4 continuity), no single "institution-adjusted project score" Φ(g(P,R,S), h(A)) with strictly increasing components exists. Corollary 3.1 converts this into three pre-registrable statistical signatures (Simpson reversal, quartile instability, Hausman divergence) that fields ranking by single scores should exhibit. Theorem 4 then derives a go/wait founding rule as an optimal-stopping threshold on σ_SU, with comparative statics mapped to policy instruments (Proposition 2).

Evidence is a retrospective "calibration" of eight composite-case projects from a single Japanese venture studio (2007–2026), with institutions type-named, calibration constants withheld as proprietary, and outcomes known at scoring time — explicitly labeled as illustration, not validation. Two vignettes (a macro-blessed technology-hollow "zombie" and a technically presentable project with zero-market economics) illustrate the failure modes of additive and compensating composites. Section 7 tells evaluators to retire institution-adjusted rankings in favor of explicit decision aids, conditional gates, and governance separation, and announces a registered falsification program under the banner of a new field, "Before Zero Studies."

What actually lands, on a careful read: a genuinely interesting *design argument* — ventures and their institutional hosts are different kinds of measurement objects, and merging them into one score destroys the information each ledger exists to reveal — wrapped in a formal apparatus that is heavier than, and in places not equal to, the claims made for it, and resting on an empirical exhibit that reports no measurements.

---

## 2. Overall editorial verdict

**Desk-reject in current form, with explicit encouragement to resubmit after major restructuring.** This is a return-without-review of the manuscript, not a rejection of the research program.

Three reasons, in order of decisiveness:

1. **The manuscript is not finished.** The abstract is flagged in the file as a placeholder; Figures 1–3 are referenced but do not exist (Figure 2 is never referenced at all); the Supplementary Material carries **21 author-flagged `[GAP:...]` markers** at load-bearing steps of Theorems 1, 3, and 4 and Propositions 1 and 2, most deferring to an unpublished "companion monograph" (mentioned 3 times in the main text, 24 times in the SM, and absent from the reference list); four works cited in the SM (Fishburn 1970, Gorman 1968, Bertola 1998, Caballero 1999) are missing from the reference list while two listed works (Atkinson 1970, Cobb & Douglas 1928) are never cited; internal production notes and changelogs remain in both files. A theorems paper whose proofs contain acknowledged holes cannot be sent to referees.

2. **The headline claim overshoots the mathematics** — and the authors' own SM documents the gap (Major Concern 1 below). The instrument the opening vignette dramatizes (quality × institutional coefficient) is *not* in Theorem 3's excluded class; it is ruled out only by axiom C3 under a strengthened reading the SM itself flags as "used implicitly but not stated."

3. **The theory-to-evidence ratio is inverted for this journal.** Sections 3–5 (formal construction: conjoint measurement, HJB systems, viscosity solutions) run ~4,000 words — 52% of the body. Section 7, the policy section, runs **515 words — 6.6% of the body — in a journal called Research Policy**. The empirical section reports no PRS value, no ERS value, no σ_SU series for any case: the measurement paper contains no measurements.

Against this: the problem is squarely RP-relevant (evaluation practice in research commercialization; composite-indicator misuse in science policy); the writing is unusually good; the honesty devices (Tier discipline, retrospective-not-predictive labeling, GAP flags, pre-registered falsifiers) are creditable and rare. A restructured resubmission — theorem claims trimmed to what is proved, formalism compressed, evidence made auditable, policy expanded — could plausibly go to review with concerns. As submitted, referees would savage it, and the authors would burn a good idea's first impression.

## 3. Major concerns

### M1. The central impossibility theorem does not cover the practice it indicts, and the open-orthant extension in the SM is wrong

This is the paper's most serious substantive problem, and it sits at the front door. The Introduction stakes everything on it:

> "Under four requirements that every member of that committee would separately endorse, *no* institution-adjusted single score exists: not the current weighting, not the proposed one, not any monotone combination however sophisticated. The committee is not miscalibrating an instrument; it is calibrating a nonexistent one." (§1)

Now read Theorem 3 as actually stated:

> "*There exists no function f satisfying C1–C4 that admits a representation f = Φ(g(P,R,S), h(A)) with g, h, and Φ strictly increasing in their arguments.*" (§4)

Consider the committee's actual instrument: f = PRS · (1 + ERS), i.e., "project score × institution coefficient." Here Φ(u,v) = u·v is **not strictly increasing in v at u = 0** — so this f fails the *hypothesis* of Theorem 3 and is untouched by it. It satisfies C1 (dead venture scores zero at any host), C2, and C4. The same is true of geometric blends √(g·h) and every C1-respecting composite used in practice: precisely because they annihilate at the boundary, they are boundary-degenerate in h and sit *outside* the excluded class. The only family Theorem 3 genuinely kills is pure addition — which is killed by C1 alone in one line (SM Lemma B.4), no conjoint machinery needed.

The multiplicative family is instead excluded via C3 (double counting; Theorem 2 and Lemma SM-B.3). But the authors' own SM concedes that this exclusion does not go through as written:

> "[GAP: the premise that the transmitted side is free of P is underspecified in the skeleton — it requires ∂²g/∂P∂R = ∂²g/∂P∂S = 0, which fails for the flagship g = PRS; there P cancels from both sides and the display is satisfiable by dynamics with ρ_k/R + σ_k/S = ∂_k log h. The exclusion of pure multiplication therefore rests on the universal-domain reading of C3 ... The skeleton uses this reading implicitly but does not state it.]" (SM-B.5)

And Remark SM-B.2 confirms the accounting: "dropping C3 readmits pure multiplication" — which can only be true if Theorem 3 (whose main proof "uses no smoothness — only C1, C2, C4," SM-B.1) never excluded it in the first place.

Worse, the SM's attempt to extend the theorem to the open orthant is mathematically incorrect:

> "if the modeler insists on the open orthant, C1 acts through limits — by C4, rows with x^a close enough to the boundary are flat within any ε>0 over the pair {A^b, A^c}, while the representation forces a fixed positive gap, giving the same contradiction." (SM-B.4)

Counterexample: f = PRS·(1+ERS) on the open orthant has Φ, g, h all strictly increasing *everywhere on that domain*, satisfies C1-as-limit, C2, C4 — and its institutional gap g(x)·(h(A^c) − h(A^b)) shrinks to zero with g(x). Nothing "forces a fixed positive gap"; strict monotonicity does not imply a uniform lower bound. The open-orthant version of Theorem 3 without C3 is simply false.

What survives is real but far more modest, and the authors half-know it ("each fails a *different* axiom," §4): (a) additive scores violate C1 — true, useful, elementary; (b) multiplicative scores double-count *if* one accepts C3 in a universal-domain form — a causal assumption, not a theorem; (c) any C1-respecting single score must let institutional differences fade to zero as the venture weakens — a *boundary-degeneracy* property, which is arguably a feature, not a bug, and is in any case not "nonexistence." The abstract's claim — "no single score combining venture and institutional readiness exists (an Arrow-style impossibility)" — and the vignette's punchline are not licensed by what is proved. The Arrow branding compounds the problem: there is no domain-richness or interprofile structure here; the proof is a two-row sign-consistency collision that SM-B.2/B.4 dispatch in a paragraph. Referees with an economics or measurement-theory background will find all of this within an afternoon, and the paper's credibility will not recover in their report.

### M2. The load-bearing axiom is C3, and it assumes away the strongest argument for the practice being condemned

Everywhere the impossibility has teeth against real instruments, the tooth is C3. Yet the SM itself calls C3 "the most contestable premise, since a direct institutional channel (brand, signaling) would break it" (SM-B.7), and defers its empirical test (GMM overidentification) to the future registry. More fundamentally: a funding committee scoring (seed, host) pairs often *intends* a multi-objective quantity — expected project value *plus* capacity-building spillovers, portfolio option value on the host's future pipeline, signaling to co-investors. Under that objective, a direct institutional channel is not an error; it is the point. C1 has the same soft spot: "nobody funds a dead project because its university has excellent bylaws" (§4) is true of *project value* but not of a capacity-building-cum-commercialization instrument. The paper's claim that the axioms are "requirements that every party to the exercise would accept" (§4) is therefore false for exactly the practitioners whose practice is being declared incoherent: they can coherently reject C3 (and even C1-as-applied) by declaring a broader objective. The manuscript never confronts this defense. The honest — and still publishable — message is conditional: *if* your score is meant to measure this project's value, and *if* you believe institutions act only through realization and survival, then adjusting by the institution double-counts and adding it ranks corpses. That is a paper about making evaluation objectives explicit. It is not "calibrating a nonexistent instrument."

### M3. The empirical section reports no measurements, cannot be reproduced even in principle, and shows a suspiciously perfect record

Section 6's own honesty about design (retrospective, composite, selected, N = 8) is commendable, but three compounding choices push it below what RP referees will accept even "as illustration":

- **No ledger values are ever exhibited.** Table 2 reports regime labels, a binary gate, a low/mid/high slack state, and the final reading. No PRS, no P/R/S components, no ERS score for any host type, no σ_SU series for any domain. The two ledgers that constitute the paper's contribution never appear in operation. A measurement paper must show at least one measurement.
- **The readings are unreproducible by construction.** "Calibration constants withheld" (Table 2 note); "reported as method, not as results — both to respect proprietary operating data" (§3); "withheld under policy PF-010" (SM-C.5). The authors argue the *theorems* are invariant to the constants — true and fine. But Table 2's GO/NO_GO readings, the regime assignments, and the gate calls are *not* invariant to them, and Table 2 is the paper's only contact with data. No reader or referee can check that Project T reads NO_GO. An internal policy code ("PF-010") appearing in an academic manuscript will itself raise eyebrows.
- **Perfect hindsight agreement.** Of the completed, readable cases, the model goes 4-for-4 against known outcomes (T, Y, K read NO_GO and failed; R read GO and progressed), with L consistent under censoring. A retrospective scoring exercise conducted by the studio on its own pipeline, with outcomes known, that produces zero disagreements has no informational content and reads as fitted. The text even claims the model "prices the case correctly ex post" (§6) — an unfalsifiable statement under this design. Meanwhile the promised texture is missing: "Two agreements and one instructive tension organize the reading" (§6) — the tension is never identified; "Across the eight cases read together (Table 2, bottom panel)" — Table 2 has no bottom panel; Project Q is "retained for the survival panel only" — no survival panel exists in the manuscript.
- **The C3 "fingerprint" paragraph should be cut.** Claiming that ERS "shows no residual association with outcomes once those trajectories are conditioned on — the qualitative fingerprint C3 predicts" (§6) from N = 8 composite cases with unreported ERS values is motivated reasoning wearing a hedge ("descriptive posterior summary under strong priors"). It invites the exact Simpson/aggregation critique the paper levels at others.

Composite cases per se are survivable with a disclosed construction protocol (how many source projects per composite, what was altered, who scored, inter-rater checks). Composites + withheld constants + outcome-aware self-scoring + no reported values is not.

### M4. Twenty-one acknowledged proof gaps and a phantom monograph

The SM is candid — and what it is candid about is disqualifying for a submission whose contribution is theorems. Beyond the Lemma B.3 gap quoted in M1: Theorem 1's application of conjoint measurement to a 5-level rubric "assumed in the source architecture rather than constructed" (solvability/Archimedean on the continuum, SM-A.1); the cross-K coherence needed for a single weight vector "used implicitly ... not stated there as a separate axiom" (SM-A.2 Step 4); Proposition 1's gate value g(1)=0.25 "fixed by rubric-design convention ... not derived" (SM-A.3); Corollary 3.1's Simpson construction "defer[red] ... to the monograph's Ch 11 BVAR machinery" (SM-B.6); and in SM-C essentially every substantive step of Theorem 4 and Proposition 2 — the comparison principle, the regime ordering (Lemma C.1(d) "asserted"), single-crossing, C¹ pasting, Jacobian non-singularity, the persistence-perturbation scheme — is marked `[GAP]` and deferred to "monograph Appendix A.5.5." The companion monograph is unpublished and uncited in the reference list. Editors cannot send referees a paper that asks them to trust an inaccessible document for the validity of its stated theorems. Either the proofs close in the SM, or the affected results must be restated as propositions-under-conditions or conjectures — with the paper's claims re-scaled accordingly.

### M5. Theory/policy balance and journal fit

Word allocation of the 7,784-word body: §3–5 formalism 4,035 (52%); §6 empirics 1,140 (15%); §7 policy 515 (6.6%). Raw length (~8,700 incl. references) is within norms; the allocation is not. Research Policy's readership will not referee viscosity solutions (SM-C), Thomsen conditions, or uniformized transition matrices, and the paper does not need them to make its policy points: §5's actual message ("macro-ready-but-tech-not and tech-ready-but-macro-not are distinct, non-compensable failure modes"; "credible policy commitment is itself a founding subsidy") is expressible in two pages with the machinery cited, not rebuilt. As written, this manuscript's natural referees are at Mathematical Social Sciences or JEBO; its natural *audience* is at RP, JTT, or Research Evaluation. The authors must choose the audience and restructure, or the editor will choose for them. I also note the intro's claim to be "the first such marriage" of stopping theory and a measurement layer, and §4's "first impossibility for aggregation across observation levels" — priority claims of this breadth, resting on a five-literature review, are hostages to any referee who knows a sixth literature.

### M6. The uniqueness claims are put in by hand

Theorem 1 is sold hard: "the weighted sum is not one convenient formula among many. Under axioms that any technology-transfer practitioner would endorse ... it is the only admissible form" (§3). But the main text's E2 says only that capabilities "partially substitute ... at some rate." Additive conjoint measurement under the main-text axioms delivers Σφ_k(A_k) with *nonlinear* φ_k. Linearity — the actual ERS formula — comes from E2-s, an equal-interval assumption on rubric numerals that appears **only in the SM** ("a rubric increment δ on axis k is exactly compensated ... at a rate w_k/w_k′ that does not depend on the base levels," SM-A.1) and is itself a design stipulation, not something practitioners would recognizably endorse. Meanwhile §3 cites Stevens (1946) to insist "all axis scores are ordinal rubrics" — and then E2-s treats those ordinals as equal-interval cardinal. The paper cannot have both. Relatedly, Theorem 2's E1-violation is cardinal-only: at any fixed venture state, ERS^α·PRS^(1−α) induces *exactly* the Theorem-1 ordering over institutional profiles, so the ordinal content of "each axis assessable separately" survives the product; what fails is level-independence of increments in rubric units. "No axis's contribution can be assessed separately once the venture score enters the product" (§3) overstates what is shown. And Proposition 1 quietly makes the institutional ledger itself multiplicative in the gate — softening the clean "ventures multiply, institutions add" slogan the title architecture rests on.

### M7. Missing literatures that RP referees will demand

For a paper whose declared home is research commercialization policy, the following absences are conspicuous, and several amount to renaming occupied ground:

- **Valley of death / proof-of-concept gap** (Auerswald & Branscomb 2003; the POC-center literature): "Before Zero" is a rebranding of a stage this literature has named and studied for two decades; it must be engaged, if only to say what the new name adds.
- **University spin-out process models** (Vohora, Wright & Lockett 2004 on critical junctures — an RP paper directly about pre-founding phase transitions; Rasmussen; Clarysse et al.; Di Gregorio & Shane 2003): the paper's institutional-nursery claims run straight through this work without touching it.
- **Composite-indicator critique inside innovation policy** (Edquist et al. on the European Innovation Scoreboard; Grupp & Schubert): §2 cites the generic OECD/JRC and Greco et al. lines but not the in-domain critiques that RP readers know.
- **Entrepreneurial decision-making under uncertainty** (Camuffo, Cordova, Gambardella & Spina 2020 on the scientific approach; effectuation; staged experimentation): directly relevant to GO/WAIT as a decision technology.
- **Real-options empirics on entry** (Folta & O'Brien 2004; O'Brien, Folta & Johnson): §5 cites only the theory canon.

### M8. The "Before Zero Studies" field declaration is a red flag, not an asset

> "Either outcome is the founding transaction of Before Zero Studies." (§8)

A single-team paper with N = 8 proprietary composite cases declaring a new research field — with the field's name trademark-styled in the abstract, §7, and the conclusion — will strike most referees as brand-building. The registered falsification program is the good part; keep it as a program (OSF deposit, falsifiers, discrimination floor) and delete the field-founding language. The same tonal trim applies to the recurring self-audit applause ("Saying so plainly is, we believe, part of what a measurement paper owes its readers," §5; "The distinction is not modesty but method," §6) and to §2's "this paper's Proposition 1 and Theorem 3 are, in one reading, that discipline arriving."

### M9. Framing device: the opening vignette is fiction presented as scene

The paper's hook — the national screening panel, the mid-session re-weighting, "two seeds swap ranks" — is revealed only in footnote 1 to be "a composite of screening settings the authors have participated in; no specific committee is depicted." Using an invented scene as the empirical anchor for a claim of proof ("This paper proves the assumption false") is rhetorically effective and refereeically dangerous, particularly when M1 shows the proof does not cover that committee's instrument. Either document a real (anonymized) committee practice — program documents mandating institution-adjusted scores would do — or mark the vignette as stylized in the text, not the footnote.

## 4. Minor issues

1. **Notation collisions:** S is survival probability, S_0/S_1/S_2 are regimes, and S(A_7, A_{−7}) is the gated score in SM-A; SM-A.4 sets "P := PRS" against P = potential scale; Project R collides with variable R. Given "letters carry no information" (Table 2 note), rename the projects.
2. **Cross-reference errors:** SM-A says "Axis numbering follows Section 2" and "the equal-interval design of the Section 2 rubric" — the axes and rubric are defined in §3. Main text references Figure 1 only; SM references Figure 3; Figure 2 is referenced nowhere; none exist yet.
3. **Table 2 internal problems:** Project N's decision point is "2027 (sched.)" inside a dataset described as "2007–2026"; Project L shows "(censored, 15 mo)" in a column headed "24-mo outcome"; Project Q's model reading is "(decision-point rubric insufficient)" yet Q is counted among the eight and retained for a nonexistent panel; the promised "one instructive tension" is never named.
4. **Corollary 3.1 logic:** the signatures are properties of the DGP under C1–C4; they hold whether or not "a field nonetheless ranks by some single score." The ranking premise is doing rhetorical, not logical, work. Also "will exhibit at least one of" is asserted while the SM defers the constructive existence of reversal-generating compositions [GAP, SM-B.6].
5. **Theorem naming:** "Theorem 3 (two-layer non-commutativity)" — nothing non-commutative is stated in the main text; the order-of-operations remark lives only in an SM bridge paragraph.
6. **Theorem 4's argument list** θ*(P, F, B, Π) silently omits r, I, b_k, s_k, on which the threshold also depends — a rhetorical selection that should be flagged as such.
7. **Reference hygiene:** Bertola (1998), Caballero (1999), Fishburn (1970), Gorman (1968) cited in SM but absent from the list; Atkinson (1970) and Cobb & Douglas (1928) listed but never cited; Mankins (1995) is gray literature carrying a load-bearing §2 claim; Popper and Lakatos are ornamental.
8. **Unsupported empirical asides:** "typically five to fifteen" years (§1) — no citation; "Every institution-adjusted score we have encountered in evaluation practice is one of these three" (§4) — anecdotal universal.
9. **Figure 3 as specified cannot inform:** SM-C.5 states "every symbol below ... is a placeholder, and no numerical output is reported," yet Figure 3 is to display numerical thresholds and sweep slopes from the withheld calibration — unreproducible decoration; and its "sign check" diagnostic is correctly admitted to be "a check of the code against the theory, not evidence for the theory."
10. **Submission apparatus missing:** abstract flagged as placeholder ("S4 で最終化 — outline 版を仮置き"); no highlights; no CRediT, data-availability, or conflict-of-interest statements (the last is mandatory and non-trivial here: the authors evaluate their own studio's pipeline and presumably operate the instrument commercially); Japanese production headnotes and changelogs remain in both files.
11. **E2/Theorem 1 friction:** Theorem 1 is stated over profiles (A_1,…,A_8) while E2 exempts axis 7 and Proposition 1 removes it from the additive block; state Theorem 1 over the seven non-gate axes to avoid the apparent contradiction.
12. **Abstract style:** contains the unexplained symbol σ_SU and the phrase "Arrow-style impossibility" that M1 shows needs retirement; at ~200 dense words it is closer to a summary of the monograph than an RP abstract.

## 5. Concrete revision suggestions, ranked by impact

**R1. Re-scale the central claim to what is actually proved, and restructure §4 around it.** Three viable paths, in descending order of ambition: (a) *Prove the right theorem*: for any weakly monotone single score satisfying C1, institutional differences must vanish as the venture approaches death — so institution-adjustment cannot perform the compensating role that motivates it at exactly the margin where committees invoke it. This "boundary-degeneracy" result is true, provable with the tools already in SM-B, covers multiplicative practice, and is policy-relevant. (b) *Make C3 the honest centerpiece*: present the result as conditional — under a stated universal-domain C3, multiplicative adjustment double-counts; under C1, additive scores rank corpses — and lead with the GMM test of C3 as the paper's empirical proposal. (c) At minimum, rewrite the abstract and §1 so that "no single score exists" becomes "every single-score family in use fails a named axiom, and which axiom it fails is diagnostic." In all cases: delete the Arrow branding or confine it to one sentence of lineage; fix or delete the open-orthant paragraph of SM-B.4 (it is false as written — see M1's counterexample).

**R2. Invert the balance for this journal.** Compress §3–5 to roughly half their current length: state each result in words with one display each, move *all* machinery (conjoint conditions, HJB/viscosity apparatus, Lemma C.2) to the SM, and delete nothing from the intuition paragraphs — they are the best writing in the paper. Reinvest the ~2,000 words in §6–7: a worked committee example (how a gap-fund panel would actually run two-ledger governance, with a mock docket); mapping Proposition 2's levers onto named instruments (SBIR-type grants, university gap funds, national program persistence); a Goodhart/gaming discussion of what happens when ERS becomes a funding criterion (one sentence exists; it deserves a page).

**R3. Make the evidence auditable or re-label the paper.** Minimum for review: (i) disclose the composite-construction protocol (sources per composite, alterations, scorer identity); (ii) an independent, outcome-blind re-scoring of the decision-point rubrics with inter-rater agreement reported; (iii) report the ledger values Table 2 currently hides — at rubric level, per composite case, including at least one σ_SU trajectory and one ERS profile; (iv) sensitivity of the GO/NO_GO readings to the withheld constants (or escrow the constants with the journal); (v) include at least one case the model reads wrongly or ambiguously, or explain why none exists; (vi) delete the N=8 C3-fingerprint paragraph. If (i)–(iv) are impossible under the proprietary policy, reposition the paper as a conceptual/methods contribution with a *hypothetical* worked example, clearly labeled — that is a publishable RP genre, and it is more honest than unverifiable composites.

**R4. Close or downgrade every GAP; exorcise the monograph.** Each of the 21 SM gaps either gets a complete proof, or the parent claim is restated with the missing condition as an explicit assumption ("under the cross-K coherence condition K*, …"), or the result is demoted to a conjecture. All deferrals to the unpublished monograph must go; if the monograph exists as a working paper, cite it properly and make the present paper self-contained anyway. Fix the reference list in both directions (add Fishburn, Gorman, Bertola, Caballero; cut or cite Atkinson, Cobb–Douglas).

**R5. Reposition within the literatures that already own this ground.** Add and engage: valley-of-death/POC (Auerswald & Branscomb), USO process models (Vohora–Wright–Lockett; Rasmussen), in-domain composite-indicator critiques (Edquist et al.), experimental entrepreneurship (Camuffo et al.), real-options empirics (Folta & O'Brien). State explicitly what "Before Zero" adds beyond a name — the defensible answer is "a measurement and aggregation theory for a stage others have described," and saying so sharpens rather than weakens the contribution. Resolve the Stevens/E2-s contradiction in the text: either defend the rubrics as designed-cardinal (and say so plainly, retiring the "only admissible form" rhetoric), or accept Σφ_k(A_k) and present linear ERS as a calibration convention.

**R6. Delete the field-founding apparatus.** Keep the OSF-registered program, the falsifiers, and the Brier floor — they are the paper's best credibility asset. Cut "Before Zero Studies" as a proper noun, "founding transaction," both "first" priority claims, and the self-commendations noted in M8. The work is more impressive without the announcement of its own importance.

**R7. Housekeeping before any resubmission:** finalize the abstract (no placeholder flag, no unexplained symbols); produce Figures 1–3 or cut the references to them; add highlights, COI (including the studio's commercial interest in the instrument), data availability, and ethics statements; strip internal notes, changelogs, and the "PF-010" code; fix the Table 2 and cross-reference defects listed in Minor Issues 1–3; rename projects to avoid the R collision; align the dataset window with Project N's 2027 decision point.

---

*Summary for the editorial file:* an inventive, well-written, over-claimed manuscript whose genuine contribution — two-ledger governance for pre-founding evaluation, with named failure modes for the composites policy keeps demanding — is currently obscured by a headline theorem that does not cover its target, an empirical section without measurements, and a scaffolding of deferred proofs. Return without review; encourage resubmission along R1–R3 as a policy-first paper. If the authors instead want the theory paper, it should go to a different journal.
