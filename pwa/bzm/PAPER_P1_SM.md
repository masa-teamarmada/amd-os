# Supplementary Material for "The Before Zero Model: measuring deep-tech ventures and their institutional nurseries before day zero"

## SM-A. Proofs for Section 3 (Theorems 1, 2 and Proposition 1)

This supplement gives complete proofs of the three formal results of Section 3: the weighted-sum representation of the institutional readiness score ECR (Theorem 1), the precondition-gate structure of the bylaws axis (Proposition 1), and the exclusion of multiplicative cross-layer combination with the project readiness score SPS (Theorem 2, stated as a reporting-scale result). SM-A.5 records the observation protocol that the missing-visibility axiom presupposes. Axis numbering follows Section 3; axis 7 is the bylaws (institutional-design) axis.

### SM-A.1 Setup, notation and axioms

**Profiles and observed sets.** An institutional profile is a vector $A=(A_1,\dots,A_8)\in([0,4]\cup\{\bot\})^8$, where $A_k$ is the rubric level of capability axis $k$ and $\bot$ marks an axis whose level has not been observed. The observed set is $K_{\mathrm{obs}}(A)=\{k:A_k\neq\bot\}$. For nonempty $K\subseteq\{1,\dots,8\}$ write $X_K=\prod_{k\in K}[0,4]$, and let $\mathbf{0}_K,\mathbf{4}_K$ denote the profiles constant at levels $0$ and $4$. The primitive of the theory is a family of institutional readiness orderings $\succsim_K$ on $X_K$, one for each nonempty $K\subseteq\{1,\dots,8\}$: profiles are compared within an observational equivalence class (equal $K$), and every score is reported together with its $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$. Every axis is assessable in principle — which axes are observed at a given institution is an audit outcome, not a restriction of the theory's domain; the point matters in Step 4 below. The domain between the rubric anchors is governed by a stated assumption:

**Assumption A-1 (continuum idealization).** Each axis domain is treated as the continuum $[0,4]$ with the five rubric anchors as landmarks, and $\succsim_K$ is defined on the continuum; restricted solvability and the Archimedean condition below are stated there. This is an idealization: assessment emits only five levels per axis, and on a finite grid the additive representation requires the full hierarchy of cancellation conditions rather than the two used here (Scott, 1964; Krantz et al., 1971, ch. 9; Fishburn, 1970, ch. 4). The cost is stated plainly: Thomsen and solvability are not directly testable on five-point data, so the empirical content of Theorem 1 is conditional on A-1.

**Definition SM-A.1 (additive conjoint structure).** For $|K|\ge 2$, the pair $(X_K,\succsim_K)$ is an *additive conjoint structure* if: (i) $\succsim_K$ is a *weak order* (complete, transitive); (ii) *independence*: the ordering induced on any one axis by fixing the remaining axes does not depend on where they are fixed; (iii) *double cancellation*, whose indifference version is the *Thomsen condition*: for axes $k\neq k'$, levels $a,b,c$ on $k$ and $x,y,z$ on $k'$, if $(a,y)\succsim(b,z)$ and $(b,x)\succsim(c,y)$ then $(a,x)\succsim(c,z)$, all other axes held fixed; (iv) *restricted solvability*: whenever varying axis $k$ alone brackets a target profile from above and below, some level of axis $k$ matches it exactly; (v) *Archimedean*: every bounded standard sequence (levels equally spaced in readiness terms) is finite; (vi) *essentiality*: each axis in $K$ can on its own reverse at least one comparison. See Krantz, Luce, Suppes and Tversky (1971, ch. 6, Def. 6.7 and adjacent definitions), hereafter KLST, and Luce and Tukey (1964).

**Axioms.** The paper imposes on each $\succsim_K$, and on any score $F_K$ representing it:

- **E1 (separability).** Ordinal form: independence, clause (ii) above. Cardinal form, meaningful once the score scale is fixed (see Step 5 below): $\partial^2 F_K/\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}=0$ for $k\neq k'$ — the contribution of one axis is assessable without knowing the others (Debreu, 1960; Gorman, 1968).
- **E2 (compensability).** Any two observed axes trade at finite positive rates; no observed axis is individually fatal to the institution. Its *strong form* E2-s encodes the equal-interval design of the Section 3 rubric: a rubric increment $\delta$ on axis $k$ is exactly compensated by a rubric decrement on axis $k'$ at a rate $w_k/w_{k'}$ that does not depend on the base levels. E2-s is a property of the rubric's *reporting convention* — the equal-interval anchor design — not an empirical claim about institutions: Theorem 1 is to be read as the unique aggregate consistent with an equal-interval reporting convention, and its statement names E2-s explicitly.
- **E3 (audited missing-visibility).** The aggregate is the *family* $\{F_K\}$: the symbol $\bot$ is never mapped into $[0,4]$, the score depends only on observed coordinates, $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is disclosed alongside the score, and $A_k=\bot$ ("unknown") is treated differently from $A_k=0$ ("not started"). The symbol $\bot$ is assigned under the *assessor's* evidence standard — an axis is $\bot$ only when evidence is genuinely unavailable after audit; an institution's *declining to evidence* an axis is not $\bot$ but is coded at the rubric floor (level $0$) and remains in $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the worst-case coding standard of credit-rating practice (full remark in SM-A.5).
- **E4 (monotonicity, strict).** Improving any observed axis strictly increases readiness: $F_K$ is strictly increasing in every $A_k$, $k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}$; equivalently, raising any single coordinate alone yields $A'\succ_K A$. Essentiality is a consequence (Step 1), not a hypothesis.
- **E5 (cross-context trade-off invariance).** For any two observed sets $K,K'$ and axes $k,k'\in K\cap K'$, the compensating rate at which a rubric step on $k$ trades against a rubric step on $k'$ — at any common base levels of the pair — is the same under $\succsim_K$ and $\succsim_{K'}$: trade-offs between two capabilities do not depend on which further axes happen to be observed. An assessor who has calibrated how gap-fund access trades against incubation capacity does not re-calibrate that judgment because the bylaws axis was unobservable at one institution. E5 is the cousin, at the level of observed sets, of the subgroup-consistency conditions of poverty measurement (Foster and Shorrocks, 1991).
- **(C) Continuity.** Each $\succsim_K$ has closed upper and lower contour sets on $X_K$.

**Practice reading of the axioms.** Each axiom is a statement about technology-transfer practice before it is mathematics. E1: committees do assess bylaws without auditing the incubator — the contribution of one axis to institutional readiness is judged without knowing the level of every other. E2: networks do substitute for in-house capacity — a thin incubation program can be compensated, at some rate, by a strong industry interface or external fund access — and no axis *other than the bylaws precondition* (axis 7, whose special status SM-A.3 formalizes) is individually fatal to the *institution*, unlike the venture's factors, where any zero kills. E4: no axis is inert — improving any evidenced capability moves readiness. The practice content of E3 — honest gap diagnosis requires that ignorance stay visible without being rewarded — and of E5 — an assessor's calibrated trade-off between two capabilities does not depend on which further axes happened to be observable — is already carried in their statements above and in the observation protocol of SM-A.5.

### SM-A.2 Proof of Theorem 1

**Theorem 1 (weighted-sum representation with a single weight vector).** *Let the family $\{\succsim_K\}$ satisfy E1–E4 (E4 strict), E5, (C), restricted solvability, the Archimedean condition and Thomsen/double cancellation under Assumption A-1, and let the rubric satisfy the equal-interval convention E2-s. Then there exists a single weight vector $w_1,\dots,w_8>0$, serving every observed set, such that each $\succsim_K$ is represented by*
$$\mathrm{ECR}(A)\;=\;100\cdot\frac{\sum_{k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}}w_k A_k}{4\sum_{k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}}w_k},$$
*unique up to positive affine transformation among scores additive in rubric units. Without E2-s, the same axioms deliver the additively separable form $\sum_{k\in K}\varphi_k(A_k)$ with continuous, strictly increasing $\varphi_k$ (weights and recalibrations not separately identified): the equal-interval reporting convention is exactly what linearizes the $\varphi_k$.*

The engine of the proof is the additive conjoint representation theorem, which we state in the form applied.

**Fact SM-A.1 (additive conjoint representation).** *Let $(X_K,\succsim_K)$ be an additive conjoint structure with every axis essential. Then there exist strictly increasing functions $\varphi_k:[0,4]\to\mathbb{R}$, $k\in K$, with*
$$A\succsim_K B\iff \sum_{k\in K}\varphi_k(A_k)\;\ge\;\sum_{k\in K}\varphi_k(B_k),$$
*and under (C) the $\varphi_k$ may be taken continuous. If $\{\psi_k\}$ is another such family, then $\psi_k=a\varphi_k+b_k$ for some common $a>0$ and constants $b_k$.* This is KLST (1971, ch. 6, Thm 6.2) for two axes together with its $n$-component extension in the same chapter; the original two-factor result is Luce and Tukey (1964), the topological route under continuity is Debreu (1960) and Wakker (1989), and the uniqueness formulation follows Fishburn (1970).

**Proof of Theorem 1.**

*Step 1 (essentiality; additive representation).* Fix $K$ with $|K|\ge 2$ (for $|K|=1$ the score is a single strictly monotone function and all claims below are immediate). E1 in ordinal form is exactly independence. Essentiality follows from strict E4 in one line: varying any axis $k\in K$ alone strictly changes readiness, hence reverses at least one comparison, so every observed axis is essential. With double cancellation, restricted solvability, the Archimedean condition and (C), the hypotheses of Fact SM-A.1 hold, so $\succsim_K$ is represented by $\sum_{k\in K}\varphi_k(A_k)$ with $\varphi_k$ continuous and strictly increasing. For $|K|\ge 3$ double cancellation is redundant given the remaining axioms (KLST, ch. 6); it is assumed uniformly because two-axis observed sets occur. Equivalently, E1 plus (C) yields additive separability by the overlapping-factors argument of Debreu (1960) and Gorman (1968), so that any representing score has the form $F_K=\Phi\!\left(\sum_k\varphi_k(A_k)\right)$ with $\Phi$ strictly increasing.

*Step 2 (linearization by E2-s).* E2 makes the substitution rate $\varphi_k'(A_k)/\varphi_{k'}'(A_{k'})$ (where derivatives exist) finite and positive: no axis is lexicographically prior, none individually fatal. E2-s says more: for every increment $\delta>0$, the representation gain $\varphi_k(t+\delta)-\varphi_k(t)$ is independent of $t$, because a fixed rubric step trades against other axes at a level-independent rate $w_k/w_{k'}$. A monotone solution of this increment equation on an interval is affine:
$$\varphi_k(t)=w_k t+c_k,\qquad w_k>0,$$
with $w_k>0$ by strict monotonicity. E2-s is a property of the numerals the rubric assigns — the equal-interval design of Section 3 — and it collapses each $\varphi_k$ to a linear function of them.

*Step 3 (calibration to the normal form).* $\sum_k(w_kA_k+c_k)$ and $\sum_k w_kA_k$ differ by a constant, hence represent the same ordering; the constants $c_k$ may be dropped. Adopt the paper's calibration convention $F_K(\mathbf{0}_K)=0$ and $F_K(\mathbf{4}_K)=100$: an institution with nothing started on any observed axis reads $0$, one fully institutionalized on every observed axis reads $100$. The unique positive affine transformation of $\sum_{k\in K}w_kA_k$ meeting both conditions is
$$F_K(A)=100\cdot\frac{\sum_{k\in K}w_kA_k}{4\sum_{k\in K}w_k},$$
since at $\mathbf{4}_K$ the numerator equals $4\sum_{k\in K}w_k$.

*Step 4 (a single weight vector across observed sets).* Steps 1–3, run separately on each $X_K$, deliver per-$K$ weights $(w_k^{(K)})_{k\in K}$, determined up to a positive scale; the scale-free content, identified with an observable in Step 2, is the compensating rate $w_k^{(K)}/w_{k'}^{(K)}$ at which a rubric step on $k$ trades against a rubric step on $k'$ under $\succsim_K$.

(a) *Cross-$K$ coherence from E5.* By E5 this rate does not depend on the observed set; write $\rho(k,k')$ for its common value. Fix a reference axis $k_0$ and set $w_{k_0}:=1$, $w_k:=\rho(k,k_0)$ — well defined precisely because $w_k^{(K)}/w_{k_0}^{(K)}$ is the same for every $K$ containing both axes. Rates chain multiplicatively: evaluating all three rates inside one observed set containing $\{k,k',k_0\}$ — such a set exists, the family being indexed by all nonempty $K$, all eight axes being assessable under the paper's protocol — gives $\rho(k,k')=\rho(k,k_0)/\rho(k',k_0)=w_k/w_{k'}$. Hence for every observed set $K$, with or without $k_0$, and all $k,k'\in K$: $w_k^{(K)}/w_{k'}^{(K)}=\rho(k,k')=w_k/w_{k'}$, so each per-$K$ weight vector is proportional to the restriction of the global $(w_1,\dots,w_8)$; since the Step 3 display is invariant to a common rescaling of its weights, the single vector serves every observed set. Disjoint observed sets are never compared directly by any $\succsim_K$; their weights cohere through chains of intermediate observed sets sharing axes, which eight assessable axes always supply.

(b) *Renormalization over $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$, licensed by E3.* Setting $K=K_{\mathrm{obs}}(A)$ yields the statement's formula. E3 is what makes the $K$-dependent denominator the right object: an axis with $A_k=0$ remains in $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$, contributing $0$ to the numerator while its weight $w_k$ stays in the denominator, whereas an axis with $A_k=\bot$ leaves both numerator and denominator. Thus "unknown" and "not started" receive different scores by construction, and the missing axis remains visible through the reported pair $(\mathrm{ECR},K_{\mathrm{obs}})$. At the interpretive level this renormalization is honest in the spirit of Rubin (1976): the score acknowledges the mechanism that hides an axis by carrying $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ explicitly, rather than silently imputing a default level for what was never assessed. The audit discipline behind the assignment of $\bot$ is recorded in SM-A.5.

*Step 5 (uniqueness; convention versus content).* Let $V$ represent $\succsim_K$ additively in rubric units, $V=\sum_{k\in K}\psi_k(A_k)$. By the uniqueness clause of Fact SM-A.1, $\psi_k=a\,w_kA_k+b_k$ with common $a>0$, so $V=a\sum_kw_kA_k+b$, a positive affine transformation of $\mathrm{ECR}$; conversely every positive affine transformation represents the same ordering. Fixing the endpoint calibration of Step 3 selects $\mathrm{ECR}$ uniquely within this class. Because the class is closed exactly under positive affine maps, the vanishing of cross-partials in E1's cardinal form is scale-invariant ($\partial^2(aF+b)/\partial A_k\partial A_{k'}=a\,\partial^2F/\partial A_k\partial A_{k'}$), so E1's two readings are mutually consistent. Finally, only the ratios $w_k/w_{k'}$ bind trade-offs. The scale normalization $\sum_kw_k=1$ is order-irrelevant. *Equal weights are not a normalization*: the ratios $w_k/w_{k'}$ are empirical content, recovered from observed trade-offs (Step 2) and portable across observed sets (E5), so the paper's default $w_k\equiv1$ is a disclosed working convention pending trade-off elicitation — a transparent value judgement in the sense of Sen (1977), not a quantity delivered by the axioms. $\blacksquare$

### SM-A.3 Proof of Proposition 1

**Proposition 1 (precondition gate on axis 7).** *Assume $7\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and let $F(A_{-7})=\sum_{k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}\setminus\{7\}}w_kA_k\,/\,4\sum_{k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}\setminus\{7\}}w_k\in[0,1]$ be the Theorem-1 aggregate over the remaining observed axes. Under (G0)–(G3) below,*
$$\mathrm{ECR}(A)=100\cdot g(A_7)\cdot F(A_{-7}),$$
*where $g:[0,4]\to[0,1]$ is a nondecreasing gate — by default the step $g(0)=0$, $g(1)=0.25$, $g(a)=1$ for $a\ge 2$, or a steep sigmoid variant.*

Proposition 1 is *not* a corollary of Theorem 1: it describes a deliberately heterogeneous structure in which E2 is withdrawn for axis 7 alone — a bylaws blackout is individually fatal — and E4 is retained for the gate axis only in its weak, nondecreasing direction (strictness would contradict a gate flat at $1$ on $[L,4]$), while E1–E4 continue to hold among the remaining axes. The architecture is the identification-then-aggregation scheme of Alkire and Foster (2011): a cutoff decision on the precondition dimension first settles whether the institution is in the frame at all, and only then does the Theorem-1 aggregation run over the remaining dimensions; the product form mirrors their multiplicative decomposition of the adjusted headcount.

The gate axioms, for the section orderings of the full score $\mathrm{ECR}(A_7,A_{-7})$:

- **(G0) Section monotonicity.** At each fixed $A_7=a$, the full score $S(a,\cdot)$ is nondecreasing in every non-gate axis, and scores are nonnegative — the section inheritance of E4 (strictness must fail on degenerate sections and is not asserted).
- **(G1) Precondition.** There is a threshold level $L$ (default $L=2$) such that (a) if $A_7=0$ the profile is readiness-equivalent to $\mathbf 0$ regardless of $A_{-7}$, and (b) if $A_7\ge L$ readiness coincides with the Theorem-1 aggregate over the remaining axes: $\mathrm{ECR}(A_7,A_{-7})=100\,F(A_{-7})$.
- **(G2′) Conditional consistency on non-degenerate sections.** For each fixed $a$ with $S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})>0$, the induced ordering over $A_{-7}$ is the Theorem-1 ordering on $K_{\mathrm{obs}}\setminus\{7\}$. The restriction to non-degenerate sections is what makes the block consistent: at $a=0$, (G1)(a) makes the section totally indifferent, and (G2′) is silent there rather than contradicted.
- **(G3) Within-level ratio preservation.** For each fixed $a$ with $S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})>0$, relative scores equal relative aggregates: $S(a,A_{-7})/S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})=F(A_{-7})$.

**Proof.** Define $g(a):=S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})/100$. If $g(a)>0$, (G3) gives $S(a,A_{-7})=100\,g(a)F(A_{-7})$ directly, and (G2′) is consistent with it: the section score is a positive multiple of $F$, whose ordering is the Theorem-1 ordering. If $g(a)=0$, then by (G0) $0\le S(a,A_{-7})\le S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})=0$, so the whole section is $0=100\,g(a)F(A_{-7})$. (G1)(a) forces $g(0)=0$; (G1)(b) forces $g\equiv1$ on $[L,4]$; monotonicity of the full score in the gate axis — E4's weak direction, the part retained for axis 7 — makes $g$ nondecreasing, hence $g:[0,4]\to[0,1]$, and the displayed factorization follows. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (the block has models).** For any nondecreasing $g_0:[0,4]\to[0,1]$ with $g_0(0)=0$ and $g_0\equiv1$ on $[L,4]$, the score $S(a,A_{-7}):=100\,g_0(a)\,F(A_{-7})$ satisfies (G0)–(G3): (G0) since $F$ is nondecreasing and $g_0\ge0$; (G1) from the boundary conditions on $g_0$; (G2′) and (G3) since on non-degenerate sections the score is a positive multiple of $F$ and $F(\mathbf 4_{-7})=1$. The default step gate and the steep sigmoid are both members, so the block is consistent by exhibition.

Two elements of the display are design, and are labeled as such rather than derived. *Gate calibration (convention).* Only $g(0)=0$ and $g\equiv1$ on $[L,4]$ follow from the block; on $(0,L)$ the axioms bound $g$ between $0$ and $1$, and the value $g(1)=0.25$ is a rubric-design convention, on the same footing as the endpoint calibration of Step 3. *Status of (G3) (stated primitive).* (G3) is the minimal premise delivering the product form and is adopted as design rather than derived: its content is the Alkire–Foster product decomposition described above.

*Remarks.* (i) If $A_7=\bot$ the gate has no argument, and E3 requires the gated score itself to be reported as missing rather than defaulted. (ii) Replacing the step by a steep sigmoid preserves E4 and (C) and approximates the step arbitrarily closely; the choice is an implementation matter. (iii) The monograph embeds $F$ in a one-parameter constant-elasticity family $\left(\sum_{k\neq7}w_kA_k^{q}/\sum_{k\neq7}w_k\right)^{1/q}$ that degenerates to the weighted sum as $q\to1$ (and to log-multiplicative and min forms as $q\to0$ and $q\to-\infty$); the paper works throughout with the $q=1$ member, which is the Theorem-1 aggregate used above.

### SM-A.4 Proof of Theorem 2

**Theorem 2 (no multiplicative cross-layer combination: a reporting-scale result).** *Let $E:=\mathrm{ECR}(A)$ be the Theorem-1 score and let $X:=\mathrm{SPS}>0$ be the project readiness score, a strictly increasing differentiable function of project variables $(X_1,\dots,X_J)$ only, multiplicative in form $X=\prod_jX_j^{\beta_j}$, $\beta_j>0$, as in Section 3. For any $\alpha\in(0,1)$, consider $Z=E^{\alpha}X^{1-\alpha}$ on the interior region $E>0$, $X>0$. Then: (i) at each fixed venture state, $Z$ induces the same institutional ordering as $E$ — ordinal separability within a venture slice is preserved; (ii) $Z$ destroys the interval-scale reporting content of the institutional ledger: the within-layer cross-partials $\partial^2Z/\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}$ are strictly negative, so a fixed rubric step no longer trades against other axes at a level-independent rate in report units — the calibrated constant-rate property (E2-s) by which published weights are audited; and (iii) $Z$ makes marginal institutional credit venture-state-contingent: $\partial^2Z/\partial A_k\,\partial X>0$ and, axis by axis, $\partial^2Z/\partial A_k\,\partial X_j>0$ — the nursery is paid once inside the venture score and again as a multiplier, the double counting whose dynamic form Section 4 develops.*

**Proof.** Clause (i): at a fixed venture state $X$ is a positive constant, so $Z=E^{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{const}^{1-\alpha}$ is a strictly increasing transform of $E$. Clauses (ii) and (iii) are computations. By Theorem 1, $\partial E/\partial A_k=c_k$ with the constant $c_k:=25\,w_k/\sum_{j\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}}w_j>0$ — the constancy of $c_k$ across the whole domain is precisely E1 and E2-s at work inside the institutional layer. Treat $Z$ as a function of $(A,X)$ on the interior region $E>0$, $X>0$. Then
$$\frac{\partial Z}{\partial A_k}=\alpha E^{\alpha-1}X^{1-\alpha}c_k,\qquad
\frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial A_k\,\partial X}=\alpha(1-\alpha)\,E^{\alpha-1}X^{-\alpha}c_k\;>\;0,$$
so the cross-layer bilinear term is everywhere nonzero. Through the multiplicative form of $X$, the same holds axis by axis on the project side:
$$\frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial A_k\,\partial X_j}=\alpha(1-\alpha)\,E^{\alpha-1}X^{1-\alpha}\,\frac{\beta_j c_k}{X_j}\;>\;0,$$
an $8\times J$ block of strictly positive cross-partials: the marginal credit for a one-level improvement of institutional axis $k$ depends on the state of every project axis, so institutional capability is counted a second time through the project channel — clause (iii). Within the institutional layer, for $k\neq k'$,
$$\frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}}=\alpha(\alpha-1)\,E^{\alpha-2}X^{1-\alpha}c_kc_{k'}\;<\;0,$$
so the report credit of a rubric step on axis $k$ falls with the level of every other axis: the cardinal within-layer form of E1 fails on the reported scale, and with it the constant-rate audit property — clause (ii).

The signs in (ii)–(iii) are properties of the whole affine class of reports built on $Z$ — $aZ+b$ has the same cross-partials scaled by $a>0$ — so no recalibration preserving the reported unit repairs them. Taking logarithms restores additivity between the blocks, $\log Z=\alpha\log E+(1-\alpha)\log X$, so the *order* induced by $Z$ on the interior is additively separable across the two ledgers; what the combination destroys is not ordinal structure but the calibrated rubric-unit scale: in rubric units $\partial^2\log Z/\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}=-\alpha\,c_kc_{k'}/E^{2}\neq0$, log-ECR steps are not rubric steps, and the audit property E2-s is a property of the reported scale, not of the order. Theorem 2 is accordingly a cardinal claim about the reporting instrument, and is stated as such. E1 as stated governs assessability among institutional axes: clause (ii) is its cardinal within-layer echo, and clause (iii) is a cross-layer statement E1 never governed — the measurement-level exclusion of the merger as a representation of *value* is Section 4's Theorem 3, not this result. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (the gate is not convicted).** The Proposition-1 score $100\cdot g(A_7)\cdot F(A_{-7})$ also has nonzero cross-partials between the gate axis and the others — $100\,g'(a)\,\partial F/\partial A_k$ where $g$ is differentiable — and it is not excluded, for a stated reason. Within each gate level, cross-partials among the seven axes governed by E1–E4 vanish identically (at fixed $a$ the gate is a common factor and $F$ is linear), so the interval-scale report of the additive ledger survives conditional on the gate; and axis 7 was never inside the additive claim — E2 is withdrawn for it by design, and its role is axiomatized separately by (G0)–(G3). The criterion clause (ii) applies is scale-preservation *within the set of axes for which additive reporting is claimed*; the prohibited merger scrambles exactly that set, the gate does not.

**Corollary (admissible cross-layer couplings).** *(i) Additive coupling $Z_{+}=\alpha'X+\beta'E$, $\alpha',\beta'>0$: all cross-partials vanish identically, $\partial^2Z_{+}/\partial A_k\,\partial X=0$ and $\partial^2Z_{+}/\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}=0$, so additive coupling preserves both the ordinal and the interval-scale reporting content of each ledger, and E3, E4 are inherited from each layer. (ii) Conditional coupling $X\mid A_7\ge L$: SPS is reported only when the institutional precondition of Proposition 1 is met; no composite scalar is formed, hence no cross term arises, and the rule is the project-level extension of the Proposition-1 gate. (iii) Every multiplicative coupling $E^{\alpha}X^{1-\alpha}$, $\alpha\in(0,1)$, is excluded as a reporting instrument by this theorem — and as a value representation by Theorem 3.* $\blacksquare$

The three results together give the shape claim of Section 3 its precise content: the project layer and the institutional layer answer to different axiom systems, so their aggregates take different functional forms by necessity, and the only couplings that preserve what each ledger reports are additive or conditional — never multiplicative.

### SM-A.5 Remark: audited observation and strategic non-disclosure

This subsection records the observation protocol E3 presupposes; without it, the design would invite the behavior it is meant to prevent.

Under *self-reported* missingness the $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ renormalization is strategically gameable. Withholding axis $m$ moves the score from $100\sum_{K}w_kA_k/4\sum_{K}w_k$ to the same expression over $K\setminus\{m\}$ — strictly larger exactly when $A_m$ lies below the weighted average of the other observed axes, precisely when disclosure would hurt. The mechanism is the denominator: a disclosed weakness leaves numerator and denominator together — little above, full weight below — while $\bot$ removes both at once. An institution whose weak axis would score low therefore *raises* its ECR by declining to evidence that axis. The moment ECR carries funding or reputational stakes, non-disclosure becomes the dominant move, and E3 — designed as an honesty device — becomes an opacity subsidy.

The protocol therefore fixes three rules. (i) $\bot$ is assigned under the *assessor's* evidence standard, not the institution's: an axis is $\bot$ only when evidence is genuinely unavailable after audit. (ii) Declined-to-evidence is not $\bot$: it is coded at the rubric floor (level $0$) and the axis remains in $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ — worst-case coding, the standard response of credit-rating practice to withheld information. Under (i)–(ii) non-disclosure can only lower the score, and the incentive reverses to disclosure. (iii) The report is the pair $(\mathrm{ECR},K_{\mathrm{obs}})$, and stripping $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is out of protocol: scores computed on different observed sets are not on a common scale. A shortlist that compares institutions across observed sets must either stratify by $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ or use the decision-conservative completion — unobserved axes at the floor — disclosed as such.

The connection to Rubin (1976) is exact. Renormalization over $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is representationally harmless only when the missingness mechanism is ignorable — when *which* axes are missing carries no information about their levels. Audited $\bot$ approximates ignorability by construction: after (i)–(ii), missingness reflects the assessor's access, not the institution's choice. Self-reported $\bot$ does not — it is missingness selected on the missing value itself, the canonical non-ignorable mechanism — and a score renormalized over it inherits the selection.

## SM-B. Theorem 3 and its corollaries: the dynamic foundation

This supplement proves Theorem 3 and its two corollaries and constructs the model class in which Section 4 states them. The foundation is dynamic: the institution-adjusted value $f$ is not a primitive scalar constrained by axioms but is *defined* as the expected discounted value of the venture under institution-dependent dynamics — the same object whose optimization Section 5 studies, so Sections 4 and 5 share one value function. Three consequences organize the section. First, the structural properties Section 4 states informally — annihilation C1′, institutional monotonicity C2′, continuity C4′ — are derived, not assumed (Lemma SM-B.1), and the class is non-empty by exhibition, so the existence question for the system C1′–C4′ is settled by construction. Second, the channel statement C3′ — institutional axes enter only the laws of motion of the venture state — is the *definition of the model class*, not a restriction on a given function. This dissolves a type problem that any static formulation of the channel carries: a display such as $df/dA_k=f_R\,\partial R/\partial A_k+f_S\,\partial S/\partial A_k$ must read $R$ and $S$ simultaneously as free coordinates of $f$ and as functions of $A$; here $(R,S)$ are states, $A$ parameterizes their laws of motion, and $f$ is the value functional of the resulting process — each symbol has one type. Third, the impossibility is proved where it matters: for *weakly* monotone composites — the class evaluation practice actually uses — under either of two richness conditions with observable content (SM-B.3–B.4), the strictly monotone case having been disposed of first as the boundary phenomenon it is (SM-B.2).

### SM-B.1 The nurturing-environment class

A venture ledger state is $x=(P,R,S)\in X:=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,1]\times[0,1]$: $P$ the potential scale, constant along a venture's path; $R$ realization readiness, with $R=1$ marking completion of the pre-founding journey; $S$ the survival state. An institutional profile is $A\in\mathcal{A}:=[0,4]^{8}$, the eight rubric axes of Section 3. The *death set* is $\mathcal{D}_0:=\{x:\min(P,S)=0\}$; states with $\min(P,S)>0$ are *live* (interior). $R$ is deliberately absent from $\mathcal{D}_0$ — $R=0$ is a normal live early state, not a boundary of the model; Lemma SM-B.1(b) develops the point.

**Definition SM-B.1 (nurturing environments; the benchmark subclass $\mathfrak{D}$).** A *nurturing environment* $e$ specifies laws of motion for the venture state in which the institutional profile enters only through the coefficients:

**C3′ (channel structure, definitional).** *$A$ appears in the laws of motion of $(R,S)$ — and, in the general case, of slack $F$ and of the exposure to $\sigma_{SU}$ innovations — and nowhere else; venture value is the expected discounted payoff generated by those laws.*

The *deterministic benchmark subclass* $\mathfrak{D}$ consists of pairs $e=(\lambda,r)$ with $r\ge0$ and a hazard-intensity function $\lambda:[0,1]\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}$, continuous and non-increasing in each $A_k$, generating
$$\frac{dR_t}{dt}=1,\qquad \frac{dS_t}{dt}=-S_t\,\lambda(R_t;A),$$
with $S=0$ and $P=0$ absorbing with zero flow, completion when $R$ reaches $1$ (at $\tau=1-R_0$), and value the discounted completion payoff $f_e(x;A):=e^{-r\tau}\,P\,S_{\tau}$. Since $R_t=R+t$, integrating the linear equation for $S$ gives $S_\tau=S\exp\big(-\int_0^{1-R}\lambda(R+t;A)\,dt\big)$, i.e., substituting $u=R+t$,
$$f_e(P,R,S;A)\;=\;P\,S\,\exp\Big(-r(1-R)-\int_R^1\lambda(u;A)\,du\Big).$$
Equivalently, $S_t$ is the probability that a venture facing hazard rate $\lambda(R_t;A)$ is still alive at $t$, and $f_e=\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-r\tau}P\,\mathbf{1}\{\text{alive at }\tau\}\big]$: the state reading and the hazard reading coincide. Thicker nurseries lower the hazard along the *remaining* maturation path $[R,1]$, and value integrates that reduction over exactly the road still to be travelled — the exposure structure that drives SM-B.3.

The general class extends $\mathfrak{D}$ with C3′ kept as the defining restriction: $(R,S,F)$ is a strong Markov process, each $A_k$ enters its generator monotonically (drift of $R$ up, hazard behind $S$ down, slack accumulation up, loading on $\sigma_{SU}$ innovations up), and $A$ appears nowhere outside the generator. Section 5's dynamics are exactly of this form, and one point deserves emphasis: financial slack $F$ and the $\sigma_{SU}$-exposure — the carriers through which Section 5 and SM-D route the funding axis $A_5$ and the interface axes $A_4$, $A_8$ — are *inside* the licensed channel by construction, so the stopping model and the channel structure are consistent by definition, not by assumption. Because death states are absorbing with zero flow, every member satisfies C1′ directly from the definition of value; the remaining structural properties transfer from $\mathfrak{D}$ by the coupling and comparison arguments standard in the stopping literature (McDonald and Siegel, 1986; Dixit and Pindyck, 1994). We cite rather than reproduce these because nothing below uses them: every result of this supplement is proven inside $\mathfrak{D}$, and impossibility theorems need verified witnesses, not the largest possible class. Members of the class are called *admissible*.

**Lemma SM-B.1 (well-posedness, structure, non-emptiness).** *For every $e\in\mathfrak{D}$, with $\Lambda:=\max\lambda$ (finite, $\lambda$ being continuous on a compact domain):*

*(a) (C4′) $f_e$ is well defined, $0\le f_e\le PS\le P$, and jointly continuous in $(x,A)$.*

*(b) (C1′) If $\min(P,S)=0$ then $f_e(x;A)=0$ for every $A$, and $\mathcal{D}_0$ is absorbing. $R$ does not annihilate: if $\min(P,S)>0$ then $f_e(P,0,S;A)\ge PS\,e^{-r-\Lambda}>0$.*

*(c) (C2′) $f_e$ is non-decreasing in each $A_k$; at live $x$ it increases strictly along an increment $t\,e_k$, $t>0$, if and only if the remaining-path response is strict, $\int_R^1\big[\lambda(u;A+te_k)-\lambda(u;A)\big]\,du<0$. When $\lambda$ is $C^1$ in $A_k$,*
$$\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial A_k}(x;A)=-f_e(x;A)\int_R^1\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial A_k}(u;A)\,du\;>\;0\qquad\text{wherever }f_e>0\text{ and }\int_R^1\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial A_k}\,du<0.$$

*(d) $f_e$ is non-decreasing in $P$, $S$ and $R$: strictly in $P$ (resp. $S$) wherever $S>0$ (resp. $P>0$), and strictly in $R$ wherever $f_e>0$ and $r+\lambda(R;A)>0$.*

*The class is non-empty — $\lambda\equiv\lambda_0>0$ is a member, as are the two witness environments of SM-B.3, whose channels are operative — and every member satisfies C1′–C4′.*

*Proof.* All claims are read off the closed form. (a) The exponent is $\le0$ ($r\ge0$, $\lambda\ge0$), giving the bounds. Continuity: $\lambda$ is bounded and uniformly continuous on $[0,1]\times\mathcal{A}$, so $(R,A)\mapsto\int_R^1\lambda(u;A)\,du$ is continuous — $\big|\int_R^1-\int_{R'}^1\big|\le\Lambda|R-R'|$, and $\sup_u|\lambda(u;A)-\lambda(u;A')|\to0$ as $A'\to A$ — and $f_e$ is a product and composition of continuous maps. (b) The factor $PS$ vanishes on $\mathcal{D}_0$, for every $A$; absorption holds because $P$ is constant, $S=0$ is a fixed point of $dS_t=-S_t\lambda\,dt$, and both states are absorbing with zero flow by specification. At $R=0$, $\int_0^1\lambda\,du\le\Lambda$ gives the lower bound. (c) $A$ enters the closed form only through $-\int_R^1\lambda(u;A)\,du$, so at live $x$
$$\frac{f_e(x;A+te_k)}{f_e(x;A)}=\exp\Big(-\int_R^1\big[\lambda(u;A+te_k)-\lambda(u;A)\big]\,du\Big),$$
which is $\ge1$ since $\lambda$ is non-increasing in $A_k$, and $>1$ exactly when the integrated response is strict; the displayed derivative is the chain rule with differentiation under the integral. (d) $\partial f_e/\partial P=Se^{(\cdot)}$, $\partial f_e/\partial S=Pe^{(\cdot)}$, and $\partial f_e/\partial R=\big(r+\lambda(R;A)\big)f_e$ by the Leibniz rule. $\square$

*Remark.* Part (b) is a design decision the model class makes and the paper defends: annihilation is carried by $P$ and $S$ only — the weakest-link boundary (Kremer, 1993) runs through potential and survival — while $R=0$ is a live state with strictly positive option value. An unrealized seed is an option, not a corpse: the gap-fund axis ($A_5$) exists precisely to fund seeds at $R\approx0$, and a model that killed them at the boundary would delete the very objects that funding channel exists for. Section 6's instant-fail type dies of $P\to0$, not of low $R$. A founder-side microfoundation of C1′ is treated in the companion monograph; the property stands on the mortality argument alone.

### SM-B.2 Boundary degeneracy of strictly increasing composites

Write $\succsim_e$ for the value ordering induced on $X\times\mathcal{A}$: $(x,A)\succsim_e(x',A')$ iff $f_e(x;A)\ge f_e(x';A')$. A triple $(g,h,\Phi)$ — $g:X\to\mathbb{R}$, $h:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}$, $\Phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ — *represents* $\succsim_e$ if
$$f_e(x;A)\ge f_e(x';A')\iff \Phi\big(g(x),h(A)\big)\ge\Phi\big(g(x'),h(A')\big)\qquad\text{for all pairs};$$
the biconditional transports strict inequalities and ties: $f_e>f_e'$ iff $\Phi>\Phi'$, and $f_e=f_e'$ iff $\Phi=\Phi'$. Call the institutional channel *operative* at a live state $x^{*}$ if there are an axis $k$, a profile $A^{\circ}$ and $t>0$ with $f_e(x^{*};A^{\circ}+te_k)>f_e(x^{*};A^{\circ})$.

**Lemma SM-B.2 (boundary degeneracy).** *Let $e$ be admissible with the channel operative somewhere. Then no triple with $\Phi$ strictly increasing in each argument represents $\succsim_e$.*

*Proof.* At the operative $x^{*}$ the representation gives $\Phi(g(x^{*}),h(A^{\circ}+te_k))>\Phi(g(x^{*}),h(A^{\circ}))$; were $h(A^{\circ}+te_k)\le h(A^{\circ})$, monotonicity of $\Phi$ would force $\le$; hence $h(A^{\circ}+te_k)>h(A^{\circ})$. On any boundary row $x_a\in\mathcal{D}_0$, C1′ gives $f_e(x_a;A^{\circ}+te_k)=f_e(x_a;A^{\circ})=0$, so the representation requires a tie at $x_a$ — while the $h$-gap and strict increase of $\Phi$ in its second argument force a strict gap. Contradiction. $\square$

This is the easy result, and we frame it as such: a function forced to vanish on a set where its institutional factor should still separate points cannot be strictly institution-sensitive there. It is an instance, transported across observation levels, of the known phenomenon that veto and annihilation structures defeat strictly monotone separable representations in noncompensatory aggregation (Fishburn, 1976; Bouyssou and Marchant, 2007); in separability language, C1′ negates single-factor independence of the institutional sector (Gorman, 1968; Blackorby, Primont and Russell, 1978). Its practical bite is limited. The composites evaluation practice actually uses — pure multiplication $g(x)h(A)$ with $g=0$ on the death set, geometric blends, CES aggregates with complements, min gates — are *weakly* monotone: flat in the institution exactly on the death set, sensitive off it, so they escape the lemma untouched. The real question is whether weakly monotone composites survive. They do not, under either of two richness conditions with observable content.

### SM-B.3 Two richness conditions and their witnesses

**Condition SH (stage-contingent operability).** *There exist axes $k\neq k'$, a base profile $A^{\circ}$, increments $t,s>0$, and live states $x_{\mathrm{lo}},x_{\mathrm{hi}}$ such that*
$$f_e(x_{\mathrm{lo}};A^{\circ}+te_k)>f_e(x_{\mathrm{lo}};A^{\circ}+se_{k'})\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_e(x_{\mathrm{hi}};A^{\circ}+te_k)<f_e(x_{\mathrm{hi}};A^{\circ}+se_{k'}).$$

This is the direct form: an institutional comparison that strictly reverses across venture strata. A structural sufficient condition in $\mathfrak{D}$ is stage-disjointness of the response supports — axis $k$ operative on early strata ($\lambda$ responds to $A_k$ only at low $u$), axis $k'$ on late strata. Its economic content is a standard fact of technology-transfer practice: incubation and entrepreneurship support ($A_3$) and seed scouting ($A_1$) act early in the realization journey, while licensing execution ($A_2$) and the corporate interface ($A_4$) act late, when there is something to license and someone to interface with (cf. the rubric anchors of SM-D.1). Different institutional capabilities bind at different venture stages.

**Witness (SH).** In $\mathfrak{D}$ take, for $\beta>0$ and $\lambda_0\ge8\beta$,
$$\lambda(u;A)=\lambda_0-\beta\,\mathbf{1}\big[u\in[0,\tfrac{3}{5})\big]\,A_k-\beta\,\mathbf{1}\big[u\in[\tfrac{3}{5},1]\big]\,A_{k'},$$
so that $\lambda\ge\lambda_0-4\beta\ge4\beta>0$ on the whole rubric range and $\lambda$ is non-increasing in each axis. (The stage cutoff can be smoothed to meet the continuity clause of Definition SM-B.1: replacing each indicator by a piecewise-linear ramp on an $\varepsilon$-window around $u=\tfrac{3}{5}$ perturbs every integral below by at most $8\beta\varepsilon$, hence every log-ratio below by at most $16\beta\varepsilon$, while the strict log-gaps displayed are $\beta/5$ and $2\beta/5$ — so any $\varepsilon<1/80$ preserves both strict inequalities; we compute with the sharp cutoff for arithmetic transparency.) Take one rubric step on each axis, $A:=A^{\circ}+e_k$ and $A':=A^{\circ}+e_{k'}$ (any base with $A^{\circ}_k,A^{\circ}_{k'}\le3$), and states $x_{\mathrm{lo}}=(P,0,S)$, $x_{\mathrm{hi}}=(P,\tfrac{3}{5},S)$, any $P,S>0$. In value ratios the discount and base-profile terms cancel; only the increments' integrated hazard reductions remain. At $x_{\mathrm{lo}}$ ($R=0$),
$$\int_0^1\lambda(u;A)\,du=\int_0^1\lambda(u;A^{\circ})\,du-\tfrac{3}{5}\beta,\qquad\int_0^1\lambda(u;A')\,du=\int_0^1\lambda(u;A^{\circ})\,du-\tfrac{2}{5}\beta,$$
so $f_e(x_{\mathrm{lo}};A)/f_e(x_{\mathrm{lo}};A')=\exp\big(\tfrac{3\beta}{5}-\tfrac{2\beta}{5}\big)=e^{\beta/5}>1$. At $x_{\mathrm{hi}}$ ($R=\tfrac{3}{5}$) the remaining path $[\tfrac{3}{5},1]$ misses the support of axis $k$ entirely, so
$$\int_{3/5}^1\lambda(u;A)\,du=\int_{3/5}^1\lambda(u;A^{\circ})\,du,\qquad\int_{3/5}^1\lambda(u;A')\,du=\int_{3/5}^1\lambda(u;A^{\circ})\,du-\tfrac{2}{5}\beta,$$
and $f_e(x_{\mathrm{hi}};A)/f_e(x_{\mathrm{hi}};A')=\exp\big(0-\tfrac{2\beta}{5}\big)=e^{-2\beta/5}<1$. SH holds with $t=s=1$: one rubric step on the early axis beats one on the late axis for a seed at the start of its journey, and loses to it for a seed past the early stage.

**Condition ED (exposure-duration asymmetry).** Institutions act over the venture's *remaining* journey, and ventures differ in how much journey remains. Direct form: *there exist live states $x,x'$ and profiles $A\le A'$ (componentwise, $A'$ strictly better on an operative axis) such that*
$$f_e(x;A)>f_e(x';A)\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_e(x;A')<f_e(x';A')$$
— a *venture* comparison that strictly reverses as the institution strengthens.

**Witness (ED).** In $\mathfrak{D}$ take the stage-uniform hazard $\lambda(u;A)=c\,(4-A_k)$ with $c>0$ (i.e. $\lambda_0=4c$ exactly; any $\lambda_0\in[4c,6c)$ delivers both signs below, and $\lambda_0=4c$ is cleanest) and $r=0$; $\lambda\ge0$ on the rubric range and is non-increasing in $A_k$. The closed form reads $f_e=PS\,e^{-c(4-A_k)(1-R)}$. Take a nearly-done and an early state half a journey apart, $x=(P,\tfrac{3}{4},S)$ and $x'=(P',\tfrac{1}{4},S')$, with mass ratio $P'S'=e^{c}\,PS$ (feasible since $P$ is unbounded: e.g. $S'=S$, $P'=e^{c}P$), and let $A'$ improve $A$ by the full rubric range on axis $k$: $A_k=0$, $A'=A+4e_k$. Then
$$\log\frac{f_e(x;A)}{f_e(x';A)}=\log\frac{PS}{P'S'}+c\,(4-A_k)\Big[\big(1-\tfrac{1}{4}\big)-\big(1-\tfrac{3}{4}\big)\Big]=-c+\frac{c\,(4-A_k)}{2},$$
which at $A_k=0$ equals $-c+2c=c>0$ — the nearly-done seed wins at the weak nursery — and at $A'_k=4$ (hazard zero) equals $-c<0$ — the early seed wins at the strong one. Raw values make the mechanism visible: at $A_k=0$, $f_e(x;A)=PS\,e^{-c}$ against $f_e(x';A)=PS\,e^{-2c}$; at $A'_k=4$, $f_e(x;A')=PS$ against $f_e(x';A')=e^{c}PS$. Less remaining hazard favors the nearly-realized seed at a weak nursery; a strong nursery's nursing compounds over the early seed's longer remaining road ($1-R'=\tfrac{3}{4}$ against $1-R=\tfrac{1}{4}$) and reverses the ranking.

Two features of the pair deserve note. First, in the ED witness the institutional ordering itself is venture-uniform — $f_e(\cdot\,;A')\ge f_e(\cdot\,;A)$ everywhere, by Lemma SM-B.1(c) — and the hazard is stage-uniform, so this witness satisfies ED while violating SH; it is the *venture* comparison that flips. SH and ED are logically independent mechanisms: SH reverses institutional comparisons across venture strata, ED reverses venture comparisons across institutional strength. Second, both conditions are *empirical statements with observable content*, not normative axioms: whether early-acting and late-acting capabilities exist, and whether institutional value scales with remaining exposure, are inspectable features of a technology-transfer environment. They play exactly the role that domain richness plays in preference-aggregation impossibilities (Arrow, 1963), and the registered program of Section 7 treats them as premises to be audited, not assumed.

### SM-B.4 Theorem 3: the single-index impossibility

**Theorem 3 (single-index impossibility).** *Let $e$ be an admissible environment satisfying SH or ED. Then there exist no functions $g$ of the ledger state, $h$ of the institutional profile, and $\Phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ such that $\Phi(g(x),h(A))$ represents the value ordering $\succsim_e$ — that is, $f_e(x;A)\ge f_e(x';A')\iff\Phi(g(x),h(A))\ge\Phi(g(x'),h(A'))$ for all pairs — with $\Phi$ non-decreasing in its institutional argument (under SH), or with $\Phi$ non-decreasing in its venture argument (under ED).*

*Proof.* Under SH, let $(A,A',x_{\mathrm{lo}},x_{\mathrm{hi}})$, $A=A^{\circ}+te_k$, $A'=A^{\circ}+se_{k'}$, realize the reversal. The representation at $x_{\mathrm{lo}}$ requires $\Phi(g(x_{\mathrm{lo}}),h(A))>\Phi(g(x_{\mathrm{lo}}),h(A'))$; were $h(A)\le h(A')$, $\Phi$ non-decreasing in its institutional argument would force $\le$; hence $h(A)>h(A')$. At $x_{\mathrm{hi}}$ the true order requires $\Phi(g(x_{\mathrm{hi}}),h(A))<\Phi(g(x_{\mathrm{hi}}),h(A'))$; but $h(A)>h(A')$ and monotonicity force $\ge$. Contradiction. Under ED the argument is symmetric in the first coordinate: $f_e(x;A)>f_e(x';A)$ forces $g(x)>g(x')$, and then $f_e(x;A')<f_e(x';A')$ requires $\Phi(g(x),h(A'))<\Phi(g(x'),h(A'))$ while $g(x)>g(x')$ and monotonicity in the venture argument force $\ge$. $\blacksquare$

*Remarks.* (i) *Scope.* Nothing was assumed about $g$ and $h$ — no monotonicity, no continuity; the only structure used is that the institutional information reaches the score through a single index used monotonically (under ED, symmetrically, the venture information). The excluded class therefore contains every composite in evaluation practice — pure multiplication, weighted addition, geometric and CES blends, min/max gates, lexicographic hybrids — and every monotone recalibration of any of them. Practice endorses monotonicity in *both* arguments — a better venture score, or a better institution score, should never lower the composite — so either richness condition alone is fatal.

(ii) *Why a static axiomatization cannot deliver this.* On the static domain, weakly monotone separable scores consistent with annihilation and institutional monotonicity exist: $f=\min\big(SPS,\kappa(A)\big)$, with $\kappa$ any strictly increasing index calibrated to the institution's induced response, vanishes with the venture ledger and never decreases in any argument, and an axiom system that constrains $f$ pointwise cannot exclude it. What excludes it is stage-contingency of the *dynamics*: under SH the value ordering of two institutions genuinely reverses across venture strata, and no single institutional index — however cleverly calibrated — can lie on both sides of a reversal. The dynamic foundation is therefore not a technical reformulation of Section 4 but the source of the theorem's content.

(iii) *Tightness.* Where the channel is inoperative ($\lambda$ free of $A$), $f_e$ does not depend on $A$ and $\Phi(g,\mathrm{const})$ with $g=f_e$ trivially represents $\succsim_e$. In stage-uniform, duration-degenerate corners of the class the same holds on restricted slices: with $\lambda(u;A)=\bar\lambda(A)$ and comparisons confined to a common readiness $R=\bar R$, the closed form is exactly multiplicative, $f_e=[PS]\cdot[e^{-(r+\bar\lambda(A))(1-\bar R)}]$, and the monotone composite $\Phi(g,h)=g\,h$ represents that slice of $\succsim_e$. The exclusions are thus tied to the named structures: where the channel is null the composite is trivially available, and where stage-contingency and duration heterogeneity are both switched off, monotone composites can represent restricted slices of the ordering. Whether they can represent the full ordering in such degenerate corners is not needed here and not claimed. The premises are observable: SH and ED belong to the registered program of Section 7, so the theorem's empirical discipline is explicit.

(iv) *Boundary versus interior.* Lemma SM-B.2's obstruction lives on the death boundary; Theorem 3's lives at live states — both witnesses have $\min(P,S)>0$. The impossibility bites exactly where committees operate: on pre-screened live portfolios from which the corpses have already been removed.

### SM-B.5 Corollary 3.2: the portfolio impossibility

**Corollary 3.2 (portfolio impossibility).** *Let $\mathfrak{E}$ be a family of admissible environments such that for some axis $k$, profile $A^{\circ}$, increment $t>0$ and live state $x^{*}$ there are $e,e'\in\mathfrak{E}$ with the channel operative in $e$ — $f_e(x^{*};A^{\circ}+te_k)>f_e(x^{*};A^{\circ})$ — and null in $e'$ — $f_{e'}(x^{*};A^{\circ}+te_k)=f_{e'}(x^{*};A^{\circ})$. Then no single triple $(g,h,\Phi)$ — indeed no environment-blind function $F(x,A)$ whatsoever — represents $\succsim_e$ for every $e\in\mathfrak{E}$.*

*Proof.* A single $F$ assigns one order to the pair $\{(x^{*},A^{\circ}+te_k),(x^{*},A^{\circ})\}$; representing $\succsim_e$ requires the strict order, representing $\succsim_{e'}$ requires the tie. $\blacksquare$

*Remark.* This is the Arrow-style reading of the paper's result: as in Arrow (1963), what defeats the aggregation rule is richness of the admissible *domain* — a committee portfolio spanning technology domains whose response structures differ — not any single profile. It is the formal statement of the committee-room fact that one formula is applied to forty seeds drawn from forty response structures. We state its weight honestly: the proof is immediate and the content is entirely in the domain condition (a hazard responsive to $A_2$ in a licensing-driven domain, unresponsive where the binding constraint lies elsewhere). The within-environment Theorem 3, which defeats the single index inside one fixed response structure, is the sharper result.

### SM-B.6 Corollary 3.1: registered statistical diagnostics of single-score practice

**Setting: one class, two conditioning sets, three diagnostics.** Suppose the data are generated by an admissible environment with operative channels, and suppose practice ranks — and funds, and therefore samples — by a single monotone composite $V=\Phi(g(x),h(A))$. Theorem 3 locates the error of the composite exactly: $h(A)$ is a venture-uniform institutional index, while institutional value is stage-contingent (SH) and exposure-scaled (ED). This subsection converts that location into registered statistics, and the conversion requires two pieces of bookkeeping that the corollary's earlier statements left implicit. First, *timing*: every venture regressor below is timestamped at the decision date, so that the institution still has the remaining journey to act on; we call conditioning on such regressors *decision-time stratification*, and conditioning on realized post-decision paths *trajectory conditioning*. The two conditioning sets carry opposite predictions from the same model — positive institutional slopes under the first, a residual zero under the second — and keeping them named prevents the apparent contradiction discussed after Proposition SM-B.2. Second, *sampling*: field data are funded portfolios, and the funding rule is part of the data-generating process. The three diagnostics are not on an equal footing, and we say exactly how: (ii) is a proposition of the class under decision-time stratification (Proposition SM-B.2) and is the primary registered diagnostic, with (iii) its test form; (i) is a proposition of the class *plus one auditable premise about practice* — that the observed sample was selected on a single monotone score (Definition SM-B.2) — and Proposition SM-B.1 derives it in closed form, with the selection premise doing exactly the work the corollary's prose always claimed for the field's ranking habit.

**Notation.** For a candidate seed at its decision date, write $v$ for the decision-time venture index (decision-time $\mathrm{SPS}$ or a strictly increasing transform of it), $a$ for the registered institutional regressor (a single axis $A_k$ or the $\mathrm{ECR}$ aggregate; multi-axis statements stack axes), and $y$ for the registry outcome at the stated horizon, ordered as $f_e$. The protocol strata are the *annihilated cell* $\mathcal{Q}_0:=\{\min(P,S)\le\underline{s}\}$, split out at a registered liveness floor $\underline{s}$, together with quartiles $\mathcal{Q}_1,\dots,\mathcal{Q}_4$ of decision-time $\mathrm{SPS}$ among live states; the split matters because $\mathrm{SPS}$ conflates the annihilation margin with the earliness margin, and the two carry opposite slope predictions (collapse at $\mathcal{Q}_0$; maximal remaining exposure at low live quartiles). $\gamma^{q}$ (with axis subscript $\gamma_k^{q}$ where needed) denotes the institutional slope within stratum $\mathcal{Q}_q$ estimated with the continuous decision-time control retained inside the stratum, and $\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$ the coefficient on $a$ in the pooled regression of $y$ on $a$ alone — the marginal association, no venture control. Signature (i) is the marginal-versus-partial sign contrast in Simpson's (1951) sense; the stratification serves signature (ii)'s slope-heterogeneity claim, the continuous within-stratum control serves the collider algebra below.

#### SM-B.6.1 The selection data-generating process

**Definition SM-B.2 (score-selected sampling).** A *score-selected sample* is generated in three steps.

(D1) *Population.* Candidate seeds carry $(v,a,\varepsilon)$ with $v\perp a$, $\varepsilon\perp(v,a)$, and finite second moments. Population independence of $v$ and $a$ is the conservative benchmark — it hands the selection mechanism nothing to work with; the assortative case is Remark (b) below.

(D2) *Outcomes, per the class.* $y=\beta_v v+\beta_a a+\varepsilon$ with $\beta_v>0$, $\beta_a\ge0$. This is the linear read-out of the benchmark $\mathfrak{D}$: by Lemma SM-B.1(c) the institutional slope of log value over the remaining journey is $\Gamma_k(R;A)=-\int_R^1\partial\lambda/\partial A_k\,du\ge0$, and $\beta_a$ is its forward, post-decision average — the part of the institutional effect *not yet transmitted* into $v$. Proposition SM-B.1 needs only the algebraic form and is stated for a homogeneous $\beta_a$, the presentation least favorable to producing a reversal in the sense that stratum-varying slopes are extra structure, not extra negativity: the collider term below is a property of the selected $(v,a)$ distribution alone and does not depend on the outcome equation. Stratum-varying slopes are the subject of Proposition SM-B.2.

(D3) *Selection.* The field funds — and the analyst therefore observes — by a single monotone score: $D=\mathbf{1}\{s(v,a)\ge\bar s\}$ with $s$ strictly increasing in both arguments and $0<\Pr(D=1)<1$. A committee that ranks by one composite and funds above a bar is exactly this object; nothing else about $s$ is used in Lemma SM-B.3. The *Gaussian benchmark* specializes: $v\sim N(\mu_v,\sigma_v^2)$, $a\sim N(\mu_a,\sigma_a^2)$ independent, $s=w_vv+w_aa$ with $w_v,w_a>0$.

**Lemma SM-B.3 (single-score selection induces negative venture–institution dependence).** *In the model of Definition SM-B.2, without the Gaussian specialization:*

*(a) $\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D=1)\le 0$, with strict inequality whenever the selection boundary genuinely trades the two arguments — the marginal threshold $\psi(v):=\inf\{a: s(v,a)\ge\bar s\}$ is non-constant across a positive-probability set of funded $v$ values whose truncated means of $a$ differ.*

*(b) If selection is instead by conditional gating — $D=\mathbf{1}\{v\ge c_v\}\,\mathbf{1}\{a\ge c_a\}$, a product event — then $v$ and $a$ remain independent in the funded sample and the induced covariance is exactly zero.*

*Proof.* (a) Since $s$ is strictly increasing in $a$, the funded event is $\{a\ge\psi(v)\}$ with $\psi$ non-increasing in $v$ ($s$ increasing in $v$: a stronger venture clears the bar with a weaker institution). By $v\perp a$, the conditional law of $a$ given $(v,D=1)$ is the marginal law of $a$ truncated to $[\psi(v),\infty)$, so $m(v):=E[a\mid v,D=1]=E[a\mid a\ge\psi(v)]$. The truncated mean $t\mapsto E[a\mid a\ge t]$ is non-decreasing, hence $m$ is non-increasing in $v$. By the tower property and the two-copy (Chebyshev association) argument — for i.i.d. draws $(v_1,v_2)$ from the funded sample, $E[(v_1-v_2)(m(v_1)-m(v_2))]\le0$ pointwise since $m$ is non-increasing —
$$\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D=1)=\operatorname{Cov}\big(v,\,m(v)\mid D=1\big)=\tfrac12\,E\big[(v_1-v_2)(m(v_1)-m(v_2))\big]\;\le\;0,$$
strictly when $m$ is non-constant on the funded support, which is clause (a)'s condition. (b) With a product event, $\psi\equiv c_a$ on the funded support and the joint conditional law factorizes: $(v,a)\mid D=1$ is the product of the two one-sided truncations. Independence, hence zero covariance. $\square$

No distributional assumption, no linearity of the score, no Gaussianity: the negative dependence is a property of the *practice* — compensatory single-score funding — not of a parametric benchmark. Conditioning on being funded is Berkson's (1946) hospital-admission mechanism [needs S6 verification] transported to the committee room, and clause (b) is its exact off-switch: the conditional-gating design that Section 4 recommends, because it never trades the two ledgers against each other, inscribes no dependence at all.

**Lemma SM-B.4 (Gaussian selection moments).** *In the Gaussian benchmark, let $\sigma_s^2=w_v^2\sigma_v^2+w_a^2\sigma_a^2$, $\zeta=(\bar s-\mu_s)/\sigma_s$, and let $\delta(\zeta)=M(\zeta)\{M(\zeta)-\zeta\}\in(0,1)$ with $M(\zeta)=\phi(\zeta)/\overline N(\zeta)$, where $\phi$ and $\overline N$ are the standard normal density and upper tail (no relation to the composite $\Phi$ of Theorem 3, which we reserve). Then, in the funded sample,*
$$\operatorname{Var}(s\mid D)=(1-\delta)\,\sigma_s^2,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D)=-\,\delta\,\frac{w_vw_a\,\sigma_v^2\sigma_a^2}{\sigma_s^2},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(a\mid D)=\sigma_a^2\Big(1-\delta\,\frac{w_a^2\sigma_a^2}{\sigma_s^2}\Big)>0,$$
*and symmetrically for $\operatorname{Var}(v\mid D)$. The truncation factor $\delta$ is strictly increasing in $\zeta$, with $\delta\to0$ as $\zeta\to-\infty$ (no selection) and $\delta\to1$ as $\zeta\to+\infty$ (knife-edge selection); since $\Pr(D=1)=\overline N(\zeta)$, $\delta$ is pinned by the program's acceptance rate alone: $\delta=2/\pi\approx0.64$ at a $50\%$ acceptance rate, $\approx0.76$ at $25\%$, $\approx0.83$ at $10\%$.*

*Proof.* Decompose $u=b_u s+u_\perp$ for $u\in\{v,a\}$, with $b_u=\operatorname{Cov}(u,s)/\sigma_s^2$ and $u_\perp\perp s$ by joint normality. Conditioning on the $s$-event $\{s\ge\bar s\}$ leaves the laws of $(v_\perp,a_\perp)$ and their independence of $s$ untouched, so for $u,u'\in\{v,a\}$,
$$\operatorname{Cov}(u,u'\mid D)=b_ub_{u'}\operatorname{Var}(s\mid D)+\operatorname{Cov}(u_\perp,u'_\perp),$$
with $\operatorname{Var}(s\mid D)=(1-\delta)\sigma_s^2$ the standard one-sided normal truncation formula and $\operatorname{Cov}(v_\perp,a_\perp)=0-b_vb_a\sigma_s^2=-w_vw_a\sigma_v^2\sigma_a^2/\sigma_s^2$ from population independence. Substituting $b_v=w_v\sigma_v^2/\sigma_s^2$, $b_a=w_a\sigma_a^2/\sigma_s^2$ gives the displays; positivity of $\operatorname{Var}(a\mid D)$ follows from $\delta<1$ and $\sigma_s^2-w_a^2\sigma_a^2=w_v^2\sigma_v^2>0$. Monotonicity and the limits of $\delta$ are standard properties of one-sided normal truncation. $\square$

#### SM-B.6.2 Signature (i): the Simpson reversal, derived

**Proposition SM-B.1 (pooled-sign reversal under score-selected sampling).** *In the model of Definition SM-B.2 with the Gaussian benchmark:*

*(a) (Selection-robustness of the conditional regression.) In the funded sample, the linear projection of $y$ on $(1,v,a)$ has slope coefficients exactly $(\beta_v,\beta_a)$: since $D$ is $(v,a)$-measurable and $\varepsilon\perp(v,a)$, the conditional mean $E[y\mid v,a,D=1]=\beta_vv+\beta_aa+E[\varepsilon]$ is unchanged by selection, and a linear projection recovers a linear conditional mean under any regressor distribution. Decision-time stratification with the within-stratum continuous control therefore estimates $\beta_a\ge0$ without selection bias.*

*(b) (Pooled coefficient.) The funded-sample regression of $y$ on $a$ alone has slope*
$$\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}\;=\;\beta_a\;+\;\beta_v\,\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D)}{\operatorname{Var}(a\mid D)}
\;=\;\beta_a\;-\;\beta_v\,\frac{\delta\,w_vw_a\,\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_s^2-\delta\,w_a^2\sigma_a^2}\,.$$

*(c) (Reversal condition.) $\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}<0$ — the pooled institutional coefficient negative while every venture-controlled slope equals $\beta_a\ge0$ — if and only if*
$$\delta\;>\;\bar\delta\;:=\;\frac{\beta_a\,\sigma_s^2}{\beta_v\,w_vw_a\,\sigma_v^2+\beta_a\,w_a^2\,\sigma_a^2}\,,$$
*and $\bar\delta<1$ — a non-empty reversal region in truncation severity — if and only if*
$$\frac{w_a}{w_v}\;>\;\frac{\beta_a}{\beta_v}\,,$$
*i.e. the committee's score weights the institution, relative to the venture, more heavily than the forward outcome process does. In the limiting case $\beta_a=0$ — the institution's effect fully transmitted into the decision-time state — any selection severity $\delta>0$ makes $\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$ strictly negative against a within-stratum slope of exactly zero: the pure collider artifact.*

*Proof.* (a) is argued in the statement. (b) is omitted-variable algebra in the funded sample, $\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}=\beta_a+\beta_v\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D)/\operatorname{Var}(a\mid D)$, with Lemma SM-B.4 substituted. (c) The collider term in (b) is strictly increasing in $\delta$ (numerator increasing, denominator decreasing), running from $0$ at $\delta=0$ to $\beta_v\,w_a/w_v$ at $\delta=1$ (using $\sigma_s^2-w_a^2\sigma_a^2=w_v^2\sigma_v^2$); solving $\beta_a=\beta_v\,\delta\,w_vw_a\sigma_v^2/(\sigma_s^2-\delta w_a^2\sigma_a^2)$ for $\delta$ gives $\bar\delta$, and $\bar\delta<1$ reduces to $\beta_aw_v<\beta_vw_a$. $\blacksquare$

*Remark (the mechanism is the ranking practice — the corollary's new reading).* Every ingredient of the pooled coefficient is non-negative except one, and the negative one is manufactured by the funding rule: within-stratum slopes are $\ge0$ by Lemma SM-B.1(c), the population dependence is zero (or favorable; Remark (b)), and the sole negative term is the collider covariance that exists because the observed portfolio was *ranked into existence* by a compensatory score. The earlier formulation of this corollary carried a logical oddity the referees were right to press: the field's practice ("suppose a field nonetheless ranks by a single score") played no role in generating the predicted patterns. It now plays the leading role. Signature (i) is not "misspecification leaves traces"; it is: *the act of ranking by one score writes the scar* — and under conditional gating (Lemma SM-B.3(b)) the scar vanishes identically, so (i) doubles as an audit statistic for which selection regime generated a portfolio. Two consequences for the registry protocol. First, (i)'s null is uninformative wherever the sampled programs gate rather than rank, so the protocol's instrument audit (SM-B.6.6) records, program by program, which regime applies; absent that record neither rejection nor non-rejection is attributable. Second, (i) does not require SH or ED: it fires even in stage-uniform corners of the class, because its engine is C3′-transmission plus compensatory selection. The division of labor is exact — (i) tests the *practice*, (ii) tests the *stage-contingency* that powers Theorem 3.

*Remark (how demanding is the reversal region?).* Not demanding, on either axis of the condition. On severity: by Lemma SM-B.4, $\delta$ is pinned by the acceptance rate and is large at every realistic one — $0.64$ at a $50\%$ rate, $0.83$ at $10\%$ — so $\bar\delta$ is crossed unless the committee's institutional weight is nearly aligned with the forward process. On the weight condition: $\beta_a$ is only the *forward remainder* of the institutional effect — the part not yet transmitted into $v$ at the decision date — so a committee that credits the institution its *total* contribution, past nurturing included, mechanically overweights it relative to $\beta_a/\beta_v$. Over-crediting is the generic case, not a knife-edge. (A worked point on the numbers: $\beta_v=1$, $\beta_a=0.15$, unit variances, equal weights gives $\bar\delta\approx0.26$; a $10\%$-acceptance program has $\delta\approx0.83$ and a pooled slope of $\approx-0.56$ against a within-stratum slope of $+0.15$.)

*Remark (assortative populations).* If instead $\operatorname{Cov}(v,a)=\sigma_{va}>0$ in the population — stronger institutions attract better seeds — the same orthogonal decomposition yields $\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D)=\sigma_{va}-\delta\operatorname{Cov}(v,s)\operatorname{Cov}(a,s)/\sigma_s^2$, and the conclusion survives with a shifted threshold: as $\delta\to1$ the funded sample concentrates on a score level set, along which $a$ is an exactly decreasing linear function of $v$, so $\operatorname{Corr}(v,a\mid D)\to-1$ for any population correlation short of one. Sufficient selection severity forces the observed dependence negative regardless of favorable sorting; only the crossing point moves.

*Remark (the Section 6 anchor).* The observable face of Lemma SM-B.3 is compositional before it is regressional: in a score-selected portfolio, stronger institutions hold earlier-stage — lower-$v$ — projects, because $\operatorname{Corr}(v,a\mid D)<0$. Practitioners read this tilt as a fact about where hard projects go; the lemma derives it as a property of the funding rule, with no causal statement about institutions preferring early seeds. Section 6's cross-case paragraph reports exactly this tilt in the studio panel and routes it here rather than to a causal reading; it is the paper's empirical anchor for the selection mechanism, and — being a first moment rather than a regression — it is the piece of (i) that small samples can actually exhibit.

#### SM-B.6.3 Signature (ii): decision-time stratification, the primary diagnostic

**Proposition SM-B.2 (within-stratum institutional slopes under decision-time stratification).** *Let outcomes be generated by an admissible environment; work in the benchmark $\mathfrak{D}$ for closed forms. By the hazard reading of Definition SM-B.1, the conditional mean of the realized outcome given the decision-time state is $f_e$, so slopes of outcome regressions on decision-time regressors are slopes of $f_e$; the latent-linear implementation writes $y^{*}=\log f_e+\eta$ with $\eta\perp A$ (the ordered-outcome implementation is SM-B.6.5(1)). Stratify on the protocol strata $\mathcal{Q}_0,\mathcal{Q}_1,\dots,\mathcal{Q}_4$ at the decision date, and let $\gamma_k^{q}$ be the within-stratum slope on axis $k$ with the continuous decision-time control retained. Then:*

*(a) (Operative strata are positive.) $\gamma_k^{q}=E\big[\Gamma_k(R;A)\mid\mathcal{Q}_q\big]\ge0$, with $\Gamma_k(R;A)=-\int_R^1\partial\lambda/\partial A_k(u;A)\,du$ the remaining-path response of Lemma SM-B.1(c) — an exact equality for hazards linear in $A_k$, as in both witnesses, and the average-derivative estimand in general; $\gamma_k^{q}>0$ exactly where the axis's response support intersects the stratum's remaining journeys — the institution still to act.*

*(b) (The annihilated stratum collapses.) In the level metric, $\partial f_e/\partial A_k=f_e\,\Gamma_k\le PS\,\bar\Gamma$ with $\bar\Gamma:=\sup\Gamma_k<\infty$, so the institutional slope vanishes uniformly in $A$ as $\min(P,S)\to0$: $\gamma_k^{0}\approx0$ at $\mathcal{Q}_0$, not because the channel is closed but because there is nothing left for it to save (C1′). The annihilated-cell null is a prediction about the level (outcome-probability) metric, which is where the registry estimates it.*

*(c) (SH: slopes vary across strata, with an axis-resolved crossing.) Under stage-local response supports the slope profile is axis-specific: in the SH witness of SM-B.3, the early axis carries $\Gamma_k(R)=\beta\,(\tfrac35-R)^{+}$ — strictly declining across live strata and exactly zero once $R\ge\tfrac35$ — while the late axis carries $\Gamma_{k'}(R)=\beta\,\big(1-\max(R,\tfrac35)\big)$ — flat at $\tfrac{2\beta}{5}$ over early strata, declining only late. Within-stratum slopes on the two axes therefore cross across strata; a stratum-common institutional coefficient is misspecified, and the misspecification has a sign structure.*

*(d) (ED: slopes scale with remaining exposure.) In the ED witness, $\log f_e$ is linear in $A_k$ with coefficient $c\,(1-R)$, so $\gamma_k^{q}=c\,E[1-R\mid\mathcal{Q}_q]$: largest at early live strata, declining monotonically to the top stratum, with the annihilated cell's $\approx0$ breaking the pattern at the bottom. The registered profile across $(\mathcal{Q}_0,\mathcal{Q}_1,\dots,\mathcal{Q}_4)$ is thus $\approx0$, then high, then declining.*

*(e) (Trajectory conditioning: the residual zero.) Conditional on the realized carrier path — the trajectory of $(R_t,S_t,F_t)$ and its $\sigma_{SU}$-exposure up to the outcome horizon — the conditional outcome law is free of $A$: in $\mathfrak{D}$, realized value is $e^{-r\tau}PS_\tau$, a functional of the realized path alone, and $A$ enters only the law of that path (C3′). The residual institutional slope under trajectory conditioning is exactly zero.*

*Proof.* (a) and (d): $A$ enters the closed form of $f_e$ only through $-\int_R^1\lambda(u;A)\,du$, so $\partial\log f_e/\partial A_k=\Gamma_k(R;A)$; averaging over the stratum and using Lemma SM-B.1(c) for the sign. (b): $0\le f_e\le PS$ (Lemma SM-B.1(a)) and the product rule. (c): integrate the witness hazard as in SM-B.3. (e): read off the closed form; in the general class, $A$ appears only in the generator of the carrier process, so conditioning on the realized carrier path exhausts the transmission. $\blacksquare$

*Remark (two regressions, two timestamps: the Section 6 tension dissolved).* Clause (a) and clause (e) are the two regressions Section 6 reports in one paragraph, and they are not in tension — they are the same model read at two timestamps. *Decision-time stratification* conditions on pre-transmission information: the institution's remaining action is ahead of the conditioning set, so its slope is the forward transmission, positive at operative strata. *Trajectory conditioning* conditions on post-transmission information: everything the institution transmits is already recorded in the conditioning set, so the residual slope is zero — and this zero is nothing other than the observational face of channel completeness, the hypothesis SM-B.7 tests; a nonzero residual here is a direct channel there. A reader who collapses the two conditionings will believe the paper's own fingerprint paragraph contradicts signature (ii); the corollary's registered test is the first regression, the C3′ fingerprint is the second, and both are predictions of the same class. Section 6's cross-case paragraph now names each with one clause and points here.

*Remark (mediator status and why the timestamp fixes it).* Decision-time $\mathrm{SPS}$ is downstream of $A$: the nursery acted before the decision date too, so the stratifier is a mediator of the institution's total effect, and $\gamma^{q}$ estimates the *forward* (post-decision) transmission only. Both halves of this are intended. On the estimand: Corollary 3.1 targets the wedge between a venture-uniform institutional index and stage-contingent institutional value, and that wedge is a statement about the forward effect at each stage — SH and ED are forward statements — so the mediator is the *correct* conditioning variable, and the decision-time timestamp is what makes it well defined: the stratifier is measurable strictly before the exposure whose effect is being read, so the design conditions on a pre-exposure covariate of the remaining journey, not on post-outcome information, and pooled-versus-conditional differences are not the ordinary mediation arithmetic of a mistimed control. What the diagnostic does *not* estimate — deliberately — is the total effect of $A$, past transmission included; no clause of the corollary needs it. On the caveat: conditioning on a mediator can open a non-causal path between $a$ and the outcome disturbance if pre-decision unobservables that moved $v$ persist into post-decision hazards; the benchmark of Definition SM-B.2 excludes this by $\varepsilon\perp(v,a)$, and the registry meets its field analogue with pre-decision covariate adjustment and the errors-in-variables strategy of SM-B.6.5(2).*

*Remark (what is distinctive, against generic complementarity).* Garden-variety complementarity — "strong TTOs help good projects more" — predicts institutional slopes *increasing* in venture quality, uniformly across axes. The class predicts the opposite shape on two margins and a crossing on a third: the annihilated cell's collapse (b), ED's *decline* of slopes across live strata (d), and SH's axis-resolved crossing (c) in which early-axis slopes die out across strata while late-axis slopes persist. These, not bare slope inequality, are the registered patterns; bare inequality would reject additivity without distinguishing the class from complementarity, and the protocol scores the profile, not merely the omnibus test.

#### SM-B.6.4 Signature (iii): the slope-equality test, registered form

The registered estimating equation, per institutional regressor and with $j(i)$ the host institution of seed $i$:
$$y_i\;=\;\sum_{q=0}^{4}\mathbf{1}\{Q_i=q\}\big(\alpha_q+\gamma^{q}a_i+\phi_q\,v_i\big)+x_i'\beta+\varepsilon_i,\qquad \varepsilon_i \text{ clustered by } j(i),$$
with $x_i$ pre-decision covariates. The diagnostic is the Wald test — equivalently a Chow-type test of slope equality — of
$$H_0:\ \gamma^{0}=\gamma^{1}=\cdots=\gamma^{4},\qquad
W=(R\hat\gamma)'\big[R\,\hat V\,R'\big]^{-1}(R\hat\gamma)\ \sim\ \chi^2_{4}\ \text{under }H_0,$$
where $R$ stacks the four independent equality restrictions and $\hat V$ is the cluster-robust covariance of $\hat\gamma=(\hat\gamma^{0},\dots,\hat\gamma^{4})'$; clustering at the institution is not optional, since seeds sharing a nursery share its shocks. Equivalently, $W$ is the cluster-robust Wald test that all $\mathrm{ECR}\times\mathrm{SPS}$-stratum interactions vanish. Under Proposition SM-B.2 the noncentrality is carried by the $\Gamma$-profile, and two directional contrasts are registered alongside the omnibus statistic because they carry the class's signed predictions: the annihilated-cell contrast $\gamma^{0}$ versus the pooled operative slopes (clause (b)), and the ED trend across live strata (clause (d)).

*Remark (Hausman contrast, demoted).* The earlier formulation registered a Hausman (1978) statistic contrasting the stratum-common estimator $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{pool}}$ with the stratum-free $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{strat}}$. It tests the same null and adds no identifying content, and it is retained only as a remark, in corrected form: $H=d'\big(\hat V_{\mathrm{strat}}-\hat V_{\mathrm{pool}}\big)^{-}d$ with $d=\hat\theta_{\mathrm{strat}}-\hat\theta_{\mathrm{pool}}$ is asymptotically $\chi^2$ with degrees of freedom equal to the *number of equality restrictions* (here $4$, the rank of the variance contrast) — not $\dim\theta$ — and its variance-difference form presumes the pooled estimator efficient under the null, which pooled OLS is not under the clustered, heteroskedastic error structures registry data will carry; the contrast then need not be positive semi-definite. A pairs-cluster bootstrap of $d$ repairs the size where the contrast form is wanted as a robustness companion. The Wald form above is the same test without the efficiency premise and is the registered primary.

#### SM-B.6.5 Estimation caveats (inherited verbatim by the registry protocol)

**(1) Ordered outcome and the cross-stratum comparison problem.** The registry outcome is a three-class ordered variable, while Propositions SM-B.1–B.2 are derived on a linear (latent-index) link. Ordered logit/probit coefficients are identified only up to stratum-specific residual scale, so equality of index coefficients across strata is not testable from ordered-model coefficients alone: apparent slope instability can be manufactured, and true instability masked, by residual-variance differences across strata (Mood, 2010) [needs S6 verification]. Prescription: primary estimation by linear probability models on the outcome-class indicators — the estimands of the propositions in the level metric, which is also where the annihilated-cell prediction (b) lives — or by the latent-$y^{*}$ formulation with pre-registered equivalence bounds on cross-stratum scale ratios; ordered-model coefficients are reported as secondary descriptives only.

**(2) Generated regressors and errors-in-variables.** The stratifier and the institutional regressor are estimated indices — rubric-scored, calibration-mapped. Classical measurement error in $v$ misclassifies strata and attenuates within-stratum slopes toward zero, an artifact that *mimics* the annihilated-cell null and blurs the cross-stratum profile; error in $a$ attenuates every institutional slope. The protocol therefore requires an errors-in-variables strategy, not a disclaimer: a repeat-scoring reliability subsample (two independent raters, blind to outcomes, with reliability reported), regression-calibration or instrumenting one rater's score with the other's, and full two-step bootstrap of the resulting standard errors. The annihilated-cell null is evidentially meaningful only in the reliability-corrected fit and only *alongside* strictly positive corrected slopes at operative strata: the registered pattern is the contrast, never the null alone.

**(3) Harmonized error rates.** The diagnostic family consists of exactly two registered contrasts — (i)'s pooled-versus-partial sign contrast (Proposition SM-B.1) and the slope-equality Wald test (SM-B.6.4; signatures (ii) and (iii) are one test stated twice) — held at a single family-wise error rate $\alpha=0.05$ by Holm's step-down. The earlier mixture of standards — a $0.95$-posterior rule for (i), Bonferroni $0.05/3$ for the family, a $0.10$ region for the $J$ test — is withdrawn. SM-B.7's $J$ test is a premise check *outside* this family and states its own size there.

**(4) Power, minimum detectable effects, and the equivalence logic of the negative criterion.** Rehabilitating the single-score benchmark is an equivalence claim and is registered as one. The protocol pre-registers a minimum detectable slope difference for the Wald contrast — calibrated from the ED witness at the registry's hazard calibration — and a design power of at least $0.8$ at the family $\alpha$; the negative criterion is available only in samples meeting the requirement. Non-rejection alone never rehabilitates: rehabilitation requires TOST-style equivalence — every pairwise stratum-slope difference within the pre-registered bounds, the pooled-versus-partial contrast within its bound, and SM-B.7's $J$ test failing to reject with instruments passing the registered strength threshold. An underpowered non-rejection is recorded as *uninformative*, not as rehabilitation: the earlier wording rewarded underpower, and this one does not.

**(5) Selection on unobservables.** Proposition SM-B.1(a) makes decision-time stratification robust to selection *on the recorded ledgers*; committees that select on soft signals correlated with post-decision innovations reintroduce bias in every conditioning set. The protocol's mitigations are the instrument audit below (what did the committee actually score?), archived score sheets where obtainable, and the pre-decision covariate set; the residual threat is disclosed in the registry protocol rather than assumed away.

#### SM-B.6.6 Coverage: which instrument families the diagnostics bite

Honesty about targets, demanded by the referee record and owed anyway. A weakly monotone multiplicative committee score — the opening vignette's instrument, $g\cdot h$ flat in the institution exactly at venture death — is excluded as a *value representation* by Theorem 3 under SH/ED; but as a *data-generating practice* it is visible to (i)–(iii) only through the selection channel. Computed but never used to allocate, it leaves no scar these diagnostics can see — outcomes are generated by the class, not by the committee's arithmetic. Used to fund, it is a single monotone score in the sense of Definition SM-B.2(D3) and manufactures signature (i). Its *residual-halo* variant — a direct premium on measured value at fixed trajectory — is outside the class altogether and is the designated target of the Hansen $J$ test (SM-B.7), not of this subsection. The decision matrix:

| Instrument family (how the field scores and funds) | Status under the theory | (i) pooled-sign reversal | (ii)/(iii) slope diagnostics | Hansen $J$ (SM-B.7) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strictly monotone composite (weighted addition; scorecard totals) | fails C1′ at the boundary (Lemma SM-B.2); excluded by Theorem 3 | fires iff it allocates funding (selection channel) | fire wherever SH/ED hold (analyst-side misspecification) | inside the null |
| Weakly monotone single-index composite (pure multiplication, geometric/CES blends, min gates) | excluded by Theorem 3 under SH/ED | fires iff it allocates funding — the *only* channel by which (i)–(iii) see this family | fire wherever SH/ED hold | inside the null |
| Any of the above plus a residual halo (direct premium at fixed trajectory) | outside the class; Theorem 3's premises void | not designed for it | not designed for it | **rejects** — the designated diagnostic |
| Conditional gating (knockout thresholds, then venture-ledger ranking) | not a single score; admissible practice (Section 4) | structurally silent — product selection, Lemma SM-B.3(b) | fire only if the *analyst* imposes a venture-uniform index | inside the null |

The registry's instrument audit records, for every sampled program, which row it occupies — named scoring instruments, documented as strictly monotone composites, weakly monotone composites, or gates. Without that record, neither rejection nor non-rejection of (i) is attributable; with it, row 4's silence is itself informative, since a gated field is one that has already adopted the paper's recommended design.

**Negative criterion (falsifiability commitment, restated).** The single-score benchmark is rehabilitated on the data only under the conjunction of SM-B.6.5(4): equivalence — not mere non-rejection — on both registered contrasts at the family-wise $\alpha$, in a design meeting the pre-registered power floor, together with SM-B.7's channel-completeness test failing to reject under its stated conditions. Any one firing diagnostic supports the empirical content of Theorem 3 within its row of the decision matrix: (ii)/(iii) against the venture-uniform institutional index, (i) against compensatory score-selection as a portfolio practice. Diagnostics firing outside their row indict nothing and are reported as anomalies.

### SM-B.7 The Hansen $J$ test: channel completeness

The diagnostics of SM-B.6 test consequences of the theorem within the maintained class — and, for signature (i), of the field's selection practice given the class; the class structure itself is testable, and the two exercises must not be conflated. C3′ says that institutional axes affect value only through the laws of motion of $(R,S,F)$ and the $\sigma_{SU}$-exposure; a *direct* channel — a brand or halo premium accruing at a fixed venture path — lies outside the class and would void Theorem 3's premises. The tested hypothesis is therefore *channel completeness*: the licensed carriers exhaust the transmission from $A$ to value. Writing $\rho_k^{R},\rho_k^{S},\rho_k^{F},\rho_k^{\sigma}$ for the responses of the four carriers to axis $k$, the moment conditions equate the measured total effect of axis $k$ on the value process with the effect transmitted through the measured carrier responses:
$$E\Big[\Big(\frac{df_e}{dA_k}-\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial R}\,\rho_k^{R}-\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial S}\,\rho_k^{S}-\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial F}\,\rho_k^{F}-\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial \sigma_{SU}}\,\rho_k^{\sigma}\Big)\cdot z\Big]=0,$$
with $z$ a vector of instruments built from shocks to institutional axes external to the venture state (policy shifts to funding or staffing rules used as natural experiments). The bracketed factor is the direct-channel residual — the total institutional effect net of everything transmitted through the four carriers — so the restriction tests completeness of the licensed channel as a whole, not a two-term subset of it: the slack and exposure responses sit inside the null exactly as they sit inside the class (SM-B.1). With $\dim(m)>\dim(\theta)$, where $m$ stacks the moments across axes and instruments and $\theta$ collects the parameters of $(f_e,\rho)$, Hansen's (1982) overidentification statistic
$$J=N\,g_N(\hat\theta)'\,\hat W\,g_N(\hat\theta)\ \sim\ \chi^2_{\dim(m)-\dim(\theta)}$$
is central under channel completeness, with the conservative rejection region $p<0.10$ (loosened because failing to reject is the informative outcome for a premise). Decision matrix: $J$ rejects $\Rightarrow$ a direct institutional channel exists, the environment lies outside the class, and the premises of Theorem 3 are void regardless of the diagnostics; $J$ fails to reject and a diagnostic fires $\Rightarrow$ the class stands and the corresponding indictment holds — signatures (ii)/(iii) against the venture-uniform institutional index, signature (i) against compensatory score-selection; $J$ fails to reject and no diagnostic fires $\Rightarrow$ the negative criterion of SM-B.6 applies, whose rehabilitation of the scalar benchmark requires the pre-registered equivalence and power showings of SM-B.6.5(4), not the bare non-rejection. The $J$ test's rejection region ($p<0.10$, conservative because failing to reject is the informative outcome for a premise) sits outside the SM-B.6 diagnostic family, whose error rates are harmonized there. ### SM-B.8 Certification effects and the channel restriction (expanded)

Section 4 states in three sentences why certification effects do not break C3′; this subsection preserves the full argument as drafted for the main text, unchanged.

The channel restriction must face, rather than wave at, the strongest empirical objection to it. A substantial literature documents *certification effects*: affiliation with prominent partners raises young ventures' survival and speeds their capital acquisition (Stuart, Hoang and Hybels, 1999); entrepreneurs accept discounted terms to obtain high-reputation venture backing (Hsu, 2004); early-stage public grants causally improve subsequent private financing (Howell, 2017) — and every practitioner in our context knows the folk version, the premium a national-university seal commands. Do these findings break C3′? The class answers with a distinction between *price* and *value*. What certification demonstrably moves is the venture's access to resources: capital arrives sooner, on better terms, from better partners. In the model those are trajectory consequences — endorsement and access operate on the slack $F$, the survival hazard, and the exposure to $\sigma_{SU}$ innovations, carriers *inside* the licensed channel, and SM-D routes exactly the axes that carry certification in practice ($A_4$, $A_5$, $A_8$) through them. What the class excludes is only the residual: a premium on measured value at a *fixed* venture trajectory — a halo that changes what a committee writes down without changing what the venture does or survives. That residual is precisely what the Section 7 overidentification test estimates; certification effects that work through trajectories sit inside its null, not against it. And the theorem degrades gracefully if the test rejects: Theorem 3's proof uses only the reversal condition SH, a directly observable property of value orderings, so a documented direct channel would change the class's interpretation — value partly constituted by perception — without rescuing the single score, unless the direct premium were strong and uniform enough to undo every stage-contingent reversal: itself an empirical question, and one the registered program is built to price.

### SM-B.9 Lineage and location of the result

Section 4 compresses the result's genealogy to the Arrow template and the Nelson–Winter antecedent; this subsection preserves the fuller statement of where the theorem does and does not innovate. Arrow (1963) showed that individually reasonable axioms on *preference aggregation across people* are jointly unsatisfiable; Holmström and Milgrom (1991) showed that single-index incentives distort effort across *tasks*; the index-number literature has long known that no single deflator serves all purposes. The boundary lemma's mathematics — annihilation defeats strict separability — is noncompensatory-aggregation territory (Fishburn, 1976; Bouyssou and Marchant, 2007), and the failure of the institutional sector to separate from the venture sector can be phrased in the classical separability vocabulary (Gorman, 1968; Blackorby, Primont and Russell, 1978); SM-B.2 develops both connections, which are therefore only named here. What is new, to our knowledge, is the *location* and the *mechanism* of the impossibility: aggregation across observation levels — the project and the institution that hosts it — defeated not by a normative axiom but by two observable facts about how nurseries act on seeds, inside a value model whose channel structure is itself registered for test (SM-B.7). The nearest antecedents in evolutionary economics (Nelson and Winter, 1982) argued *informally* that selection environments and organizational routines are incommensurable; Theorem 3 is that intuition made exact, with named premises and testable consequences.

### C.0 Primitive problem, funds architecture, and standing assumptions

#### C.0.1 The problem and the financing block

The pair chooses a stopping time $\tau$ (founding) solving

$$\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_g}\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\,e^{-r\tau}\,O(\sigma_\tau,F_\tau,k_\tau)\,\mathbf{1}\{\tau<T_0\}\,\Big|\ \mathcal{F}_0\Big],$$

where $O$ is the *entry payoff* — the entered (post-founding) value $G$ of C.0.4 net of the irreversible founding cost $I>0$, whose financing convention is specified two paragraphs down and is a first-order modeling choice — everything discounted at the founding date (the earlier draft's undiscounted treatment of $I$ is corrected), $T_0=\inf\{t:F_t=0\}$, and $\mathcal{T}_g$ the gate-admissible times of C.4. Entry is one-shot and irreversible: before day zero the pair holds an option on a venture, not a venture (McDonald and Siegel 1986; McGrath 1997).

**Funds law (pre-entry).** The financing constraint is a stock constraint on the funds process, not a flow inequality:
$$dF_t=(R_{\mathrm{net}}-B)\,dt,\qquad F_t\ge 0,\qquad F=0\ \text{absorbing (forced abandonment; the option dies)},$$
with $B$ the constant burn parameter and $R_{\mathrm{net}}$ the pair's self-generated net revenue rate. The main text's displayed constraint is patched accordingly (see `MAIN_EDITS.md`): the object solved is $F_t\ge 0$ under this funds law.

**The deterministic clock.** In the empirically relevant deep-tech case $R_{\mathrm{net}}<B$, the funds law makes $F$ a *deterministic clock*: $F_t=F_0-(B-R_{\mathrm{net}})t$, so the pre-entry problem is a finite-horizon, time-inhomogeneous stopping problem with deadline $T_0=F_0/(B-R_{\mathrm{net}})$ — an American option with expiry, not a perpetual one. We own this structure throughout: Theorem 4 is stated for the genuinely two-dimensional problem and delivers a free-boundary *curve* $\theta^*_\sigma(k;F)$, with the stationary case $R_{\mathrm{net}}=B$ (funds frozen) retained as the classical benchmark (C.2.1). The knife-edge nature of the benchmark is stated, not hidden.

**How the founding cost is financed: two conventions, both carried.** The earlier draft treated the financing of $I$ as a detail; the executed numerics (C.5) prove it is *first-order* — thresholds, $F$-slopes, and the deadline limit all change between the two natural conventions, so we state both and flag every convention-dependent claim.

*(E) External financing (display baseline).* $I$ is paid at entry by committed outside money; the funds stock passes through unchanged: $O^{E}(\sigma,F,k)=G(\sigma,F,k)-I$. This is the convention of the earlier draft's value-matching and of Figure 3's baseline. Its deadline behavior: below $F^{E}_{\min}(k):=\inf\{F:\sup_\sigma G(\sigma,F,k)\ge I\}$ the obstacle is dead ($G<I$ everywhere) and the $F\downarrow$ limit is **forced non-entry** — the earlier "forced entry or exit" heuristic is withdrawn for this convention.

*(J) Runway-funded founding (entry-time jump).* $I$ is paid *from the earmarked stock*: entry at $\tau$ requires $F_\tau\ge I$ and the funds jump to $F_\tau-I$, so $O^{J}(\sigma,F,k)=G(\sigma,F-I,k)$ on $F\ge I$ — the cost is the jump itself, not a second subtraction. Its deadline behavior is different in kind: just above $F=I$ the post-jump venture is short-lived but *free at the margin* (unspent earmarked money dies anyway), so the rule collapses to "enter iff the flow is positive" — the threshold falls to the **Marshallian flow root** $\pi=0$ at the deadline, the purest Boyle–Guthrie forced-early-exercise effect; below $F=I$ the option dies worthless. The old heuristic's "forced entry" branch lives in *this* world, now derived rather than asserted.

The two conventions bracket institutional reality (external founding finance versus gap-fund-paid founding transactions); C.3(iii) derives the funds comparative static under each, and they differ *qualitatively* — under (E) the threshold curve is dip-shaped in $F$, under (J) it is increasing throughout (C.5). Results below are stated for (E) unless flagged; every (J) counterpart is reported alongside. The choice is declared per application, not hidden in a value-matching display.

**Earmarked, non-fungible pre-entry funds (interpretation).** The objective books no payoff flow while waiting and assigns no terminal value to unspent $F$: pre-entry funds are *earmarked, use-it-or-lose-it* money — gap funds, fellowships, laboratory residence budgets — legally restricted to the pre-founding preparation of this project, neither bankable by the founders nor transferable to post-entry operations except — under convention (J) — through the founding transaction itself, in which the founding cost $I$ (incorporation, licensing execution, initial capitalization) is paid out of the remaining stock. This is the institutional reality of the pre-founding stage the paper models, and we state it as the maintained reading rather than leave it implicit. Waiting is therefore *not costless*: it generates no objective flow, but it burns the earmarked stock at rate $B-R_{\mathrm{net}}$ and thereby shortens the option's life — the deadline channel of C.3(iii).

**Funds law (post-entry) and which $F$ the entered value runs on.** The entered venture runs on its own funds process, initialized at entry — at $F_\tau$ under (E), at $F_\tau-I$ under (J):
$$dF^{\mathrm{post}}_t=(R_{\mathrm{net}}-B)\,dt,\qquad F^{\mathrm{post}}=0\ \text{absorbing (forced exit of the entered venture; all flows cease)},$$
with no revenue feedback into the stock beyond the constant $R_{\mathrm{net}}$ — the literal earmarked-funds reading, under which post-entry life is itself deterministic, $T^{\mathrm{post}}_0=F^{\mathrm{post}}_0/(B-R_{\mathrm{net}})$. This displayed equation closes the former (A6) gap: the post-entry drift is now separately specified, and it is the *same* funds law — the paper's declared baseline. Letting the phases differ ($R^{\mathrm{pre}}_{\mathrm{net}}\ne R^{\mathrm{post}}_{\mathrm{net}}$) changes only the constants in the respective transport terms and adds the correction term noted after the transversality display in C.2.2; the decoupled variant is used *only* as a disclosed outside-region exhibit (C.5, Diagnostic E). Post-entry, $R_{\mathrm{net}}$ includes project revenue; pre-entry it is the pair's contract-research and licensing income. Under (J), earmarked pre-entry money reaches the venture only through the founding transaction — exactly what the jump encodes.

**Survival accounting (no double-counted mortality).** Throughout the paper, $S$ is the *non-financing* survival probability: it aggregates technical, team, and market mortality, and *excludes* funding exhaustion, which the model prices separately and dynamically as the $F=0$ absorbing barrier. With this convention the flow payoff $P\cdot R\cdot S$ and the barrier are mutually consistent — each death channel is counted once. The corresponding one-line patch to the §3 definition of $S$ is in `MAIN_EDITS.md`.

#### C.0.2 State variables and the measurement-to-model interface

**(i) Macro alignment.** The constructed index of Section 3 is bounded by construction: each momentum is mapped to a bounded scale (SM-D.3), so $\sigma_{SU}\in(-1,\bar\sigma)$ with the lower bound from the $-1$ shift. The dynamic model runs on a fixed strictly increasing transform $\tilde\sigma=\psi(\sigma_{SU})$, $\psi:(-1,\bar\sigma)\to\mathbb{R}$ (logit-type; the synthetic exercise of C.5 works directly on this scale), and it is $\tilde\sigma$ that follows the regime-modulated *mean-reverting* diffusion
$$d\tilde\sigma_t=\kappa\,\big(m_{k_t}-\tilde\sigma_t\big)\,dt+s_{k_t}\,dW_t,\qquad m_{S_0}<m_{S_1}<m_{S_2},$$
with $k_t\in\{S_0,S_1,S_2\}$ (dormant, warming, aligned) an independent continuous-time Markov chain, $\kappa>0$ a common reversion speed, $m_k$ the regime-dependent alignment *levels*, and $s_k>0$ the regime loadings. This replaces the earlier draft's constant per-regime drifts $b_k$: a constant-drift specification is non-stationary ($\sigma\to\pm\infty$ a.s.) and incoherent with the boundary data its own numerical scheme imposes — the executed C.5 forced the repair, and the regime structure is economically a statement about *levels* of alignment in any case, so the OU form is the honest reading, not a strengthening. Because $\psi$ is strictly increasing, thresholds transform monotonically and every statement below about $\tilde\sigma$-thresholds is a statement about $\sigma_{SU}$-thresholds; a threshold escaping to the edge of the working range is a threshold at the attainable ceiling on the measurement scale — the reading "founding is never optimal in regime $k$ at that runway," which is economically meaningful, feeds Definition C.1 (NO_GO), and is *realized* (not hypothetical): the C.5 corner exhibits produce exactly such boundary thresholds outside the interior region. We henceforth write $\sigma$ for the transformed state and restate interiority *relative to the attainable range* in Theorem 4; the previous draft's unbounded-state interiority claim is withdrawn as such. This resolves the boundedness mismatch flagged by the referee (M10(i)) without changing the measurement layer.

**(ii) Regime process, generator notation (units fixed).** The chain's generator is defined by uniformization at a stated rate: $Q:=\lambda_u(\Pi-\mathrm{Id})$ with $\Pi=(\pi_{kj})$ row-stochastic and $\lambda_u=1/\text{yr}$, so that the off-diagonal $q_{kj}=\lambda_u\pi_{kj}$ are per-year switching intensities, the exit intensity of regime $k$ is $\lambda_k=\lambda_u(1-\pi_{kk})$ (expected sojourn $1/\lambda_k$ years), and $\pi_{kk}$ enters only through row-stochasticity — this fixes the units the earlier draft left ambiguous ("probability per what?"): $\pi_{kk}$ is dimensionless, its persistence content is the sojourn $1/\lambda_u(1-\pi_{kk})$. The switching operator is $(\mathcal{Q}\varphi)(k)=\lambda_u\sum_{j\ne k}\pi_{kj}[\varphi(j)-\varphi(k)]$. Where the main text speaks of "regime persistence $\pi_{kk}$," the formal perturbation is: raise $\pi_{kk}$ with the off-diagonal mass rescaled proportionally (conditional jump distribution fixed) — the scheme is now part of the statement of Proposition 2(iv). The natural macro structure is nearest-neighbor-dominant (warming and cooling pass through $S_1$); the proofs that need it assume the *birth–death form* $\pi_{S_0S_2}=\pi_{S_2S_0}=0$ as part of (ROF) below, while the numerics carry small corner entries and confirm the conclusions are not artifacts of exact tridiagonality.

**(iii) Technology gate.** $g_{TRL}(t)\in\{0,1\}$, deterministic, exogenous and non-decreasing; it restricts admissible stopping to $\mathcal{T}_g=\{\tau: g_{TRL}(\tau)=1\}$. C.1–C.3 condition on an open gate; C.4 states, defends and delimits the exogeneity assumption and restores the gate.

**Flow payoff.** $\pi(\sigma,k)=P\cdot R(\sigma,k)\cdot S(\sigma,k)-B$, with $R$ (realization readiness) and $S$ (non-financing survival) the upstream constructs of Section 3, treated as primitive functions with the regularity below. $\pi$ does not depend on $F$: funds affect value through the funds law and barrier, not through the flow.

#### C.0.4 The entered value $G$

$G(\sigma,f,k)=\mathbb{E}\big[\int_0^{T^{\mathrm{post}}_0}e^{-rt}\,\pi(\sigma_t,k_t)\,dt\ \big|\ \sigma_0=\sigma,F^{\mathrm{post}}_0=f,k_0=k\big]$, with $T^{\mathrm{post}}_0$ the post-entry funds exhaustion time. $G$ solves the weakly coupled linear system
$$\mathcal{L}_kG+\pi-rG=0,\qquad G(\sigma,0,k)=0,$$
where for functions $\varphi(\cdot,\cdot,k)$
$$(\mathcal{L}_k\varphi)(\sigma,f)=\kappa(m_k-\sigma)\,\varphi_\sigma+\tfrac12 s_k^2\varphi_{\sigma\sigma}+(R_{\mathrm{net}}-B)\,\varphi_f+\lambda_u\sum_{j\ne k}\pi_{kj}\big[\varphi(\sigma,f,j)-\varphi(\sigma,f,k)\big].$$
Boundary and asymptotic behavior (completing the former omission): $|G|\le\frac{\sup|\pi|}{r}\big(1-e^{-rT^{\mathrm{post}}_0}\big)$, so $G$ is bounded; by (A1-m) and the saturation of the response functions, $G(\sigma,f,k)\to G^{\pm}(f,k)$ monotonically at the ends of the working range, with $G_\sigma\to0$ there (the numerical scheme implements this as zero-flux $\sigma$-boundaries; C.5), $G^{-}(f,k)\le0$ (flow tends to its infimum $\ge-B$) and $G^{+}(f,k)\le\frac{P\bar R\bar S-B}{r}$, the perpetual bound at saturated readiness. These limits are the envelopes used for $V$'s boundary conditions in C.1 and for the numerical boundary data in C.5. The entry payoff per financing convention (C.0.1) is
$$O^{E}(\sigma,F,k):=G(\sigma,F,k)-I;\qquad O^{J}(\sigma,F,k):=G(\sigma,F-I,k)\ \text{ on }F\ge I,\ \text{entry infeasible on }F<I,$$
with feasibility floors $F^{E}_{\min}(k)=\inf\{F:\sup_\sigma G(\sigma,F,k)\ge I\}$ (obstacle death: forced non-entry below) and $F^{J}_{\min}=I$ (option death below; just above it, entry at the flow root $\sigma^0_M(k)$ — the deadline-exercise limit). We write $O$ for the convention in force, (E) unless flagged.

### C.1 Well-posedness and structure of the value function

Waiting generates no payoff flow in the objective — though it is not costless: it consumes the earmarked funds stock and with it the option's remaining life (C.0.1). Never entering yields zero, so with the entry payoff $O$ of C.0.4 the pre-entry value is
$$V(\sigma,F,k)=\sup_{\tau}\ \mathbb{E}\big[e^{-r\tau}\,O(\sigma_\tau,F_\tau,k_\tau)\,\mathbf{1}\{\tau<T_0\}\,\mathbf{1}\{(F_\tau,\tau)\ \text{feasible}\}\big]\ \vee\ 0,$$
feasibility being vacuous under (E) and $F_\tau\ge I$ under (J). Dynamic programming gives the weakly coupled variational inequality (obstacle problem)
$$\max\big\{\ \mathcal{L}_kV-rV,\ \ O-V\ \big\}=0\ \ \text{on the feasible region},\qquad \mathcal{L}_kV-rV=0\ \ \text{on the infeasible region (under (J): }0<F<I),$$
for $k\in\{S_0,S_1,S_2\}$, with $V(\sigma,0,k)=0$, $V\ge 0$, $V\to0$ at the lower end of the working range, and $V$ squeezed against the entered-value envelope $\max(O,0)$ at the upper end (the $\max(\cdot,0)$ matters: at funds levels where the entry region is empty the entered branch alone is the wrong boundary datum — a repair forced by the executed numerics, C.5). The pre-entry transport term $(R_{\mathrm{net}}-B)V_F$ makes the problem genuinely two-dimensional — equivalently, time-inhomogeneous with $F$ as the clock; nothing below reduces it to $\sigma$-sections.

**Lemma C.1 (well-posedness and structure).** *Under (A1)–(A6): (a) $V$ exists, is bounded and Lipschitz in $(\sigma,F)$; (b) $V$ is the unique bounded viscosity solution of the system above with the stated boundary data; (c) $V$ is non-decreasing in $\sigma$ and in $F$; (d) under (ROF) with common diffusion loading $s_k\equiv s$, $V(\cdot,\cdot,S_2)\ge V(\cdot,\cdot,S_1)\ge V(\cdot,\cdot,S_0)$.*

*Proof.* (a) Boundedness of $\pi$ and $r>0$ bound $G$, hence $O$, hence $V$; Lipschitz data propagate through the flow map of the (Lipschitz) state dynamics in the standard way. (b) The strong Markov property yields the dynamic programming principle, hence the viscosity sub- and supersolution properties (Crandall, Ishii and Lions 1992). The switching operator $\mathcal{Q}$ is a monotone coupling, so the system is *weakly coupled* in the standard sense, and the comparison principle for weakly coupled second-order systems applies: Ishii and Koike (1991) establish comparison and uniqueness for exactly this class (monotone systems of second-order elliptic PDEs); the regime-switching control setting, including obstacle problems of the present form, is treated in textbook form by Pham (2009, ch. 4–5). The former deferral (C-G3) is closed by these citations: growth is not an issue ($V$ bounded), and the degenerate transport direction $F$ is covered by the viscosity framework, which never requires non-degeneracy in $F$. Comparison gives uniqueness among bounded solutions. (c) Monotonicity in $\sigma$: couple two copies of $(\sigma,k)$ from initial points $\sigma\le\sigma'$ with the same Brownian path and the same regime path; under the common-$\kappa$ OU dynamics the gap contracts but never crosses, $\sigma'_t-\sigma_t=(\sigma'-\sigma)e^{-\kappa t}\ge0$ when the loadings coincide along the (common) regime path, and remains non-negative in general because it solves $d(\sigma'-\sigma)=-\kappa(\sigma'-\sigma)dt$; $\pi$ and hence $G$ and $O$ are non-decreasing in $\sigma$ by (A1-m), and the supremum of pathwise-ordered payoffs is ordered. Monotonicity in $F$: larger $F$ postpones $T_0$ and raises $F_\tau$ pathwise (the clock is deterministic), $O$ is non-decreasing in $F$ ($G$ non-decreasing in its funds argument: longer post-entry life; under (J) the feasibility indicator also relaxes), and the admissible set of stopping times enlarges. (d) Deferred two paragraphs down, after the representation that makes it a four-line coupling argument. $\square$

**Remark C.1 (normalization translation — written out, closing C-G2 — and the waiting-premium representation).** The earlier draft cited, without spelling out, a "translated normalization" of the HJB. Here is the translation, and it does real work below. Let $W:=V-O$ on the feasible region (the waiting premium). On the continuation region $\mathcal{L}_kV-rV=0$; and by Feynman–Kac on the entered system (C.0.4), the entry payoff satisfies, when pre- and post-entry funds drifts agree (C.0.1),
$$rO-\mathcal{L}_kO\;=\;c-\pi(\sigma,k)\ \text{ with the sign convention below},\qquad c:=\begin{cases} rI & \text{convention (E): } O=G-I\\ 0 & \text{convention (J): } O=G(\cdot,F-I,\cdot)\end{cases}$$
— precisely, $rO-\mathcal{L}_kO=\pi-c$ with $c=rI$ under (E) (the constant $-I$ contributes $-rI$ through $rO$ and vanishes in derivatives and switching differences) and $c=0$ under (J) (the shift $F\mapsto F-I$ is transparent to $\mathcal{L}_k$ because the transport coefficient is the same constant; if the phase drifts differ, add the correction $(d^{\mathrm{post}}-d^{\mathrm{pre}})G_f$). Subtracting, the premium solves
$$\max\big\{\ \mathcal{L}_kW-rW+\big(c-\pi(\sigma,k)\big),\ -W\ \big\}=0,$$
the "flow normalization": the stopping set is $\{W=0\}$ and the running flow is $c-\pi$. The earlier draft's translated form omitted the $-rI$ term of convention (E); the omission is corrected. Dynkin's formula on $O$ gives the equivalent stochastic representation
$$W(\sigma,F,k)\;=\;\sup_{\tau}\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\int_0^{\tau}e^{-rt}\big(c-\pi(\sigma_t,k_t)\big)\,dt\Big]\qquad(\ast)$$
up to the boundary layer near funds exhaustion (where the truncation adds a boundary term; the comparisons below use $(\ast)$ on the interior region and are preserved under the truncation, which is monotone in the same direction). $(\ast)$ is the early-exercise-premium representation adapted to entry: *the value of waiting is the optimally stopped accumulation of the shortfall of flow relative to the carry $c$ on the founding cost*. It makes four things transparent. The Marshallian roots of (A5) are where the integrand changes sign — $\sigma^I_M$ under (E), $\sigma^0_M$ under (J). Monotone couplings act on $W$ through the single scalar $c-\pi(\sigma_t,k_t)$. Every comparative static below is a statement about how a perturbation tilts that integrand or the admissible horizon. And the financing conventions differ in the premium source by exactly the carry term: under external financing waiting *saves interest on $I$*, under runway-funded founding there is no carry to save (unspent earmarked money dies anyway) — the intuition behind the executed numerics' finding that the (J)-thresholds sit uniformly below the (E)-thresholds and collapse to the flow root at the deadline (C.5).

*Proof of Lemma C.1(d).* Start two copies at regimes $k\le k'$, same $\sigma_0,F_0$, same Brownian path, regimes coupled monotonically — possible by (ROF)(iii), birth–death generators being stochastically monotone — so $k_t\le k'_t$ for all $t$. With $s_k\equiv s$ the Brownian terms cancel in the gap, which solves $d(\sigma'_t-\sigma_t)=\kappa\big(m_{k'_t}-m_{k_t}\big)dt-\kappa(\sigma'_t-\sigma_t)\,dt$: whenever the gap touches zero its drift is $\kappa(m_{k'}-m_k)\ge0$ by (ROF)(ii), so $\sigma'_t\ge\sigma_t$ for all $t$; the funds clock is common. Then $\pi(\sigma'_t,k'_t)\ge\pi(\sigma_t,k_t)$ by (ROF)(i) and (A1-m), so the integrand in $(\ast)$ is pointwise smaller for the primed copy at every $\tau$: $W(\sigma,F,k')\le W(\sigma,F,k)$. Since also $O(\cdot,k')\ge O(\cdot,k)$ (same coupling on the entered system), $V=O+W$ is ordered as claimed. With regime-dependent $s_k$ the pathwise coupling fails and the ordering is *not* a theorem — a sufficiently volatile aligned regime can carry a larger waiting premium than a warming one, which is economically real, not a technicality — so (d) is stated under the common-loading clause and checked numerically otherwise (C.5(iv), Figure 3 panel a). $\square$

**Lemma C.3 (sufficient condition for single crossing).** *Under (A1)–(A6) with common diffusion loading $s_k\equiv s$, the waiting premium $W$ is non-increasing in $\sigma$; hence for every $(k,F)$ the entry set $\mathcal{E}_k(F)=\{W=0\}$ is an up-set in $\sigma$, i.e. (SC) holds.*

*Proof.* Fix $\sigma\le\sigma'$ and couple with the same Brownian path and the same regime path; with a common reversion speed and common loading, the gap is deterministic and contracting, $\sigma'_t-\sigma_t=(\sigma'-\sigma)e^{-\kappa t}\ge0$ — order-preserving for all $t$. The funds clock and feasibility truncation are identical. By (A1-m), $c-\pi(\sigma'_t,k_t)\le c-\pi(\sigma_t,k_t)$ pointwise, so for every stopping time the integrand in $(\ast)$ is smaller for the primed copy; taking suprema, $W(\sigma',F,k)\le W(\sigma,F,k)$. Since $W\ge0$ and continuous, $W(\sigma,F,k)=0$ implies $W(\sigma',F,k)=0$ for all $\sigma'\ge\sigma$. The argument is convention-uniform ($c\in\{rI,0\}$). $\square$

*Discussion (when (SC) can fail, and how it is monitored).* Two ingredients of the proof are substantive. First, the *order-preserving coupling*: it survives mean reversion at a common speed $\kappa$ (the gap contracts but never crosses), but a regime-dependent reversion speed $\kappa_k$ would break it. Second, *common loading* $s_k\equiv s$: with regime-dependent $s_k$ the coupling fails, and the referee's disconnection scenario becomes live — where $\pi(\cdot,S_0)$ has long flat segments (dormant-regime readiness insensitive to alignment, which is economically natural), entry in $S_0$ is driven almost entirely by the switching term, $V$ and $O$ can run nearly parallel over intervals, and multiple tangencies are possible exactly as in the regime-switching stopping literature, where threshold geometry is proved, not assumed (Zhang and Guo 2004; Guo, Miao and Morellec 2005; Jobert and Rogers 2006). Accordingly: (SC) is *proved* under common loading; under heterogeneous loading it enters Theorem 4 as a named assumption, and the numerical protocol carries a registered up-set diagnostic — C.5(iv) — that scans the computed entry set for interior gaps at every $(k,F)$, because the naive threshold extractor (lowest crossing) would silently mask a disconnected stopping set. The executed diagnostic reports zero violations across every disclosed parameterization, including both financing conventions and the outside-region exhibits — support, not proof, and labeled as such. This closes C-G5 in the promote-and-diagnose mode the referee offered.

### C.2 Theorem 4: the entry boundary

The earlier draft proved a one-dimensional theorem about a two-dimensional model: its Step 4 reduced the continuation system to $\sigma$-sections "at each runway level," a reduction valid only in the knife-edge case $R_{\mathrm{net}}=B$, and its constant-counting ignored the interface structure of ordered regime boundaries. Both defects are repaired by restructuring: C.2.1 states the stationary case as a *benchmark* in which the classical value-matching/smooth-pasting display is exact, with the interface bookkeeping done by citation to the literature that does it correctly; C.2.2 states and proves Theorem 4 for the honest time-inhomogeneous problem, where the object delivered is a free-boundary *curve* $\theta^*_\sigma(k;F)$, under the named assumptions (SC) and (GEN) carried explicitly in the statement.

#### C.2.1 The stationary benchmark ($R_{\mathrm{net}}=B$): classical smooth fit, exact

When self-generated revenue exactly covers burn, $F$ freezes: the funds state is a fixed parameter, the two financing conventions coincide up to the constant $I$, the problem is one-dimensional in $\sigma$ per regime, and the classical apparatus applies verbatim. On the continuation region of regime $k$,
$$\tfrac12 s_k^2 V''_k+\kappa(m_k-\sigma)V'_k+\lambda_u\textstyle\sum_{j\ne k}\pi_{kj}(V_j-V_k)-rV_k=0,$$
and at a regime-$k$ boundary point $\theta^*_k$,
$$V_k(\theta^*_k)=G_k(\theta^*_k)-I,\qquad V'_k(\theta^*_k)=G'_k(\theta^*_k)\qquad\text{(value matching, smooth pasting)},$$
with $G$ the (funds-inert) perpetual regime-switching annuity. The perpetual thresholds $\theta_\infty(k)$ computed from this benchmark are the right-edge anchors of the threshold curves in Figure 3 (C.5). This display — the one the main text paraphrases — is exact here, and we label it as the benchmark, not the theorem. On the counting of conditions: with ordered thresholds $\theta^*(S_0)>\theta^*(S_1)>\theta^*(S_2)$ the state space per $\sigma$-line decomposes into intervals on which different subsets of regimes are stopped; on $(\theta^*(S_2),\theta^*(S_1))$, regime $S_2$ is stopped and its value $V_{S_2}=G_{S_2}-I$ enters the continuing regimes' equations as a *source term*; on $(\theta^*(S_1),\theta^*(S_0))$ the active set changes again; each continuing component therefore has a different general solution on each interval, and the additional constants are pinned by $C^0/C^1$ interface conditions at the *other* regimes' boundaries. The correct bookkeeping for exactly this structure is carried out for the two-regime case by Guo, Miao and Morellec (2005), whose construction — stopped regimes acting as sources on the continuation intervals of the still-active regimes, with smooth fit imposed interface by interface — extends mechanically (though tediously) to $K=3$ with the birth–death generator of (A3). We adopt their accounting by citation and do not re-run the earlier draft's "three constants plus three boundaries against six pasting conditions" count, which omitted the interface layer and was wrong even in the benchmark. Closed forms per interval are exponential-polynomial in the roots of the coupled characteristic system (Zhang and Guo 2004 give the closed-form machinery for the two-regime perpetual put; Jobert and Rogers 2006 the general Markov-modulated computation scheme); the benchmark's role in this paper is to anchor the numerics (C.5 degenerate-limit and benchmark checks) and the decomposition of Remark C.2, not to carry the theorem.

#### C.2.2 The general problem: a free-boundary curve

**Theorem 4 (restated).** *Under (A1)–(A6):*

*(i) (Well-posedness.) The pre-entry value $V$ exists and is the unique bounded viscosity solution of the weakly coupled obstacle system of C.1 (Lemma C.1(a,b); Ishii and Koike 1991; Pham 2009).*

*(ii) (Threshold curve.) Under (SC) — which holds by Lemma C.3 when $s_k\equiv s$, and is otherwise assumed and monitored by the diagnostic C.5(iv) — for each regime $k$ and feasible funds level $F>F_{\min}(k)$ the entry set is an up-set and*
$$\theta^*_\sigma(k;F):=\inf\{\sigma:\ V(\sigma,F,k)=O(\sigma,F,k)\}$$
*is a well-defined free-boundary curve; the optimal policy is the curve rule*
$$\mathrm{GO}(t,i)=\mathbf{1}\big[\sigma_{SU}(t)\ge\theta^*_\sigma(k_t;F_t)\big]\cdot g_{TRL}(t).$$
*The funds floor is convention-specific (C.0.4): under (E), $F_{\min}^{E}(k)=\inf\{F:\sup_\sigma G\ge I\}$ and the entry set is empty below it (forced non-entry); under (J), $F^{J}_{\min}=I$ and as $F\downarrow I$ the curve collapses to the Marshallian flow root $\sigma^0_M(k)$ (deadline exercise), the option dying worthless below.*

*(iii) (Regularity in the generic case.) At any boundary point where the strict transversality condition (GEN) below holds, the curve is locally unique and locally $C^1$ in $F$ and in the primitives $(P,B,R_{\mathrm{net}},I,r,\kappa,m,s,\Pi,\lambda_u)$; on the attainable range of the measurement scale, $\theta^*_\sigma(k;F)\in(-1,\bar\sigma\,]$, with the boundary value $\theta^*=\bar\sigma$ carrying the reading "founding is never optimal in regime $k$ at funds level $F$" (Definition C.1).*

*Proof.* (i) is Lemma C.1. (ii): under (SC) the entry set per $(k,F)$ is an up-set; it is closed by continuity of $V-O$; hence it is a closed ray and its infimum is well defined whenever non-empty. Non-emptiness for $F$ large under (A5): for $\sigma$ above the relevant Marshallian root the premium's integrand in $(\ast)$ is negative and, at the upper end of the working range, $W\to0$ against the envelope (C.1 boundary data) while $O$ tends to a strictly positive entered value for large funds by (A5); so the entry set is non-empty and proper, and the rule $\mathbf{1}[\sigma\ge\theta^*]$ is the optimal stopping rule by the standard verification: the pasted candidate is a viscosity solution by construction, and uniqueness (i) identifies it with $V$; alternatively, on smooth pieces the classical Itô verification applies (Peskir and Shiryaev 2006, ch. IV; the supermartingale/martingale argument of the earlier draft, unchanged in substance). Below the funds floor: under (E), $O<0$ everywhere (the entered value cannot repay $I$), never entering dominates, and the entry set is empty — forced non-entry; under (J), entry is infeasible below $I$, while just above $I$ the near-costless marginal founding (Remark C.1: no carry) drives the boundary down to the flow root $\sigma^0_M(k)$ — deadline exercise. Together these replace the earlier draft's "forced entry or exit" heuristic with the two derived limits, one per financing convention; C.5 computes both.

(iii): smooth fit in $\sigma$ holds at any boundary point with $s_k>0$: the boundary is regular for the $\sigma$-diffusion in the direction of the diffusion, and $C^1$-fit across free boundaries of regime-switching obstacle problems under non-degenerate diffusion is established in the regime-switching stopping literature (Zhang and Guo 2004 for the two-regime closed form; Peskir and Shiryaev 2006, ch. IV.9, for the general smooth-fit principle at regular boundary points) — this closes C-G6 by citation; the earlier draft's perturbation heuristic (Dixit and Pindyck 1994) is retained as intuition only. Differentiating value matching along the curve and combining with $\sigma$-smooth fit gives $C^1$-fit in $F$ along the curve wherever the curve is locally Lipschitz in $F$ (the tangential identity $V_F-O_F=(O_\sigma-V_\sigma)\,\theta^{*\prime}=0$). For local uniqueness and smoothness, write $D:=V-O$ and evaluate the continuation equation at the boundary from the continuation side, using $D=D_\sigma=0$ and $D_F=0$ there:
$$\tfrac12 s_k^2\,\big(V_{\sigma\sigma}-O_{\sigma\sigma}\big)\Big|_{\theta^*(k;F)}\;=\;\big(\pi(\theta^*(k;F),k)-c\big)\;-\;\lambda_u\sum_{j\ne k}\pi_{kj}\,D\big(\theta^*(k;F),F,j\big),$$
with $c$ the convention carry of Remark C.1 ($rI$ under (E), $0$ under (J)). **(GEN)** is the requirement that the right side be strictly positive:
$$\pi\big(\theta^*(k;F),k\big)-c\;>\;\lambda_u\sum_{j\ne k}\pi_{kj}\,\big[V-O\big]\big(\theta^*(k;F),F,j\big)\;\ge\;0 ,$$
*the flow premium at the boundary strictly exceeds the switching-weighted waiting premia of the other regimes at that point.* (If pre- and post-entry funds drifts differ, add $(d^{\mathrm{post}}-d^{\mathrm{pre}})G_f$ to the left side; C.0.1.) Under (GEN) the contact is strictly second-order ($V_{\sigma\sigma}>O_{\sigma\sigma}$ at the boundary): the pasting system for the boundary point at each $(k,F)$ has a non-vanishing derivative in $\theta$, and the implicit function theorem applied to $D_\sigma(\theta^*(k;F),F,k)=0$ — the equation whose regular zero the boundary is, given $D=0$ and $D_{\sigma\sigma}>0$ — yields local uniqueness of the crossing and local $C^1$ dependence of $\theta^*(k;F)$ on $F$ and on the primitives, which enter $D$ continuously by Lemma C.1. (SC) upgrades local to global uniqueness of the *threshold* (there is only one crossing to be locally unique about); without (SC), (GEN) still makes each connected boundary component locally smooth, but the policy is then a union of intervals and the theorem's threshold reading fails — which is exactly why (SC) is carried in the statement.

Three honesty notes on (GEN). First, it is checkable: both sides are computable from the solution, and the numerical protocol evaluates the display at every reported boundary point (C.5, diagnostics). Second, it is economically readable: stopping is strictly justified by flow at the margin, net of what waiting for a regime switch is still worth; it fails when switching prospects alone carry the entry decision (fast switching out of a regime whose own flow premium at the boundary is nil) — the same corner where (SC) is fragile. Third, it is claimed *generically*, not universally: we exhibit the sufficient condition and verify it numerically where used; we do not claim to have verified it over the whole primitive space. In the no-switching limit ($\pi_{kj}\to0$, $j\ne k$) it reduces to $\pi(\theta^*)>c$ — under (E), the classical strict-transversality condition $\pi(\theta^*)>rI$ of the entry problem, which holds there because the perpetual threshold strictly exceeds the carry root (Dixit and Pindyck 1994); under (J), simply $\pi(\theta^*)>0$, strict flow positivity at the boundary, consistent with the deadline collapse to $\sigma^0_M$. $\square$

**Remark C.2 (decomposition, and the complete argument list).** The threshold curve depends on every primitive in the problem; we write it henceforth as
$$\theta^*_\sigma(k;F)=\Theta_k\big(F;\ P,\ B,\ R_{\mathrm{net}},\ I,\ r,\ \kappa,\ m,\ s,\ \Pi,\ \lambda_u;\ R(\cdot,\cdot),\ S(\cdot,\cdot);\ \text{convention (E)/(J)}\big),$$
correcting the earlier truncated list $\Theta_k(P,F,B,\Pi)$, which hid $r$, $I$, $R_{\mathrm{net}}$, the diffusion parameters, the response functions, and the financing convention. In the stationary benchmark, $\theta^*$ separates conceptually into (a) the Marshallian component — anchored by the roots of (A5); (b) a Dixit–Pindyck uncertainty premium increasing in the regime-$k$ loading $s_k^2$; (c) an irreversibility premium scaling with $I$ and degenerate at $I=0$ (Bertola 1998); (d) a regime-switching option term determined by $(\Pi,\lambda_u)$ — switching toward better regimes raises the value of waiting in bad ones (Guo, Miao and Morellec 2005). In the single-regime limit with the diffusion specialized to the geometric Brownian benchmark, the threshold collapses to the McDonald and Siegel (1986)/Dixit and Pindyck (1994) closed form — retained as numerical diagnostic C.5(ii), run in a dedicated check-world. Component (c) makes the founding cost a *fifth policy lever*: $\partial\theta^*/\partial I>0$ on the region of C.3 — under (E) because the premium source $rI-\pi$ rises pointwise while the obstacle falls; under (J) because the obstacle $G(\cdot,F-I,\cdot)$ falls and the feasibility floor rises — so instruments that cut founding costs (standardized incorporation, subsidized licensing execution, one-stop spin-out procedures) lower the bar exactly as scale does; the main text's instrument map is patched to say so (`MAIN_EDITS.md`, §7).

### C.3 Proposition 2: comparative statics as channels, not slogans

The earlier draft claimed four uniform signs on a region $\mathcal{R}$. The executed numerics (C.5) now discipline every claim. One survives strengthened to a global weak statement (P). One survives with its opposing channel isolated and exhibited (B). Two are *restructured* because the uniform claims are demonstrably false as stated: the $F$-static is non-monotone — the computed threshold curve is dip-shaped under the external-financing convention and globally increasing under the runway-funded convention, so the earlier uniform sign fails at *large* $F$, exactly where the earlier region clause "F bounded away from zero" offered no protection — and the persistence static fails for the dormant regime, where the computed sign is *positive*. The regime ordering survives and is proved under the coupling package. Every sign below is argued through the waiting-premium representation $(\ast)$ of Remark C.1: a perturbation moves the threshold down exactly when it lowers the premium $W$ (tilting the integrand $c-\pi$ down, or shrinking the admissible horizon) and/or raises the obstacle, and the two effects are tracked separately.

*The region $\mathcal{R}$ (restated in the model's own terms).* (R1) (GEN) holds along the reported boundary, and — corrected: the earlier clause fenced the wrong end — $F$ lies in the *band* on which the claimed sign is asserted, made explicit claim by claim below. (R2) — used only for the burn-rate sign — the funding deadline is remote relative to the discounting and switching horizons: $T_0(F)=F/(B-R_{\mathrm{net}})$ large relative to $1/r$ and to the expected regime cycle length $\max_k 1/\lambda_u(1-\pi_{kk})$. The earlier clause "$\Pi$ stationary over the decision horizon" is deleted: it was vacuous in this paper's constant-generator setting (it imported a two-stage construction excluded by Remark C.3).

*Method.* Under (SC) the threshold is the edge of the zero set of $W$; monotone perturbation arguments on $(\ast)$ move that edge directly. Where a local rate is needed, the displacement identity from differentiating the smooth-fit equation $D_\sigma(\theta^*(x),x)=0$ gives $\partial\theta^*/\partial x=-D_{\sigma x}/D_{\sigma\sigma}$ at the boundary, with $D_{\sigma\sigma}>0$ under (GEN) — the envelope structure of the earlier draft, now with its denominator identified with the transversality quantity of C.2.2.

**Proposition 2 (restated).** *Under (A1)–(A6) and (SC):*

*(i) (Scale.) $\theta^*_\sigma(k;F)$ is non-increasing in $P$, under either financing convention; strictly decreasing on $\mathcal{R}$ wherever $R\cdot S>0$ in the boundary band.*

*(ii) (Burn.) On $\mathcal{R}$ (including R2), $\theta^*_\sigma(k;F)$ is increasing in $B$. The opposing waiting-cost (deadline) channel is real: isolated — by a disclosed pre/post decoupling in which the burn increase hits only the waiting stage — it moves the threshold the other way (weakly down; strictly in the warmer regimes), the Boyle–Guthrie channel in burn form (C.5, Diagnostic E(i)). Within the single-stock model the composite sign did not reverse anywhere on the disclosed family, including thin runway: the reversal R2 guards against lives in the pre/post split, and the qualifier is thereby shown to have content without being needed on the tested range.*

*(iii) (Funds: two channels, Lemma C.4; convention-dependent, non-monotone.) Under the external-financing convention (E) the threshold curve $F\mapsto\theta^*_\sigma(k;F)$ is* dip-shaped*: forced non-entry below $F^{E}_{\min}(k)$; strictly decreasing on a band $[F^{E}_{\min}(k),F_{\mathrm{dip}}(k)]$ where the post-entry slack channel dominates; increasing beyond the dip toward the stationary anchor $\theta_\infty(k)$ as the option-life channel takes over, with the dip located earlier and the rise steeper the worse the regime (largest switch upside). The earlier uniform claim $\partial\theta^*/\partial F<0$ is therefore withdrawn; it holds exactly on the band. Under the runway-funded convention (J) the slope is non-negative essentially everywhere: the curve rises from the Marshallian flow root at the deadline $F=I$ toward the stationary anchor — the Boyle–Guthrie direction globally. The two-channel decomposition of Lemma C.4 explains both shapes; the composite is settled by the computed curve (C.5(vi)).*

*(iv) (Persistence; per-regime, scheme explicit.) Perturbation: raise $\pi_{kk}$ with off-diagonal mass rescaled proportionally. For the aligned regime, $\theta^*_\sigma(S_2;F)$ is non-increasing in $\pi_{S_2S_2}$ — proved under (ROF) with common loading, and clean numerically. For the warming regime the computed own-sign is likewise negative (no stochastic-comparison proof exists; two opposing channels). For the dormant regime the computed own-sign is* positive *— making a bad regime stickier raises its bar; the flow-weight channel beats the shrunk switch-option channel, as the coupling argument predicts — so the earlier uniform-in-$k$ claim is withdrawn. The policy-relevant cross-derivative is stated as its own claim: $\theta^*_\sigma(S_1;F)$ is non-increasing in $\pi_{S_2S_2}$ — proved under the coupling package, confirmed numerically — durability of the aligned regime lowers the warming regime's bar. Under regime-dependent loadings all of (iv) is registered numerical.*

*(v) (Ordering.) Under (ROF) with common loading, $\theta^*_\sigma(S_0;F)\ge\theta^*_\sigma(S_1;F)\ge\theta^*_\sigma(S_2;F)$ for every $F$, strict on $\mathcal{R}$ under strict flow ordering; the computed curves satisfy it at every common-feasible funds level under both conventions.*

*Proofs and discussion.*

(i) *Scale.* Raising $P$ raises $\pi=P\,RS-B$ pointwise wherever $RS>0$, hence lowers the integrand $c-\pi$ of $(\ast)$ pointwise along every path and every stopping time ($c$ does not involve $P$, so the argument is convention-uniform): $W$ falls pointwise. Simultaneously $O$ rises pointwise ($\partial G/\partial P=\mathbb{E}\int e^{-rt}RS\,dt\ge0$). The zero set of $W$ — an up-set under (SC) — therefore expands, and $\theta^*$ weakly falls; strictness on $\mathcal{R}$ follows from the displacement identity with $D_{\sigma P}<0$ at a boundary where $RS>0$. No parameter region is needed for the weak statement — an improvement over the earlier draft. Computed: monotone $-$ in all three regimes across the disclosed sweep.

(ii) *Burn.* $B$ enters three times. (a) Integrand: $c-\pi$ rises pointwise — waiting accumulates more premium, threshold up. (b) Obstacle: $G$ falls (worse flow, and faster post-entry funds depletion) — threshold up. (c) Clock: pre-entry funds depletion accelerates, the deadline and the feasibility horizon shrink, the admissible set of stopping times contracts — $W$ falls, threshold *down*. On R2 the clock channel is exponentially damped (its weight at an interior point decays like $e^{-rT_I(F)}$, $T_I$ the time to the funds floor), while (a)+(b) act undamped at the evaluation point: $\partial\theta^*/\partial B>0$. Two executed findings sharpen the honesty here. First, within the single-stock model the composite sign did *not* reverse anywhere tested — including thin runway — because the same $B$ that shortens the wait also truncates post-entry life, reinforcing (b); the "enter while you still can" flip requires the burn increase to hit *only* the waiting stage. Second, exactly that decoupled sweep (pre-entry drain raised, flow-$B$ and post-entry law pinned; a disclosed outside-model exhibit) produces the reversal — signs $0/-/-$ across the regimes — isolating the Boyle–Guthrie waiting-cost channel in burn form (C.5, Diagnostic E(i)). The qualifier R2 is thereby demonstrated to guard against a real channel, and its content is exhibited rather than asserted.

(iii) *Funds.* **Lemma C.4 (two-channel decomposition).** *Decompose the funds effect as follows. (a) Option-life (pre-entry) channel: in the auxiliary problem in which entry delivers a venture of fixed post-entry endowment $\bar f$ (obstacle $\bar O(\sigma,k)$ funds-free; pre-entry funds relevant only through the deadline), the map $F\mapsto$ threshold is non-decreasing: for $F\le F'$ the admissible stopping sets are nested, so $W^{F}\le W^{F'}$ pointwise with the obstacle fixed, and the zero set shrinks as $F$ grows. Waiting is worth more when the option lives longer; a pair with less earmarked runway rationally enters at lower $\sigma$ — the earmarked-funds analogue of Boyle and Guthrie (2003), in which prospective funding shortfalls erode the value of waiting and liquidity-constrained firms invest earlier than unconstrained ones. (b) Slack (post-entry) channel: perturb by an entry-time refinancing injection $a\ge0$ (the obstacle's funds argument shifted by $+a$, pre-entry clock untouched). By Remark C.1 the premium's source $\pi-c$ is independent of $a$; the $a$-dependence of $W$ enters only through boundary data on the funds-floor layer, transported to interior points with discount weight $e^{-rT_I(F)}$, while the obstacle at the evaluation point rises undamped ($G_f>0$). Under* **(DF)** — *$r>M_f\,(B-R_{\mathrm{net}})$, where $M_f$ is a Lipschitz bound for $\log G_f$ in the funds argument on the operating range ("discounting outpaces the growth of marginal runway value along the clock") — the obstacle effect dominates and $\partial\theta^*/\partial a\le0$: post-entry slack lowers the bar.* $\square$

The composite along the model's single stock moves both channels at once, and the executed curve (C.5(vi)) settles their race — differently per financing convention. Under (E): the curve is capped by forced non-entry at $F^{E}_{\min}(k)$, falls steeply on $[F^{E}_{\min},F_{\mathrm{dip}}(k)]$ where the slack channel (b) dominates ($G_f$ largest near exhaustion), turns at an interior dip, and then *rises* toward the stationary anchor $\theta_\infty(k)$ as the option-life channel (a) takes over — more runway strengthening the waiting option (in particular, waiting for a regime switch) faster than it strengthens the entered value at the boundary. The mechanism's fingerprint is regime-ordered: the dip comes earliest and the rise is largest in the dormant regime (largest switch upside), while the aligned regime's rise is invisible on the computed range (nothing better to wait for). Under (J): channel (a) dominates essentially everywhere — the curve rises from the Marshallian flow root at the deadline toward the anchor, and the slack channel never wins because the marginal founding is paid from money that would otherwise die unspent. This resolves C-G9 by *exhibiting* the coexistence — the deadline behavior and the interior slopes are features of one computed curve — rather than fencing off a boundary; and it corrects the earlier region clause, which excluded small $F$ although the violation of the claimed sign in fact lives at large $F$. The policy consequence is sharp and carried into §7 (`MAIN_EDITS.md`): *gap funds and bridging fellowships are pre-entry earmarked money and act through channel (a) — they fund WAIT, buying option life, with a threshold effect that is zero-to-positive; committed post-entry finance (follow-on commitments, entry-time matching funds, a patient first customer) acts through channel (b) and lowers the bar — and dominates only on the thin-runway band.* The earlier draft sold both stories as one derivative; they are two derivatives with opposite signs, and the computed curve says which rules where.

(iv) *Persistence.* The perturbation scheme is now part of the claim: raise $\pi_{kk}$, rescaling the off-diagonal mass proportionally — equivalently, lower the exit intensity $\lambda_k=\lambda_u(1-\pi_{kk})$ holding the conditional jump distribution fixed. *Aligned regime (proved).* Couple the perturbed (slow-exit) chain with the baseline so the slow chain dominates pathwise (from $S_2$, thin the exit events; elsewhere the generators agree; both chains are stochastically monotone, so the order is maintained after decoupling — a standard monotone coupling for birth–death chains with ordered generators). With common loading and (ROF)(ii) the $\sigma$-paths inherit the order, so $\pi$ rises pointwise along the coupled paths: $W$ falls everywhere, $O$ rises everywhere, and every threshold weakly falls — in particular $\theta^*(S_2;F)$, and in particular the *cross*-effect $\theta^*(S_1;F)$: durability of the aligned regime lowers the warming regime's bar. Both are confirmed by the executed sweeps, which are monotone in the stated directions. Interpretation: durability of the aligned regime is a founding subsidy (cf. policy-uncertainty effects on investment timing, Hassett and Metcalf 1999) — and it is the cross-derivative, not own-persistence in the abstract, that carries the main text's "credible policy commitment" lever: a founder in the warming regime cares about the durability of the regime she may switch into, and the main text is rewritten to attribute exactly this (`MAIN_EDITS.md`). *Dormant regime (sign reversed — earlier claim withdrawn).* The same coupling applied to $\lambda_{S_0}$ shows dormant-regime persistence weakly *raises* every threshold under the package (worse dominated paths: integrand up, obstacle down) — waiting is precisely most valuable when bad flows persist, since the premium $(\ast)$ accumulates the avoided shortfall — and the executed sweep confirms it: the computed own-sign for $S_0$ is *positive*, monotone across the sweep. The earlier draft's uniform $\partial\theta^*(k)/\partial\pi_{kk}<0$ is therefore false for the dormant regime, not merely unproved. *Warming regime (numerically clean, unproved).* The perturbation (slower exit, fixed up/down mix) admits no stochastic comparison — longer warming sojourns delay both the $S_2$ upside and the $S_0$ downside — so no sign is proved; the executed own-sweep is cleanly negative on the disclosed family, and the claim is carried as computed, not proved. *Scope.* All coupling proofs use the common-loading package; with regime-dependent $s_k$ — empirically relevant, since the loadings are the regime uncertainty premia — every part of (iv) is registered as numerical (C.5 sweeps, including the cross-sweep $\hat\theta^*(S_1)$ against $\pi_{S_2S_2}$).

(v) *Ordering.* By the coupling of Lemma C.1(d), $W(\sigma,F,S_0)\ge W(\sigma,F,S_1)\ge W(\sigma,F,S_2)$ pointwise: starting in a better regime, the coupled path carries pointwise higher flows, hence a pointwise smaller premium integrand at every stopping time. The zero sets are therefore nested, $\mathcal{E}_{S_0}(F)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{S_1}(F)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{S_2}(F)$, and the thresholds reversely ordered. This closes C-G4/C-G11 with an in-SM argument under (ROF) + common loading; the executed curves satisfy the ordering at every common-feasible funds level under both financing conventions (Figure 3, panel a). Under heterogeneous loadings the ordering is displayed, not proved, and a violation would itself be economically informative (a wild aligned regime can be worth waiting out). Cross-venture dispersion in $(P,F,B,I)$ disperses thresholds — the microfoundation for lumpy-entry aggregation in the sense of Caballero (1999) is unchanged.

*Remark C.3 (scope: event-time regime shifts).* A two-stage generator ($Q^{\mathrm{pre}}\to Q^{\mathrm{post}}$ at a known event date) is outside this paper's constant-generator setting; no result here uses it, and no claim about it is made.

#### C.3.6 The institutional channel: ECR as a parameter of the laws of motion

The main text (§5) narrates the institutional ledger as acting on the stopping problem "through the C3′ carriers." The earlier formal layer contained no such object; this subsection adds the minimal formalization that licenses a repaired version of the paragraph, and `MAIN_EDITS.md` rewrites the paragraph to claim exactly this much.

Let $e\in[0,100]$ be the institutional profile summary (ECR, or any fixed monotone index of the profile $A$; the axis-level refinement routes as in SM-D.1). Following the class definition of SM-B.1 (C3′), $e$ enters the *coefficients only*, monotonically per carrier: realization/survival, $R(\sigma,k;e)\,S(\sigma,k;e)$ non-decreasing in $e$; exposure, the alignment levels $m_k(e)$ non-decreasing in $e$ (an institution wired into policy and industry converts regime warming into project-facing alignment at a higher level); funding, $R_{\mathrm{net}}(e)$ non-decreasing in $e$. (A parsimonious special case sufficient for everything below: a multiplicative carrier $R\,S(\sigma,k;e)=m(e)\,R\,S(\sigma,k)$ with $m$ non-decreasing, $m>0$.)

**Lemma C.5 (institutional monotonicity of the stopping layer).** *Under (A1)–(A6), (SC), and the carrier monotonicity above: (a) $G$ and $V$ are non-decreasing in $e$; (b) through the realization/survival and exposure carriers, $\theta^*_\sigma(k;F;e)$ is non-increasing in $e$; (c) through the funding carrier, the effect on $V$ is again non-negative, while the threshold effect inherits the two-channel structure of Proposition 2(iii) (a slower clock buys patience; richer post-entry funds lower the bar) and is not signed uniformly.*

*Proof.* (a),(b): the realization/survival carrier raises $\pi$ pointwise (for the multiplicative case this is Proposition 2(i) verbatim with effective scale $P_{\mathrm{eff}}=m(e)P$); the exposure carrier raises the levels $m_k$, and with the common-Brownian coupling the gap of the coupled $\sigma$-paths has non-negative drift whenever it touches zero (the comparison ODE of Lemma C.1(d)), so the paths rise pathwise and $\pi$ rises along paths by (A1-m). In both cases the premium integrand of $(\ast)$ falls pointwise and the obstacle rises: $V$ up, zero set of $W$ expands, $\theta^*$ down. (c): $R_{\mathrm{net}}(e)$ up slows the pre-entry clock ($T_0$, $T_I$ recede: nested stopping sets, $W$ up) and enriches the post-entry funds path ($G$ up, obstacle up): value unambiguously up; the threshold moves by the race of Lemma C.4(a) against C.4(b). $\square$

So a stronger nursery raises the value of the pair's option in every carrier, and lowers the founding bar through readiness, survival, and exposure; through funding it may instead *fund patience* — which is a feature of the instrument, not a defect of the nursery (C.3(iii)). SM-D.1's routing gives the axis-level reading: $A_1$–$A_3$, $A_6$ act through (b)'s carriers; $A_4$, $A_8$ through exposure (also (b)); $A_5$ through (c).

*Remark C.4 (separation: what is proved, what is conjectured).* Within the model class, the optimal policy depends on the institutional profile only through the carriers — by construction (C3′ is the class definition, SM-B.1), and Lemma C.5 gives the resulting monotone comparative statics in the parameterized form. The earlier main-text sentence "nothing decision-relevant was lost by refusing the merger" claimed more: decision-sufficiency of the two-ledger report for the founding problem. We downgrade it explicitly. **Conjecture C.1 (two-ledger decision sufficiency).** *For every admissible environment, the optimal founding rule is measurable with respect to the two ledgers, the macro state, and the funds stock — no function of $A$ outside the C3′ carriers improves the rule.* Within the class this holds by construction; as a statement about the world it is exactly channel completeness, and it stands or falls with the Hansen-$J$ test of SM-B.7 and the registered program. The main text now says so (`MAIN_EDITS.md`).

### C.4 The TRL gate: exogeneity stated, defended, and delimited

**Assumption (TG) (exogenous deterministic maturation).** *$g_{TRL}$ is a deterministic, exogenous, non-decreasing $\{0,1\}$-valued function of time: the maturation ramp neither responds to $(\sigma,k,F)$, nor is chosen, nor fails.* The earlier draft stated conditional independence $\sigma_{SU}\perp g_{TRL}\mid k$; as the referee observes, for a deterministic gate that statement is vacuous — a deterministic function is independent of everything — so we now state the assumption that does the work. (TG) is an assumption about the world, and the main text's "the factorization is a theorem rather than an assumption" is softened accordingly (`MAIN_EDITS.md`): the factorization is a theorem *given* (TG).

*Defense.* Pre-founding technology maturation in the university and national-laboratory setting runs on administrative clocks: stage-gated grant programs, facility construction schedules, doctoral and postdoctoral timelines — instruments committed at award and rarely re-optimized at the frequency at which macro regimes move. Development intensity is likewise committed, not continuously chosen: before day zero there is no firm to reallocate resources inside. And the *certified* gate is ratchet-like by construction of TRL practice: certification does not regress; technical setbacks appear in the venture ledger as slower $R$ and worse $S$ — which is where the model's survival accounting (C.0.1) books technical mortality — rather than as gate reversals. Within this paper's scope (TG) is therefore a reasonable first-order description, and it is *auditable*: the registered program logs scheduled against realized gate dates, and a systematic correlation of maturation-schedule deviations with regime states would reject (TG) directly.

*Limitation.* Each relaxation breaks the factorization, in an instructive direction. Regime-dependent maturation (hot fields accelerate through funding cycles) couples the gate to $k$: the policy no longer factorizes and $\theta^*$ becomes gate-state-dependent. Endogenous development intensity turns the problem into joint control-and-stopping. A stochastic gate with failure risk introduces the *waiting-to-learn* motive — the compound/sequential R&D option of McGrath (1997) and staged-investment analyses — which is absent from this model *by design*: here waiting reveals macro information only, never technical information. The tagline "two non-compensable failure modes" is thus a consequence of (TG), not a discovery about deep tech, and the main text now presents it as such.

**Lemma C.2 (gate factorization).** *Under (TG) and (A1)–(A6), the optimal policy separates: $\mathrm{GO}(t,i)=\mathbf{1}[\sigma_{SU}(t)\ge\theta^*_\sigma(k_t;F_t)]\cdot g_{TRL}(t)$, with $\theta^*_\sigma$ the Theorem 4 curve. The gate shifts feasibility, not the threshold.*

*Proof (self-contained).* The non-decreasing binary gate opens once, at the deterministic date $t_g=\inf\{t:g_{TRL}(t)=1\}$, so $\mathcal{T}_g=\{\tau:\tau\ge t_g\}$. For any $\tau\in\mathcal{T}_g$, the tower property gives $\mathbb{E}[e^{-r\tau}O(X_\tau)]=\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-rt_g}\,\mathbb{E}[e^{-r(\tau-t_g)}O(X_\tau)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t_g}]\big]$, and the inner expectation is at most $V(X_{t_g})$ — the unconstrained value at the time-$t_g$ state — because the post-$t_g$ problem *is* the unconstrained problem: the state $(\sigma,F,k)$ is Markov and carries the only clock ($F$), so no explicit time dependence survives. Equality is attained by following the Theorem 4 rule from $t_g$. Hence $\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_g}=\mathbb{E}[e^{-rt_g}V(X_{t_g})]$, the optimal post-$t_g$ policy is the threshold-curve rule, and before $t_g$ every admissible policy idles; the product form follows. $\square$

This closes C-G12 for the paper's declared binary scope. The graded $[0,1]$-ramp case is *not* proved here and is not used: Table 2's graded-gate vocabulary is retired (see the mapping below and `MAIN_EDITS.md`).

**Definition C.1 (decision trichotomy).** Fix a venture at state $(\sigma,F,k)$ at date $t$, and let $\mathcal{A}(\sigma,F,k)$ be its attainable set — the states reachable with positive probability before funds death; under the deterministic clock, $\{(\sigma',F',k'):F'=F-(B-R_{\mathrm{net}})u,\ u\in[0,T_0)\}$ with $\sigma',k'$ ranging over the diffusion-chain support. Then:

- $\mathrm{GO}$: $g_{TRL}(t)=1$, $F\ge I$, and $\sigma\ge\theta^*_\sigma(k;F)$ — stopping is optimal now.
- $\mathrm{WAIT}$: not GO, and $V(\sigma,F,k)>0$ — continuation is optimal (gate closed with a live option, or gate open below the curve); the option retains value.
- $\mathrm{NO\_GO}$: $V(\sigma,F,k)=0$ — equivalently, the entry region is empty on the attainable set: $O(\sigma',F',k')<0$, with $O$ the entry payoff of the convention in force, at every feasible $(\sigma',F',k')\in\mathcal{A}(\sigma,F,k)$ (ties broken toward NO_GO). Entry never becomes strictly profitable along any attainable path: the option is worthless, not merely unripe.

Canonical sufficient condition for NO_GO: venture-ledger annihilation $P\to0$, under which $\pi=-B<0$ and $G\le0$, so $O^{E}\le-I<0$ and $O^{J}\le0$ everywhere (with ties broken toward NO_GO) — Table 2's Project Y. A second sufficient condition is terminal funds thinness: $F'$ below the convention's funds floor over the whole attainable set (the entered venture can never repay, or never afford, its founding). A maturation program abandoned before opening ($t_g=\infty$) also yields $V=0$ and is classified NO_GO. *Table 2 vocabulary mapping (for the empirical section):* Project N's "GO conditional on milestone" is, in the trichotomy, $\mathrm{WAIT}$ *(gate; GO scheduled)*: by Lemma C.2's restart structure the post-gate policy is the same threshold rule, so "GO at the milestone date provided $\sigma\ge\theta^*$ then holds" is well-posed shorthand for WAIT plus a dated re-read — a consequence of the factorization, not a fourth reading; the row label and the "gate set at elevated threshold" phrase are patched accordingly.

Because the funds drift is negative, the discrete stationary system is lower-triangular across $F$-levels and the fixed-point loop of the earlier pseudocode is executed as a single upward march in $F$ (one factorization); an independent exact solve of each level's obstacle problem as an LCP (policy iteration) bounds the projection error of the march.

```text
inputs: disclosed synthetic (P, B, Rnet, I, r, kappa, s, m_k, Pi, lambda_u), grids, tol
G <- solve  L_k G + pi - r G = 0,  G(.,0,k)=0, zero-flux sigma-boundaries   # coupled sparse solve
O <- G - I                      # convention (E); or O <- interp_F(G, F - I) on F >= I  # convention (J)
V <- 0 at F=0; march F upward:
  per F-level: solve the regime-coupled obstacle problem
      max{ L_k V - r V, O - V } = 0   (obstacle branch only on the feasible region)
      [upward march + obstacle projection; independent exact LCP solve as cross-check]
E_k(F) <- { sigma_j : V - O = 0 }    # entry set kept as a SET (float tolerance, not a modeling band)
theta_hat(k; F) <- min E_k(F) where nonempty; report "empty" otherwise
```

### C.6 Operating the rule on filtered readings

The main text (§5) states in two sentences that the real-time rule runs on filtered readings whose error the registered program measures; this subsection carries the full discussion, displaced from the body at revision.

"Operable" must survive contact with real data. As constructed, $\sigma_{SU}$ is a bounded index built from smoothed, low-frequency series subject to revision (C.0.2(i); SM-D.3), and the regime $k_t$ is a latent state: nobody observes "warming" directly. In practice the rule runs on a filtered reading — a smoothed $\sigma_{SU}$ and a retrospectively assigned or filtered regime — so its real-time output carries classification risk and revision lag, and threshold crossings near regime boundaries are the least reliable readings exactly when they matter most. The theory of stopping under such partial observation replaces state thresholds with belief thresholds (Décamps, Mariotti and Villeneuve, 2005); that extension is out of this paper's scope and is a declared Tier-B item, consistent with the deferral recorded in this supplement's preamble. What the paper commits to is the disciplined version of the naive rule: the registry files GO/WAIT/NO_GO readings computed from the deposited series definitions and smoothing windows, time-stamped before outcomes, with the filtering convention part of the deposit — so the rule's real-time error is *measured* by the program rather than assumed away by the model.

Three coding rules from the main text (E3) bear repeating: an axis that cannot be evidenced *after audit* is coded $\perp$ (unknown) and excluded from $K_{obs}$ — it is never silently imputed to zero; "not started" (evidence of absence) is a 0, which *is* included; and an institution that *declines to evidence* an axis is coded at the floor (0) and kept in $K_{obs}$, not treated as $\perp$ (the anti-gaming rule of SM-A.5). **The axis-7 anchor ladder (levels 0–4).** Because axis 7 carries the Proposition 1 gate — the difference between an institution scored and an institution gated to idle — its full anchor ladder is published here rather than summarized. The rubric observes four rule blocks (spin-out approval; conflict-of-interest management; equity policy; leave and return policy) in two institutional states: *written* (rules formally adopted) and *exercised* (at least one founding processed through the pathway). The anchors:

- **Level 0.** No written rules and no exercised pathway: no spin-out has been approved by any route, formal or improvised. Only this state — not in the game by any door — gates to zero.
- **Level 1.** An exercised improvised pathway without written rules — at least one spin-out approved ad hoc, at founder-borne friction, with nothing codified — *or* written drafts covering at most one of the four blocks that have never processed a founding. An improvisation that has produced a company scores 1, not 0: an exercised de-facto pathway is a capability, however costly its exercise.
- **Level 2** (the gate value $L=2$ of Proposition 1). Core rules written and exercised: spin-out approval and conflict-of-interest rules formally adopted, and at least one founding processed under them. Equity and leave may remain case-by-case. The gate is anchored at *written-and-exercised core*, not at completeness: unexercised model bylaws, however complete on paper, do not clear it, because administrator-drafted rules that no deal has tested are a documented pathology rather than a pathway (Section 7 of the main text).
- **Level 3.** Complete written rule set across all four blocks, exercised, with minor gaps of detail in equity or leave — equity terms negotiated case-by-case inside a written framework, say, or leave provisions adopted but not yet used.
- **Level 4.** Complete, exercised bylaws across all four blocks (the table's anchor): every block written, every block used at least once, terms stable across recent foundings.

Two boundaries carry the design. The $0/1$ boundary splits a state the summary table previously conflated: "no rules and no de-facto pathway" and "no rules but exercised improvisation" are different institutions — the second founds companies, slowly — and only the first is outside the game. The $1/2$ boundary is the gate, and it is an *exercised-pathway* test on the two blocks without which no founding closes (approval and COI), consistent with the sequencing discussion of Section 7: the first deals typically precede and produce the rules, and the gate scores the residue of that co-evolution, not the order in which it happened.

**Correlated measurement.** At mid-tier institutions, axes $A_2$, $A_4$, $A_5$, and $A_8$ are frequently staffed by one small office: the same few people run licensing, the corporate interface, the gap-fund window, and the government-program channel. The four scores are then not measurement-independent even though the four capabilities are conceptually distinct, and the implication is stated rather than hidden: a thin office caps several axes at once, so the portfolio reading of Theorem 1 survives — the axes remain distinct objects with distinct evidence anchors — but independence of the measurement *errors* does not, and a user treating the eight scores as eight independent observations is over-counting evidence. The protocol's response is twofold. The evidence anchors are function-specific — a license negotiated, a joint-research contract converted, a gap-fund tranche disbursed, a program award administered — so one office can genuinely hold all four track records, and then deserves all four scores. And the reliability subsample of SM-B.6.5(2) estimates the cross-axis error correlation directly, so the registered diagnostics inherit a measured, not assumed, error structure.

**A deliberate omission.** Leadership commitment and faculty incentive design — the career consequences of founding: promotion weight, evaluation penalties, the institution's live enthusiasm — is by wide practitioner testimony among the strongest determinants of whether seeds come forward at all. It is deliberately not an axis, because it resists document-anchored observation at the rubric discipline this supplement enforces: commitment statements are cheap to produce, incentive rules are gameable on paper, and the reliable residue of real commitment is hard to distinguish from the residue the existing axes already score. It therefore enters the ledger through what it produces — exercised pathways ($A_7$), EIR placements and matching track records ($A_6$), scouting throughput ($A_1$) — rather than through what it declares. The cost is acknowledged: an institution on the eve of a genuine leadership turn scores no better than one without it, until the turn produces evidence.

### SM-D.2 The calibration panel behind Table 2

The eight projects of Section 6 are composites drawn from one studio's operating records (2007–2026). For each project, the panel records at the founding-decision point: the macro regime assignment ($S_0/S_1/S_2$, assigned retrospectively under the calibration procedure of Section 3 from the three momenta series); the technology-gate reading ($g_{TRL} \in \{0,1\}$, with the domain-specific provisional mapping for drug discovery flagged); the slack state ($F$: low / mid / high, from funding records); the venture-ledger reading and the model's implied decision; the decision actually taken and by whom (studio-involved or external); and the outcome class at 24 months (progressing / stagnant / terminated; right-censored where applicable). Calibration constants mapping rubric levels into model ranges are described procedurally in the main text and withheld as values; the theorems are invariant to them, and the prospective registry deposits them under embargo so that the pre-registered predictions of Section 7 are checkable without public disclosure.

### SM-D.3 Momenta series construction (summary)

The three momenta are constructed from observable series per domain: $\mu_A$ from publication dynamics in the seed's research domain; $\mu_I$ from industrial commitment indicators (corporate participation, procurement signals); $\mu_G$ from policy budgets and program calls. Each series is smoothed, growth-transformed, and mapped to a bounded momentum scale before entering $\sigma_{SU} = \sqrt[3]{\prod (\mu + 1)} - 1$. Series definitions and windows are part of the deposited protocol; regime boundaries ($S_0/S_1/S_2$) are calibrated, not estimated, in this paper (Tier discipline of Section 3). Domain boundaries — the definition of the seed's domain, over which all three series are computed — are fixed ex ante in the deposited protocol and are contestable on the record: the domain is the most gameable object in the system, since whoever draws the boundary chooses the tailwind, and the protocol treats it as such (boundaries fixed before scoring, amendments logged, momenta recomputed under the registered boundary rather than the advocate's).

### SM-D.4 Venture-ledger scoring disciplines and the disclosure layer split

This subsection records the operational scoring conventions of the venture ledger and the disclosure architecture, both stated in compressed form in Section 3 of the main text; SM-D.1 is their institutional-ledger counterpart.

**How $(P, R, S)$ are read.** Each venture-ledger factor is scored on a five-level ordinal rubric anchored by observable states — evidence documents and demonstrated capabilities, not judgment scales — and every reading is taken *at the decision date, from the records available at that date*: for $R$, the documented maturation state of technology, team, and commercialization preparations (a prototype demonstrated outside its originating laboratory, a key hire signed, a regulatory pre-consultation held); for $S$, the evidenced mortality structure (single-point technical dependencies, team fragility, the documented state of the market window); for $P$, the two diligence objects of the main text's construct — addressable demand and attainable unit economics — whose anchors are kept separate so that a healthy reading on one cannot be quoted for the other.

**Three scoring disciplines.** *Information sets are dated.* Retrospective scoring (Section 6 of the main text) admits only evidence knowable at the decision date, and the registry's prospective readings are timestamped before outcomes arrive (Section 7), so the information set is auditable in both directions. *Measurement capacity is endogenous.* Scoring $P$ at rubric discipline requires unit-economics diligence — capacity of exactly the kind axis $A_1$ measures — so the quality of a venture-ledger reading depends on who reads it: an argument for scoring at the agency or studio level rather than by self-assessment. *Domain boundaries are fixed ex ante.* The seed's domain — the object over which the macro momenta are computed — is set in the deposited protocol before scoring and is contestable on the record; the rule, and the reason the domain is the most gameable object in the system, are recorded in SM-D.3 and are not repeated here.

**The disclosure layer split.** The disclosure architecture is a declared layer split, and the split is stated as a split. On the public side sit the theorems, the rubrics, the anchor logic, and the coding conventions: all axis scores are ordinal rubrics (Stevens, 1946) anchored by observable states, not judgment scales; the distinction between "unknown" and "not started" is coded explicitly and propagates to $K_{obs}$ (Little and Rubin, 2019), under the audited observation protocol of SM-A.5; and institutions are reported as anonymized types and projects as composites (Section 6 of the main text). On the proprietary side sit the studio's fitted calibration constants — the mapping of rubric levels into the model's numerical ranges — treated as a *calibration procedure* and reported as method, not as results, both to respect proprietary operating data and because the theorems of the paper are invariant to them; the registry's deposit-under-embargo mechanics for these constants are given in SM-D.2. Everything an adopting evaluator needs in order to rescore from its own records is on the public side of the split.

### SM-D.5 Outcome classes and the twenty-four-month window

The twenty-four-month outcome classification of Table 2 (progressing / stagnant / terminated; right-censored where applicable) is an early-trajectory classifier, not a terminal outcome measure. *Progressing* requires documented milestone or revenue advance against the decision-date plan; *stagnant* is survival without such advance, whatever pays for the survival; *terminated* is wind-up. The classifier reads what funding purchased, not where funding came from: a competitive, milestone-gated public grant that buys maturation milestones reads *progressing* (Project R), while tailwind-funded persistence with no milestone movement is the class definition of stagnation (Project T) — the distinction that keeps the T reading (zombie) and the R reading (progressing) from being an asymmetry of sympathy rather than of evidence.

The window's known failure mode is the slow domain. In drug discovery or advanced materials, "stagnant at twenty-four months" can be the normal early trajectory of a good project, so a domain-uniform window would systematically misclassify slow-good ventures in exactly the domains this paper claims for its scope. The registry therefore carries domain-specific outcome-class definitions — drug discovery flagged provisional, as Table 2's note records for its readiness mapping — and the misclassification risk of slow-good ventures is registered as a known limitation with a stated mitigation: longer-horizon re-reads at forty-eight and sixty months as the registry matures, so that the early classifier is itself audited against the horizons its slowest domains run on. Re-read outcomes at the longer horizons are adjudicated under the same deposited definitions, by a party other than the forecaster, as the main text's registry commitments state.
