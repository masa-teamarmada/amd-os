# Supplementary Material for "The Before Zero Model: measuring deep-tech ventures and their institutional nurseries before day zero"

## SM-A. Proofs for Section 3 (Theorems 1, 2 and Proposition 1)

This supplement gives complete proofs of the three formal results of Section 3: the weighted-sum representation of the institutional readiness score ERS (Theorem 1), the precondition-gate structure of the bylaws axis (Proposition 1), and the exclusion of multiplicative cross-layer combination with the project readiness score PRS (Theorem 2, stated as a reporting-scale result). SM-A.5 records the observation protocol that the missing-visibility axiom presupposes. Axis numbering follows Section 3; axis 7 is the bylaws (institutional-design) axis.

### SM-A.1 Setup, notation and axioms

**Profiles and observed sets.** An institutional profile is a vector $A=(A_1,\dots,A_8)\in([0,4]\cup\{\bot\})^8$, where $A_k$ is the rubric level of capability axis $k$ and $\bot$ marks an axis whose level has not been observed. The observed set is $K_{\mathrm{obs}}(A)=\{k:A_k\neq\bot\}$. For nonempty $K\subseteq\{1,\dots,8\}$ write $X_K=\prod_{k\in K}[0,4]$, and let $\mathbf{0}_K,\mathbf{4}_K$ denote the profiles constant at levels $0$ and $4$. The primitive of the theory is a family of institutional readiness orderings $\succsim_K$ on $X_K$, one for each nonempty $K\subseteq\{1,\dots,8\}$: profiles are compared within an observational equivalence class (equal $K$), and every score is reported together with its $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$. Every axis is assessable in principle — which axes are observed at a given institution is an audit outcome, not a restriction of the theory's domain; the point matters in Step 4 below. The domain between the rubric anchors is governed by a stated assumption:

**Assumption A-1 (continuum idealization).** Each axis domain is treated as the continuum $[0,4]$ with the five rubric anchors as landmarks, and $\succsim_K$ is defined on the continuum; restricted solvability and the Archimedean condition below are stated there. This is an idealization: assessment emits only five levels per axis, and on a finite grid the additive representation requires the full hierarchy of cancellation conditions rather than the two used here (Scott, 1964; Krantz et al., 1971, ch. 9; Fishburn, 1970, ch. 4). The cost is stated plainly: Thomsen and solvability are not directly testable on five-point data, so the empirical content of Theorem 1 is conditional on A-1.

**Definition SM-A.1 (additive conjoint structure).** For $|K|\ge 2$, the pair $(X_K,\succsim_K)$ is an *additive conjoint structure* if: (i) $\succsim_K$ is a *weak order* (complete, transitive); (ii) *independence*: the ordering induced on any one axis by fixing the remaining axes does not depend on where they are fixed; (iii) *double cancellation*, whose indifference version is the *Thomsen condition*: for axes $k\neq k'$, levels $a,b,c$ on $k$ and $x,y,z$ on $k'$, if $(a,y)\succsim(b,z)$ and $(b,x)\succsim(c,y)$ then $(a,x)\succsim(c,z)$, all other axes held fixed; (iv) *restricted solvability*: whenever varying axis $k$ alone brackets a target profile from above and below, some level of axis $k$ matches it exactly; (v) *Archimedean*: every bounded standard sequence (levels equally spaced in readiness terms) is finite; (vi) *essentiality*: each axis in $K$ can on its own reverse at least one comparison. See Krantz, Luce, Suppes and Tversky (1971, ch. 6, Def. 6.7 and adjacent definitions), hereafter KLST, and Luce and Tukey (1964).

**Axioms.** The paper imposes on each $\succsim_K$, and on any score $F_K$ representing it:

- **E1 (separability).** Ordinal form: independence, clause (ii) above. Cardinal form, meaningful once the score scale is fixed (see Step 5 below): $\partial^2 F_K/\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}=0$ for $k\neq k'$ — the contribution of one axis is assessable without knowing the others (Debreu, 1960; Gorman, 1968).
- **E2 (compensability).** Any two observed axes trade at finite positive rates; no observed axis is individually fatal to the institution. Its *strong form* E2-s encodes the equal-interval design of the Section 3 rubric: a rubric increment $\delta$ on axis $k$ is exactly compensated by a rubric decrement on axis $k'$ at a rate $w_k/w_{k'}$ that does not depend on the base levels. E2-s is a property of the rubric's *reporting convention* — the equal-interval anchor design — not an empirical claim about institutions: Theorem 1 is to be read as the unique aggregate consistent with an equal-interval reporting convention, and its statement names E2-s explicitly.
- **E3 (audited missing-visibility).** The aggregate is the *family* $\{F_K\}$: the symbol $\bot$ is never mapped into $[0,4]$, the score depends only on observed coordinates, $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is disclosed alongside the score, and $A_k=\bot$ ("unknown") is treated differently from $A_k=0$ ("not started"). The symbol $\bot$ is assigned under the *assessor's* evidence standard — an axis is $\bot$ only when evidence is genuinely unavailable after audit; an institution's *declining to evidence* an axis is not $\bot$ but is coded at the rubric floor (level $0$) and remains in $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$, the worst-case coding standard of credit-rating practice (full remark in SM-A.5).
- **E4 (monotonicity, strict).** Improving any observed axis strictly increases readiness: $F_K$ is strictly increasing in every $A_k$, $k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}$; equivalently, raising any single coordinate alone yields $A'\succ_K A$. Essentiality is a consequence (Step 1), not a hypothesis.
- **E5 (cross-context trade-off invariance).** For any two observed sets $K,K'$ and axes $k,k'\in K\cap K'$, the compensating rate at which a rubric step on $k$ trades against a rubric step on $k'$ — at any common base levels of the pair — is the same under $\succsim_K$ and $\succsim_{K'}$: trade-offs between two capabilities do not depend on which further axes happen to be observed. An assessor who has calibrated how gap-fund access trades against incubation capacity does not re-calibrate that judgment because the bylaws axis was unobservable at one institution. E5 is the cousin, at the level of observed sets, of the subgroup-consistency conditions of poverty measurement (Foster and Shorrocks, 1991).
- **(C) Continuity.** Each $\succsim_K$ has closed upper and lower contour sets on $X_K$.

### SM-A.2 Proof of Theorem 1

**Theorem 1 (weighted-sum representation with a single weight vector).** *Let the family $\{\succsim_K\}$ satisfy E1–E4 (E4 strict), E5, (C), restricted solvability, the Archimedean condition and Thomsen/double cancellation under Assumption A-1, and let the rubric satisfy the equal-interval convention E2-s. Then there exists a single weight vector $w_1,\dots,w_8>0$, serving every observed set, such that each $\succsim_K$ is represented by*
$$\mathrm{ERS}(A)\;=\;100\cdot\frac{\sum_{k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}}w_k A_k}{4\sum_{k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}}w_k},$$
*unique up to positive affine transformation among scores additive in rubric units. Without E2-s, the same axioms deliver the additively separable form $\sum_{k\in K}\varphi_k(A_k)$ with continuous, strictly increasing $\varphi_k$ (weights and recalibrations not separately identified): the equal-interval reporting convention is exactly what linearizes the $\varphi_k$.*

The engine of the proof is the additive conjoint representation theorem, which we state in the form applied.

**Fact SM-A.1 (additive conjoint representation).** *Let $(X_K,\succsim_K)$ be an additive conjoint structure with every axis essential. Then there exist strictly increasing functions $\varphi_k:[0,4]\to\mathbb{R}$, $k\in K$, with*
$$A\succsim_K B\iff \sum_{k\in K}\varphi_k(A_k)\;\ge\;\sum_{k\in K}\varphi_k(B_k),$$
*and under (C) the $\varphi_k$ may be taken continuous. If $\{\psi_k\}$ is another such family, then $\psi_k=a\varphi_k+b_k$ for some common $a>0$ and constants $b_k$.* This is KLST (1971, ch. 6, Thm 6.2) for two axes together with its $n$-component extension in the same chapter; the original two-factor result is Luce and Tukey (1964), the topological route under continuity is Debreu (1960) and Wakker (1989), and the uniqueness formulation follows Fishburn (1970).

**Proof of Theorem 1.**

*Step 1 (essentiality; additive representation).* Fix $K$ with $|K|\ge 2$ (for $|K|=1$ the score is a single strictly monotone function and all claims below are immediate). E1 in ordinal form is exactly independence. Essentiality follows from strict E4 in one line: varying any axis $k\in K$ alone strictly changes readiness, hence reverses at least one comparison, so every observed axis is essential. With double cancellation, restricted solvability, the Archimedean condition and (C), the hypotheses of Fact SM-A.1 hold, so $\succsim_K$ is represented by $\sum_{k\in K}\varphi_k(A_k)$ with $\varphi_k$ continuous and strictly increasing. For $|K|\ge 3$ double cancellation is redundant given the remaining axioms (KLST, ch. 6); it is assumed uniformly because two-axis observed sets occur. Equivalently, E1 plus (C) yields additive separability by the overlapping-factors argument of Debreu (1960) and Gorman (1968), so that any representing score has the form $F_K=\Phi\!\left(\sum_k\varphi_k(A_k)\right)$ with $\Phi$ strictly increasing.

*Step 2 (linearization by E2-s).* E2 makes the substitution rate $\varphi_k'(A_k)/\varphi_{k'}'(A_{k'})$ (where derivatives exist) finite and positive: no axis is lexicographically prior, none individually fatal. E2-s says more: for every increment $\delta>0$, the representation gain $\varphi_k(t+\delta)-\varphi_k(t)$ is independent of $t$, because a fixed rubric step trades against other axes at a level-independent rate $w_k/w_{k'}$. A monotone solution of this increment equation on an interval is affine:
$$\varphi_k(t)=w_k t+c_k,\qquad w_k>0,$$
with $w_k>0$ by strict monotonicity. E2-s is a property of the numerals the rubric assigns — the equal-interval design of Section 3 — and it collapses each $\varphi_k$ to a linear function of them.

*Step 3 (calibration to the normal form).* $\sum_k(w_kA_k+c_k)$ and $\sum_k w_kA_k$ differ by a constant, hence represent the same ordering; the constants $c_k$ may be dropped. Adopt the paper's calibration convention $F_K(\mathbf{0}_K)=0$ and $F_K(\mathbf{4}_K)=100$: an institution with nothing started on any observed axis reads $0$, one fully institutionalized on every observed axis reads $100$. The unique positive affine transformation of $\sum_{k\in K}w_kA_k$ meeting both conditions is
$$F_K(A)=100\cdot\frac{\sum_{k\in K}w_kA_k}{4\sum_{k\in K}w_k},$$
since at $\mathbf{4}_K$ the numerator equals $4\sum_{k\in K}w_k$.

*Step 4 (a single weight vector across observed sets).* Steps 1–3, run separately on each $X_K$, deliver per-$K$ weights $(w_k^{(K)})_{k\in K}$, determined up to a positive scale; the scale-free content, identified with an observable in Step 2, is the compensating rate $w_k^{(K)}/w_{k'}^{(K)}$ at which a rubric step on $k$ trades against a rubric step on $k'$ under $\succsim_K$.

(a) *Cross-$K$ coherence from E5.* By E5 this rate does not depend on the observed set; write $\rho(k,k')$ for its common value. Fix a reference axis $k_0$ and set $w_{k_0}:=1$, $w_k:=\rho(k,k_0)$ — well defined precisely because $w_k^{(K)}/w_{k_0}^{(K)}$ is the same for every $K$ containing both axes. Rates chain multiplicatively: evaluating all three rates inside one observed set containing $\{k,k',k_0\}$ — such a set exists, the family being indexed by all nonempty $K$, all eight axes being assessable under the paper's protocol — gives $\rho(k,k')=\rho(k,k_0)/\rho(k',k_0)=w_k/w_{k'}$. Hence for every observed set $K$, with or without $k_0$, and all $k,k'\in K$: $w_k^{(K)}/w_{k'}^{(K)}=\rho(k,k')=w_k/w_{k'}$, so each per-$K$ weight vector is proportional to the restriction of the global $(w_1,\dots,w_8)$; since the Step 3 display is invariant to a common rescaling of its weights, the single vector serves every observed set. Disjoint observed sets are never compared directly by any $\succsim_K$; their weights cohere through chains of intermediate observed sets sharing axes, which eight assessable axes always supply.

(b) *Renormalization over $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$, licensed by E3.* Setting $K=K_{\mathrm{obs}}(A)$ yields the statement's formula. E3 is what makes the $K$-dependent denominator the right object: an axis with $A_k=0$ remains in $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$, contributing $0$ to the numerator while its weight $w_k$ stays in the denominator, whereas an axis with $A_k=\bot$ leaves both numerator and denominator. Thus "unknown" and "not started" receive different scores by construction, and the missing axis remains visible through the reported pair $(\mathrm{ERS},K_{\mathrm{obs}})$. At the interpretive level this renormalization is honest in the spirit of Rubin (1976): the score acknowledges the mechanism that hides an axis by carrying $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ explicitly, rather than silently imputing a default level for what was never assessed. The audit discipline behind the assignment of $\bot$ is recorded in SM-A.5.

*Step 5 (uniqueness; convention versus content).* Let $V$ represent $\succsim_K$ additively in rubric units, $V=\sum_{k\in K}\psi_k(A_k)$. By the uniqueness clause of Fact SM-A.1, $\psi_k=a\,w_kA_k+b_k$ with common $a>0$, so $V=a\sum_kw_kA_k+b$, a positive affine transformation of $\mathrm{ERS}$; conversely every positive affine transformation represents the same ordering. Fixing the endpoint calibration of Step 3 selects $\mathrm{ERS}$ uniquely within this class. Because the class is closed exactly under positive affine maps, the vanishing of cross-partials in E1's cardinal form is scale-invariant ($\partial^2(aF+b)/\partial A_k\partial A_{k'}=a\,\partial^2F/\partial A_k\partial A_{k'}$), so E1's two readings are mutually consistent. Finally, only the ratios $w_k/w_{k'}$ bind trade-offs. The scale normalization $\sum_kw_k=1$ is order-irrelevant. *Equal weights are not a normalization*: the ratios $w_k/w_{k'}$ are empirical content, recovered from observed trade-offs (Step 2) and portable across observed sets (E5), so the paper's default $w_k\equiv1$ is a disclosed working convention pending trade-off elicitation — a transparent value judgement in the sense of Sen (1977), not a quantity delivered by the axioms. $\blacksquare$

### SM-A.3 Proof of Proposition 1

**Proposition 1 (precondition gate on axis 7).** *Assume $7\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and let $F(A_{-7})=\sum_{k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}\setminus\{7\}}w_kA_k\,/\,4\sum_{k\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}\setminus\{7\}}w_k\in[0,1]$ be the Theorem-1 aggregate over the remaining observed axes. Under (G0)–(G3) below,*
$$\mathrm{ERS}(A)=100\cdot g(A_7)\cdot F(A_{-7}),$$
*where $g:[0,4]\to[0,1]$ is a nondecreasing gate — by default the step $g(0)=0$, $g(1)=0.25$, $g(a)=1$ for $a\ge 2$, or a steep sigmoid variant.*

Proposition 1 is *not* a corollary of Theorem 1: it describes a deliberately heterogeneous structure in which E2 is withdrawn for axis 7 alone — a bylaws blackout is individually fatal — and E4 is retained for the gate axis only in its weak, nondecreasing direction (strictness would contradict a gate flat at $1$ on $[L,4]$), while E1–E4 continue to hold among the remaining axes. The architecture is the identification-then-aggregation scheme of Alkire and Foster (2011): a cutoff decision on the precondition dimension first settles whether the institution is in the frame at all, and only then does the Theorem-1 aggregation run over the remaining dimensions; the product form mirrors their multiplicative decomposition of the adjusted headcount.

The gate axioms, for the section orderings of the full score $S(A_7,A_{-7})$:

- **(G0) Section monotonicity.** At each fixed $A_7=a$, the full score $S(a,\cdot)$ is nondecreasing in every non-gate axis, and scores are nonnegative — the section inheritance of E4 (strictness must fail on degenerate sections and is not asserted).
- **(G1) Precondition.** There is a threshold level $L$ (default $L=2$) such that (a) if $A_7=0$ the profile is readiness-equivalent to $\mathbf 0$ regardless of $A_{-7}$, and (b) if $A_7\ge L$ readiness coincides with the Theorem-1 aggregate over the remaining axes: $S(A_7,A_{-7})=100\,F(A_{-7})$.
- **(G2′) Conditional consistency on non-degenerate sections.** For each fixed $a$ with $S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})>0$, the induced ordering over $A_{-7}$ is the Theorem-1 ordering on $K_{\mathrm{obs}}\setminus\{7\}$. The restriction to non-degenerate sections is what makes the block consistent: at $a=0$, (G1)(a) makes the section totally indifferent, and (G2′) is silent there rather than contradicted.
- **(G3) Within-level ratio preservation.** For each fixed $a$ with $S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})>0$, relative scores equal relative aggregates: $S(a,A_{-7})/S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})=F(A_{-7})$.

**Proof.** Define $g(a):=S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})/100$. If $g(a)>0$, (G3) gives $S(a,A_{-7})=100\,g(a)F(A_{-7})$ directly, and (G2′) is consistent with it: the section score is a positive multiple of $F$, whose ordering is the Theorem-1 ordering. If $g(a)=0$, then by (G0) $0\le S(a,A_{-7})\le S(a,\mathbf 4_{-7})=0$, so the whole section is $0=100\,g(a)F(A_{-7})$. (G1)(a) forces $g(0)=0$; (G1)(b) forces $g\equiv1$ on $[L,4]$; monotonicity of the full score in the gate axis — E4's weak direction, the part retained for axis 7 — makes $g$ nondecreasing, hence $g:[0,4]\to[0,1]$, and the displayed factorization follows. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (the block has models).** For any nondecreasing $g_0:[0,4]\to[0,1]$ with $g_0(0)=0$ and $g_0\equiv1$ on $[L,4]$, the score $S(a,A_{-7}):=100\,g_0(a)\,F(A_{-7})$ satisfies (G0)–(G3): (G0) since $F$ is nondecreasing and $g_0\ge0$; (G1) from the boundary conditions on $g_0$; (G2′) and (G3) since on non-degenerate sections the score is a positive multiple of $F$ and $F(\mathbf 4_{-7})=1$. The default step gate and the steep sigmoid are both members, so the block is consistent by exhibition.

Two elements of the display are design, and are labeled as such rather than derived. *Gate calibration (convention).* Only $g(0)=0$ and $g\equiv1$ on $[L,4]$ follow from the block; on $(0,L)$ the axioms bound $g$ between $0$ and $1$, and the value $g(1)=0.25$ is a rubric-design convention, on the same footing as the endpoint calibration of Step 3. *Status of (G3) (stated primitive).* (G3) is the minimal premise delivering the product form and is adopted as design rather than derived: its content is the Alkire–Foster product decomposition described above.

*Remarks.* (i) If $A_7=\bot$ the gate has no argument, and E3 requires the gated score itself to be reported as missing rather than defaulted. (ii) Replacing the step by a steep sigmoid preserves E4 and (C) and approximates the step arbitrarily closely; the choice is an implementation matter. (iii) The monograph embeds $F$ in a one-parameter constant-elasticity family $\left(\sum_{k\neq7}w_kA_k^{\rho}/\sum_{k\neq7}w_k\right)^{1/\rho}$ that degenerates to the weighted sum as $\rho\to1$ (and to log-multiplicative and min forms as $\rho\to0$ and $\rho\to-\infty$); the paper works throughout with the $\rho=1$ member, which is the Theorem-1 aggregate used above.

### SM-A.4 Proof of Theorem 2

**Theorem 2 (no multiplicative cross-layer combination: a reporting-scale result).** *Let $E:=\mathrm{ERS}(A)$ be the Theorem-1 score and let $P:=\mathrm{PRS}>0$ be the project readiness score, a strictly increasing differentiable function of project variables $(P_1,\dots,P_J)$ only, multiplicative in form $P=\prod_jP_j^{\beta_j}$, $\beta_j>0$, as in Section 3. For any $\alpha\in(0,1)$, consider $S=E^{\alpha}P^{1-\alpha}$ on the interior region $E>0$, $P>0$. Then: (i) at each fixed venture state, $S$ induces the same institutional ordering as $E$ — ordinal separability within a venture slice is preserved; (ii) $S$ destroys the interval-scale reporting content of the institutional ledger: the within-layer cross-partials $\partial^2S/\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}$ are strictly negative, so a fixed rubric step no longer trades against other axes at a level-independent rate in report units — the calibrated constant-rate property (E2-s) by which published weights are audited; and (iii) $S$ makes marginal institutional credit venture-state-contingent: $\partial^2S/\partial A_k\,\partial P>0$ and, axis by axis, $\partial^2S/\partial A_k\,\partial P_j>0$ — the nursery is paid once inside the venture score and again as a multiplier, the double counting whose dynamic form Section 4 develops.*

**Proof.** Clause (i): at a fixed venture state $P$ is a positive constant, so $S=E^{\alpha}\cdot\mathrm{const}^{1-\alpha}$ is a strictly increasing transform of $E$. Clauses (ii) and (iii) are computations. By Theorem 1, $\partial E/\partial A_k=c_k$ with the constant $c_k:=25\,w_k/\sum_{j\in K_{\mathrm{obs}}}w_j>0$ — the constancy of $c_k$ across the whole domain is precisely E1 and E2-s at work inside the institutional layer. Treat $S$ as a function of $(A,P)$ on the interior region $E>0$, $P>0$. Then
$$\frac{\partial S}{\partial A_k}=\alpha E^{\alpha-1}P^{1-\alpha}c_k,\qquad
\frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial A_k\,\partial P}=\alpha(1-\alpha)\,E^{\alpha-1}P^{-\alpha}c_k\;>\;0,$$
so the cross-layer bilinear term is everywhere nonzero. Through the multiplicative form of $P$, the same holds axis by axis on the project side:
$$\frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial A_k\,\partial P_j}=\alpha(1-\alpha)\,E^{\alpha-1}P^{1-\alpha}\,\frac{\beta_j c_k}{P_j}\;>\;0,$$
an $8\times J$ block of strictly positive cross-partials: the marginal credit for a one-level improvement of institutional axis $k$ depends on the state of every project axis, so institutional capability is counted a second time through the project channel — clause (iii). Within the institutional layer, for $k\neq k'$,
$$\frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}}=\alpha(\alpha-1)\,E^{\alpha-2}P^{1-\alpha}c_kc_{k'}\;<\;0,$$
so the report credit of a rubric step on axis $k$ falls with the level of every other axis: the cardinal within-layer form of E1 fails on the reported scale, and with it the constant-rate audit property — clause (ii).

The signs in (ii)–(iii) are properties of the whole affine class of reports built on $S$ — $aS+b$ has the same cross-partials scaled by $a>0$ — so no recalibration preserving the reported unit repairs them. Taking logarithms restores additivity between the blocks, $\log S=\alpha\log E+(1-\alpha)\log P$, so the *order* induced by $S$ on the interior is additively separable across the two ledgers; what the combination destroys is not ordinal structure but the calibrated rubric-unit scale: in rubric units $\partial^2\log S/\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}=-\alpha\,c_kc_{k'}/E^{2}\neq0$, log-ERS steps are not rubric steps, and the audit property E2-s is a property of the reported scale, not of the order. Theorem 2 is accordingly a cardinal claim about the reporting instrument, and is stated as such. E1 as stated governs assessability among institutional axes: clause (ii) is its cardinal within-layer echo, and clause (iii) is a cross-layer statement E1 never governed — the measurement-level exclusion of the merger as a representation of *value* is Section 4's Theorem 3, not this result. $\blacksquare$

**Remark (the gate is not convicted).** The Proposition-1 score $100\cdot g(A_7)\cdot F(A_{-7})$ also has nonzero cross-partials between the gate axis and the others — $100\,g'(a)\,\partial F/\partial A_k$ where $g$ is differentiable — and it is not excluded, for a stated reason. Within each gate level, cross-partials among the seven axes governed by E1–E4 vanish identically (at fixed $a$ the gate is a common factor and $F$ is linear), so the interval-scale report of the additive ledger survives conditional on the gate; and axis 7 was never inside the additive claim — E2 is withdrawn for it by design, and its role is axiomatized separately by (G0)–(G3). The criterion clause (ii) applies is scale-preservation *within the set of axes for which additive reporting is claimed*; the prohibited merger scrambles exactly that set, the gate does not.

**Corollary (admissible cross-layer couplings).** *(i) Additive coupling $S_{+}=\alpha'P+\beta'E$, $\alpha',\beta'>0$: all cross-partials vanish identically, $\partial^2S_{+}/\partial A_k\,\partial P=0$ and $\partial^2S_{+}/\partial A_k\,\partial A_{k'}=0$, so additive coupling preserves both the ordinal and the interval-scale reporting content of each ledger, and E3, E4 are inherited from each layer. (ii) Conditional coupling $P\mid A_7\ge L$: PRS is reported only when the institutional precondition of Proposition 1 is met; no composite scalar is formed, hence no cross term arises, and the rule is the project-level extension of the Proposition-1 gate. (iii) Every multiplicative coupling $E^{\alpha}P^{1-\alpha}$, $\alpha\in(0,1)$, is excluded as a reporting instrument by this theorem — and as a value representation by Theorem 3.* $\blacksquare$

The three results together give the shape claim of Section 3 its precise content: the project layer and the institutional layer answer to different axiom systems, so their aggregates take different functional forms by necessity, and the only couplings that preserve what each ledger reports are additive or conditional — never multiplicative.

### SM-A.5 Remark: audited observation and strategic non-disclosure

This subsection records the observation protocol E3 presupposes; without it, the design would invite the behavior it is meant to prevent.

Under *self-reported* missingness the $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ renormalization is strategically gameable. Withholding axis $m$ moves the score from $100\sum_{K}w_kA_k/4\sum_{K}w_k$ to the same expression over $K\setminus\{m\}$ — strictly larger exactly when $A_m$ lies below the weighted average of the other observed axes, precisely when disclosure would hurt. The mechanism is the denominator: a disclosed weakness leaves numerator and denominator together — little above, full weight below — while $\bot$ removes both at once. An institution whose weak axis would score low therefore *raises* its ERS by declining to evidence that axis. The moment ERS carries funding or reputational stakes, non-disclosure becomes the dominant move, and E3 — designed as an honesty device — becomes an opacity subsidy.

The protocol therefore fixes three rules. (i) $\bot$ is assigned under the *assessor's* evidence standard, not the institution's: an axis is $\bot$ only when evidence is genuinely unavailable after audit. (ii) Declined-to-evidence is not $\bot$: it is coded at the rubric floor (level $0$) and the axis remains in $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ — worst-case coding, the standard response of credit-rating practice to withheld information. Under (i)–(ii) non-disclosure can only lower the score, and the incentive reverses to disclosure. (iii) The report is the pair $(\mathrm{ERS},K_{\mathrm{obs}})$, and stripping $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is out of protocol: scores computed on different observed sets are not on a common scale. A shortlist that compares institutions across observed sets must either stratify by $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ or use the decision-conservative completion — unobserved axes at the floor — disclosed as such.

The connection to Rubin (1976) is exact. Renormalization over $K_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is representationally harmless only when the missingness mechanism is ignorable — when *which* axes are missing carries no information about their levels. Audited $\bot$ approximates ignorability by construction: after (i)–(ii), missingness reflects the assessor's access, not the institution's choice. Self-reported $\bot$ does not — it is missingness selected on the missing value itself, the canonical non-ignorable mechanism — and a score renormalized over it inherits the selection.

## SM-B. Theorem 3 and its corollaries: the dynamic foundation

This supplement proves Theorem 3 and its two corollaries and constructs the model class in which Section 4 states them. The foundation is dynamic: the institution-adjusted value $f$ is not a primitive scalar constrained by axioms but is *defined* as the expected discounted value of the venture under institution-dependent dynamics — the same object whose optimization Section 5 studies, so Sections 4 and 5 share one value function. Three consequences organize the section. First, the structural properties Section 4 states informally — annihilation C1′, institutional monotonicity C2′, continuity C4′ — are derived, not assumed (Lemma SM-B.1), and the class is non-empty by exhibition, so the existence question for the system C1′–C4′ is settled by construction. Second, the channel statement C3′ — institutional axes enter only the laws of motion of the venture state — is the *definition of the model class*, not a restriction on a given function. This dissolves a type problem that any static formulation of the channel carries: a display such as $df/dA_k=f_R\,\partial R/\partial A_k+f_S\,\partial S/\partial A_k$ must read $R$ and $S$ simultaneously as free coordinates of $f$ and as functions of $A$; here $(R,S)$ are states, $A$ parameterizes their laws of motion, and $f$ is the value functional of the resulting process — each symbol has one type. Third, the impossibility is proved where it matters: for *weakly* monotone composites — the class evaluation practice actually uses — under either of two richness conditions with observable content (SM-B.3–B.4), the strictly monotone case having been disposed of first as the boundary phenomenon it is (SM-B.2).

### SM-B.1 The nurturing-environment class

A venture ledger state is $x=(P,R,S)\in X:=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,1]\times[0,1]$: $P$ the potential scale, constant along a venture's path; $R$ realization readiness, with $R=1$ marking completion of the pre-founding journey; $S$ the survival state. An institutional profile is $A\in\mathcal{A}:=[0,4]^{8}$, the eight rubric axes of Section 3. The *death set* is $\mathcal{D}_0:=\{x:\min(P,S)=0\}$; states with $\min(P,S)>0$ are *live* (interior). $R$ is deliberately absent from $\mathcal{D}_0$ — $R=0$ is a normal live early state, not a boundary of the model; Lemma SM-B.1(b) develops the point.

**Definition SM-B.1 (nurturing environments; the benchmark subclass $\mathfrak{D}$).** A *nurturing environment* $e$ specifies laws of motion for the venture state in which the institutional profile enters only through the coefficients:

**C3′ (channel structure, definitional).** *$A$ appears in the laws of motion of $(R,S)$ — and, in the general case, of slack $F$ and of the exposure to $\sigma_{SU}$ innovations — and nowhere else; venture value is the expected discounted payoff generated by those laws.*

The *deterministic benchmark subclass* $\mathfrak{D}$ consists of pairs $e=(\lambda,r)$ with $r\ge0$ and a hazard-intensity function $\lambda:[0,1]\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}$, continuous and non-increasing in each $A_k$, generating
$$\frac{dR_t}{dt}=1,\qquad \frac{dS_t}{dt}=-S_t\,\lambda(R_t;A),$$
with $S=0$ and $P=0$ absorbing with zero flow, completion when $R$ reaches $1$ (at $\tau=1-R_0$), and value the discounted completion payoff $f_e(x;A):=e^{-r\tau}\,P\,S_{\tau}$. Since $R_t=R+t$, integrating the linear equation for $S$ gives $S_\tau=S\exp\big(-\int_0^{1-R}\lambda(R+t;A)\,dt\big)$, i.e., substituting $u=R+t$,
$$f_e(P,R,S;A)\;=\;P\,S\,\exp\Big(-r(1-R)-\int_R^1\lambda(u;A)\,du\Big).$$
Equivalently, $S_t$ is the probability that a venture facing hazard rate $\lambda(R_t;A)$ is still alive at $t$, and $f_e=\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-r\tau}P\,\mathbf{1}\{\text{alive at }\tau\}\big]$: the state reading and the hazard reading coincide. Thicker nurseries lower the hazard along the *remaining* maturation path $[R,1]$, and value integrates that reduction over exactly the road still to be travelled — the exposure structure that drives SM-B.3.

The general class extends $\mathfrak{D}$ with C3′ kept as the defining restriction: $(R,S,F)$ is a strong Markov process, each $A_k$ enters its generator monotonically (drift of $R$ up, hazard behind $S$ down, slack accumulation up, loading on $\sigma_{SU}$ innovations up), and $A$ appears nowhere outside the generator. Section 5's dynamics are exactly of this form, and one point deserves emphasis: financial slack $F$ and the $\sigma_{SU}$-exposure — the carriers through which Section 5 and SM-D route the funding axis $A_5$ and the interface axes $A_4$, $A_8$ — are *inside* the licensed channel by construction, so the stopping model and the channel structure are consistent by definition, not by assumption. Because death states are absorbing with zero flow, every member satisfies C1′ directly from the definition of value; the remaining structural properties transfer from $\mathfrak{D}$ by the coupling and comparison arguments standard in the stopping literature (McDonald and Siegel, 1986; Dixit and Pindyck, 1994). We cite rather than reproduce these because nothing below uses them: every result of this supplement is proven inside $\mathfrak{D}$, and impossibility theorems need verified witnesses, not the largest possible class. Members of the class are called *admissible*.

**Lemma SM-B.1 (well-posedness, structure, non-emptiness).** *For every $e\in\mathfrak{D}$, with $\Lambda:=\max\lambda$ (finite, $\lambda$ being continuous on a compact domain):*

*(a) (C4′) $f_e$ is well defined, $0\le f_e\le PS\le P$, and jointly continuous in $(x,A)$.*

*(b) (C1′) If $\min(P,S)=0$ then $f_e(x;A)=0$ for every $A$, and $\mathcal{D}_0$ is absorbing. $R$ does not annihilate: if $\min(P,S)>0$ then $f_e(P,0,S;A)\ge PS\,e^{-r-\Lambda}>0$.*

*(c) (C2′) $f_e$ is non-decreasing in each $A_k$; at live $x$ it increases strictly along an increment $t\,e_k$, $t>0$, if and only if the remaining-path response is strict, $\int_R^1\big[\lambda(u;A+te_k)-\lambda(u;A)\big]\,du<0$. When $\lambda$ is $C^1$ in $A_k$,*
$$\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial A_k}(x;A)=-f_e(x;A)\int_R^1\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial A_k}(u;A)\,du\;>\;0\qquad\text{wherever }f_e>0\text{ and }\int_R^1\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial A_k}\,du<0.$$

*(d) $f_e$ is non-decreasing in $P$, $S$ and $R$: strictly in $P$ (resp. $S$) wherever $S>0$ (resp. $P>0$), and strictly in $R$ wherever $f_e>0$ and $r+\lambda(R;A)>0$.*

*The class is non-empty — $\lambda\equiv\lambda_0>0$ is a member, as are the two witness environments of SM-B.3, whose channels are operative — and every member satisfies C1′–C4′.*

*Proof.* All claims are read off the closed form. (a) The exponent is $\le0$ ($r\ge0$, $\lambda\ge0$), giving the bounds. Continuity: $\lambda$ is bounded and uniformly continuous on $[0,1]\times\mathcal{A}$, so $(R,A)\mapsto\int_R^1\lambda(u;A)\,du$ is continuous — $\big|\int_R^1-\int_{R'}^1\big|\le\Lambda|R-R'|$, and $\sup_u|\lambda(u;A)-\lambda(u;A')|\to0$ as $A'\to A$ — and $f_e$ is a product and composition of continuous maps. (b) The factor $PS$ vanishes on $\mathcal{D}_0$, for every $A$; absorption holds because $P$ is constant, $S=0$ is a fixed point of $dS_t=-S_t\lambda\,dt$, and both states are absorbing with zero flow by specification. At $R=0$, $\int_0^1\lambda\,du\le\Lambda$ gives the lower bound. (c) $A$ enters the closed form only through $-\int_R^1\lambda(u;A)\,du$, so at live $x$
$$\frac{f_e(x;A+te_k)}{f_e(x;A)}=\exp\Big(-\int_R^1\big[\lambda(u;A+te_k)-\lambda(u;A)\big]\,du\Big),$$
which is $\ge1$ since $\lambda$ is non-increasing in $A_k$, and $>1$ exactly when the integrated response is strict; the displayed derivative is the chain rule with differentiation under the integral. (d) $\partial f_e/\partial P=Se^{(\cdot)}$, $\partial f_e/\partial S=Pe^{(\cdot)}$, and $\partial f_e/\partial R=\big(r+\lambda(R;A)\big)f_e$ by the Leibniz rule. $\square$

*Remark.* Part (b) is a design decision the model class makes and the paper defends: annihilation is carried by $P$ and $S$ only — the weakest-link boundary (Kremer, 1993) runs through potential and survival — while $R=0$ is a live state with strictly positive option value. An unrealized seed is an option, not a corpse: the gap-fund axis ($A_5$) exists precisely to fund seeds at $R\approx0$, and a model that killed them at the boundary would delete the very objects that funding channel exists for. Section 6's instant-fail type dies of $P\to0$, not of low $R$.

### SM-B.2 Boundary degeneracy of strictly increasing composites

Write $\succsim_e$ for the value ordering induced on $X\times\mathcal{A}$: $(x,A)\succsim_e(x',A')$ iff $f_e(x;A)\ge f_e(x';A')$. A triple $(g,h,\Phi)$ — $g:X\to\mathbb{R}$, $h:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}$, $\Phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ — *represents* $\succsim_e$ if
$$f_e(x;A)\ge f_e(x';A')\iff \Phi\big(g(x),h(A)\big)\ge\Phi\big(g(x'),h(A')\big)\qquad\text{for all pairs};$$
the biconditional transports strict inequalities and ties: $f_e>f_e'$ iff $\Phi>\Phi'$, and $f_e=f_e'$ iff $\Phi=\Phi'$. Call the institutional channel *operative* at a live state $x^{*}$ if there are an axis $k$, a profile $A^{\circ}$ and $t>0$ with $f_e(x^{*};A^{\circ}+te_k)>f_e(x^{*};A^{\circ})$.

**Lemma SM-B.2 (boundary degeneracy).** *Let $e$ be admissible with the channel operative somewhere. Then no triple with $\Phi$ strictly increasing in each argument represents $\succsim_e$.*

*Proof.* At the operative $x^{*}$ the representation gives $\Phi(g(x^{*}),h(A^{\circ}+te_k))>\Phi(g(x^{*}),h(A^{\circ}))$; were $h(A^{\circ}+te_k)\le h(A^{\circ})$, monotonicity of $\Phi$ would force $\le$; hence $h(A^{\circ}+te_k)>h(A^{\circ})$. On any boundary row $x_a\in\mathcal{D}_0$, C1′ gives $f_e(x_a;A^{\circ}+te_k)=f_e(x_a;A^{\circ})=0$, so the representation requires a tie at $x_a$ — while the $h$-gap and strict increase of $\Phi$ in its second argument force a strict gap. Contradiction. $\square$

This is the easy result, and we frame it as such: a function forced to vanish on a set where its institutional factor should still separate points cannot be strictly institution-sensitive there. It is an instance, transported across observation levels, of the known phenomenon that veto and annihilation structures defeat strictly monotone separable representations in noncompensatory aggregation (Fishburn, 1976; Bouyssou and Marchant, 2007); in separability language, C1′ negates single-factor independence of the institutional sector (Gorman, 1968; Blackorby, Primont and Russell, 1978). Its practical bite is limited. The composites evaluation practice actually uses — pure multiplication $g(x)h(A)$ with $g=0$ on the death set, geometric blends, CES aggregates with complements, min gates — are *weakly* monotone: flat in the institution exactly on the death set, sensitive off it, so they escape the lemma untouched. The real question is whether weakly monotone composites survive. They do not, under either of two richness conditions with observable content.

### SM-B.3 Two richness conditions and their witnesses

**Condition SH (stage-contingent operability).** *There exist axes $k\neq k'$, a base profile $A^{\circ}$, increments $t,s>0$, and live states $x_{\mathrm{lo}},x_{\mathrm{hi}}$ such that*
$$f_e(x_{\mathrm{lo}};A^{\circ}+te_k)>f_e(x_{\mathrm{lo}};A^{\circ}+se_{k'})\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_e(x_{\mathrm{hi}};A^{\circ}+te_k)<f_e(x_{\mathrm{hi}};A^{\circ}+se_{k'}).$$

This is the direct form: an institutional comparison that strictly reverses across venture strata. A structural sufficient condition in $\mathfrak{D}$ is stage-disjointness of the response supports — axis $k$ operative on early strata ($\lambda$ responds to $A_k$ only at low $u$), axis $k'$ on late strata. Its economic content is a standard fact of technology-transfer practice: incubation and entrepreneurship support ($A_3$) and seed scouting ($A_1$) act early in the realization journey, while licensing execution ($A_2$) and the corporate interface ($A_4$) act late, when there is something to license and someone to interface with (cf. the rubric anchors of SM-D.1). Different institutional capabilities bind at different venture stages.

**Witness (SH).** In $\mathfrak{D}$ take, for $\beta>0$ and $\lambda_0\ge8\beta$,
$$\lambda(u;A)=\lambda_0-\beta\,\mathbf{1}\big[u\in[0,\tfrac{3}{5})\big]\,A_k-\beta\,\mathbf{1}\big[u\in[\tfrac{3}{5},1]\big]\,A_{k'},$$
so that $\lambda\ge\lambda_0-4\beta\ge4\beta>0$ on the whole rubric range and $\lambda$ is non-increasing in each axis. (The stage cutoff can be smoothed to meet the continuity clause of Definition SM-B.1: replacing each indicator by a piecewise-linear ramp on an $\varepsilon$-window around $u=\tfrac{3}{5}$ perturbs every integral below by at most $8\beta\varepsilon$, hence every log-ratio below by at most $16\beta\varepsilon$, while the strict log-gaps displayed are $\beta/5$ and $2\beta/5$ — so any $\varepsilon<1/80$ preserves both strict inequalities; we compute with the sharp cutoff for arithmetic transparency.) Take one rubric step on each axis, $A:=A^{\circ}+e_k$ and $A':=A^{\circ}+e_{k'}$ (any base with $A^{\circ}_k,A^{\circ}_{k'}\le3$), and states $x_{\mathrm{lo}}=(P,0,S)$, $x_{\mathrm{hi}}=(P,\tfrac{3}{5},S)$, any $P,S>0$. In value ratios the discount and base-profile terms cancel; only the increments' integrated hazard reductions remain. At $x_{\mathrm{lo}}$ ($R=0$),
$$\int_0^1\lambda(u;A)\,du=\int_0^1\lambda(u;A^{\circ})\,du-\tfrac{3}{5}\beta,\qquad\int_0^1\lambda(u;A')\,du=\int_0^1\lambda(u;A^{\circ})\,du-\tfrac{2}{5}\beta,$$
so $f_e(x_{\mathrm{lo}};A)/f_e(x_{\mathrm{lo}};A')=\exp\big(\tfrac{3\beta}{5}-\tfrac{2\beta}{5}\big)=e^{\beta/5}>1$. At $x_{\mathrm{hi}}$ ($R=\tfrac{3}{5}$) the remaining path $[\tfrac{3}{5},1]$ misses the support of axis $k$ entirely, so
$$\int_{3/5}^1\lambda(u;A)\,du=\int_{3/5}^1\lambda(u;A^{\circ})\,du,\qquad\int_{3/5}^1\lambda(u;A')\,du=\int_{3/5}^1\lambda(u;A^{\circ})\,du-\tfrac{2}{5}\beta,$$
and $f_e(x_{\mathrm{hi}};A)/f_e(x_{\mathrm{hi}};A')=\exp\big(0-\tfrac{2\beta}{5}\big)=e^{-2\beta/5}<1$. SH holds with $t=s=1$: one rubric step on the early axis beats one on the late axis for a seed at the start of its journey, and loses to it for a seed past the early stage.

**Condition ED (exposure-duration asymmetry).** Institutions act over the venture's *remaining* journey, and ventures differ in how much journey remains. Direct form: *there exist live states $x,x'$ and profiles $A\le A'$ (componentwise, $A'$ strictly better on an operative axis) such that*
$$f_e(x;A)>f_e(x';A)\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_e(x;A')<f_e(x';A')$$
— a *venture* comparison that strictly reverses as the institution strengthens.

**Witness (ED).** In $\mathfrak{D}$ take the stage-uniform hazard $\lambda(u;A)=c\,(4-A_k)$ with $c>0$ (i.e. $\lambda_0=4c$ exactly; any $\lambda_0\in[4c,6c)$ delivers both signs below, and $\lambda_0=4c$ is cleanest) and $r=0$; $\lambda\ge0$ on the rubric range and is non-increasing in $A_k$. The closed form reads $f_e=PS\,e^{-c(4-A_k)(1-R)}$. Take a nearly-done and an early state half a journey apart, $x=(P,\tfrac{3}{4},S)$ and $x'=(P',\tfrac{1}{4},S')$, with mass ratio $P'S'=e^{c}\,PS$ (feasible since $P$ is unbounded: e.g. $S'=S$, $P'=e^{c}P$), and let $A'$ improve $A$ by the full rubric range on axis $k$: $A_k=0$, $A'=A+4e_k$. Then
$$\log\frac{f_e(x;A)}{f_e(x';A)}=\log\frac{PS}{P'S'}+c\,(4-A_k)\Big[\big(1-\tfrac{1}{4}\big)-\big(1-\tfrac{3}{4}\big)\Big]=-c+\frac{c\,(4-A_k)}{2},$$
which at $A_k=0$ equals $-c+2c=c>0$ — the nearly-done seed wins at the weak nursery — and at $A'_k=4$ (hazard zero) equals $-c<0$ — the early seed wins at the strong one. Raw values make the mechanism visible: at $A_k=0$, $f_e(x;A)=PS\,e^{-c}$ against $f_e(x';A)=PS\,e^{-2c}$; at $A'_k=4$, $f_e(x;A')=PS$ against $f_e(x';A')=e^{c}PS$. Less remaining hazard favors the nearly-realized seed at a weak nursery; a strong nursery's nursing compounds over the early seed's longer remaining road ($1-R'=\tfrac{3}{4}$ against $1-R=\tfrac{1}{4}$) and reverses the ranking.

Two features of the pair deserve note. First, in the ED witness the institutional ordering itself is venture-uniform — $f_e(\cdot\,;A')\ge f_e(\cdot\,;A)$ everywhere, by Lemma SM-B.1(c) — and the hazard is stage-uniform, so this witness satisfies ED while violating SH; it is the *venture* comparison that flips. SH and ED are logically independent mechanisms: SH reverses institutional comparisons across venture strata, ED reverses venture comparisons across institutional strength. Second, both conditions are *empirical statements with observable content*, not normative axioms: whether early-acting and late-acting capabilities exist, and whether institutional value scales with remaining exposure, are inspectable features of a technology-transfer environment. They play exactly the role that domain richness plays in preference-aggregation impossibilities (Arrow, 1963), and the registered program of Section 7 treats them as premises to be audited, not assumed.

### SM-B.4 Theorem 3: the single-index impossibility

**Theorem 3 (single-index impossibility).** *Let $e$ be an admissible environment satisfying SH or ED. Then there exist no functions $g$ of the ledger state, $h$ of the institutional profile, and $\Phi:\mathbb{R}^2\to\mathbb{R}$ such that $\Phi(g(x),h(A))$ represents the value ordering $\succsim_e$ — that is, $f_e(x;A)\ge f_e(x';A')\iff\Phi(g(x),h(A))\ge\Phi(g(x'),h(A'))$ for all pairs — with $\Phi$ non-decreasing in its institutional argument (under SH), or with $\Phi$ non-decreasing in its venture argument (under ED).*

*Proof.* Under SH, let $(A,A',x_{\mathrm{lo}},x_{\mathrm{hi}})$, $A=A^{\circ}+te_k$, $A'=A^{\circ}+se_{k'}$, realize the reversal. The representation at $x_{\mathrm{lo}}$ requires $\Phi(g(x_{\mathrm{lo}}),h(A))>\Phi(g(x_{\mathrm{lo}}),h(A'))$; were $h(A)\le h(A')$, $\Phi$ non-decreasing in its institutional argument would force $\le$; hence $h(A)>h(A')$. At $x_{\mathrm{hi}}$ the true order requires $\Phi(g(x_{\mathrm{hi}}),h(A))<\Phi(g(x_{\mathrm{hi}}),h(A'))$; but $h(A)>h(A')$ and monotonicity force $\ge$. Contradiction. Under ED the argument is symmetric in the first coordinate: $f_e(x;A)>f_e(x';A)$ forces $g(x)>g(x')$, and then $f_e(x;A')<f_e(x';A')$ requires $\Phi(g(x),h(A'))<\Phi(g(x'),h(A'))$ while $g(x)>g(x')$ and monotonicity in the venture argument force $\ge$. $\blacksquare$

*Remarks.* (i) *Scope.* Nothing was assumed about $g$ and $h$ — no monotonicity, no continuity; the only structure used is that the institutional information reaches the score through a single index used monotonically (under ED, symmetrically, the venture information). The excluded class therefore contains every composite in evaluation practice — pure multiplication, weighted addition, geometric and CES blends, min/max gates, lexicographic hybrids — and every monotone recalibration of any of them. Practice endorses monotonicity in *both* arguments — a better venture score, or a better institution score, should never lower the composite — so either richness condition alone is fatal.

(ii) *Why a static axiomatization cannot deliver this.* On the static domain, weakly monotone separable scores consistent with annihilation and institutional monotonicity exist: $f=\min\big(PRS,\kappa(A)\big)$, with $\kappa$ any strictly increasing index calibrated to the institution's induced response, vanishes with the venture ledger and never decreases in any argument, and an axiom system that constrains $f$ pointwise cannot exclude it. What excludes it is stage-contingency of the *dynamics*: under SH the value ordering of two institutions genuinely reverses across venture strata, and no single institutional index — however cleverly calibrated — can lie on both sides of a reversal. The dynamic foundation is therefore not a technical reformulation of Section 4 but the source of the theorem's content.

(iii) *Tightness.* Where the channel is inoperative ($\lambda$ free of $A$), $f_e$ does not depend on $A$ and $\Phi(g,\mathrm{const})$ with $g=f_e$ trivially represents $\succsim_e$. In stage-uniform, duration-degenerate corners of the class the same holds on restricted slices: with $\lambda(u;A)=\bar\lambda(A)$ and comparisons confined to a common readiness $R=\bar R$, the closed form is exactly multiplicative, $f_e=[PS]\cdot[e^{-(r+\bar\lambda(A))(1-\bar R)}]$, and the monotone composite $\Phi(g,h)=g\,h$ represents that slice of $\succsim_e$. The exclusions are thus tied to the named structures: where the channel is null the composite is trivially available, and where stage-contingency and duration heterogeneity are both switched off, monotone composites can represent restricted slices of the ordering. Whether they can represent the full ordering in such degenerate corners is not needed here and not claimed. The premises are observable: SH and ED belong to the registered program of Section 7, so the theorem's empirical discipline is explicit.

(iv) *Boundary versus interior.* Lemma SM-B.2's obstruction lives on the death boundary; Theorem 3's lives at live states — both witnesses have $\min(P,S)>0$. The impossibility bites exactly where committees operate: on pre-screened live portfolios from which the corpses have already been removed.

### SM-B.5 Corollary 3.2: the portfolio impossibility

**Corollary 3.2 (portfolio impossibility).** *Let $\mathfrak{E}$ be a family of admissible environments such that for some axis $k$, profile $A^{\circ}$, increment $t>0$ and live state $x^{*}$ there are $e,e'\in\mathfrak{E}$ with the channel operative in $e$ — $f_e(x^{*};A^{\circ}+te_k)>f_e(x^{*};A^{\circ})$ — and null in $e'$ — $f_{e'}(x^{*};A^{\circ}+te_k)=f_{e'}(x^{*};A^{\circ})$. Then no single triple $(g,h,\Phi)$ — indeed no environment-blind function $F(x,A)$ whatsoever — represents $\succsim_e$ for every $e\in\mathfrak{E}$.*

*Proof.* A single $F$ assigns one order to the pair $\{(x^{*},A^{\circ}+te_k),(x^{*},A^{\circ})\}$; representing $\succsim_e$ requires the strict order, representing $\succsim_{e'}$ requires the tie. $\blacksquare$

*Remark.* This is the Arrow-style reading of the paper's result: as in Arrow (1963), what defeats the aggregation rule is richness of the admissible *domain* — a committee portfolio spanning technology domains whose response structures differ — not any single profile. It is the formal statement of the committee-room fact that one formula is applied to forty seeds drawn from forty response structures. We state its weight honestly: the proof is immediate and the content is entirely in the domain condition (a hazard responsive to $A_2$ in a licensing-driven domain, unresponsive where the binding constraint lies elsewhere). The within-environment Theorem 3, which defeats the single index inside one fixed response structure, is the sharper result.

### SM-B.6 Corollary 3.1: registered statistical diagnostics of single-score practice

**Setting: one class, two conditioning sets, three diagnostics.** Suppose the data are generated by an admissible environment with operative channels, and suppose practice ranks — and funds, and therefore samples — by a single monotone composite $V=\Phi(g(x),h(A))$. Theorem 3 locates the error of the composite exactly: $h(A)$ is a venture-uniform institutional index, while institutional value is stage-contingent (SH) and exposure-scaled (ED). This subsection converts that location into registered statistics, and the conversion requires two pieces of bookkeeping that the corollary's earlier statements left implicit. First, *timing*: every venture regressor below is timestamped at the decision date, so that the institution still has the remaining journey to act on; we call conditioning on such regressors *decision-time stratification*, and conditioning on realized post-decision paths *trajectory conditioning*. The two conditioning sets carry opposite predictions from the same model — positive institutional slopes under the first, a residual zero under the second — and keeping them named prevents the apparent contradiction discussed after Proposition SM-B.2. Second, *sampling*: field data are funded portfolios, and the funding rule is part of the data-generating process. The three diagnostics are not on an equal footing, and we say exactly how: (ii) is a proposition of the class under decision-time stratification (Proposition SM-B.2) and is the primary registered diagnostic, with (iii) its test form; (i) is a proposition of the class *plus one auditable premise about practice* — that the observed sample was selected on a single monotone score (Definition SM-B.2) — and Proposition SM-B.1 derives it in closed form, with the selection premise doing exactly the work the corollary's prose always claimed for the field's ranking habit.

**Notation.** For a candidate seed at its decision date, write $v$ for the decision-time venture index (decision-time $\mathrm{PRS}$ or a strictly increasing transform of it), $a$ for the registered institutional regressor (a single axis $A_k$ or the $\mathrm{ERS}$ aggregate; multi-axis statements stack axes), and $y$ for the registry outcome at the stated horizon, ordered as $f_e$. The protocol strata are the *annihilated cell* $\mathcal{Q}_0:=\{\min(P,S)\le\underline{s}\}$, split out at a registered liveness floor $\underline{s}$, together with quartiles $\mathcal{Q}_1,\dots,\mathcal{Q}_4$ of decision-time $\mathrm{PRS}$ among live states; the split matters because $\mathrm{PRS}$ conflates the annihilation margin with the earliness margin, and the two carry opposite slope predictions (collapse at $\mathcal{Q}_0$; maximal remaining exposure at low live quartiles). $\gamma^{q}$ (with axis subscript $\gamma_k^{q}$ where needed) denotes the institutional slope within stratum $\mathcal{Q}_q$ estimated with the continuous decision-time control retained inside the stratum, and $\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$ the coefficient on $a$ in the pooled regression of $y$ on $a$ alone — the marginal association, no venture control. Signature (i) is the marginal-versus-partial sign contrast in Simpson's (1951) sense; the stratification serves signature (ii)'s slope-heterogeneity claim, the continuous within-stratum control serves the collider algebra below.

#### SM-B.6.1 The selection data-generating process

**Definition SM-B.2 (score-selected sampling).** A *score-selected sample* is generated in three steps.

(D1) *Population.* Candidate seeds carry $(v,a,\varepsilon)$ with $v\perp a$, $\varepsilon\perp(v,a)$, and finite second moments. Population independence of $v$ and $a$ is the conservative benchmark — it hands the selection mechanism nothing to work with; the assortative case is Remark (b) below.

(D2) *Outcomes, per the class.* $y=\beta_v v+\beta_a a+\varepsilon$ with $\beta_v>0$, $\beta_a\ge0$. This is the linear read-out of the benchmark $\mathfrak{D}$: by Lemma SM-B.1(c) the institutional slope of log value over the remaining journey is $\Gamma_k(R;A)=-\int_R^1\partial\lambda/\partial A_k\,du\ge0$, and $\beta_a$ is its forward, post-decision average — the part of the institutional effect *not yet transmitted* into $v$. Proposition SM-B.1 needs only the algebraic form and is stated for a homogeneous $\beta_a$, the presentation least favorable to producing a reversal in the sense that stratum-varying slopes are extra structure, not extra negativity: the collider term below is a property of the selected $(v,a)$ distribution alone and does not depend on the outcome equation. Stratum-varying slopes are the subject of Proposition SM-B.2.

(D3) *Selection.* The field funds — and the analyst therefore observes — by a single monotone score: $D=\mathbf{1}\{s(v,a)\ge\bar s\}$ with $s$ strictly increasing in both arguments and $0<\Pr(D=1)<1$. A committee that ranks by one composite and funds above a bar is exactly this object; nothing else about $s$ is used in Lemma SM-B.3. The *Gaussian benchmark* specializes: $v\sim N(\mu_v,\sigma_v^2)$, $a\sim N(\mu_a,\sigma_a^2)$ independent, $s=w_vv+w_aa$ with $w_v,w_a>0$.

**Lemma SM-B.3 (single-score selection induces negative venture–institution dependence).** *In the model of Definition SM-B.2, without the Gaussian specialization:*

*(a) $\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D=1)\le 0$, with strict inequality whenever the selection boundary genuinely trades the two arguments — the marginal threshold $\psi(v):=\inf\{a: s(v,a)\ge\bar s\}$ is non-constant across a positive-probability set of funded $v$ values whose truncated means of $a$ differ.*

*(b) If selection is instead by conditional gating — $D=\mathbf{1}\{v\ge c_v\}\,\mathbf{1}\{a\ge c_a\}$, a product event — then $v$ and $a$ remain independent in the funded sample and the induced covariance is exactly zero.*

*Proof.* (a) Since $s$ is strictly increasing in $a$, the funded event is $\{a\ge\psi(v)\}$ with $\psi$ non-increasing in $v$ ($s$ increasing in $v$: a stronger venture clears the bar with a weaker institution). By $v\perp a$, the conditional law of $a$ given $(v,D=1)$ is the marginal law of $a$ truncated to $[\psi(v),\infty)$, so $m(v):=E[a\mid v,D=1]=E[a\mid a\ge\psi(v)]$. The truncated mean $t\mapsto E[a\mid a\ge t]$ is non-decreasing, hence $m$ is non-increasing in $v$. By the tower property and the two-copy (Chebyshev association) argument — for i.i.d. draws $(v_1,v_2)$ from the funded sample, $E[(v_1-v_2)(m(v_1)-m(v_2))]\le0$ pointwise since $m$ is non-increasing —
$$\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D=1)=\operatorname{Cov}\big(v,\,m(v)\mid D=1\big)=\tfrac12\,E\big[(v_1-v_2)(m(v_1)-m(v_2))\big]\;\le\;0,$$
strictly when $m$ is non-constant on the funded support, which is clause (a)'s condition. (b) With a product event, $\psi\equiv c_a$ on the funded support and the joint conditional law factorizes: $(v,a)\mid D=1$ is the product of the two one-sided truncations. Independence, hence zero covariance. $\square$

No distributional assumption, no linearity of the score, no Gaussianity: the negative dependence is a property of the *practice* — compensatory single-score funding — not of a parametric benchmark. Conditioning on being funded is Berkson's (1946) hospital-admission mechanism [needs S6 verification] transported to the committee room, and clause (b) is its exact off-switch: the conditional-gating design that Section 4 recommends, because it never trades the two ledgers against each other, inscribes no dependence at all.

**Lemma SM-B.4 (Gaussian selection moments).** *In the Gaussian benchmark, let $\sigma_s^2=w_v^2\sigma_v^2+w_a^2\sigma_a^2$, $\zeta=(\bar s-\mu_s)/\sigma_s$, and let $\delta(\zeta)=M(\zeta)\{M(\zeta)-\zeta\}\in(0,1)$ with $M(\zeta)=\phi(\zeta)/\overline N(\zeta)$, where $\phi$ and $\overline N$ are the standard normal density and upper tail (no relation to the composite $\Phi$ of Theorem 3, which we reserve). Then, in the funded sample,*
$$\operatorname{Var}(s\mid D)=(1-\delta)\,\sigma_s^2,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D)=-\,\delta\,\frac{w_vw_a\,\sigma_v^2\sigma_a^2}{\sigma_s^2},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(a\mid D)=\sigma_a^2\Big(1-\delta\,\frac{w_a^2\sigma_a^2}{\sigma_s^2}\Big)>0,$$
*and symmetrically for $\operatorname{Var}(v\mid D)$. The truncation factor $\delta$ is strictly increasing in $\zeta$, with $\delta\to0$ as $\zeta\to-\infty$ (no selection) and $\delta\to1$ as $\zeta\to+\infty$ (knife-edge selection); since $\Pr(D=1)=\overline N(\zeta)$, $\delta$ is pinned by the program's acceptance rate alone: $\delta=2/\pi\approx0.64$ at a $50\%$ acceptance rate, $\approx0.76$ at $25\%$, $\approx0.83$ at $10\%$.*

*Proof.* Decompose $u=b_u s+u_\perp$ for $u\in\{v,a\}$, with $b_u=\operatorname{Cov}(u,s)/\sigma_s^2$ and $u_\perp\perp s$ by joint normality. Conditioning on the $s$-event $\{s\ge\bar s\}$ leaves the laws of $(v_\perp,a_\perp)$ and their independence of $s$ untouched, so for $u,u'\in\{v,a\}$,
$$\operatorname{Cov}(u,u'\mid D)=b_ub_{u'}\operatorname{Var}(s\mid D)+\operatorname{Cov}(u_\perp,u'_\perp),$$
with $\operatorname{Var}(s\mid D)=(1-\delta)\sigma_s^2$ the standard one-sided normal truncation formula and $\operatorname{Cov}(v_\perp,a_\perp)=0-b_vb_a\sigma_s^2=-w_vw_a\sigma_v^2\sigma_a^2/\sigma_s^2$ from population independence. Substituting $b_v=w_v\sigma_v^2/\sigma_s^2$, $b_a=w_a\sigma_a^2/\sigma_s^2$ gives the displays; positivity of $\operatorname{Var}(a\mid D)$ follows from $\delta<1$ and $\sigma_s^2-w_a^2\sigma_a^2=w_v^2\sigma_v^2>0$. Monotonicity and the limits of $\delta$ are standard properties of one-sided normal truncation. $\square$

#### SM-B.6.2 Signature (i): the Simpson reversal, derived

**Proposition SM-B.1 (pooled-sign reversal under score-selected sampling).** *In the model of Definition SM-B.2 with the Gaussian benchmark:*

*(a) (Selection-robustness of the conditional regression.) In the funded sample, the linear projection of $y$ on $(1,v,a)$ has slope coefficients exactly $(\beta_v,\beta_a)$: since $D$ is $(v,a)$-measurable and $\varepsilon\perp(v,a)$, the conditional mean $E[y\mid v,a,D=1]=\beta_vv+\beta_aa+E[\varepsilon]$ is unchanged by selection, and a linear projection recovers a linear conditional mean under any regressor distribution. Decision-time stratification with the within-stratum continuous control therefore estimates $\beta_a\ge0$ without selection bias.*

*(b) (Pooled coefficient.) The funded-sample regression of $y$ on $a$ alone has slope*
$$\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}\;=\;\beta_a\;+\;\beta_v\,\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D)}{\operatorname{Var}(a\mid D)}
\;=\;\beta_a\;-\;\beta_v\,\frac{\delta\,w_vw_a\,\sigma_v^2}{\sigma_s^2-\delta\,w_a^2\sigma_a^2}\,.$$

*(c) (Reversal condition.) $\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}<0$ — the pooled institutional coefficient negative while every venture-controlled slope equals $\beta_a\ge0$ — if and only if*
$$\delta\;>\;\bar\delta\;:=\;\frac{\beta_a\,\sigma_s^2}{\beta_v\,w_vw_a\,\sigma_v^2+\beta_a\,w_a^2\,\sigma_a^2}\,,$$
*and $\bar\delta<1$ — a non-empty reversal region in truncation severity — if and only if*
$$\frac{w_a}{w_v}\;>\;\frac{\beta_a}{\beta_v}\,,$$
*i.e. the committee's score weights the institution, relative to the venture, more heavily than the forward outcome process does. In the limiting case $\beta_a=0$ — the institution's effect fully transmitted into the decision-time state — any selection severity $\delta>0$ makes $\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}$ strictly negative against a within-stratum slope of exactly zero: the pure collider artifact.*

*Proof.* (a) is argued in the statement. (b) is omitted-variable algebra in the funded sample, $\gamma^{\mathrm{pool}}=\beta_a+\beta_v\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D)/\operatorname{Var}(a\mid D)$, with Lemma SM-B.4 substituted. (c) The collider term in (b) is strictly increasing in $\delta$ (numerator increasing, denominator decreasing), running from $0$ at $\delta=0$ to $\beta_v\,w_a/w_v$ at $\delta=1$ (using $\sigma_s^2-w_a^2\sigma_a^2=w_v^2\sigma_v^2$); solving $\beta_a=\beta_v\,\delta\,w_vw_a\sigma_v^2/(\sigma_s^2-\delta w_a^2\sigma_a^2)$ for $\delta$ gives $\bar\delta$, and $\bar\delta<1$ reduces to $\beta_aw_v<\beta_vw_a$. $\blacksquare$

*Remark (the mechanism is the ranking practice — the corollary's new reading).* Every ingredient of the pooled coefficient is non-negative except one, and the negative one is manufactured by the funding rule: within-stratum slopes are $\ge0$ by Lemma SM-B.1(c), the population dependence is zero (or favorable; Remark (b)), and the sole negative term is the collider covariance that exists because the observed portfolio was *ranked into existence* by a compensatory score. The earlier formulation of this corollary carried a logical oddity the referees were right to press: the field's practice ("suppose a field nonetheless ranks by a single score") played no role in generating the predicted patterns. It now plays the leading role. Signature (i) is not "misspecification leaves traces"; it is: *the act of ranking by one score writes the scar* — and under conditional gating (Lemma SM-B.3(b)) the scar vanishes identically, so (i) doubles as an audit statistic for which selection regime generated a portfolio. Two consequences for the registry protocol. First, (i)'s null is uninformative wherever the sampled programs gate rather than rank, so the protocol's instrument audit (SM-B.6.6) records, program by program, which regime applies; absent that record neither rejection nor non-rejection is attributable. Second, (i) does not require SH or ED: it fires even in stage-uniform corners of the class, because its engine is C3′-transmission plus compensatory selection. The division of labor is exact — (i) tests the *practice*, (ii) tests the *stage-contingency* that powers Theorem 3.

*Remark (how demanding is the reversal region?).* Not demanding, on either axis of the condition. On severity: by Lemma SM-B.4, $\delta$ is pinned by the acceptance rate and is large at every realistic one — $0.64$ at a $50\%$ rate, $0.83$ at $10\%$ — so $\bar\delta$ is crossed unless the committee's institutional weight is nearly aligned with the forward process. On the weight condition: $\beta_a$ is only the *forward remainder* of the institutional effect — the part not yet transmitted into $v$ at the decision date — so a committee that credits the institution its *total* contribution, past nurturing included, mechanically overweights it relative to $\beta_a/\beta_v$. Over-crediting is the generic case, not a knife-edge. (A worked point on the numbers: $\beta_v=1$, $\beta_a=0.15$, unit variances, equal weights gives $\bar\delta\approx0.26$; a $10\%$-acceptance program has $\delta\approx0.83$ and a pooled slope of $\approx-0.56$ against a within-stratum slope of $+0.15$.)

*Remark (assortative populations).* If instead $\operatorname{Cov}(v,a)=\sigma_{va}>0$ in the population — stronger institutions attract better seeds — the same orthogonal decomposition yields $\operatorname{Cov}(v,a\mid D)=\sigma_{va}-\delta\operatorname{Cov}(v,s)\operatorname{Cov}(a,s)/\sigma_s^2$, and the conclusion survives with a shifted threshold: as $\delta\to1$ the funded sample concentrates on a score level set, along which $a$ is an exactly decreasing linear function of $v$, so $\operatorname{Corr}(v,a\mid D)\to-1$ for any population correlation short of one. Sufficient selection severity forces the observed dependence negative regardless of favorable sorting; only the crossing point moves.

*Remark (the Section 6 anchor).* The observable face of Lemma SM-B.3 is compositional before it is regressional: in a score-selected portfolio, stronger institutions hold earlier-stage — lower-$v$ — projects, because $\operatorname{Corr}(v,a\mid D)<0$. Practitioners read this tilt as a fact about where hard projects go; the lemma derives it as a property of the funding rule, with no causal statement about institutions preferring early seeds. Section 6's cross-case paragraph reports exactly this tilt in the studio panel and routes it here rather than to a causal reading; it is the paper's empirical anchor for the selection mechanism, and — being a first moment rather than a regression — it is the piece of (i) that small samples can actually exhibit.

#### SM-B.6.3 Signature (ii): decision-time stratification, the primary diagnostic

**Proposition SM-B.2 (within-stratum institutional slopes under decision-time stratification).** *Let outcomes be generated by an admissible environment; work in the benchmark $\mathfrak{D}$ for closed forms. By the hazard reading of Definition SM-B.1, the conditional mean of the realized outcome given the decision-time state is $f_e$, so slopes of outcome regressions on decision-time regressors are slopes of $f_e$; the latent-linear implementation writes $y^{*}=\log f_e+\eta$ with $\eta\perp A$ (the ordered-outcome implementation is SM-B.6.5(1)). Stratify on the protocol strata $\mathcal{Q}_0,\mathcal{Q}_1,\dots,\mathcal{Q}_4$ at the decision date, and let $\gamma_k^{q}$ be the within-stratum slope on axis $k$ with the continuous decision-time control retained. Then:*

*(a) (Operative strata are positive.) $\gamma_k^{q}=E\big[\Gamma_k(R;A)\mid\mathcal{Q}_q\big]\ge0$, with $\Gamma_k(R;A)=-\int_R^1\partial\lambda/\partial A_k(u;A)\,du$ the remaining-path response of Lemma SM-B.1(c) — an exact equality for hazards linear in $A_k$, as in both witnesses, and the average-derivative estimand in general; $\gamma_k^{q}>0$ exactly where the axis's response support intersects the stratum's remaining journeys — the institution still to act.*

*(b) (The annihilated stratum collapses.) In the level metric, $\partial f_e/\partial A_k=f_e\,\Gamma_k\le PS\,\bar\Gamma$ with $\bar\Gamma:=\sup\Gamma_k<\infty$, so the institutional slope vanishes uniformly in $A$ as $\min(P,S)\to0$: $\gamma_k^{0}\approx0$ at $\mathcal{Q}_0$, not because the channel is closed but because there is nothing left for it to save (C1′). The annihilated-cell null is a prediction about the level (outcome-probability) metric, which is where the registry estimates it.*

*(c) (SH: slopes vary across strata, with an axis-resolved crossing.) Under stage-local response supports the slope profile is axis-specific: in the SH witness of SM-B.3, the early axis carries $\Gamma_k(R)=\beta\,(\tfrac35-R)^{+}$ — strictly declining across live strata and exactly zero once $R\ge\tfrac35$ — while the late axis carries $\Gamma_{k'}(R)=\beta\,\big(1-\max(R,\tfrac35)\big)$ — flat at $\tfrac{2\beta}{5}$ over early strata, declining only late. Within-stratum slopes on the two axes therefore cross across strata; a stratum-common institutional coefficient is misspecified, and the misspecification has a sign structure.*

*(d) (ED: slopes scale with remaining exposure.) In the ED witness, $\log f_e$ is linear in $A_k$ with coefficient $c\,(1-R)$, so $\gamma_k^{q}=c\,E[1-R\mid\mathcal{Q}_q]$: largest at early live strata, declining monotonically to the top stratum, with the annihilated cell's $\approx0$ breaking the pattern at the bottom. The registered profile across $(\mathcal{Q}_0,\mathcal{Q}_1,\dots,\mathcal{Q}_4)$ is thus $\approx0$, then high, then declining.*

*(e) (Trajectory conditioning: the residual zero.) Conditional on the realized carrier path — the trajectory of $(R_t,S_t,F_t)$ and its $\sigma_{SU}$-exposure up to the outcome horizon — the conditional outcome law is free of $A$: in $\mathfrak{D}$, realized value is $e^{-r\tau}PS_\tau$, a functional of the realized path alone, and $A$ enters only the law of that path (C3′). The residual institutional slope under trajectory conditioning is exactly zero.*

*Proof.* (a) and (d): $A$ enters the closed form of $f_e$ only through $-\int_R^1\lambda(u;A)\,du$, so $\partial\log f_e/\partial A_k=\Gamma_k(R;A)$; averaging over the stratum and using Lemma SM-B.1(c) for the sign. (b): $0\le f_e\le PS$ (Lemma SM-B.1(a)) and the product rule. (c): integrate the witness hazard as in SM-B.3. (e): read off the closed form; in the general class, $A$ appears only in the generator of the carrier process, so conditioning on the realized carrier path exhausts the transmission. $\blacksquare$

*Remark (two regressions, two timestamps: the Section 6 tension dissolved).* Clause (a) and clause (e) are the two regressions Section 6 reports in one paragraph, and they are not in tension — they are the same model read at two timestamps. *Decision-time stratification* conditions on pre-transmission information: the institution's remaining action is ahead of the conditioning set, so its slope is the forward transmission, positive at operative strata. *Trajectory conditioning* conditions on post-transmission information: everything the institution transmits is already recorded in the conditioning set, so the residual slope is zero — and this zero is nothing other than the observational face of channel completeness, the hypothesis SM-B.7 tests; a nonzero residual here is a direct channel there. A reader who collapses the two conditionings will believe the paper's own fingerprint paragraph contradicts signature (ii); the corollary's registered test is the first regression, the C3′ fingerprint is the second, and both are predictions of the same class. Section 6's cross-case paragraph now names each with one clause and points here.

*Remark (mediator status and why the timestamp fixes it).* Decision-time $\mathrm{PRS}$ is downstream of $A$: the nursery acted before the decision date too, so the stratifier is a mediator of the institution's total effect, and $\gamma^{q}$ estimates the *forward* (post-decision) transmission only. Both halves of this are intended. On the estimand: Corollary 3.1 targets the wedge between a venture-uniform institutional index and stage-contingent institutional value, and that wedge is a statement about the forward effect at each stage — SH and ED are forward statements — so the mediator is the *correct* conditioning variable, and the decision-time timestamp is what makes it well defined: the stratifier is measurable strictly before the exposure whose effect is being read, so the design conditions on a pre-exposure covariate of the remaining journey, not on post-outcome information, and pooled-versus-conditional differences are not the ordinary mediation arithmetic of a mistimed control. What the diagnostic does *not* estimate — deliberately — is the total effect of $A$, past transmission included; no clause of the corollary needs it. On the caveat: conditioning on a mediator can open a non-causal path between $a$ and the outcome disturbance if pre-decision unobservables that moved $v$ persist into post-decision hazards; the benchmark of Definition SM-B.2 excludes this by $\varepsilon\perp(v,a)$, and the registry meets its field analogue with pre-decision covariate adjustment and the errors-in-variables strategy of SM-B.6.5(2).*

*Remark (what is distinctive, against generic complementarity).* Garden-variety complementarity — "strong TTOs help good projects more" — predicts institutional slopes *increasing* in venture quality, uniformly across axes. The class predicts the opposite shape on two margins and a crossing on a third: the annihilated cell's collapse (b), ED's *decline* of slopes across live strata (d), and SH's axis-resolved crossing (c) in which early-axis slopes die out across strata while late-axis slopes persist. These, not bare slope inequality, are the registered patterns; bare inequality would reject additivity without distinguishing the class from complementarity, and the protocol scores the profile, not merely the omnibus test.

#### SM-B.6.4 Signature (iii): the slope-equality test, registered form

The registered estimating equation, per institutional regressor and with $j(i)$ the host institution of seed $i$:
$$y_i\;=\;\sum_{q=0}^{4}\mathbf{1}\{Q_i=q\}\big(\alpha_q+\gamma^{q}a_i+\phi_q\,v_i\big)+x_i'\beta+\varepsilon_i,\qquad \varepsilon_i \text{ clustered by } j(i),$$
with $x_i$ pre-decision covariates. The diagnostic is the Wald test — equivalently a Chow-type test of slope equality — of
$$H_0:\ \gamma^{0}=\gamma^{1}=\cdots=\gamma^{4},\qquad
W=(R\hat\gamma)'\big[R\,\hat V\,R'\big]^{-1}(R\hat\gamma)\ \sim\ \chi^2_{4}\ \text{under }H_0,$$
where $R$ stacks the four independent equality restrictions and $\hat V$ is the cluster-robust covariance of $\hat\gamma=(\hat\gamma^{0},\dots,\hat\gamma^{4})'$; clustering at the institution is not optional, since seeds sharing a nursery share its shocks. Equivalently, $W$ is the cluster-robust Wald test that all $\mathrm{ERS}\times\mathrm{PRS}$-stratum interactions vanish. Under Proposition SM-B.2 the noncentrality is carried by the $\Gamma$-profile, and two directional contrasts are registered alongside the omnibus statistic because they carry the class's signed predictions: the annihilated-cell contrast $\gamma^{0}$ versus the pooled operative slopes (clause (b)), and the ED trend across live strata (clause (d)).

*Remark (Hausman contrast, demoted).* The earlier formulation registered a Hausman (1978) statistic contrasting the stratum-common estimator $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{pool}}$ with the stratum-free $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{strat}}$. It tests the same null and adds no identifying content, and it is retained only as a remark, in corrected form: $H=d'\big(\hat V_{\mathrm{strat}}-\hat V_{\mathrm{pool}}\big)^{-}d$ with $d=\hat\theta_{\mathrm{strat}}-\hat\theta_{\mathrm{pool}}$ is asymptotically $\chi^2$ with degrees of freedom equal to the *number of equality restrictions* (here $4$, the rank of the variance contrast) — not $\dim\theta$ — and its variance-difference form presumes the pooled estimator efficient under the null, which pooled OLS is not under the clustered, heteroskedastic error structures registry data will carry; the contrast then need not be positive semi-definite. A pairs-cluster bootstrap of $d$ repairs the size where the contrast form is wanted as a robustness companion. The Wald form above is the same test without the efficiency premise and is the registered primary.

#### SM-B.6.5 Estimation caveats (inherited verbatim by the registry protocol)

**(1) Ordered outcome and the cross-stratum comparison problem.** The registry outcome is a three-class ordered variable, while Propositions SM-B.1–B.2 are derived on a linear (latent-index) link. Ordered logit/probit coefficients are identified only up to stratum-specific residual scale, so equality of index coefficients across strata is not testable from ordered-model coefficients alone: apparent slope instability can be manufactured, and true instability masked, by residual-variance differences across strata (Mood, 2010) [needs S6 verification]. Prescription: primary estimation by linear probability models on the outcome-class indicators — the estimands of the propositions in the level metric, which is also where the annihilated-cell prediction (b) lives — or by the latent-$y^{*}$ formulation with pre-registered equivalence bounds on cross-stratum scale ratios; ordered-model coefficients are reported as secondary descriptives only.

**(2) Generated regressors and errors-in-variables.** The stratifier and the institutional regressor are estimated indices — rubric-scored, calibration-mapped. Classical measurement error in $v$ misclassifies strata and attenuates within-stratum slopes toward zero, an artifact that *mimics* the annihilated-cell null and blurs the cross-stratum profile; error in $a$ attenuates every institutional slope. The protocol therefore requires an errors-in-variables strategy, not a disclaimer: a repeat-scoring reliability subsample (two independent raters, blind to outcomes, with reliability reported), regression-calibration or instrumenting one rater's score with the other's, and full two-step bootstrap of the resulting standard errors. The annihilated-cell null is evidentially meaningful only in the reliability-corrected fit and only *alongside* strictly positive corrected slopes at operative strata: the registered pattern is the contrast, never the null alone.

**(3) Harmonized error rates.** The diagnostic family consists of exactly two registered contrasts — (i)'s pooled-versus-partial sign contrast (Proposition SM-B.1) and the slope-equality Wald test (SM-B.6.4; signatures (ii) and (iii) are one test stated twice) — held at a single family-wise error rate $\alpha=0.05$ by Holm's step-down. The earlier mixture of standards — a $0.95$-posterior rule for (i), Bonferroni $0.05/3$ for the family, a $0.10$ region for the $J$ test — is withdrawn. SM-B.7's $J$ test is a premise check *outside* this family and states its own size there.

**(4) Power, minimum detectable effects, and the equivalence logic of the negative criterion.** Rehabilitating the single-score benchmark is an equivalence claim and is registered as one. The protocol pre-registers a minimum detectable slope difference for the Wald contrast — calibrated from the ED witness at the registry's hazard calibration — and a design power of at least $0.8$ at the family $\alpha$; the negative criterion is available only in samples meeting the requirement. Non-rejection alone never rehabilitates: rehabilitation requires TOST-style equivalence — every pairwise stratum-slope difference within the pre-registered bounds, the pooled-versus-partial contrast within its bound, and SM-B.7's $J$ test failing to reject with instruments passing the registered strength threshold. An underpowered non-rejection is recorded as *uninformative*, not as rehabilitation: the earlier wording rewarded underpower, and this one does not.

**(5) Selection on unobservables.** Proposition SM-B.1(a) makes decision-time stratification robust to selection *on the recorded ledgers*; committees that select on soft signals correlated with post-decision innovations reintroduce bias in every conditioning set. The protocol's mitigations are the instrument audit below (what did the committee actually score?), archived score sheets where obtainable, and the pre-decision covariate set; the residual threat is disclosed in the registry protocol rather than assumed away.

#### SM-B.6.6 Coverage: which instrument families the diagnostics bite

Honesty about targets, demanded by the referee record and owed anyway. A weakly monotone multiplicative committee score — the opening vignette's instrument, $g\cdot h$ flat in the institution exactly at venture death — is excluded as a *value representation* by Theorem 3 under SH/ED; but as a *data-generating practice* it is visible to (i)–(iii) only through the selection channel. Computed but never used to allocate, it leaves no scar these diagnostics can see — outcomes are generated by the class, not by the committee's arithmetic. Used to fund, it is a single monotone score in the sense of Definition SM-B.2(D3) and manufactures signature (i). Its *residual-halo* variant — a direct premium on measured value at fixed trajectory — is outside the class altogether and is the designated target of the Hansen $J$ test (SM-B.7), not of this subsection. The decision matrix:

| Instrument family (how the field scores and funds) | Status under the theory | (i) pooled-sign reversal | (ii)/(iii) slope diagnostics | Hansen $J$ (SM-B.7) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strictly monotone composite (weighted addition; scorecard totals) | fails C1′ at the boundary (Lemma SM-B.2); excluded by Theorem 3 | fires iff it allocates funding (selection channel) | fire wherever SH/ED hold (analyst-side misspecification) | inside the null |
| Weakly monotone single-index composite (pure multiplication, geometric/CES blends, min gates) | excluded by Theorem 3 under SH/ED | fires iff it allocates funding — the *only* channel by which (i)–(iii) see this family | fire wherever SH/ED hold | inside the null |
| Any of the above plus a residual halo (direct premium at fixed trajectory) | outside the class; Theorem 3's premises void | not designed for it | not designed for it | **rejects** — the designated diagnostic |
| Conditional gating (knockout thresholds, then venture-ledger ranking) | not a single score; admissible practice (Section 4) | structurally silent — product selection, Lemma SM-B.3(b) | fire only if the *analyst* imposes a venture-uniform index | inside the null |

The registry's instrument audit records, for every sampled program, which row it occupies — named scoring instruments, documented as strictly monotone composites, weakly monotone composites, or gates. Without that record, neither rejection nor non-rejection of (i) is attributable; with it, row 4's silence is itself informative, since a gated field is one that has already adopted the paper's recommended design.

**Negative criterion (falsifiability commitment, restated).** The single-score benchmark is rehabilitated on the data only under the conjunction of SM-B.6.5(4): equivalence — not mere non-rejection — on both registered contrasts at the family-wise $\alpha$, in a design meeting the pre-registered power floor, together with SM-B.7's channel-completeness test failing to reject under its stated conditions. Any one firing diagnostic supports the empirical content of Theorem 3 within its row of the decision matrix: (ii)/(iii) against the venture-uniform institutional index, (i) against compensatory score-selection as a portfolio practice. Diagnostics firing outside their row indict nothing and are reported as anomalies.

### SM-B.7 The Hansen $J$ test: channel completeness

The diagnostics of SM-B.6 test consequences of the theorem within the maintained class — and, for signature (i), of the field's selection practice given the class; the class structure itself is testable, and the two exercises must not be conflated. C3′ says that institutional axes affect value only through the laws of motion of $(R,S,F)$ and the $\sigma_{SU}$-exposure; a *direct* channel — a brand or halo premium accruing at a fixed venture path — lies outside the class and would void Theorem 3's premises. The tested hypothesis is therefore *channel completeness*: the licensed carriers exhaust the transmission from $A$ to value. Writing $\rho_k^{R},\rho_k^{S},\rho_k^{F},\rho_k^{\sigma}$ for the responses of the four carriers to axis $k$, the moment conditions equate the measured total effect of axis $k$ on the value process with the effect transmitted through the measured carrier responses:
$$E\Big[\Big(\frac{df_e}{dA_k}-\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial R}\,\rho_k^{R}-\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial S}\,\rho_k^{S}-\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial F}\,\rho_k^{F}-\frac{\partial f_e}{\partial \sigma_{SU}}\,\rho_k^{\sigma}\Big)\cdot z\Big]=0,$$
with $z$ a vector of instruments built from shocks to institutional axes external to the venture state (policy shifts to funding or staffing rules used as natural experiments). The bracketed factor is the direct-channel residual — the total institutional effect net of everything transmitted through the four carriers — so the restriction tests completeness of the licensed channel as a whole, not a two-term subset of it: the slack and exposure responses sit inside the null exactly as they sit inside the class (SM-B.1). With $\dim(m)>\dim(\theta)$, where $m$ stacks the moments across axes and instruments and $\theta$ collects the parameters of $(f_e,\rho)$, Hansen's (1982) overidentification statistic
$$J=N\,g_N(\hat\theta)'\,\hat W\,g_N(\hat\theta)\ \sim\ \chi^2_{\dim(m)-\dim(\theta)}$$
is central under channel completeness, with the conservative rejection region $p<0.10$ (loosened because failing to reject is the informative outcome for a premise). Decision matrix: $J$ rejects $\Rightarrow$ a direct institutional channel exists, the environment lies outside the class, and the premises of Theorem 3 are void regardless of the diagnostics; $J$ fails to reject and a diagnostic fires $\Rightarrow$ the class stands and the corresponding indictment holds — signatures (ii)/(iii) against the venture-uniform institutional index, signature (i) against compensatory score-selection; $J$ fails to reject and no diagnostic fires $\Rightarrow$ the negative criterion of SM-B.6 applies, whose rehabilitation of the scalar benchmark requires the pre-registered equivalence and power showings of SM-B.6.5(4), not the bare non-rejection. The $J$ test's rejection region ($p<0.10$, conservative because failing to reject is the informative outcome for a premise) sits outside the SM-B.6 diagnostic family, whose error rates are harmonized there. ## SM-C. Optimal stopping: proofs for Theorem 4 and Proposition 2

This supplement records the optimal-stopping theory behind the paper's GO gate. Monograph correspondence (ch. 5.5): Lemma C.1 = Prop. 5.5.1; Theorem 4 = Prop. 5.5.2, with existence input from Prop. 5.5.1; Proposition 2 = Prop. 5.5.3; Lemma C.2 = Prop. 5.5.4. Proofs are given at the level of architecture; steps the monograph defers to its Appendix A.5.5 are marked `[GAP: ...]` rather than reconstructed. The index $i$ labels ventures; primitives $(P,F,B,\Pi)$ are venture-specific; the regime is fully observed (belief-dependent extension out of scope).

### C.0 Primitive problem and standing assumptions

The founder–institution pair, modeled as a single joint-value-maximizing agent, chooses a stopping time $\tau$ solving

$$\sup_{\tau}\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\tau}^{\infty} e^{-rt}\big(P\cdot R(t)\cdot S(t)-B(t)\big)\,dt \;-\; I \,\Big|\,\mathcal{F}_0\Big]\qquad \text{s.t.}\quad (B-R_{\mathrm{net}})\le F .$$

Entry is one-shot and irreversible with sunk cost $I$: before day zero the pair holds an option on a venture, not a venture (McDonald and Siegel 1986; McGrath 1997). Following the monograph we set $B(t)\equiv B$, a constant burn parameter.

State variables. (i) Market alignment $\sigma_{SU}(t)\in\mathbb{R}$ follows a regime-modulated diffusion $d\sigma=b_k\,dt+s_k\,dW_t$, where $k_t\in\{S_0\ \text{dormant},\,S_1\ \text{warming},\,S_2\ \text{aligned}\}$ is a $K=3$ Markov regime process with row-stochastic (uniformized) transition matrix $\Pi=(\pi_{kj})$. (ii) Runway $F_t$ obeys $dF=(R_{\mathrm{net}}-B)\,dt$, with $F=0$ absorbing (forced exit; all flows cease) — the monograph's declared dynamic implementation of the displayed financing constraint. (iii) The deterministic TRL gate $g_{TRL}(t)\in\{0,1\}$ restricts admissible stopping times to the gate-open set $\mathcal{T}_g=\{\tau:g_{TRL}(\tau)=1\}$; C.1–C.3 condition on an open gate, Lemma C.2 restores it.

The flow payoff is $\pi(\sigma,F,k)=P\cdot R(\sigma,k)\cdot S(\sigma,k)-B$, where $R$ (realization readiness) and $S$ (survival probability) are the paper's upstream constructs (Section 3), treated here as primitives with the stated regularity.

Standing assumptions. **(A1)** $r>0$; $\pi$ bounded, Lipschitz in $(\sigma,F)$, non-decreasing in $\sigma$ (implicit in the monograph); $(\sigma,F,k)$ is strong Markov. **(A2)** $b_k,s_k$ Lipschitz, $s_k$ non-degenerate. **(A3)** $\Pi$ irreducible: no absorbing regime. **(A4)** $I>0$ constant. **(A5)** Interior primitives: $P,B,F$ interior to the positive orthant, $P/B$ non-extreme (excluding the monograph's degenerate cases). **(A6)** Post-entry runway follows the same funds law. `[GAP: the skeleton records a single funds equation and never separately specifies the post-entry drift; we read R_net as including post-entry project revenue.]`

Entered value. $G(\sigma,F,k)=\mathbb{E}\big[\int_0^{T_0}e^{-rt}\pi\,dt\big]$ with $T_0=\inf\{t:F_t=0\}$ solves the coupled linear system $\mathcal{L}_kG+\pi-rG=0$, $G(\sigma,0,k)=0$, where for $\varphi(\cdot,\cdot,k)$

$$(\mathcal{L}_k\varphi)(\sigma,F)=b_k\varphi_\sigma+\tfrac12 s_k^2\varphi_{\sigma\sigma}+(R_{\mathrm{net}}-B)\varphi_F+\sum_j\pi_{kj}\big[\varphi(\sigma,F,j)-\varphi(\sigma,F,k)\big].$$

### C.1 Well-posedness (Lemma C.1 = monograph Prop. 5.5.1)

Waiting yields no flow, so the pre-entry value is $V(\sigma,F,k)=\sup_\tau\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-r\tau}(G-I)(\sigma_\tau,F_\tau,k_\tau)\,\mathbf{1}_{\tau<T_0}\big]$ (never entering yields zero). Dynamic programming gives the coupled variational inequality

$$\max\big\{\,\mathcal{L}_kV-rV,\;(G-I)-V\,\big\}=0,\qquad k\in\{S_0,S_1,S_2\},$$

with $V(\sigma,0,k)=0$, $V\to0$ as $\sigma\to-\infty$, and $V$ bounded above by the entered-value envelope as $\sigma\to+\infty$ (all recorded in the skeleton).

*Remark C.1.* The skeleton writes the HJB in a translated normalization, $\max\{\mathcal{L}_kV+\pi-rV,\,0\}=0$ with stopping set $\{V=0\}$. `[GAP: the translation between the two normalizations — via the Feynman–Kac representation of G — is not spelled out in the skeleton; we work in the entry normalization, which its own value-matching condition presupposes.]`

**Lemma C.1.** Under (A1)–(A5): (a) $V$ exists, is bounded and Lipschitz; (b) $V$ is the unique viscosity solution (Crandall, Ishii and Lions 1992) of the coupled system with the stated boundary conditions; (c) $V$ is non-decreasing in $\sigma$ and in $F$; (d) $V(\cdot,\cdot,S_2)\ge V(\cdot,\cdot,S_1)\ge V(\cdot,\cdot,S_0)$.

*Proof sketch.* (a) Boundedness of $\pi$ with $r>0$ bounds $G$, hence $V$; Lipschitz data give Lipschitz continuity. (b) The strong Markov property yields the dynamic programming principle, hence the viscosity sub- and supersolution properties. The switching term is a monotone coupling, so the system is weakly coupled in the sense required for a comparison principle in the Crandall–Ishii–Lions framework; comparison gives uniqueness. `[GAP: the growth and regularity conditions under which comparison holds for the K=3 coupled system are checked in monograph Appendix A.5.5, not reproduced here.]` (c) Monotone coupling of trajectories in the initial state, $\pi$ non-decreasing in $\sigma$, and survival non-decreasing in $F$. (d) `[GAP: the regime ordering is asserted as monograph Prop. 5.5.1(d); the coupling argument (regime-ordered flows plus irreducibility) is deferred to its appendix.]` $\square$

### C.2 Proof of Theorem 4 (= monograph Prop. 5.5.2)

**Theorem 4.** Under (A1)–(A5), value-matching and smooth-pasting determine, for each regime $k$, a unique implicit threshold $\theta^*_\sigma(k)=\Phi_k(P,F,B,\Pi)$; the optimal policy is $GO(t,i)=\mathbf{1}[\sigma_{SU}(t)\ge\theta^*_\sigma(k_t)]\cdot g_{TRL}(t)$; the threshold is strictly interior for interior primitives and continuous in $(P,F,B,\Pi)$.

*Step 1 (free boundary).* Let $\mathcal{C}=\{V>G-I\}$ be the continuation region; on $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathcal{L}_kV=rV$. Per regime and runway level the entry region is an up-set in $\sigma$: $\{\sigma\ge\theta^*_\sigma(k;F)\}$. `[GAP: the single-crossing property behind this threshold geometry is asserted from monotonicity of V and G in σ; verification is in the monograph appendix.]`

*Step 2 (value-matching).* Continuity of the viscosity solution forces $V=G-I$ on $\partial\mathcal{C}$, i.e. $V(\theta^*_\sigma(k),F,k)=G(\theta^*_\sigma(k),F,k)-I$.

*Step 3 (smooth-pasting).* $\partial V/\partial\sigma=\partial G/\partial\sigma$ at $\sigma=\theta^*_\sigma(k)$. With $s_k$ non-degenerate the boundary is regular for the $\sigma$-diffusion; a kink in the pasted function would admit a locally profitable boundary perturbation (the second-order argument of Dixit and Pindyck 1994); monotonicity (Lemma C.1(c)) and irreducibility (A3) exclude tangency degeneracies. `[GAP: the C¹-pasting regularity conditions are checked under K=3 coupling in monograph Appendix A.5.5; the free-boundary regularity theory invoked there is not reproduced.]`

*Step 4 (implicit system and uniqueness).* At each runway level the $\sigma$-sections of the continuation equation form three coupled second-order equations. `[GAP: the skeleton treats these sections as an ODE system, leaving the runway-drift term's role in that reduction unrecorded.]` Selecting bounded solutions consumes one constant per regime; the six pasting conditions then balance three integration constants plus three unknown boundaries. Eliminating $V$ yields a finite system $\Psi(\theta_{S_0},\theta_{S_1},\theta_{S_2};P,F,B,\Pi)=0$. For interior primitives the Jacobian $\partial\Psi/\partial\theta$ is non-singular — degenerate cases (extreme $P/B$, absorbing regimes) are excluded by (A3)/(A5) `[GAP: non-singularity itself is checked in the monograph appendix]` — so the implicit function theorem gives a locally unique, continuously differentiable $\Phi_k(P,F,B,\Pi)$; Step 1's up-set geometry upgrades local to global uniqueness, and continuity in primitives follows.

*Step 5 (interiority).* As $\sigma\to-\infty$, $G-I<0\le V$, so entry is strictly suboptimal; as $\sigma\to+\infty$ the waiting premium $V-(G-I)$ vanishes against the entered-value envelope and entry becomes optimal. Hence $-\infty<\theta^*_\sigma(k)<+\infty$ for interior primitives.

*Step 6 (policy and verification).* Itô's formula on the pasted $C^1$ candidate shows $e^{-rt}V$ is a supermartingale everywhere and a martingale on $\mathcal{C}$, so the threshold rule attains the supremum; the gate multiplies in by Lemma C.2, giving $GO(t,i)=\mathbf{1}[\sigma_{SU}(t)\ge\theta^*_\sigma(k_t)]\cdot g_{TRL}(t)$. $\square$

*Remark C.2 (four-component decomposition).* $\theta^*_\sigma(k)$ separates into (a) the Marshallian break-even root of $G=I$; (b) a Dixit–Pindyck uncertainty premium increasing in the regime-$k$ diffusion loading $s_k^2$; (c) a Bertola (1998) irreversibility premium scaling with $I$ and degenerate at $I=0$; (d) a regime-switching option-value term determined by $\Pi$ (switching toward better regimes raises the value of waiting in bad ones). In the single-regime geometric-Brownian limit the threshold collapses to the McDonald and Siegel (1986) / Dixit and Pindyck (1994) closed form, the monograph's analytic anchor.

### C.3 Proof of Proposition 2 (= monograph Prop. 5.5.3)

**Proposition 2.** On the empirically relevant parameter region $\mathcal{R}$ defined below: $\partial\theta^*/\partial P<0$, $\partial\theta^*/\partial B>0$, $\partial\theta^*/\partial F<0$, $\partial\theta^*(k)/\partial\pi_{kk}<0$, and $\theta^*(S_0)>\theta^*(S_1)>\theta^*(S_2)$.

*The region $\mathcal{R}$.* (R1) Interior primitives with non-singular Jacobian (Theorem 4, Step 4), $F$ bounded away from zero. (R2) For the burn-rate sign only, the monograph's qualifier: the waiting-cost effect is dominated by the post-entry-cost effect; the recorded sufficient condition is runway $F$ sufficiently large and $\Pi$ stationary over the decision horizon.

*Method.* Implicit differentiation of $\Psi=0$: $\partial\theta^*/\partial x=-(\partial\Psi/\partial\theta)^{-1}(\partial\Psi/\partial x)$ for $x\in\{P,B,F,\pi_{kk}\}$. Smooth-pasting makes the boundary displacement second-order (an envelope property), so each sign reduces to the relative boundary sensitivities of entered and waiting values.

(i) *Price.* $\partial G/\partial P=\mathbb{E}\int e^{-rt}RS\,dt>0$, and at the boundary the entered value gains more than the waiting value, which responds only via the diffusion–regime detour, so the crossing moves down: $\partial\theta^*/\partial P<0$.

(ii) *Burn rate.* Two opposing channels: a waiting-cost channel — higher $B$ drains runway toward the absorbing barrier, making waiting costlier and pushing $\theta^*$ down — and a post-entry-cost channel — higher $B$ lowers $\pi$ and hence $G$ everywhere, pushing $\theta^*$ up. On $\mathcal{R}$ (R2: barrier remote, switching stationary) the post-entry channel dominates and $\partial\theta^*/\partial B>0$. Outside $\mathcal{R}$ the sign can reverse; the paper claims it only on $\mathcal{R}$.

(iii) *Runway.* Exit risk near the barrier depresses the entered value, so $\partial G/\partial F>0$ strictly (the monograph's type-A "GO postponed on thin runway" structure), while the waiting value is less $F$-sensitive at the boundary on $\mathcal{R}$; hence $\partial\theta^*/\partial F<0$: financial slack lowers the entry hurdle. `[GAP: the skeleton records a heuristic F→0 limit (threshold collapse, "forced entry or exit"); its reconciliation with interior monotonicity is deferred to the monograph's exit-hazard chapter, and R stays away from F=0.]`

(iv) *Persistence.* Differentiate along a row-stochastic perturbation raising $\pi_{kk}$ with off-diagonal mass rescaled proportionally. `[GAP: the skeleton does not record which compensation scheme its sign is proved under.]` Higher own-persistence raises the weight of regime-$k$ flows in $G$ and shrinks component (d) of Remark C.2 — waiting to switch regimes is worth less when switching is rarer — so on $\mathcal{R}$, $\partial\theta^*(k)/\partial\pi_{kk}<0$.

(v) *Regime ordering.* By Lemma C.1(d) and regime-ordered flows, the gap $(G-I)-V$ at fixed $\sigma$ is ordered across regimes, so the crossing points are reversely ordered: $\theta^*(S_0)>\theta^*(S_1)>\theta^*(S_2)$. `[GAP: the skeleton records this as a sketch from value monotonicity in k; the dominance argument on the pasting system is completed in its appendix.]`

Cross-venture dispersion in $(P,F,B)$ disperses thresholds — the microfoundation for lumpy-entry aggregation in the sense of Caballero (1999); Figure 3 (C.5) displays the ordering and the four slopes. $\square$

*Remark C.3 (scope).* Monograph Prop. 5.5.3 additionally characterizes an event-time jump of $\theta^*$ under a two-stage transition-matrix construction ($\Pi^{\mathrm{pre}}\to\Pi^{\mathrm{post}}$), outside the paper's single-$\Pi$ setting and not reproduced here.

### C.4 Orthogonal factorization of the TRL gate (Lemma C.2 = monograph Prop. 5.5.4)

*TRL-orthogonality (assumption).* The macro regime process and the TRL ramp are conditionally independent given the regime path, $\sigma_{SU}\perp g_{TRL}\mid k$; and $g_{TRL}$ is declared deterministic, a function of time only, bounded, and non-decreasing; its functional form is left to the monograph's estimation layer.

**Lemma C.2.** Under TRL-orthogonality the optimal policy separates multiplicatively, $GO(t,i)=\mathbf{1}[\sigma_{SU}(t)\ge\theta^*_\sigma(k_t)]\cdot g_{TRL}(t)$, with $\theta^*_\sigma$ the Theorem 4 threshold: the gate shifts feasibility, not the threshold.

*Proof sketch.* In the paper's binary baseline the non-decreasing gate opens once, at the deterministic date $t_g=\inf\{t:g_{TRL}(t)=1\}$, so $\mathcal{T}_g=\{\tau\ge t_g\}$ and the constrained problem is the unconstrained problem restarted at $t_g$. Conditional independence implies the continuation values, hence the pasting system $\Psi=0$, carry no dependence on the ramp; time-homogeneity of $(\sigma,F,k)$ makes $\theta^*$ date-invariant. After $t_g$ the optimal rule is therefore the Theorem 4 threshold rule; before $t_g$ every admissible policy is idle, so the product form holds: the gate enters the admissibility set, not the generator. `[GAP: the skeleton derives the factorization by an HJB variable-separation argument whose details it does not record; the graded [0,1]-valued ramp case (sigmoid, piecewise-linear, Gompertz candidates) is declared but not proved here — the paper uses only the binary gate.]` $\square$

### C.5 Numerical scheme for Figure 3 (specification only)

*Purpose.* Figure 3 reports $\hat\theta^*(k)$, $k\in\{S_0,S_1,S_2\}$, at baseline and traces the four comparative-statics sweeps of Proposition 2. Calibration constants are withheld under policy PF-010; every symbol below ($P_0,B_0,F_{\mathrm{ref}},\Pi_0,r_0,I_0,\dots$) is a placeholder, and no numerical output is reported.

*Grids and boundaries.* Uniform grids $\sigma\in[\sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max}]$ ($N_\sigma$ nodes) and $F\in[0,F_{\max}]$ ($N_F$ nodes), with three regime layers; the problem is stationary conditional on an open gate, so no time grid. Boundary data follow Lemma C.1: $V=0$ at $F=0$ and at $\sigma=\sigma_{\min}$ (proxying $\sigma\to-\infty$); at $\sigma_{\max}$ impose the entered branch $V=G-I_0$, with $\sigma_{\max}$ far above the provisional threshold, checked ex post to lie inside the entry region.

*Scheme.* First solve the linear coupled system $\mathcal{L}_kG+\pi-r_0G=0$ for $G$ (one sparse solve; discretization as in the pseudocode). Then iterate the regime-coupled obstacle problem.

```text
inputs: P0, B0, F_ref, Pi0, r0, I0, grids (N_sigma, N_F), tol eps, band eps_b
G <- solve  L_k G + pi - r0 G = 0           # entered value, coupled linear solve
V <- max(G - I0, 0)                         # initialization
repeat
  for k in {S0, S1, S2}:
      W(.,.,k) <- implicit finite-difference step of  L_k V - r0 V = 0
                  # upwind drifts, central diffusion, Pi0-row coupling
  V_new <- max(W, G - I0)                   # obstacle (entry) branch
  delta <- sup_norm(V_new - V);  V <- V_new
until delta < eps
theta_hat(k; F) <- min{ sigma_j : V(sigma_j,F,k) - (G(sigma_j,F,k) - I0) <= eps_b }
report theta_hat(k) at F = F_ref
```

*Sweeps.* For each $x\in\{P,B,F_{\mathrm{ref}},\pi_{kk}\}$, vary $x$ over a placeholder interval $[x_{\mathrm{lo}},x_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ holding the rest at baseline — for $\pi_{kk}$, rescale off-diagonal mass proportionally to preserve row-stochasticity — and recompute $\hat\theta^*(k)$. Figure 3 then shows (panel a) the baseline ordering $\hat\theta^*(S_0)>\hat\theta^*(S_1)>\hat\theta^*(S_2)$ and (panels b–e) $\hat\theta^*(k)$ against each swept primitive, one line per regime.

*Diagnostics required before the figure is accepted.* (i) Mesh refinement: halving both mesh widths moves each $\hat\theta^*$ by less than a preset tolerance. (ii) Degenerate-limit check: a single-regime geometric-Brownian collapse must reproduce the McDonald and Siegel (1986) / Dixit and Pindyck (1994) closed-form threshold within tolerance. (iii) Sign check: sweep slopes and regime ordering must agree with Proposition 2 on the swept region (a check of the code against the theory, not evidence for the theory). The regime-filtering and smoothing layers of the monograph's estimation pipeline are not needed: Figure 3 conditions on the regime directly.

## SM-D. The ERS rubric and the eight-project calibration panel

### SM-D.1 The eight institutional axes

Each axis is scored on a five-level ordinal rubric (0–4) anchored by observable institutional states — documents, staffed functions, and operating track records — rather than judgment scales. The full anchor sheets are maintained in the study's operating protocol; the table below summarizes what each axis observes and the logic of its anchors. Blank-sheet checklists are deliberately not reproduced: the rubric is designed to be scored from evidence, and the registered program (Section 7 of the main text) deposits the scoring protocol with the registry.

| Axis | Observes | Level-0 anchor (illustrative) | Level-4 anchor (illustrative) | Main text correspondence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $A_1$ Seed scouting & technology assessment | How systematically the institution finds and evaluates commercializable seeds | No scouting function; discovery is accidental | Standing scouting process with documented technology-assessment criteria and periodic review | feeds $R$ drift |
| $A_2$ IP & licensing (TLO function) | Patenting discipline, licensing practice, disclosure management | No institutional IP management | Professionalized TLO with negotiated licenses and disclosure-before-publication discipline | feeds $R$, $S$ |

Three coding rules from the main text (E3) bear repeating: an axis that cannot be evidenced *after audit* is coded $\perp$ (unknown) and excluded from $K_{obs}$ — it is never silently imputed to zero; "not started" (evidence of absence) is a 0, which *is* included; and an institution that *declines to evidence* an axis is coded at the floor (0) and kept in $K_{obs}$, not treated as $\perp$ (the anti-gaming rule of SM-A.5). The renormalization over $K_{obs}$ in Theorem 1, under audited observation, is what makes this honesty representationally harmless.

### SM-D.2 The calibration panel behind Table 2

The eight projects of Section 6 are composites drawn from one studio's operating records (2007–2026). For each project, the panel records at the founding-decision point: the macro regime assignment ($S_0/S_1/S_2$, assigned retrospectively under the calibration procedure of Section 3 from the three momenta series); the technology-gate reading ($g_{TRL} \in \{0,1\}$, with the domain-specific provisional mapping for drug discovery flagged); the slack state ($F$: low / mid / high, from funding records); the venture-ledger reading and the model's implied decision; the decision actually taken and by whom (studio-involved or external); and the outcome class at 24 months (progressing / stagnant / terminated; right-censored where applicable). Calibration constants mapping rubric levels into model ranges are described procedurally in the main text and withheld as values; the theorems are invariant to them, and the prospective registry deposits them under embargo so that the pre-registered predictions of Section 7 are checkable without public disclosure.

### SM-D.3 Momenta series construction (summary)

The three momenta are constructed from observable series per domain: $\mu_A$ from publication dynamics in the seed's research domain; $\mu_I$ from industrial commitment indicators (corporate participation, procurement signals); $\mu_G$ from policy budgets and program calls. Each series is smoothed, growth-transformed, and mapped to a bounded momentum scale before entering $\sigma_{SU} = \sqrt[3]{\prod (\mu + 1)} - 1$. Series definitions and windows are part of the deposited protocol; regime boundaries ($S_0/S_1/S_2$) are calibrated, not estimated, in this paper (Tier discipline of Section 3).
