# The Before Zero Model: measuring deep-tech ventures and their institutional nurseries before day zero

**Abstract**: Deep-tech ventures born from universities accumulate — or fail to accumulate — most of their value before incorporation, yet this "Before Zero" stage has no measurement theory. Evaluation practice fills the vacuum with institution-adjusted single scores — a project's rating adjusted by its host institution's capabilities — and committees debate the correct weighting. We prove the debate has no answer. Under four requirements any committee member would separately endorse (a dead venture is worth zero anywhere; a better host never hurts; hosts act on value through what they do to the venture; value moves continuously) together with two facts of technology-transfer practice (institutional capabilities bind at different venture stages, and act over the venture's remaining journey), no monotone single score merging venture and institution can represent venture value — not the current weighting, not any weighting: an impossibility in the Arrow mold. Fields that rank and fund by such scores anyway leave statistical scars, which we derive and pre-register — including a Simpson reversal manufactured by score-based selection itself. What survives is a complete working design: a multiplicative venture ledger ($\mathrm{SPS}$ = potential × realization × survival), an additive institutional ledger ($\mathrm{ECR}$, proven the unique admissible aggregate), a shared Triple-Helix macro state, and a founding-timing rule that reads both ledgers without merging them. Conditional gating — the design the field's most serious agencies already run — is thereby proven right rather than merely customary. A retrospective calibration on eight deep-tech projects (2007–2026) illustrates the system, and a registered falsification program, Before Zero Studies, states in advance the evidence that would prove it wrong.
**Keywords**: deep-tech ventures; university spin-outs; readiness measurement; technology readiness levels; Triple Helix; impossibility theorem; real options; research commercialization

## 1. Introduction

A national screening panel convenes to rank some forty university deep-tech seeds competing for a commercialization fund.¹ Its instrument is an "institution-adjusted project score": each seed's evaluated quality, multiplied by a coefficient reflecting its host university's technology-transfer capabilities. Midway through the session a member proposes re-weighting the institutional coefficient; two seeds swap ranks; a debate follows about which weighting is *correct* — a debate that assumes its own answer: that some weighting is, that the committee's task is calibration. This paper proves the assumption false. Under requirements every member of that committee would separately endorse — a dead venture is worth zero anywhere; a better institution never hurts — together with two facts of technology-transfer practice — institutional capabilities bind at different venture stages, and act over the venture's remaining journey — *no* institution-adjusted single score exists: not the current weighting, not the proposed one, not any monotone combination however sophisticated. The committee is not miscalibrating an instrument; it is calibrating a nonexistent one.

The setting is the *pre-founding* stage of deep-tech venturing, which we call **Before Zero**: the years — typically five to fifteen — during which a research result inside a university or national laboratory matures toward incorporation ("day zero"). Along that road the decisive assets accumulate — technology readiness, intellectual property, regulatory position, founding team, institutional scaffolding (Shane, 2004; Grimaldi et al., 2011; Perkmann et al., 2013) — so that by day zero much of a deep-tech venture's fate is already committed. Yet the stage is a measurement vacuum: too early for valuation, it is governed by readiness vocabularies (Mankins, 1995) and scorecards, and increasingly by policy demands for composite rankings of seeds, institutions, and ecosystems (Nardo et al., 2008).

This paper proposes a measurement theory for that vacuum: the **Before Zero Model (BZM)**. Its architecture is deliberately dual. The *venture ledger* scores each project multiplicatively by the *seed prospect score*, $\mathrm{SPS} = P \cdot R \cdot S$ (potential scale × realization readiness × survival probability), because a pre-founding venture is a conjunction of necessary conditions in which any zero is fatal. The *institutional ledger* scores the hosting nursery additively by the *ecosystem construction rate*, $\mathrm{ECR}$, a weighted sum over eight rubric-scored capability axes, because an institution is a portfolio of partially substitutable capabilities whose gaps must stay visible. A shared macro state $\sigma_{SU}$ — the shifted geometric mean of academic, industrial, and governmental momenta, Triple Helix in conception (Etzkowitz and Leydesdorff, 2000) — drives the venture ledger's dynamics.

The central result explains why the architecture *must* be dual. We define the institution-adjusted value of a seed honestly — the expected discounted value of the venture under its institution's nurturing dynamics — so that a dead venture is worth zero anywhere, a better nursery never hurts, institutions act on value through what they do to the venture, and value moves continuously. **Theorem 3** establishes that once institutional capabilities are stage-contingent — scouting and incubation bind early, licensing and the corporate interface bind late — no single-score merger of the two ledgers, monotone in its indices, can represent that value: the true comparison of two nurseries reverses across venture strata, and one index cannot lie on both sides of a reversal. **Corollary 3.1** converts the impossibility into empirics: a field that ranks — and therefore funds, and therefore samples — by single scores leaves *statistical scars*, registered as diagnostics with error rates fixed in advance — including a Simpson reversal manufactured by score-selected funding itself.

The result reads plainly. When a committee debates which institutional weighting is *correct*, the debate has no answer: every weighting is right for ventures at one stage of the journey and provably wrong for ventures at another, because what a nursery does for a seed depends on where the seed stands. What the theorem forbids is not evaluation but compression. What survives is everything a working committee actually needs: an additive shortlist whose weights are published as the political choices they are, guarded so that no dead venture outranks a live one; conditional gates — rank ventures on their own merits, fund them only at institutions that clear stated thresholds — which Section 4 proves correct rather than merely customary; and a governance split in which project money follows the venture ledger and capacity-building money follows the institutional ledger. A committee that already gates has been right all along; this paper supplies the reason, and Section 7 the audit answer for the ministry that demands the composite anyway.

Two further results complete the system. **Theorem 1** shows the weighted sum is the *unique* institutional aggregate — in rubric units, under five practice-grounded axioms and the rubric's equal-interval reporting convention — by additive conjoint measurement (Krantz, Luce, Suppes and Tversky, 1971), with a precondition gate for formal bylaws (Proposition 1) in the spirit of Alkire and Foster (2011). **Theorem 4** shows the system is operable: the go/wait decision is formalized as an optimal-stopping rule in the regime-switching real-options tradition (Dixit and Pindyck, 1994; Guo, Miao and Morellec, 2005), with a threshold derived from project economics and — distinctively — monitored on a state variable *constructed by the auditable measurement layer of Section 3*. We order the contributions deliberately: measurement first; the decision operator demonstrates completeness rather than headlining the paper.

We exercise the system on the operating records of a single venture studio: eight deep-tech projects across Japanese universities and national laboratories, 2007–2026, reported as composites with type-named institutions. The exercise is retrospective calibration under explicit Tier discipline — outcomes were known when rubrics were scored, so it demonstrates organization of history, not prediction. Its two vignettes are instructive: a macro-blessed, technology-hollow project any composite would have founded (history did, into a decade of stagnation), and a technically presentable project whose potential-scale zero the multiplicative ledger catches while additive scores rank it above the first.

Three contributions, then, to this journal's readership. *A measurement theory for an unmeasured stage*: axiomatic foundations for evaluating ventures that do not yet exist, where valuation is structurally silent. *An impossibility as policy guardrail*: proof that the single-score instrument evaluation practice keeps demanding cannot exist, together with what legitimately survives. *A falsification-registered research program* — **Before Zero Studies** — whose central claims are built to be testable and whose violation signatures are specified in advance (Popper, 1959).

Section 2 locates the gap; Section 3 constructs the ledgers and proves the institutional aggregate unique; Section 4 states the impossibility theorem and its falsifiable corollary; Section 5 derives the GO operator; Section 6 reports the retrospective calibration; Section 7 draws design implications and registers the program; Section 8 concludes. Scope is declared at the outset: deep-tech ventures, Japanese university and national-laboratory context, pre-founding stage. Whether the theory travels is an empirical question the program is built to answer, not a claim we make.

## 2. Two evaluation problems, one field

Readiness scales are the pre-founding evaluator's incumbent technology. Technology Readiness Levels, born as a NASA communication protocol for hardware maturity (Mankins, 1995), have diffused into mandates across European framework programs and national funding agencies, multiplying into business, societal, and organizational variants. Their strength is real: a shared ordinal ladder disciplines conversation across communities. Their limits are equally documented (Olechowski et al., 2020): each scale is a single ordinal axis with no aggregation theory — nothing says how technology maturity should combine with market, team, or institutional maturity into an assessment of the *venture*, and nothing prevents practitioners from averaging ordinal levels as if they were cardinal. Readiness scales, in short, supply vocabulary without measurement theory.

Where multiple axes must become one number, practice reaches for composite indicators, whose methodological literature has long sounded alarms: the canonical handbook catalogs the arbitrariness of weighting and normalization choices (Nardo et al., 2008); systematic reviews document rank reversals under defensible alternative constructions (Greco et al., 2019); and the deepest critique is Sen's (1977) — an index that buries heterogeneous dimensions in a single number conceals precisely the trade-offs that deliberation should surface. The constructive exception: the Alkire–Foster poverty measures (Alkire and Foster, 2011) survive scrutiny by axiomatizing *identification* separately from *aggregation*. Innovation evaluation has imported the composite habit without importing the axiomatic discipline; this paper's Proposition 1 and Theorem 3 are, in one reading, that discipline arriving.

Nor is the demand hypothetical: the merger is load-bearing inside the world's largest evaluation systems. The U.S. National Institutes of Health long scored five review criteria — among them the host *environment* — and merged them into the single Overall Impact score that orders its funding; its simplified review framework, in force since January 2025, removed the numerical score from precisely the expertise-and-resources factor, converting it to a sufficiency judgment — a system-scale move toward the gate structure this paper derives [verify at S6: NIH NOT-OD-24-010]. The United Kingdom operates both sides of the question: the Research Excellence Framework merges an institutional *environment* profile, at a stated weight, into the single quality profile that allocates public research funding, while its knowledge-exchange counterpart, the KEF, deliberately publishes perspective dashboards and refuses any composite ranking [verify at S6]. National innovation scoreboards compress whole systems into single indices whose fragility is documented in this journal (Grupp and Mogee, 2004; Grupp and Schubert, 2010). The merger this paper studies is the modal instrument — and where practice has begun abandoning it, it has done so by instinct, without the theorem that says the instinct is sound.

A third literature studies our institutional layer directly: technology-transfer offices, gap funds, incubators, entrepreneurs-in-residence, and spin-out policies (Bozeman, 2000; Shane, 2004; Grimaldi et al., 2011; Munari et al., 2016; Hayter et al., 2018), whose collective finding is that institutional capabilities materially shape spin-out formation and survival. Those capabilities serve the institutional face of the valley of death (Auerswald and Branscomb, 2003), and its stage-wise crossing — critical junctures at which different resources bind (Vohora, Wright and Lockett, 2004) — is precisely the stage-contingency Section 4 turns into an impossibility. Yet this literature measures the institution *separately* from the venture, while evaluation practice merges the two levels daily — and the merger itself has, to our knowledge, never been theorized. Deployed instruments exist at the institutional layer — HEInnovate, the KEF dashboards, the AUTM and ASTP surveys [verify at S6] — and Section 7 positions our ledger against each; none carries an aggregation theory, and none theorizes the cross-level merger. The gap this paper fills is the *aggregation across levels* that practice performs without a license.

The Triple Helix program (Etzkowitz and Leydesdorff, 2000) supplies our macro layer's conceptual frame — innovation capacity emerges from the co-evolution of academia, industry, and government — made quantitative in mutual-information synergy indicators (Leydesdorff, 2003). What it lacks is a *venture-facing* state variable — a quantity a founder–institution pair can observe and a stopping rule can condition on: $T_{AIG}$ diagnoses a national or regional *system*, and no decision about a particular seed follows from it. Our $\sigma_{SU}$ is built for exactly that vacant slot, and Section 3 keeps the two objects distinct.

Entry timing under uncertainty is the fifth ingredient, supplied in mature form by the real-options literature: irreversible investment, option value of waiting, threshold policies (McDonald and Siegel, 1986; Dixit and Pindyck, 1994), staged innovation investment (McGrath, 1997), regime-switching thresholds, financing-state effects, policy uncertainty, and stopping under incomplete information (Zhang and Guo, 2004; Guo, Miao and Morellec, 2005; Jobert and Rogers, 2006; Hassett and Metcalf, 1999; Boyle and Guthrie, 2003; Décamps, Mariotti and Villeneuve, 2005). One feature fails before day zero: the monitored state is a firm-level cash flow or output price — nonexistent for pre-founding ventures — so the threshold floats free of any *measurement system*. Section 5 marries that stopping machinery to Section 3's measurement layer — the first such marriage in this domain, we believe; the marriage, not the machinery, is the claim.

Finally, the tools of this paper's central result come from a tradition innovation studies has rarely visited: the measurement-theoretic and social-choice lineage. Arrow (1963) established the template — reasonable axioms, jointly unsatisfiable — and the conjoint-measurement school (Debreu, 1960; Luce and Tukey, 1964; Krantz et al., 1971; Wakker, 1989) characterized exactly when multi-attribute orderings admit additive or separable representations. These results are sixty years old, rigorous, and almost entirely absent from the evaluation practices they indict. Importing them is the methodological move: Theorem 1 applies conjoint measurement to an institutional object those authors never considered, and Section 4 rebuilds Arrow's template at the venture–institution interface, richness supplied not by preference profiles but by how nurseries act on seeds.

The synthesis: five literatures each hold one piece — vocabulary, aggregation warnings, the institutional layer, the macro frame, timing — and none the connective theory: one that respects venture multiplicativity, derives institutional additivity, proves the layers unmergeable, and hands the result to a stopping rule. The Before Zero Model is built to be that theory — and built so its central claims can be wrong in public, axioms stated for inspection, violation signatures registered for detection.

## 3. The two-layer observation system

A research seed $i$ at institution $j$, before incorporation, offers no cash flows, market shares, or churn — only rubric-scored states: technology maturity and its preparations, the host's capabilities, and the attention of academia, industry, and government to its domain. Organizing these observables into decision support without false precision is the Before Zero measurement problem; our answer is two ledgers of different mathematical shape plus one shared macro state.

The **venture ledger** assigns each project the triple $(P, R, S)$ and the *seed prospect score*
$$\mathrm{SPS}_{i}(t) = P_i(t) \cdot R_i(t) \cdot S_i(t),$$
where $P$ is the *potential scale* conditional on full realization, $R$ is *realization readiness*, the progress of technology, team, and commercialization preparations toward it, and $S$ is the *non-financing survival probability* of completing the journey (funding exhaustion is priced once, at Section 5's runway barrier). The form is not stylistic: a pre-founding venture is a conjunction of necessary conditions, a weakest-link economy in the O-ring sense (Kremer, 1993) where excellence in two factors cannot rescue a zero in the third; a seed with world-class science but a market of zero economic size is not "average" but dead — a mortality that multiplication, unlike any averaging rule, encodes.

Practice runs two diligence objects together as "market size." $P$ is **attainable-unit-economics-priced addressable scale**, the opportunity's value ceiling conditional on full realization, priced at physically attainable performance and cost: a market-size zero (no demand at any price) and a margin-viability zero (each unit sold destroys value) are both $P$-zeros. The rubric anchors keep the two sub-objects distinct — one cannot be quoted for the other — and Project Y's $P \to 0$ in Section 6 is a viability zero, not a size zero.

All readings are five-level ordinal rubrics anchored by observable states, taken at the decision date, under three disciplines — dated information sets, endogenous measurement capacity (hence agency- or studio-level scoring), and ex-ante-fixed domain boundaries (SM-D.3, SM-D.4). Disclosure is a declared layer split: theorems, rubrics, anchor logic, and coding conventions are public; fitted calibration constants are proprietary, reported as procedure; everything an adopter needs to rescore from its own records is public (SM-D.4).

The ledgers share one **macro state**. With $\mu_A$, $\mu_I$, and $\mu_G$ the momenta of academic, industrial, and governmental attention to the seed's domain, define
$$\sigma_{SU}(t) = \sqrt[3]{(\mu_A + 1)(\mu_I + 1)(\mu_G + 1)} - 1.$$
This is Triple Helix in conception (Etzkowitz and Leydesdorff, 2000): the geometric-mean form, an equal-weight Cobb–Douglas aggregate (Cobb and Douglas, 1928), makes tailwind a *joint* property of the three helices. $\sigma_{SU}$ is not Leydesdorff's (2003) mutual-information indicator $T_{AIG}$ — a system-level synergy diagnostic rather than a venture-facing state variable. It evolves through regimes (dormant $S_0$, warming $S_1$, aligned $S_2$) on which the venture ledger's dynamics — the drift of $R$, the hazard behind $S$ — condition.

The **institutional ledger** observes the nursery rather than the seed: institution $j$ is scored on eight axes $A_1, \ldots, A_8$ (0–4 rubrics; SM-D) — seed scouting and technology assessment; IP and licensing; incubation and entrepreneurship support; industry-collaboration interface; funding access, including gap funds; entrepreneurial management talent; entrepreneurship bylaws and governance; and government and policy linkage. Five axioms discipline aggregation. **E1 (separability)**: each axis's contribution is assessable without knowing the others. **E2 (compensability)**: observed capabilities partially substitute; only the bylaws precondition (Proposition 1) is individually fatal. **E3 (audited missing-visibility)**: "unknown" and "not started" are different states (Rubin, 1976), and declining to evidence an axis scores the floor, so opacity cannot raise a score (SM-A.5). **E4 (monotonicity)**: improving any observed axis raises readiness; no axis is inert. **E5 (cross-context invariance)**: trade-off rates between observed capabilities do not depend on which further axes are observed. Each axiom is a statement of technology-transfer practice before it is mathematics (practice readings: SM-A.1).

**Theorem 1** (additive representation; proof in SM-A). *Let institutional readiness be a family of continuous orderings, one for each observed set $K_{obs} \subseteq \{1,\ldots,8\}$, satisfying E1–E5 together with the standard conjoint-measurement solvability and Archimedean conditions and the Thomsen/double-cancellation property (on the continuum idealization of the rubric stated in SM-A), and let the rubric carry the equal-interval anchor convention (E2-s). Then a single weight vector $w_1, \ldots, w_8 > 0$ serves every observed set, and each ordering is representable as*
$$\mathrm{ECR}_j = 100 \cdot \frac{\sum_{k \in K_{obs}} w_k A_{jk}}{4 \sum_{k \in K_{obs}} w_k},$$
*unique up to positive affine transformation among scores additive in rubric units.* The result applies additive conjoint measurement (Krantz, Luce, Suppes and Tversky, 1971, ch. 6): the weighted sum in rubric units is the *unique aggregate consistent with an equal-interval reporting convention*; without E2-s the axioms deliver per-axis monotone recalibrations, and the convention buys exactly the linearity committees audit. The ratios $w_k/w_{k'}$ are empirical content; equal weights are a disclosed working convention pending elicitation, not a consequence of the axioms.

One qualification sharpens the additive form. **Proposition 1** (precondition gate). *Axis 7 — entrepreneurship bylaws and formal rules — enters not as an additive term but as a gate: $\mathrm{ECR}_j = 100 \cdot g(A_{j7}) \cdot F(A_{j,-7})$, where $g$ is a step (or steep sigmoid) function and $F$ is the Theorem-1 aggregate over the remaining axes.* An institution scoring 44% on capabilities but with no legal pathway for a professor to found a company has idle capacity, not readiness. The gate echoes Alkire and Foster's (2011) identification-then-aggregation discipline; E2 is withdrawn for axis 7 alone, so the gate refines rather than contradicts Theorem 1 (SM-A).

One merger is prohibited outright. **Theorem 2** (no multiplicative cross-combination; proof in SM-A). *Any multiplicative cross-combination $\mathrm{ECR}^{\alpha} \cdot \mathrm{SPS}^{1-\alpha}$, $\alpha \in (0,1)$, preserves the institutional ordering at each fixed venture state — but destroys the calibrated reporting scale of the institutional ledger: rubric steps no longer trade at level-independent rates within the institutional layer, and the marginal credit for every institutional axis becomes contingent on the venture state, so the nursery is paid once inside the venture score and again as a multiplier.* What the merger breaks is the interval-scale report by which committees audit published weights; the venture-contingent credit is double counting, whose value-level form Section 4 proves. It disqualifies the most popular composite as a *reporting instrument*; Section 4 asks whether *any* single score is possible.

The shape difference's economic logic drives everything downstream. A venture is a *conjunction*: every necessary condition must hold, so the informative question is "what kills this deal?", and the multiplicative ledger makes the killer visible as the smallest factor. An institution is a *portfolio*: capabilities substitute at the margin, so the informative question is "what should we build next?", and the additive ledger makes the answer visible as the largest weighted gap. Forcing either object into the other's shape destroys the information the evaluator needs: an additive venture score hides the fatal zero; a multiplicative institution score turns one weak axis into a false death sentence.

## 4. The impossibility theorem

The temptation this section formalizes is ubiquitous: a committee with both ledgers' scores wants one number per (project, institution) pair, $f(P, R, S; A) \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$, an "institution-adjusted project value" for ranking a national portfolio. We take the demand seriously and prove its target empty: admit two observable facts about how nurseries act on seeds, and no monotone merger of the ledgers represents venture value.

Honestly defined, a seed is worth what it will become under its institution's care, so $f(P, R, S; A)$ is the value function of a *nurturing environment*: $f$ is the expected discounted payoff under laws of motion whose coefficients depend on $A$ (SM-B's admissible class). Three requirements become structural properties. **C1′ (venture annihilation)**: a venture with no market or no survival ($\min(P, S) = 0$) is absorbed at zero regardless of $A$: nobody funds a dead project because its university has excellent bylaws. Realization is deliberately excluded: $R = 0$ is an early seed's normal state — an option gap funds exist to fund, not a corpse. **C2′ (institutional monotonicity)**: a better nursery never makes a live project less valuable — improving any axis improves drifts and hazards, hence value. **C4′ (continuity)**: value moves continuously in states and profiles. The defining restriction is **C3′ (causal channel)**: institutional axes enter *only* the venture's laws of motion (realization, survival, slack, exposure) and carry no direct premium at a fixed venture path; bylaws, gap funds, and mentors work by accelerating realization, improving survival, and buying runway. Section 7's Hansen-GMM overidentification test targets exactly this excluded residual (Hansen, 1982): registered, not dogma.

The restriction must face the certification evidence: prominent affiliations raise survival and speed capital acquisition (Stuart, Hoang and Hybels, 1999), entrepreneurs pay for reputable backing (Hsu, 2004), early-stage public grants causally improve subsequent private financing (Howell, 2017). But certification demonstrably moves *resource access*, trajectory consequences inside the licensed carriers; the class excludes only the residual halo at fixed trajectory, exactly what the Section 7 test estimates. And Theorem 3 relies only on the observable reversal SH: a documented direct channel would reinterpret the class, not rescue the single score (SM-B.8).

**SH (stage-contingent operability)**: capabilities bind at different stages of the journey — scouting and incubation early, licensing execution and the corporate interface late (Vohora, Wright and Lockett, 2004) — so the ranking of two institutional profiles can genuinely *reverse* across venture strata. **ED (exposure-duration asymmetry)**: institutions act over the venture's *remaining* journey, compounding strongly for an early seed and weakly for one nearly done, so the ranking of two *ventures* can reverse as their shared institution strengthens (SM-B.3 exhibits both in closed form). Both are observable, standard technology-transfer talk, and enter the registered program as testable premises.

A lemma disposes of a class unused in practice: composites $\Phi(g(P,R,S), h(A))$ *strictly* increasing in both arguments die at the death set $\min(P,S)=0$, where every institutional comparison ties at zero (Lemma SM-B.2). Fielded composites are *weakly* monotone — flat in the institution where the venture is dead — so the question is whether weakly monotone mergers survive SH and ED.

**Theorem 3** (single-index impossibility; proof in SM-B). *Let venture value be generated by any admissible nurturing environment satisfying SH (respectively ED). Then no functions $g$, $h$ and no merging rule $\Phi$ non-decreasing in its institutional argument (respectively its venture argument) represent the value ordering: $f = \Phi\big(g(P,R,S),\, h(A)\big)$ fails on a pair of live, interior states.* The argument is short enough to state. Under SH the value comparison of two institutional profiles reverses across two live venture states. A single score reads the institution through one index $h(A)$: at the early state the representation forces $h(A) > h(A')$, and monotonicity of $\Phi$ then forces the composite to rank $A$ above $A'$ at *every* venture state, including the late one, where the true ranking has reversed. No calibration escapes: nothing is assumed about $g$ and $h$ beyond existence, so the exclusion covers every weighting a committee might tune — multiplication, weighted addition, geometric means, CES mixes, min-gated and lexicographic hybrids, and every monotone recalibration. Under ED the same dichotomy runs through the venture index. A composite not monotone in its own indices defeats the purpose of scoring, so the demanded object does not exist. The committee of Section 1 is not miscalibrating an instrument; it is calibrating a nonexistent one.

Nor are the dynamics decoration: on a static domain $f = \min(\mathrm{SPS}, \kappa(A))$ survives annihilation and monotonicity; it is stage-contingency that kills it (SM-B.4). And the witnesses are interior, live states: pre-screened portfolios, not boundary pathologies.

**Corollary 3.2** (portfolio impossibility; SM-B.5). *If the portfolio spans technology domains rich enough that some institutional axis is operative for one seed's environment and inert for another's, then no environment-blind score $F(P,R,S;A)$ — separable or not — represents value across the portfolio.* The proof is immediate; the content is the domain condition: richness defeats the rule exactly as in preference aggregation (Arrow, 1963).

Table 1 disposes of the four fielded families: the three mergers each fail for their own reason; the fourth survives by not merging. *Pure addition* dies at the boundary: a dead venture at a strong host outranks a live one at a weak host; *pure multiplication* passes the boundary but falls to Theorem 3 and double-counts the channel (Theorem 2); *general monotone blends* are Theorem 3's quarry. *Gated-additive* designs — Horizon Europe's standard evaluation [verify at S6], institutional certification gates in national programs [verify at S6] — never merge the ledgers into one score: Section 7's conditional gating, fielded at scale.

**Corollary 3.1** (registered diagnostics; SM-B.6). *Let outcomes be generated by an admissible environment with operative, stage-contingent channels, and let venture strata be decision-time $\mathrm{SPS}$-quartiles — timestamped before the institution acts on the remaining journey. Then: (ii) quartile instability — within-stratum institutional slopes positive at operative strata, collapsing at the annihilated stratum, and unequal across strata, varying with each axis's operative stage under SH and scaling with remaining exposure under ED — holds as a derived proposition (SM-B.6, Proposition SM-B.2); (iii), its test form, is the cluster-robust Wald (slope-equality) test on the $\mathrm{ECR}\times\mathrm{SPS}$-stratum interactions; and (i) Simpson reversal — a pooled institution coefficient opposite in sign to the within-stratum coefficients (Simpson, 1951) — is derived under one further, auditable premise: the field funds by a single monotone score, so the observed portfolio is selected on that score, and score selection is collider conditioning that induces negative venture–institution dependence in the funded sample, with a closed-form reversal condition (SM-B.6, Lemma SM-B.3 and Proposition SM-B.1).* Signature (i) exists *because* the field ranks by one score — ranking manufactures the scar (Figure 2 illustrates the reversal geometry); conditional gating switches it off identically — while (ii) survives score-selected sampling and tests stage-contingency itself; both are registered under one family-wise error rate (SM-B.6; Section 7): diagnostics, not deterministic claims.

What survives is what committees should build instead. *Additive shortlisting* $\alpha \mathrm{SPS} + \beta \mathrm{ECR}$ remains available — not as measurement (C1′ forbids that) but as a decision aid with public political weights $(\alpha, \beta)$ and a liveness guard against ranking corpses. *Conditional gating* — rank ventures by $\mathrm{SPS}$ only where institutions clear stated thresholds ($A_7 \geq L$, say) — respects both ledgers without merging them. *Governance separation* routes project money by the venture ledger, capacity-building money by the institutional ledger. Impossibility, correctly read, is design guidance.

The result sits in a recognizable lineage: Arrow (1963) established the template — individually reasonable requirements, jointly unsatisfiable. New, to our knowledge, are the location and the mechanism: aggregation across observation levels, defeated by two observable facts about nurseries inside a registered channel structure. Evolutionary economics argued *informally* that selection environments and organizational routines are incommensurable (Nelson and Winter, 1982); Theorem 3 is that intuition made exact, with named premises and testable consequences (SM-B.9).

## 5. Operating on the two ledgers: the GO operator

The go/wait decision is *derived*, not asserted: an optimal-stopping rule whose threshold is a function of project economics, not checklist folklore. The operator is deliberately subordinate: proof that Sections 3–4's measurement leaves nothing operational on the table; the stopping mathematics is inherited, the monitored object new.

The founder–institution pair (one joint agent; SM-C flags the abstraction) chooses a founding time $\tau$:
$$\sup_{\tau} \; \mathbb{E}\Big[\, e^{-r\tau}\big( G(\sigma_\tau, F_\tau, k_\tau) - I \big) \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_0 \Big] \qquad \text{s.t.} \quad dF_t = (R_{net} - B)\,dt,\;\; F_t \ge 0,$$
with $G$ the entered venture's expected discounted value (SM-C.0), $B$ the burn rate, $R_{net}$ net revenue, $I$ the irreversible founding cost ($F=0$ absorbing); $\sigma_{SU}$ moves through regimes $S_0/S_1/S_2$, the gate $g_{TRL}(t)\in\{0,1\}$ opening at a program-set date. $S$ is non-financing survival (Section 3), so mortality counts once, funding death at the barrier; pre-entry funds are earmarked, use-it-or-lose-it money — waiting books no flow yet burns the stock; under $R_{net}<B$ the option expires. Who pays $I$ is first-order: external (E, the display) versus runway-funded (J; SM-C.0) financing reverses the runway comparative static's sign (Proposition 2(iii)) — deadline endings: forced non-entry (E), Marshallian exercise (J).

The value function $V(\sigma,F,k)$ is the unique bounded viscosity solution of a weakly coupled Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman obstacle system (Crandall, Ishii and Lions, 1992; Ishii and Koike, 1991; Pham, 2009; SM-C.1); the barrier at $F=0$ *is* the financing constraint, economics rather than technique.

**Theorem 4** (the GO operator; proof and named conditions in SM-C). *At the boundary of the continuation region, value-matching and smooth-pasting hold, and — under a single-crossing condition SC that SM-C proves when the regime layers share a common diffusion loading and otherwise states, defends, and monitors numerically, and a generic-transversality condition GEN with a checkable sufficient form — they determine a free-boundary curve $\theta_\sigma^*(k; F)$ per regime, such that the optimal founding policy is*
$$\mathrm{GO}(t, i) = \mathbb{1}\big[\sigma_{SU}(t) \geq \theta_\sigma^*(k_t; F_t)\big] \cdot g_{TRL}(t).$$
*The threshold varies continuously with the primitives where GEN holds; on the attainable range of the constructed index it is interior wherever the entry region is non-empty, and the boundary reading — "founding is never optimal in regime $k$ at that runway" — is a well-defined output of the rule, not a failure of it (SM-C exhibits computed parameterizations just outside the interior region where exactly this occurs).* At $R_{net}=B$ the curve collapses to the stationary benchmark (SM-C.2); the regime-switching machinery is established terrain (Zhang and Guo, 2004; Guo, Miao and Morellec, 2005; Jobert and Rogers, 2006; Hassett and Metcalf, 1999; Boyle and Guthrie, 2003). The monitored state is no firm-level cash flow but Section 3's constructed, auditable $\sigma_{SU}$, welded to the measurement layer: every argument of $\theta_\sigma^*$ is a rubric-disciplined observable or declared calibration object, so the rule inherits that system's audit trail and is exactly as meaningful as that system — no more.

**Proposition 2** (comparative statics as policy channels; proofs and computed signs in SM-C). *Under the named conditions: (i) $\theta_\sigma^*(k;F)$ is non-increasing in $P$ — proved globally, computed monotone. (ii) It is increasing in $B$ on the stated region; the opposing waiting-cost channel is real and is exhibited in isolation (a disclosed sweep in which extra burn hits only the waiting stage lowers the bar), while the composite sign did not reverse anywhere on the disclosed family. (iii) The funds effect is genuinely two-signed, and the computed threshold curve settles where each sign rules: under external financing of $I$ the curve over runway is dip-shaped — forced non-entry at the floor, a falling band where post-entry slack recedes exit risk, then a rise toward the perpetual threshold as runway increasingly buys option life (the Boyle–Guthrie channel: liquidity-constrained pairs rationally found earlier); under runway-funded founding the slope is positive throughout and the deadline limit is Marshallian. The earlier uniform claim $\partial\theta^*/\partial F < 0$ is withdrawn; it holds on the thin-runway band only. (iv) Persistence is regime-specific, under the now-explicit proportional-rescaling scheme: durability of the aligned regime lowers the aligned bar and — the policy-relevant cross-effect, proved under SM-C's coupling package and confirmed numerically — lowers the warming regime's bar; warming-regime persistence computes negative (unproved); dormant-regime persistence *raises* its bar — making a bad regime stickier is a founding tax, not a subsidy. (v) $\theta_\sigma^*(S_0;F) > \theta_\sigma^*(S_1;F) > \theta_\sigma^*(S_2;F)$ under regime-ordered flows, at every computed runway level.* The funds lever is deliberately *two* levers with opposite signs — dip-shaped under (E), $\partial\theta^*/\partial F<0$ only on the thin-runway band — and Section 7 rewrites the instrument catalogue. Dormant-regime stickiness is a founding tax; aligned-regime durability is a founding subsidy without a yen of transfer — credibility of the good state, not stickiness in general. Figure 3 computes everything at disclosed synthetic values (diagnostics: SM-C.5).

The gate enters multiplicatively; the factorization is a theorem *given* the deterministic, exogenous maturation assumption defended and delimited in SM-C.4, and falsifiable: the registry audits scheduled against realized gate dates. The lesson: *macro-ready but tech-not* and *tech-ready but macro-not* are distinct, non-compensable failure modes, the discipline composites cannot enforce (Section 4). SM-C's Definition C.1 fixes the trichotomy GO/WAIT/NO_GO, and Section 6 uses exactly this vocabulary.

Waiting is a priced option: the continuation value $V-(G-I)$ is what the pair *owns* by not founding, and "too early" acquires a precise meaning — exercising while $\sigma_{SU}<\theta_\sigma^*(k;F)$; patience has a funding requirement, met by Section 7's earmarked instruments.

Absent from the rule, the institutional ledger acts only through Section 4's C3′ carriers (Lemma C.5): option value is non-decreasing in the profile summary $e$, the bar non-increasing through readiness, survival, and exposure, the funding carrier buying *patience*, two-signed by Proposition 2(iii). Converse sufficiency — nothing decision-relevant escapes the ledgers — is not claimed as a theorem: within the class it holds by construction; in the world it is channel completeness (Conjecture C.1), facing Section 7's Hansen-$J$ test. The two-ledger design is, on present evidence, complete; whether the world agrees is what the registry is for.

The real-time rule runs on filtered readings — smoothed $\sigma_{SU}$, latent regime — whose error is measured, not assumed away (SM-C.6); the partial-observation extension is declared Tier-B. Nothing here estimates $\sigma_{SU}$'s process parameters, identifies regime dates, or claims predictive validation — all deferred to the registered program. The model is calibrated and exercised, not yet tested; saying so plainly is what a measurement paper owes its readers.

## 6. Retrospective calibration: eight projects

We now put numbers — deliberately coarse ones — behind the framework. The data are one deep-tech venture studio's operating records with Japanese universities and national laboratories, 2007–2026: eight projects — five with decisions taken and twenty-four-month outcomes complete, one recently founded (right-censored), and two approaching scheduled decision points (readings reported, outcomes pending). Five disclosures govern what follows. The cases are *composites*: details altered, institutions anonymized as types, fidelity maintained on the variables the model reads. The exercise is *retrospective calibration, not validation*: outcomes were known when the rubrics were scored, so agreement demonstrates organization of known history, not prediction — the registered, prospective test is Section 7's business. The sample is *selected*: one studio's pipeline, with survivorship and idiosyncrasy we do not pretend to correct at $N = 8$; the section's role is illustration under honest labels. The concordance accounting is *asymmetric by construction*: of the six decisions so far taken, four were taken by parties other than the studio — T, Y, and K, which the instrument reads, and Q, which it cannot — while the studio-involved cases (R, L, and the pending M and N) are nearly definitionally concordant — the model formalizes the studio's own decision process — so the effective externally-decided retrodiction sample is four cases, three readable, and the section should be cited at that size. And the timeline carries a *circularity we can bound but not remove*: the decisions at T, Y, K, and Q — and the studio's early involvement at R — predate any version of the model (formalized 2025–2026), while the most recent readings (L, M, N) were taken in an environment already informed by its precursors; concordance there is evidence about the model-plus-studio bundle, not an independent test.

Table 2 reports, per project: the $\sigma_{SU}$ regime at the decision point; the technology gate $g_{TRL}$; the slack state $F$; the model's reading ($\mathrm{GO}$ / $\mathrm{WAIT}$ / $\mathrm{NO\_GO}$); the decision actually taken; and a twenty-four-month outcome class (progressing / stagnant / terminated; right-censored where the decision is recent or pending). The classes carry a distinction the panel would otherwise blur: *progressing* requires documented milestone or revenue advance against the decision-date plan; *stagnant* is survival without such advance, whatever pays for it — the classifier reads what funding purchased, not where it came from (SM-D.5): R's milestone-gated grant reads progress, T's tailwind-funded persistence reads stagnation. Scores are at rubric level; calibration constants are procedure, not results (Section 3). Two agreements and one instructive tension organize the reading; two projects deserve vignettes.

The first vignette is the *zombie type*. Project T (materials; host type NU-A) sat in a domain whose macro alignment was exceptional — decarbonization policy, industrial pull, and academic output jointly holding $S_2$ at the decision date. The technology, however, had never left its originating laboratory: realization readiness was near the rubric floor ($g_{TRL} = 0$ under any defensible gate). The model reads $\mathrm{WAIT}$ — a hold on the technology axis regardless of macro alignment, because the gate multiplies rather than averages. The historical decision was to found on the tailwind's strength; the outcome was a decade of stagnation kept alive by the very tailwind that motivated the founding — a zombie. The counterfactual is the point: *any* monotone composite would have let the glowing environment compensate the missing technology — the compensation error whose portfolio-level scar Corollary 3.1 registers. The two-ledger reading, refusing the compensation, prices the case correctly ex post.

The second vignette is the *instant-fail type*. Project Y (energy; host type RU-B) presents the mirror image: a technology gate that plausibly passes, attached to a potential-scale factor that unit-economics arithmetic drives to zero at attainable performance — a margin-viability zero (Section 3's $P$ construct), not a market-size zero. Multiplicative annihilation reads the project as unfoundable at any macro state, matching the eventual assessment after real resources had been spent discovering it; the inputs were knowable at the decision date, so what history purchased was the conclusion, not the data. The instructive comparison is with T: an additive or averaged score ranks Y *above* T — decent technology, decent institution, modest macro — inverting the model's (and hindsight's) ordering. The pair exhibits Table 1's two composite failure modes with live ammunition: addition ranks corpses; unguarded compensation founds zombies.

Across the eight cases read together, the type-level pattern is consistent with the causal-channel structure — read, as SM-B.6 insists, as two different regressions. Under *decision-time stratification* (venture states fixed at the decision date), higher-$\mathrm{ECR}$ types show faster realization drift and better survival; under *trajectory conditioning* (realized trajectories held fixed), $\mathrm{ECR}$ shows no residual association — the zero C3′ requires once everything the institution transmits is recorded in the trajectory that transmitted it. The instructive tension is compositional: the stronger host types tend to hold the earlier-stage, lower-$\mathrm{SPS}$ projects — perverse as a causal reading, predicted as a selection reading: a funded portfolio is a score-selected sample in which SM-B.6's selection lemma induces exactly this negative dependence (the collider scar behind signature (i)). All three are descriptive readings of a small, selected panel; the identified versions are what the registered program tests.

An accounting is owed of what the panel does not contain. No discordant retrospective reading was omitted: the projects reported are the projects scored; no case was dropped for disagreeing with its outcome. The awkward material is in the table, and we point at it. Project Q is a *reading failure of the instrument*: its decision-point record does not support rubric scoring at the evidence standard of Section 3, and the instrument returns "insufficient evidence" rather than a forced reading — a designed output class: the honest price of an evidence-anchored rubric is that some historical cases cannot be read. Project R's GO was *marginal*: the reading sat near the entry-region boundary, close enough that modest perturbations of calibration or a single rubric level would return WAIT; we report GO because the procedure returns it, and the margin because a reader should know how little daylight it has.

The twenty-four-month window is an *early-trajectory classifier*, not a terminal outcome: by then a pre-founding decision has revealed the shape of the trajectory it produced, and that shape is all the class claims to read. Its known failure mode is the slow domain: in drug discovery or advanced materials, "stagnant at twenty-four months" can be the normal trajectory of a good project. The registry therefore carries domain-specific outcome-class definitions, and longer-horizon re-reads at forty-eight and sixty months audit the early classifier against the horizons its slowest domains run on; SM-D.5 states the definitions and the mitigation.

The limitations are short because the design already stated most of them: $N = 8$, one studio, one national ecosystem, retrospective scoring, composite disclosure. Two defects no amount of care removes from a hindsight design deserve their own clause: the panel is *single-rater* and *outcome-aware* — one scorer, who knew every completed outcome. The registry escapes them prospectively: two independent raters, blind to outcomes, reliability reported (SM-B.6.5(2)), and outcome classes adjudicated under the deposited definitions by a party other than the forecaster. What the section establishes is deliberately bounded: the two-ledger system *can* be scored from records that exist before day zero, its readings organize known outcomes without epicycles, and the composite alternatives fail on the same records in exactly the modes the theory names. What it does not establish is prediction; for that the model must put its thresholds on record *before* outcomes arrive — the program to which we now turn.

## 7. Policy implications and a research program

For evaluators the instruction is negative and liberating: retire the institution-adjusted single ranking — Theorem 3 shows the object it approximates does not exist. What replaces it is explicit structure. Where a one-dimensional shortlist is required, use $\alpha \mathrm{SPS} + \beta \mathrm{ECR}$ *as a decision aid*, liveness precondition in force, $(\alpha, \beta)$ published as the political trade-off they are. Where structure permits, prefer conditional gating: rank ventures by the venture ledger within the population whose institutions clear stated thresholds. Both devices carry their assumptions on the outside.

Neither device is untried. Horizon Europe's standard evaluation — three award criteria, each scored 0–5 with thresholds of 3 per criterion and 10 overall [verify at S6] — is a gated-additive design, Table 1's fourth family fielded at the scale of the world's largest research program. The United Kingdom's Knowledge Exchange Framework publishes perspective-level dashboards and deliberately declines to compress them into a composite ranking [verify at S6]: a national funding body operating, in production, the refusal Theorem 3 licenses. HEInnovate, the European Commission–OECD self-assessment for entrepreneurial universities, scores dimensions overlapping SM-D's axes [verify at S6] but is a self-diagnostic: no aggregation theory licenses a total, no audited evidence standard disciplines the scoring, nothing links the profile to a venture-level object. The AUTM and ASTP surveys observe activity metrics — disclosures, licenses, spin-outs — downstream of the capabilities $\mathrm{ECR}$ scores [verify at S6]: complements to a capability rubric, not substitutes. The correspondence table in SM-D.6 states the position instrument by instrument. What axiomatization adds is the aggregation license (Theorem 1), the gate (Proposition 1), the audited missing-data discipline (E3), and the causal linkage to a venture ledger (the C3′ carriers); what it borrows from KEF and Horizon Europe is the existence proof. Our devices sharpen what the best committees already grope toward — and name what those designs still get wrong: merging layers, imputing unknowns silently, leaving gates unanchored, and yielding, each budget cycle, to the ministry's demand for the composite.

For research institutions, the additive form is the point: $\mathrm{ECR}$ is a gap-diagnosis instrument, not a beauty contest. Its decomposability tells a technology-transfer office *which axis*, improved next, moves readiness most — and Proposition 1 names the binding constraint: capabilities behind a missing legal pathway are idle capacity. The gate is not a rules-first sequencing instruction: in observed practice the first deals precede and produce the rules, and bylaws drafted in advance of any deal are a known pathology, blocking the deals they were written to enable; what the gate demands is an *exercised* pathway (SM-D's level-2 anchor), scoring the residue of deal–rule co-evolution rather than its order. The temptation the ledger removes is the tournament reading: a published national $\mathrm{ECR}$ ranking would recreate at the institutional layer the pathology Theorem 3 diagnoses at the merged layer.

One deployed instrument deserves a paragraph of its own, because the model prices it exactly and the price is negative. Programs that condition their grants on incorporation within the program period are negative timing instruments: they impose an artificial deadline on the founding option, and a deadline forces exercise at the boundary — the rule collapses toward "found iff the flow is positive" (SM-C's Marshallian deadline exercise) at whatever macro state prevails: the instrument manufactures founding at $\sigma_{SU} < \theta_\sigma^*(k;F)$ by construction. This is how zombies are made at scale: the instrument pays for the premature GO; the tailwind keeps the venture alive without milestone progress — Section 6's T-type — and the program's success metric, incorporations per period, reads the damage as achievement. The repair changes the deadline's object, not the program's generosity: deadline the *milestones*, not the incorporation — tranche funds on maturation evidence and let the founding date float — or run the deadline on the earmarked pre-entry money, where expiry prices the wait correctly. A program that counts incorporations buys incorporations; a program that wants ventures should count maturation.

The composite's persistence has a reason, and it is not statistical naivety: the composite is an accountability artifact. A single published ranking hands the program officer an audit trail, while two ledgers plus deliberation reads, inside an agency, as more discretion — what agencies fear. The answer lives in the paper's own apparatus: gates are more auditable than continuous coefficients. "We fund only ventures whose institutions clear stated, published thresholds" survives an audit better than any tuned weighting, because the question "why this cutoff?" has a stated answer — the anchor sheet and the precondition argument behind Proposition 1 — whereas "why this weight?" provably has none. Public weights, liveness guards, and institutional gates carry their assumptions on the outside; that, not the absence of judgment, is what audit-defensibility means.

For policy design, Proposition 2 converts slogans into channels that do not all point the same way — the point. Instruments that read as synonyms act on *different arguments of the founding threshold*: subsidies and demand commitments raise $P$; shared facilities lower $B$; standardized, low-cost founding procedures lower $I$ — a fifth lever, cheap because administrative. Program credibility has a precise address: durability of the *aligned* regime lowers the bar in the aligned regime and — the case that matters to a founder still warming — in the regime she may switch out of; a stickier *dormant* regime raises its bar. Credibility of the good state is a founding subsidy without a yen of transfer; stickiness in general is not — though persistence is a belief object, not a dial, exercised through commitment devices whose fiscal cost is the true price of the "free" subsidy. Funding instruments require the finest distinction: the model prices two monies. *Gap funds, bridging fellowships, laboratory residence* are earmarked pre-entry money: they extend the option's life, funding a viable project's $\mathrm{WAIT}$ — the Boyle–Guthrie channel, buying patience. *Committed post-entry finance* — follow-on commitments, matching funds, a patient first customer — lowers the founding bar itself, and dominates where runway binds: Figure 3's threshold curve is dip-shaped in runway, so the same yen is a founding subsidy on one side of the dip and a patience subsidy on the other. A program that wants earlier founding should commit post-entry money; one that wants better-timed founding should fund the wait. One honesty completes the catalogue: real instruments load on several channels — a gap fund is slack and purchased maturation in one envelope, tranche design choosing the mix — so the taxonomy's value is that loading can be chosen deliberately rather than inherited by accident.

When the ministry nonetheless demands the composite, the deliverable fits in three items a program officer can lift as written. First, a venture shortlist ranked by $\mathrm{SPS}$ within the population whose institutions clear the published gates — $A_7 \geq L$, plus any program-specific axes. Second, where a single ordering is compulsory, the additive decision aid, liveness guard in force, weights published as the political trade-off they are. Third, the two-ledger budget split: project funding by the venture ledger, capacity funding by the institutional ledger, no line item requiring the number Theorem 3 forbids. The audit file answers "why these cutoffs?" with anchor sheets and "why no weights?" with a theorem — a better position than any composite has ever defended.

The research program — **Before Zero Studies** — is registered rather than promised. Its falsifiers are stated: the Corollary 3.1 diagnostics at a single family-wise error rate, with pre-registered power and equivalence bounds (SM-B.6); channel completeness (C3′) via GMM overidentification on institutional shocks; and a prospective registry in which $\mathrm{GO}/\mathrm{WAIT}/\mathrm{NO\_GO}$ readings are filed *before* outcomes arrive, scored on twenty-four-month outcome classes, with a pre-committed Brier-score floor beneath which the model is declared to have failed (Popper, 1959; Lakatos, 1970). Protocols, rubrics, and outcome definitions are deposited on the Open Science Framework; the registry opens with Section 6's studio pipeline and is built to admit other ecosystems.

The declared boundary bears repeating: deep-tech ventures, Japanese university and national-laboratory origins, the pre-founding stage. Portability — other national systems, software-dominant ventures, corporate spin-outs — is the registry's question, not this paper's claim. Within its boundary the framework is complete: measurable with instruments that exist, impossible to collapse into the single score practice keeps demanding, operable by a rule whose every parameter has an economic name.

## 8. Conclusion

Deep-tech ventures are largely decided before they legally exist, and the deciding stage has lacked a measurement theory. This paper built one: a venture ledger that multiplies because ventures die of their weakest link; an institutional ledger that adds — uniquely, by Theorem 1 — because institutions are portfolios of substitutable capabilities; a macro state that links them; and a proof (Theorem 3) that the two ledgers cannot be merged into any monotone single score once institutional capabilities act stage-contingently through the venture's dynamics. The go/wait decision emerged as an optimal-stopping rule with threshold endogenous to project economics (Theorem 4), and eight retrospectively calibrated projects illustrated the system's readings and the live failure modes of the composites it replaces.

Before day zero, value is real but unmeasured. The Before Zero Model makes it measurable — and the impossibility theorem stands guard over the practice, ensuring the two ledgers are not quietly re-merged into the convenient number that cannot exist. If the registered program falsifies the framework, the field will have learned something definite; if it survives, evaluation practice acquires foundations where it currently has habits. Either way, Before Zero Studies begins on an empirical footing rather than a rhetorical one.
